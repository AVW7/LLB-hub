# Allocatur Completion

The single most heavily tested COS skill — a "complete the allocatur" table appears in 5 of the 6 archived sittings. The allocatur is the Taxing Master's certificate recording the amount of fees and disbursements allowed on taxation, signed by the **Taxing Master** ([[Costs Terminology]]).

## The standard build-up (High Court, graduated scale)

Work down the table in this order — every paper follows the same sequence:

1. **Subtotal** fees and disbursements as drawn.
2. **Less taxed off** — subtract items/amounts disallowed.
3. **Subtotal** (fees allowed; disbursements allowed).
4. **Add drawing fee** — a percentage of the fees allowed only (never disbursements). See [[Drawing Fee and Attendance Fee Scale]] for the exact rate, which varies by sitting/court.
5. **Subtotal**.
6. **Add disbursements** (the disbursements subtotal from step 3).
7. **Subtotal** — this running total is "the bill" for attendance-fee purposes.
8. **Add attendance fee** — a *tiered* percentage: one rate on the first R10 000 of the bill, a lower rate on the second R10 000, and a lower rate again on the balance. See [[Drawing Fee and Attendance Fee Scale]].
9. **Subtotal**.
10. **Add VAT** — calculated *separately* on (a) fees allowed, (b) the drawing fee, and (c) the attendance fee. See [[VAT on Fees and Disbursements]] — VAT is never added to the disbursements line itself.
11. **TOTAL** — expressed as "Taxed and allowed in the sum of R…", signed by the Taxing Master.

## Magistrate's Court variant

The Magistrate's Court scale is **flat**, not tiered — 5% drawing fee and 5% attendance fee on the whole bill (see [[COS 2012 Special Supp]] Q5), rather than the High Court's graduated bands. Otherwise the build-up order (subtotal → taxed off → drawing fee → disbursements → attendance fee → VAT on each fee-type line → total) is identical.

## Practice note

Always check which court the question specifies (High Court vs Magistrate's Court) before picking a rate — using the wrong scale is the single easiest way to lose the calculation marks even if the method/sequence is correct.
