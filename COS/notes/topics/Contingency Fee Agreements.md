# Contingency Fee Agreements

An attorney may agree to act on a "no win, no fee" basis — governed by the **Contingency Fees Act 66 of 1997**. Two sittings phrase the rules slightly differently; learn both phrasings as given, since either could appear in a memo.

## Can an attorney accept a contingency mandate?

Yes. A client without funds to finance litigation may ask the attorney to take their fee out of the proceeds of the claim if successful, offering to pay more than the normal fee in exchange for the risk. This is permitted.

## Requirements

Must be **in writing** and **signed by the parties**.

## The fee formula — two memo phrasings

**Phrasing A** (COS 2012 Special Supp, Q4.4): the fee payable is **double the normal fee, or 25% of the capital recovered, whichever is the lesser**.

**Phrasing B** (COS Module 2.1 Practice, Q5, tracking the Contingency Fees Act 66 of 1997 more closely):

- The practitioner is **not entitled to any fee unless the client is successful**.
- If successful, the practitioner is entitled to a fee **not exceeding 100% of the normal fee** (the "normal fee" being the attorney-and-client fee that could have been taxed absent a contingency agreement).
- The **uplift** (the amount by which the fee exceeds the normal fee) may **not exceed 25% of the total amount awarded**.

Both phrasings cap the uplift at 25% of the amount recovered, and both cap the fee itself at double (100% uplift on) the normal fee — reproduce whichever phrasing the question/memo you're working from actually uses.
