# Litigious vs Non-Litigious Matters

The distinction that decides **which fee-dispute procedure applies** — taxation, or a Law Society assessment. Tested in almost every sitting, either as a definition question or embedded in a "client is unhappy with the bill" scenario.

## Definitions

- **Litigious matters** — matters which have, or may come, before a court, **excluding criminal matters**.
- **Non-litigious matters** — matters not involved with litigation.

Note the exclusion of criminal matters is deliberate and catches students out: a criminal defence matter (DUI, bigamy, robbery) still involves court appearances, but the memo consistently treats it as **non-litigious** for the purpose of this distinction, because the client's own attorney's fee is not being recovered from an opponent through the court process.

## Why the distinction matters — two different fee-dispute routes

**Litigious matter** — a dissatisfied party's remedy is **taxation** before the Taxing Master: draw the bill, serve a Notice of Taxation, attend taxation, obtain the allocatur. See [[Recovering Costs After Judgment]] and [[Taxation Procedure]].

**Non-litigious matter** — there is no taxation. The recurring memo procedure (near word-for-word across four sittings) is:

1. The client lodges a **complaint with the Law Society** having jurisdiction over the attorney.
2. A letter is written to the Law Society advising of the client's unhappiness and asking them to **assess the account**.
3. The Law Society writes to the attorney asking for a **detailed statement of account**.
4. On receipt, the Law Society forwards it to the client for **comment**.
5. The Law Society calls for a **response from the attorney**.
6. The Law Society **convenes a taxing committee**, which invites both the attorney and the client to an **assessment session** where the account is properly assessed and finalised.

## Recurring fact pattern

This exact six-step Law Society procedure is the memo answer for four separate, near-identical scenarios: an "exorbitant fee" complaint against an attorney who charged a flat all-inclusive fee for (variously) drafting a lease, a contract of sale, or defending a DUI charge. Recognise the pattern — "client unhappy with a lump-sum/flat fee, no litigation involved" — and reproduce this six-step answer regardless of the cover story.
