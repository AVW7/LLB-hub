# Costs Terminology

"Explain the following terms" is a recurring question type — near-identical term lists appear across almost every sitting. Learn these definitions verbatim; they carry easy, low-effort marks.

## Core terms

- **Costs de bonis propriis** — costs awarded against a person acting in a representative capacity (e.g. an attorney, trustee, or liquidator) as a penalty for improper conduct — dishonesty, wilfulness, or serious negligence. The representing person pays out of their own pocket, personally. Such an order can be made as between party and party, attorney and client, or attorney and own client, depending on the court and circumstances.
- **Costs in the cause** — usually ordered in interim proceedings; the party liable for costs in the main action is also liable for costs of the interim application (e.g. costs of a postponement).
- **Reserved costs** — costs the court is not yet prepared to make a finding on at a given stage of proceedings; reserved for final determination at the end of the trial.
- **Costs of the day** — additional costs caused by a postponement of proceedings and the consequent waste of the day.
- **Collection commission** — the fee an attorney is entitled to for collecting a debt from a debtor in instalments. Calculated at **10% per instalment**, subject to a maximum of **R1 000.00 per instalment** for attorneys under the Cape Law Society and **R500.00 per instalment** for all other attorneys in South Africa. Subject to VAT — see [[VAT on Fees and Disbursements]].
- **Attorney and own client costs** — the costs a client always has to pay their own attorney, usually regulated by a fee agreement between attorney and client. Remuneration an attorney is entitled to in terms of an agreement or mandate with the client.
- **Attorney and client costs** — costs and charges payable by a losing litigant in terms of an agreement between the litigants, or by court order. More generous ("honores") than party and party costs — cover all costs and charges for services reasonably performed by the attorney at the client's request for which no remuneration is recoverable as between party and party and for which the Rules make no provision.
- **Party and party costs** — costs to which a successful litigant is entitled under the prescribed tariff (Magistrate's Court or High Court); costs and charges *necessarily* incurred by the successful litigant's attorney. Only costs necessarily and properly incurred for the attainment of justice, or in defence of that party's rights — not *all* costs.
- **Allocatur** — the certificate by the Taxing Master recording the amount of fees and disbursements allowed on taxation. Signed by the Taxing Master. See [[Allocatur Completion]].
- **Drawing fee** — the fee an attorney is entitled to for drawing a bill of costs prior to taxation. See [[Drawing Fee and Attendance Fee Scale]] for the applicable rates (and the discrepancy between the textbook definitional rate and the rates actually used in worked calculations).
- **Notice of Taxation** — the notice given to the opponent of the taxation of a bill of costs before a taxing master. See [[Taxation Procedure]].

## Distinguishing the "costs due by client" vs "costs recoverable from opponent"

A recurring exam point (see [[Recovering Costs After Judgment]]): **party and party costs** are what the *losing opponent* pays; **attorney and client costs** are what the *client* owes their *own* attorney regardless of what is recovered from the other side. The client remains liable to their own attorney for any shortfall between the two.
