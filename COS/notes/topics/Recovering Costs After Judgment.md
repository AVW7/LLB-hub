# Recovering Costs After Judgment

The procedure for a successful litigant to actually collect the costs a court order awarded them — from judgment through to payment.

## The procedure (litigious matter)

Once judgment (including costs) has been granted:

1. Because this is a **litigious matter**, the practitioner draws a **Party and Party Bill of Costs**.
2. The practitioner obtains a **date of taxation** from the Clerk of the Court by drawing a **Notice of Taxation**, to which the bill is annexed. See [[Taxation Procedure]].
3. The Notice of Taxation is **served on the opponent's attorney**.
4. At the taxation, **both parties make submissions** on any disputed items in the bill.
5. The **allocatur** is calculated — see [[Allocatur Completion]].
6. The taxed sum is **immediately due**; if the paying party fails to pay, the practitioner may proceed with a **Warrant** (of execution) to recover it.

## Costs payable by the opponent vs costs payable by your own client

A recovering client is often confused about why the amount recovered from the losing opponent doesn't cover their whole bill. Explain the distinction (see [[Costs Terminology]] for full definitions):

- **Costs payable by the Defendant/opponent** = **party and party costs** — only costs properly and necessarily incurred in the course of the litigation.
- **Costs payable by your own client** = **attorney and client costs** — all fees the attorney is entitled to recover for professional services rendered, and all disbursements incurred, on the client's behalf. The client remains liable to their own attorney for any shortfall between what is recovered from the opponent and the full attorney-and-client bill.
