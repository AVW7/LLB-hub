## Q2 — Allocatur, costs in the cause, litigious vs non-litigious (5)

> [!question] Question
> 2.1 What is the allocatur and who signs it (2)? 2.2 Explain "costs in the cause" (1). 2.3 Explain the difference between litigious and non-litigious matters (2).

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 The allocatur is the certificate by the Taxing Master recording the amount of fees and disbursements allowed on taxation.
> 2.2 "Costs in the cause" means the costs follow the result in the matter.
> 2.3 Litigious matters are those which have or may come before Court, excluding criminal matters. Non-litigious matters are matters not involved with litigation.

Related: [[Allocatur Completion]], [[Litigious vs Non-Litigious Matters]], [[Costs Terminology]]

## Q3 — True/false, taxation practice (5)

> [!question] Question
> 3.1 What is the effect in the High Court if more than 20% of the number of items or value on a Bill of Costs (excluding expenses) is taxed off (1)? 3.2 May an attorney accept a deposit against future work to be done and disbursements to be incurred (1)? 3.3 What Order can the Court make if it wishes to convey its displeasure at the conduct of the attorney of record (1)? 3.4 With whom can an attorney share his/her fees (1)? 3.5 May a cost consultant who is not an attorney appear in a High Court before a taxing master and tax a bill of costs on behalf of an attorney in the latter's absence (1)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 The Taxing Master may not allow the fees for the drafting of the bill and attending taxation.
> 3.2 An attorney may accept a deposit against future work to be done and disbursements to be incurred, such amount to be deposited in trust until work done or disbursement paid.
> 3.3 De bonis propriis.
> 3.4 With any practitioner, i.e. an attorney including a correspondent.
> 3.5 No.

Related: [[Taxation Procedure]]

## Q4 — Contingency fee agreement (5)

> [!question] Question
> A client wishes to claim damages from the SAPS arising from her husband being shot and killed while an innocent bystander. She cannot fund the action and asks whether you could accept the instruction and take your fees out of the proceeds if successful, offering to pay more than your normal fee. 4.1 Are you able to accept such a mandate (1)? 4.2 What is the name of this type of agreement (1)? 4.3 What are the requirements (1)? 4.4 What formula is used to determine the fees payable under this type of agreement (2)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 Yes.
> 4.2 Contingency Fee Agreement.
> 4.3 It must be in writing and signed by the parties.
> 4.4 The formula is double the normal fee or 25% of the capital recovered, whichever is the lesser.
