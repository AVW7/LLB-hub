## Q1 — Explain the following terms (11)

> [!question] Question
> 1.1 Costs de bonis propriis (1). 1.2 Costs in the cause (1). 1.3 Collection commission (3). 1.4 Attorney and own client costs (1). 1.5 Attorney and client costs (3). 1.6 Party and party costs (2).

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 The court will award costs de bonis propriis against the person acting in a representative capacity, e.g. an attorney, trustee or liquidator. This is a penalty for improper conduct and the representing person must pay such costs out of his own pocket.
> 1.2 Costs in the cause are usually ordered in interim proceedings. The party liable for costs in the main action will then also be liable for costs in the interim application.
> 1.3 Collection commission is the fee to which an attorney is entitled when he collects a debt from a debtor in instalments. Calculated at 10% per instalment, subject to a maximum of R1 000.00 per instalment for attorneys falling under the Cape Law Society and R500.00 for all other attorneys in South Africa. Subject to VAT.
> 1.4 These are the costs which a client always has to pay his attorney, usually regulated by a fee agreement between the attorney and his client.
> 1.5 Attorney and client costs are costs and charges payable by a losing litigant in terms of an agreement between the litigants, or by order of court. More honores than party and party costs — they relate to all costs and charges for services reasonably performed by the attorney at the client's request for which no remuneration is recoverable as between party and party and for which no provision is made in the Rules.
> 1.6 Party and party costs are those costs to which a successful litigant is entitled in terms of the prescribed tariff in the Magistrate's Court or the High Court, and relate to all costs and charges necessarily incurred by the successful litigant's attorney.

## Q3 — True/false, taxation rules (3)

> [!question] Question
> 3.1 Any party wishing to tax a bill in the High Court shall give his opponent five days written notice of the taxation (1). 3.2 A Taxing Master in the High Court may only allow the qualifying expenses of an expert on taxation if there is an order of court to this effect (1). 3.3 A litigant not satisfied with any decision of a Taxing Master may appeal against that decision (1).

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 False. The High Court requires reasonable notice, not any specific time period.
> 3.2 False. The Taxing Master may also allow qualifying expenses where there is an agreement to that effect between the parties.
> 3.3 False. The Rules of Court only provide for a Review procedure.
