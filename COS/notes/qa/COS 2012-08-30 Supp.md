## Q2 — Non-litigious fee dispute (6)

> [!question] Question
> Mr L Coll previously instructed Messrs ABC Incorporated to assist him with a charge of driving under the influence; he was convicted and paid a fine. Account received: "To our fee R20 000-00; Add 14% VAT R2 800-00; Total R22 800-00; Terms strictly 30 days." Advise Mr Coll of the steps to take to ensure the fee is fair and reasonable.

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 This is a non-litigious matter.
> 2.3 A complaint has to be lodged with the Law Society having jurisdiction over Messrs ABC Incorporated. A letter is written to the Law Society advising of Mr Coll's unhappiness and asking them to assess the account. The Law Society will then write to ABC Incorporated asking for a detailed statement of account; on receipt it is forwarded to the client for comment.
> 2.4 Once the client's remarks are submitted to the Law Society, they will call for a response from ABC Incorporated, and thereafter convene a taxing committee which will invite ABC Incorporated and Mr Coll to attend an assessment session where the account will be properly assessed and finalized.
>
> (The memo's numbered point 2.2 is blank in the source document.)

## Q4 — Explain the following terms (7)

> [!question] Question
> 4.1 Cost de bonis propriis (1). 4.2 Costs in the cause (1). 4.3 Reserved costs (1). 4.4 Drawing fee (1). 4.5 Explain the difference between litigious and non-litigious matters (2). 4.6 Party and party costs (1).

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 Costs de bonis propriis are costs awarded against an attorney, administrator, or other person acting in a representative capacity where such person made himself guilty of dishonesty, wilfulness, or serious negligence. Such costs have to be paid by the attorney/administrator/representative personally.
> 4.2 Costs in the cause mean that costs at a specific point are not determined by the Court, but the winner of the case will be awarded such costs.
> 4.3 Costs are reserved when the Court is not prepared to make a finding as to who should pay costs at any stage during the proceedings. The Court will then order that costs are reserved for final determination at the end of the trial.
> 4.4 Drawing fee is the fee an attorney is entitled to when he draws a Bill of Costs prior to taxation. In the Magistrate's Court he is entitled to 5% of the total fees allowed and in the High Court he is entitled to 6.25% of the total fees allowed.
> 4.5 Litigious matters are those which have or may come before Court, excluding criminal matters. Non-litigious matters are matters not involved with litigation.
> 4.6 Party and party costs are costs incurred by a party to legal proceedings which the other party is ordered to pay. They do not include all costs, only costs necessarily and properly incurred for the attainment of justice or defending that party's rights.

> [!warning] Textbook rate discrepancy
> As in [[COS 2010-09-07 Supp]] Q4.4, this memo's 4.4 defines the drawing fee as 6.25%/5%, but Q1's worked allocatur in this same paper uses 10.6%. See [[Drawing Fee and Attendance Fee Scale]].
