## Q3 — Non-litigious fee dispute (5)

> [!question] Question
> Attorney Smart drafts a Lease Agreement for Golden Properties (Pty) Ltd (10-year tenant: the School for Legal Practice). Statement of account: fee R25 000.00 + 14% VAT R3 500.00 = R28 500.00 total. Mr Letting of Golden Properties is unhappy about the exorbitant fee and asks your advice on what steps to take to ensure the fee is fair and reasonable.

> [!success]- Answer
> This is a non-litigious matter. Therefore a complaint has to be lodged with the Law Society having jurisdiction over Attorney Smart. I will write a letter to the Law Society advising them of my client's unhappiness and asking them to assess the account of Mr Smart.
>
> The Law Society will then write to Attorney Smart asking for a detailed statement of account. Upon receipt, the Law Society will request my comments on behalf of Golden Properties.
>
> Once I have submitted the instructions of Golden Properties to the Law Society, they will call for a response from Attorney Smart and thereafter convene a taxing committee, who will then invite Attorney Smart and Golden Properties to attend an assessment session where the account will be properly assessed and finalized.

## Q4 — Recovering costs after judgment (8)

> [!question] Question
> Miss Crash is involved in a motor collision and instructs you to issue summons for damages of R82 300 against the owner and driver of the other vehicle. The matter is defended, but judgment is granted for your client for damages of R66 000 and costs.
> 4.1 Describe in detail the procedure to recover the costs from the Defendant, from the granting of judgment to receipt of payment (4).
> 4.2 You submit a statement of account to your client for fees and disbursements payable by her, but she disputes it because you have recovered costs from the Defendant. How would you explain the difference between costs payable by the Defendant and costs payable to you by your client (4)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 As this is a litigious matter, the practitioner has to draw a Party and Party bill and then obtain a date of Taxation from the Clerk of the Court by drawing a Notice of Taxation to which the bill would be annexed. The notice would then have to be served on the Defendant's attorney. At the taxation both parties will make submissions on disputed items on the bill and finally the allocatur will be calculated. The taxed sum is immediately due and should the Defendant fail to pay, the practitioner may proceed with a Warrant.
>
> 4.2 The costs paid by the Defendant in this matter are Party and party costs — costs properly and necessarily incurred in the course of litigation. Costs due by the client are Attorney and client costs — all fees an attorney is entitled to recover from his client for professional services rendered and disbursements incurred on behalf of the client.
