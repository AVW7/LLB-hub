## Q3 — Non-litigious fee dispute (5)

> [!question] Question
> Attorney X drafts a Contract of Sale for BEE Properties (Pty) Ltd and Mr Joseph Ping. Statement of account: all-inclusive fee R15 500 + 14% VAT R2 170 = R17 670 total due by BEE Properties. Mr Tshabalala of BEE Properties is unhappy about the exorbitant fee and asks your advice on what steps to take to ensure the fee is fair and reasonable.

> [!success]- Answer
> This is a non-litigious matter. Therefore a complaint has to be lodged with the Law Society having jurisdiction over Attorney X. I will write a letter to the Law Society advising them of my client's unhappiness and asking them to assess the account of Mr X.
>
> The Law Society will then write to Attorney X, asking for a detailed statement of account. Upon receipt of the detailed statement of account, the Law Society will request my comments on behalf of BEE Properties.
>
> Once I have submitted the instructions of BEE Properties to the Law Society, they will ask for a response from Attorney X and thereafter convene a taxing committee which will then invite Attorney X and BEE Properties to attend an assessment session where the account will be properly assessed and finalized.

## Q4 — Explain the following terms (5)

> [!question] Question
> 4.1 Cost de bonis propriis. 4.2 Attorney and own client costs. 4.3 Party and party costs. 4.4 Costs of the day. 4.5 Costs in the cause.

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 The court will in appropriate circumstances award costs de bonis propriis against a person acting in a representative capacity, e.g. an attorney, trustee, or liquidator. This is a penalty for improper conduct, and the representing person must pay costs out of his own pocket. Such an order can be made as between party and party, attorney and client, or attorney and own client, depending on the court and the circumstances.
> 4.2 Remuneration that an attorney is entitled to, in terms of an agreement or mandate with the client.
> 4.3 Costs which have been incurred by a party to legal proceedings, and which the other party is ordered to pay. They do not include all costs, but only costs necessarily and properly incurred, for the attainment of justice or for defending that party's rights.
> 4.4 Additional costs caused by a postponement of proceedings, and the consequent waste of the day.
> 4.5 An order usually granted in interim proceedings. The party liable for costs in the main action will then also be liable for costs of an interim application, e.g. costs of a postponement.

## Q5 — Contingency fees under the Contingency Fees Act 66 of 1997 (4)

> [!question] Question
> Explain what you understand by the term "contingency fees" in terms of the Contingency Fees Act 66 of 1997.

> [!success]- Answer
> In terms of the Contingency Fees Act 66 of 1997 a legal practitioner and his client may agree:
> - That the practitioner is not entitled to any fees unless the client is successful.
> - That in the case of success, the practitioner is entitled to his fee, not exceeding 100% of his normal fees (normal fee being the attorney-and-client fee that could be taxed in the absence of a contingency fee agreement).
> - The uplift fee (fee higher than the normal fee) will not be allowed to exceed 25% of the total amount awarded.
