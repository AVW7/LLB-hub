# Amicus Curiae

A recurring second question once standing is settled: can an interested outsider (an NGO, an association, Amnesty International, a centre for child law) join in?

## Core framework

An amicus curiae ("friend of the court") is **not a party** to the litigation and has no direct interest in the outcome, but believes the court's decision may affect its interests. It joins as a friend of the court — regardless of which side it supports, it is neither winner nor loser and is generally **not entitled to costs**. An amicus is distinct from a party that *intervenes*.

An amicus assists the court by offering information useful to deciding the matter — typically a legal opinion (an "amicus brief") or, occasionally, testimony. Admission of the brief/testimony is at the court's discretion.

**Rule 10 of the Constitutional Court Rules (Amici Curiae):**
1. With the **written consent of all parties**, any interested person may be admitted as amicus curiae on agreed terms (or terms directed by the Chief Justice).
2. Written consent must be lodged with the Registrar within 5 days, and the amicus must then comply with agreed times for lodging written argument.
3. The Chief Justice may amend the agreed terms.
4. **Without consent**, an interested person may apply to the Chief Justice to be admitted.
5. That application must be made within 5 days of the respondent's written submissions being lodged (or the time for lodging expiring), unless other time limits are directed.
6. The application must: (a) briefly describe the amicus's interest; (b) briefly identify the position it will adopt; (c) set out the submissions it will advance, their relevance, and why they will be useful and different from the other parties' submissions.
7. The amicus may lodge written argument, provided it does not repeat other parties' argument and raises new contentions useful to the Court.
8. The amicus is limited to the record on appeal/referral and facts found proved elsewhere — it may not add new facts, and (subject to rule 31) may not present oral argument.
9. The order admitting the amicus specifies the date for lodging its written argument.
10. A costs order may provide for costs incurred by or resulting from the amicus's intervention.
