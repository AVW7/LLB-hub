# Leave to Appeal to the Constitutional Court

Distinct from confirmation of invalidity (Rule 16): this is the route for appealing an ordinary constitutional-matter decision (not an invalidity order) up to the CC.

## Core framework

**Rule 19 of the Constitutional Court Rules** applies to an application for leave to appeal where a decision on a constitutional matter — **other than an order of constitutional invalidity under s172(2)(a)** — has been given by any court, including the SCA, irrespective of whether the President (of the SCA) refused leave or special leave to appeal.

1. A litigant aggrieved by such a decision who wishes to appeal directly to the CC must, within **15 days** of the order (or, where leave was refused, from the date of that refusal), and after giving notice to the other party, lodge an application for leave to appeal with the Registrar.
2. The application must be signed by the applicant/legal representative and contain: (a) the decision appealed and grounds of dispute; (b) a statement of the constitutional matter raised, plus any connected issues; (c) supplementary information/argument; (d) a statement on whether leave/special leave has been sought elsewhere, and the outcome if known.
3. The respondent may respond in writing within **10 days**, indicating whether the application is opposed and on what grounds.
4. A respondent wishing to **cross-appeal** must lodge that application within 10 days; ordinary appeal rules apply with necessary modification.
5. The Court decides whether to grant leave — this may be dealt with summarily (no oral/written argument beyond the application) or the Court may direct that argument deal with both leave *and* the merits.
