# Confirmation of Constitutional Invalidity

A High Court (or SCA) declaration that legislation is unconstitutional has **no immediate force** — a recurring trap in these papers.

## Core framework

**s172(2)(a) Constitution:** An order of constitutional invalidity made by a court other than the Constitutional Court (i.e. the High Court or SCA) **has no force or effect unless it is confirmed by the Constitutional Court**.

**Rule 16 of the Constitutional Court Rules — Confirmation of an order of constitutional invalidity:**
1. The Registrar of the court that made the order must, within 15 days, lodge a copy with the CC Registrar.
2. A person/organ of state wishing to **appeal** against the order (s172(2)(d)) must lodge a notice of appeal within 15 days.
4. A person/organ of state wishing to **apply for confirmation** must lodge that application within 15 days.
5. If neither a notice of appeal nor a confirmation application is lodged within time, the matter is disposed of per directions from the Chief Justice.

**Practical consequence:** until confirmed, the declared-invalid provision remains in force. If interim protection is needed pending confirmation, apply for a **temporary interdict** to regulate the position.

The Constitutional Court itself, when confirming an order, has discretion over remedy — e.g. in *Nyathi* the order of invalidity was confirmed but the declaration of invalidity was **suspended for 12 months** to give Parliament time to legislate an effective enforcement mechanism.
