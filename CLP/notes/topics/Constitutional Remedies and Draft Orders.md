# Constitutional Remedies and Draft Orders

Once infringement and (if relevant) invalidity are established, the exam pattern turns to: what relief, and can you draft the order?

## Core framework

**s38 Constitution:** on a successful Bill of Rights claim, the court "may grant appropriate relief, including a declaration of rights."

**s172(1)(b) Constitution:** in constitutional matters generally, a court may make "any order that is just and equitable" — this includes an order limiting the retrospective effect of an invalidity finding, or suspending a declaration of invalidity to allow the defect to be corrected.

**What counts as "appropriate": *Fose v Minister of Safety and Security* 1997 (3) SA 786 (CC)** — appropriateness depends on the circumstances of each case. It could be a declaration of rights, an interdict, a mandamus, "or such other relief as may be required to ensure that the rights enshrined in the Constitution are protected and enforced." Per Kriegler J, the court must "carefully analyse the nature of [the] constitutional infringement, and strike effectively at its source" — the remedy must be **effective**, not merely symbolic.

**Subsidiarity — direct reliance on the Constitution is disfavoured where an adequate common-law or statutory remedy exists.** *Fose* para 98 sets its face against direct constitutional relief (e.g. constitutional damages) where an ordinary remedy — such as the *actio iniuriarum* for personality-rights infringements — already provides adequate protection (see also *Dendy v University of the Witwatersrand*).

**Typical remedy toolkit seen across the papers:**
- Interim/interdictory relief (e.g. interdicting a party from proceeding pending the outcome of litigation).
- A declaratory order (e.g. declaring conduct unlawful/unconstitutional, or declaring the basis on which future conduct will be lawful).
- A mandamus compelling a functionary to perform an outstanding duty (e.g. compelling a hearing where audi alteram partem was denied).
- Costs orders — including punitive scales, or joining an additional party (e.g. a mining company) as a respondent so relief can bind it too.

**Drafting a court order:** state the substantive declaration first (what was unlawful/unconstitutional and which sections were infringed), then ancillary relief (costs, directions for implementation/notice to relevant bodies).
