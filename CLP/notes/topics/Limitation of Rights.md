# Limitation of Rights

The backbone of almost every CLP problem: identify the right infringed, then run the s36 limitation test to see whether the infringement is justified.

## Core framework

**s36(1) Constitution — Limitation of rights**

> The rights in the Bill of Rights may be limited only in terms of law of general application to the extent that the limitation is reasonable and justifiable in an open and democratic society based on human dignity, equality and freedom, taking into account all relevant factors, including—
> (a) the nature of the right;
> (b) the importance of the purpose of the limitation;
> (c) the nature and extent of the limitation;
> (d) the relation between the limitation and its purpose; and
> (e) less restrictive means to achieve the purpose.

**s36(2):** Except as provided in subsection (1) or elsewhere in the Constitution, no law may limit any entrenched right.

**Standard two-step structure used throughout the memos:**
1. Identify which right(s) in the Bill of Rights are infringed (e.g. dignity s10, life s11, freedom and security/no cruel or inhuman punishment s12(1)(d)-(e), privacy s14, religion s15/31, equality s9, freedom of trade/occupation/profession s22).
2. Apply the s36(1) factors to test whether the limitation is reasonable and justifiable — this is where the case law and the specific facts (nature of the right, severity, less restrictive alternatives) are argued.

## Key case law appearing in the archive

- ***S v Makwanyane*** — capital punishment held inconsistent with the (interim) Constitution; the value placed on human dignity carried through into the 1996 Constitution as both a right and a founding value; the right to life has no textual exceptions but remains subject to s36.
- ***Nyathi v MEC for the Department of Health, Gauteng*** — s3 of the State Liability Act (barring execution against State property) tested against ss8, 9, 10, 34, 165(5) and 195(1)(f); found to unjustifiably differentiate between judgment creditors against the State and against private litigants, and to undermine the right of access to courts and the binding nature of court orders.
- ***Minister of Justice and Constitutional Development v Prince* [2018] ZACC 30** — private cultivation/use of cannabis for personal consumption; laws criminalising it held an unjustifiable limitation of the right to privacy (s14). See [[Right to Privacy and the Prince Judgment]] for the full application.
- ***Fose v Minister of Safety and Security*** — appropriate relief must "carefully analyse the nature of the constitutional infringement, and strike effectively at its source." See [[Constitutional Remedies and Draft Orders]].
