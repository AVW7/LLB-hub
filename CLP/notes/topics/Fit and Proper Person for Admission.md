# Fit and Proper Person for Admission

A narrower, profession-specific application of the limitation clause: can a Law Society refuse to register articles because of an applicant's admitted, ongoing unlawful conduct?

## Core framework

**Attorneys Act 53 of 1979:**
- **s2(1A)(a)** — registration of a Contract of Articles of Clerkship with the relevant Law Society.
- **s5(2)** — a candidate must disclose relevant matters (e.g. previous convictions) as part of registration.

**The "fit and proper person" argument (Law Society's side):** a person with previous convictions who **declares an intention to continue breaking the law** may be found not to be a fit and proper person for admission — the concern is that continuing unlawful conduct (e.g. ongoing possession/use of a prohibited substance) would bring the attorneys' profession into disrepute. This applies regardless of the underlying motivation (e.g. religious use) as long as the conduct itself remains unlawful.

**Countervailing constitutional rights the applicant may raise** against a refusal to register:
- Freedom of religion — ss15(1) and 31(1) (right to practise one's religion and enjoy one's culture/religion in community with others).
- Dignity — s10.
- Right to choose one's trade, occupation or profession freely — s22.
- Equality / unfair discrimination — s9.

These are then run through the ordinary [[Limitation of Rights]] s36 analysis — the Law Society's justification is protecting the integrity and reputation of the profession against a person who intends to keep breaking the law.
