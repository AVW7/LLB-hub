# Best Interests of the Child

Comes up where a constitutional remedy against a parent (here, forfeiture of the family home) has knock-on effects for children who are not themselves parties.

## Core framework

**s28 Constitution — Children**, key provisions: every child has the right to family/parental care (or appropriate alternative care); basic nutrition, shelter, health care and social services; protection from maltreatment, neglect, abuse or degradation; protection from exploitative labour practices; and (s28(1)(h)) a legal practitioner assigned at state expense in civil proceedings affecting the child if substantial injustice would otherwise result. **s28(2): "A child's best interests are of paramount importance in every matter concerning the child."** s28(3) defines "child" as a person under 18.

**Case law:** *S v M (Centre for Child Law as amicus curiae)* 2008 (3) SA 232 (CC) — law enforcement must always be child-sensitive, and courts must show due regard for children's rights at all times; a child "cannot be treated as a mere extension of his or her parents, umbilically destined to sink or swim with them" — children's needs deserve separate, focused consideration. Also referenced: *Minister of Welfare and Population Development v Fitzpatrick*.

**Onus to raise children's interests:**
- Parents are expected to invoke their children's interests in proceedings affecting them.
- But state institutions bear responsibility for this too, **even where parents fail to raise it**.
- The High Court is the **upper guardian of children**, obliged to uphold constitutional rights and values independently.
- Officers of the court (e.g. the NDPP) are expected to assist the court with all relevant information.
- A parent's failure to raise the children's interests (or manipulation of the children's situation to suit the parents' own objectives) **may not be held against the children**.

**Children's Act 38 of 2005** (supplements the Bill of Rights per s8 of the Act — binds organs of state):
- **s7** — the best-interests-of-the-child factors to weigh where relevant: relationship with parents/caregivers; parental attitude and capacity; the likely effect of any change in the child's circumstances (including separation from parents/siblings); practical difficulty/expense of maintaining contact; the need to remain with family and maintain cultural/family connection; the child's age, maturity, gender, background; physical/emotional security and development; disability or chronic illness; the need for a stable family environment; protection from physical/psychological harm and exposure to violence; family violence involving the child; and which outcome minimises further legal/administrative proceedings.
- **s9** — best interests standard applies in all matters concerning a child's care, protection and well-being.
- **s10** — a child of sufficient age/maturity has the right to participate, and their views must be given due consideration.
- **s47(1)** — if it appears to a court that a child involved in/affected by proceedings is in need of care and protection, the court must refer the question to a designated social worker for investigation.
