# Jurisdiction in Constitutional Matters

A recurring trap question: can a Magistrate's Court decide constitutionality? No — and knowing exactly why (and where to go instead) is worth easy marks.

## Core framework

**s170 Constitution:** A court of a status lower than the High Court (i.e. a Magistrate's Court) **may not enquire into or rule on the constitutionality of any legislation or of any conduct of the President**.

**s110 Magistrates' Courts Act:** confirms that a Magistrate's Court is not competent to pronounce on or deal with the validity of any law or conduct of the President — any such challenge raised before it must be dealt with as though the law/conduct were constitutionally valid.

**s173 Constitution:** the Constitutional Court, SCA and High Courts have the inherent power to protect and regulate their own process, and to develop the common law.

**Practical consequence:** the competent court to decide a constitutional challenge as a court of first instance is the **High Court** having jurisdiction. A client instructed at Magistrate's Court level whose defence turns on the constitutionality of the enabling legislation must be advised to approach the High Court instead.
