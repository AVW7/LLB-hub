# Locus Standi

Section 38 standing is the opening gate in almost every CLP fact pattern — before arguing the merits, establish who may approach the court.

## Core framework

**s38 Constitution — Enforcement of rights**

> Anyone listed in this section has the right to approach a competent court, alleging that a right in the Bill of Rights has been infringed or threatened, and the court may grant appropriate relief, including a declaration of rights. The persons who may approach a court are—
> (a) anyone acting in their own interest;
> (b) anyone acting on behalf of another person who cannot act in their own name;
> (c) anyone acting as a member of, or in the interest of, a group or class of persons;
> (d) anyone acting in the public interest; and
> (e) an association acting in the interest of its members.

- **Own interest (s38(a))** — the classic case: a client personally charged/affected under the impugned provision.
- **On behalf of another (s38(b))** — for someone who cannot act in their own name.
- **Class/group interest (s38(c))** — a readily identifiable class (e.g. property owners along a river).
- **Public interest (s38(d))** — protection of a right that benefits present and future generations (used for environmental rights, s24).
- **Association interest (s38(e))** — a voluntary association can act for its own members where that is one of its constitutional objects.

**Standing under the Bill of Rights is broader than ordinary civil standing.** *Ferreira v Levin NO* is cited for the proposition that the narrow common-law restrictions on standing do not apply to Chapter 2 litigation — Chapter 2 standing is deliberately wide to protect a broader spectrum of people and rights.

Foreigners are not excluded — see [[Application of the Bill of Rights to Foreigners]].
