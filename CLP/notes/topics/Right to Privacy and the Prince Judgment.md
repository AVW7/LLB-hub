# Right to Privacy and the Prince Judgment

The 2019 assignment is built entirely around one Constitutional Court judgment — know it in detail rather than in outline.

## Core framework

***Minister of Justice and Constitutional Development and Others v Prince; National Director of Public Prosecutions and Others v Rubin; National Director of Public Prosecutions and Others v Acton and Others* [2018] ZACC 30**

**s14 Constitution — right to privacy:**
> Everyone has the right to privacy, which includes the right not to have—
> (a) their person or home searched;
> (b) their property searched;
> (c) their possessions seized; or
> (d) the privacy of their communications infringed.

**Holding:**
- The use of cannabis **in private, by an adult, for personal consumption** is legal. Laws criminalising the cultivation of cannabis for this purpose unjustifiably limit the right to privacy (para 58) — tested against the s36 limitation clause (see [[Limitation of Rights]]).
- The court gave a **wide interpretation** to "cultivation of cannabis in private by an adult for personal consumption in private": a garden, a private dwelling, or any enclosure/room qualifies, provided it can genuinely be said to be private (para 85).
- **Not permitted:** smoking openly in public. If cannabis is found on a person, it is within **SAPS's discretion** whether the quantity indicates possession for personal consumption (para 111).
- A place open to the public — e.g. a commercial nursery — is **not** a private dwelling. Cultivating or selling cannabis there remains an offence (para 88); a person **selling** cannabis to the public (or selling material, like a cookbook, that promotes and facilitates its use/sale) is also on the wrong side of the line. **Buyers** from such a seller also commit an offence (para 88).
- The order is **not retrospective** (para 102) — it does not excuse conduct that was criminal before the judgment.
- The declaration of invalidity was **suspended for 24 months**, but the Court granted **interim relief**: private use and cultivation for personal consumption in private is lawful in the interim, pending Parliament's amending legislation.

## Applying it (worked pattern from the 2019 assignment)

- Growing a small amount of cannabis in one's own backyard for personal use → **lawful** (private dwelling, personal consumption).
- Smoking it at home → **lawful**.
- Introducing a "cannabis section" at a nursery open to the public, for cultivation/consumption on the premises → **unlawful** (not a private space).
- Selling cannabis plants/products to customers, or a cannabis cookbook intended to encourage cannabis use and sales → **unlawful**.
- Customers who buy from such a seller also commit an offence.
- Past convictions/conduct before the judgment are not retrospectively excused.
