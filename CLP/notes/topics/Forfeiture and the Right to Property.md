# Forfeiture and the Right to Property

Tested through a single, dense 100-mark assignment built around a family home used to run an unlicensed drug operation — but the POCA/s25 framework it uses is self-contained and worth knowing on its own.

## Core framework

**Prevention of Organised Crime Act 121 of 1998 (POCA)** — general objectives: combat organised crime, money laundering and criminal gang activity; ensure recovery of proceeds of unlawful activity; provide for **civil forfeiture** of criminal property.

- **s48(1)** — the NDPP may apply to the High Court for a forfeiture order where a **preservation order** is already in force over the property.
- **s50(1)(a)** — the High Court **must** grant the forfeiture order applied for (subject to s52) if satisfied, on a balance of probabilities, that the property is an "instrumentality of an offence" referred to in Schedule 1.
- **s1** — "instrumentality of an offence" = any property concerned in the commission or suspected commission of an offence, before or after POCA commenced, in or outside the Republic.
- **Schedule 1** lists qualifying offences, including (Item 33) any offence punishable by imprisonment exceeding one year without the option of a fine.

**s25 Constitution** — guarantee against arbitrary deprivation of property. Forfeiture must survive a **proportionality enquiry**: *Mohunram v National Director of Public Prosecutions* [2007] ZACC 4 para 56 — the purpose of the enquiry is to determine whether the forfeiture order would amount to an arbitrary deprivation contrary to s25(1). Relevant factors: how central the property was to the offence (repeated, sustained use vs isolated incident); whether conventional law enforcement (repeated police intervention, a preservation order) had already failed to stop the conduct; whether the property is a **residential home** (weighs against forfeiture) as opposed to a purely commercial/uninhabited building; and the impact on vulnerable occupants (children, the elderly).

**Where forfeiture leads to eviction — PIE overlay:** the Prevention of Illegal Eviction from and Unlawful Occupation of Land Act 19 of 1998 (PIE) gives effect to **s26(3)** of the Constitution — eviction may only be ordered when just and equitable, after considering all relevant circumstances. A forfeiture order under POCA and an eviction order under PIE are **separate enquiries governed by separate statutes** — a forfeiture finding does not pre-empt the PIE enquiry if and when eviction is later sought.

**s205(3) Constitution** — underlying constitutional duty on police to prevent, combat and investigate crime (see *Carmichele v Minister of Safety and Security*; *F v Minister of Safety and Security* [2011] ZACC 37).
