# CLP — 26 June 2012 (Special Supp)

Source question paper: [[CLP26Jun2012Q.doc]]
Source marking memo: [[CLP26Jun2012A.doc]]

Facts: the Vaal River Residents Association, a voluntary association whose constitution commits it to protecting the environmental integrity of the area, objects to Kole Mining (Pty) Ltd's proposed open-cast coal mine — which would destroy a filtering wetland, harm protected species, cause pollution, and depress property values. The Director of Mineral Development: Gauteng refused the association a hearing, saying the Minerals Act imposes no such obligation, and cited the need for cheap, sustainable coal for electricity.

## Q1 — Locus standi of the association (5)

> [!question] Question
> Does the association have locus standi, and on what grounds, to bring an application to stop the mining operations?

> [!success]- Answer
> Yes, on all four available s38 grounds: (a) own interest — its constitution commits it to protecting the area's environmental integrity; (c) interest of a class/group — the identifiable class of property owners near the Vaal River; (d) public interest — s24 of the Constitution protects the environment for present and future generations and for people's health/well-being; (e) interest of its members — all of whom are property owners in the area.

Related: [[Locus Standi]]

## Q2 — Arguments to oppose the mining licence (5)

> [!question] Question
> What arguments could the association raise to oppose the granting of a mining licence to Kole Mining?

> [!success]- Answer
> The mining would destroy the wetland (which filters water for human consumption), destroy bird/plant/animal life, cause pollution and ecological degradation, and reduce water quality. Separately, the Director's failure to follow *audi alteram partem* violates the right to just administrative action (s33, read with s3 of PAJA), and the licence could depress property values.

Related: [[Limitation of Rights]]

## Q3 — Intervention by another interested body (5)

> [!question] Question
> Would any other institution or organisation with an interest in the matter, but not a party to it, be permitted to intervene, and if so, how?

> [!success]- Answer
> Yes — as an amicus curiae: not a party (no direct interest in the outcome), joins as a "friend of the court," neither winner nor loser, generally no costs order in its favour. The process is set out in **Rule 10** of the Constitutional Court Rules.

Related: [[Amicus Curiae]]

## Q4 — The Director's response and s36 (5)

> [!question] Question
> Acting for the Director, if the court finds the licence infringes the association's constitutional rights, what arguments would you raise in response? Advise on prospects of success, with reference to the applicable Bill of Rights section(s).

> [!success]- Answer
> Run the full **s36** limitation-clause analysis, addressing all of s36(1)'s factors. The State could argue it has an obligation to secure a cheap, sustainable coal supply for electricity production, and that the association's rights may be limited to achieve that. The memo assesses this argument as **unlikely to succeed**.

Related: [[Limitation of Rights]]

## Q5 — Remedies available to the association (5)

> [!question] Question
> Set out the remedies that the association could ask for against the respondent.

> [!success]- Answer
> s38 requires "appropriate relief" — per *Fose v Minister of Safety and Security* 1997 (3) SA 786 (CC), appropriateness depends on the facts; it may be a declaration of rights, an interdict, a mandamus, "or such other relief as may be required." s172(1)(b) empowers the court to make "any order that is just and equitable." Since the Director failed to apply *audi alteram partem*, and per Kriegler J in *Fose* the remedy must "strike effectively at its source," the effective remedy here is an **interdict in the nature of a mandamus** compelling the Director to hear the association's objection, plus an interdict restraining the grant of any mining licence pending that hearing. The mining company should be joined as a second respondent and interdicted from mining pending the review. The association should be entitled to costs.
