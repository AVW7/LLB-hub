# CLP — July 2012 Assignment

Source question paper: [[CLPJul2012Q.doc]]
Source marking memo: [[CLPJul2012A.doc]]

Facts: Peter and Lilly Stander (married in community of property) live with their three children at a house that Peter, a pharmacist suspended for substance addiction, has been using to illegally acquire, store and sell scheduled substances. Police attended 37 times; children were removed from the house on three occasions; the Director-General of Health ordered the pharmacy closed. The NDPP obtained a preservation order and then a forfeiture order over the property under s50(1)(a) of POCA. Stander argues his conduct isn't "organised crime" and that forfeiture is disproportionate; the High Court disagreed. He now wants an opinion on his prospects before the Constitutional Court. This is the richest single paper in the module — 11 sub-questions, heavily integrated across standing, limitation, property, remedies and children's rights.

## Qi — Interested parties and who may approach the CC (10)

> [!question] Question
> Which parties have an interest in the proposed litigation? Which parties are allowed to approach the Constitutional Court where it is alleged that a right in the Bill of Rights has been infringed or threatened?

> [!success]- Answer
> Peter and Lilly Stander, and the National Director of Public Prosecutions, are the interested parties. s38 lists who may approach a court alleging a Bill of Rights infringement: (a) anyone acting in their own interest; (b) anyone acting on behalf of another who cannot act in their own name; (c) anyone acting as a member of, or in the interest of, a group or class; (d) anyone acting in the public interest; (e) an association acting in the interest of its members.

Related: [[Locus Standi]]

## Qii — Relevant POCA sections (10)

> [!question] Question
> Which parts or sections of POCA are relevant to the issues?

> [!success]- Answer
> POCA's general objectives are to combat organised crime, money laundering and criminal gang activity, ensure recovery of proceeds of unlawful activity, and provide for civil forfeiture of criminal property used to commit an offence. The Part 3, Chapter 6 forfeiture provisions are central: **s50(1)(a)** — the High Court must order forfeiture if satisfied on a balance of probabilities the property is an "instrumentality of an offence" in Schedule 1; **s1** defines "instrumentality of an offence"; **s48(1)** empowers the NDPP to apply for forfeiture where a preservation order is already in force (as here); **Schedule 1** Item 33 covers any offence punishable by imprisonment exceeding one year without the option of a fine.

Related: [[Forfeiture and the Right to Property]]

## Qiii — Applicable constitutional sections and conflicting values (10)

> [!question] Question
> Which sections in the Constitution or Bill of Rights are applicable to this matter? Are there any constitutional values/provisions in conflict?

> [!success]- Answer
> s205(3) — the constitutional duty on police to prevent, combat and investigate crime (*F v Minister of Safety and Security* [2011] ZACC 37; *Carmichele v Minister of Safety and Security*); the POCA forfeiture provisions; s25 — the guarantee against arbitrary deprivation of property; the best interests of the affected children; s26 — the right of access to adequate housing, including that no one may be evicted without a court order after considering all relevant circumstances, and no legislation may permit arbitrary evictions; s28 — children's rights, including protection from arbitrary deprivation affecting them.

Related: [[Forfeiture and the Right to Property]], [[Limitation of Rights]]

## Qiv — Is the Pharmacy Act licensing regime unconstitutional? (10)

> [!question] Question
> Evaluate whether the sections of the Pharmacy Act and its Regulations prohibiting Mr Stander from practising without a licence are unconstitutional. What is the test when legislation limiting Bill of Rights rights is considered?

> [!success]- Answer
> Apply **s36**: rights may only be limited in terms of law of general application, to the extent the limitation is reasonable and justifiable, taking into account the nature of the right, the importance of the purpose of the limitation, the nature and extent of the limitation, the relation between the limitation and its purpose, and less restrictive means to achieve the purpose.

Related: [[Limitation of Rights]]

## Qv — Is POCA forfeiture immune to constitutional challenge? (10)

> [!question] Question
> Evaluate whether the provision(s) allowing for forfeiture of property by the State under POCA is immune to constitutional challenge.

> [!success]- Answer
> No — the CC and SCA require a proportionality enquiry: *Mohunram v NDPP* [2007] ZACC 4 para 56 — the purpose of the proportionality enquiry is to determine whether forfeiture amounts to an arbitrary deprivation contrary to s25(1). Forfeiture here effectively results in an eviction, so all relevant circumstances (needs of the elderly, children, etc.) must be considered per s4(6)/(7) of PIE. On these facts: the property was used for crime over a sustained period; conventional law enforcement (37 police interventions) and a preservation order both failed to stop it; forfeiture targets the instrumentality of the crime, not punishment. But forfeiture would leave the family homeless, and it matters that this is a residential home, not an uninhabited building. PIE's requirements (eviction only just and equitable after considering all circumstances) are important to the proportionality enquiry, but the forfeiture enquiry and a later PIE eviction enquiry remain **separate, under separate statutes** — a forfeiture order doesn't pre-decide the eviction question.

Related: [[Forfeiture and the Right to Property]]

## Qvii — May Stander rely directly on the Constitution? (10)

> [!question] Question
> May Mr Stander directly rely on the Constitutional provisions and/or rights in the Bill of Rights when applying to the Constitutional Court for relief?

> [!success]- Answer
> No — direct application of the Constitution is undesirable where an adequate remedy already exists. In *Fose v Minister of Safety and Security* 1997 (3) SA 786 (CC) para 98, the CC set its face against direct constitutional relief where an adequate common-law or statutory remedy exists; the *actio iniuriarum* is appropriate for personality-rights preservation (see also *Dendy v University of the Witwatersrand*).

Related: [[Constitutional Remedies and Draft Orders]]

## Qviii — Legal implications for the children (10)

> [!question] Question
> What are the legal implications for the children, and how should this issue be dealt with?

> [!success]- Answer
> Removal from their family, and eviction from the family home. s28(2) (read with s28(1)) makes a child's best interests of paramount importance in every matter concerning the child; law enforcement must be child-sensitive and courts must show due regard for children's rights (*S v M (Centre for Child Law as amicus curiae)* 2008 (3) SA 232 (CC); *Minister of Welfare and Population Development v Fitzpatrick*). A child cannot be treated as a mere extension of the parents, "umbilically destined to sink or swim with them" — children's needs deserve separate, focused consideration.

Related: [[Best Interests of the Child]]

## Qix — Onus to raise the children's interests (5)

> [!question] Question
> On which party is the onus to consider the interests of the minor children?

> [!success]- Answer
> Parents are expected to invoke their children's interests, but state institutions bear responsibility for this too, even where parents don't raise it. The High Court is the upper guardian of children and is obliged to uphold constitutional rights and values. Officers of the court, such as the NDPP, are expected to assist the court with all relevant information. A parent's failure to emphasise the children's interests (or manipulation of their situation) may not be held against the children.

Related: [[Best Interests of the Child]]

## Qx — Standard for considering the children's interests (10)

> [!question] Question
> How, or in what manner, must the interests of the children be considered? What is the test or standard?

> [!success]- Answer
> The "best interests of the child" standard, per s28 of the Constitution and the **Children's Act 38 of 2005**. s7 of the Act lists the factors to weigh where relevant: relationship with parents/caregivers, parental attitude and capacity, likely effect of any change in circumstances (including separation), practical contact difficulties, the need to remain with family/extended family/culture, the child's age/maturity/gender/background, physical/emotional security and development, disability/chronic illness, need for a stable family environment, protection from harm and exposure to violence, family violence involving the child, and which outcome minimises further proceedings. s8 confirms the Act's rights supplement the Bill of Rights and bind organs of state; s9 makes best interests paramount in all matters concerning care/protection/well-being; s10 gives a sufficiently mature child a right to participate. s47(1) requires a court to refer a child appearing to need care and protection to a designated social worker for investigation.

Related: [[Best Interests of the Child]]

## Qxi — Could an amicus curiae intervene? (5)

> [!question] Question
> Is it foreseen that an amicus curiae may intervene in this matter? What is an amicus curiae and what are the requirements for such intervention?

> [!success]- Answer
> Yes, on behalf of the children — e.g. the **Centre for Child Law**, the Community Law Centre, or a Human Rights Centre. An amicus curiae is not a party to the litigation; it assists the court with information (a legal opinion/"amicus brief," or testimony) useful to deciding the matter, admitted at the court's discretion. Governed by **Rule 10** of the Constitutional Court Rules (Part V, Amici Curiae) — as summarised in [[Amicus Curiae]]. Because the Constitution obliges the court to consider the children's best interests before a final forfeiture determination, this could extend to appointing a **curator ad litem** to report on the impact of forfeiture on the children, grounded in s28(2).

Related: [[Amicus Curiae]], [[Best Interests of the Child]]
