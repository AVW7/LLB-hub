## Q(a)+(b) — Rights at issue and the Harksen v Lane test (15 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Ms Anna Smith applied for admission to Pretoria Boys High School, a state school with a boys-only policy; she was told significant changes (e.g. bathrooms/change rooms) would be required to admit girls, and that the school believes it is not acting unfairly. There is a girls' school 20 minutes away, but Smith lives next door to the boys' school and wants to take Drama and Dance, not offered at the girls' school.
> (a) Explain to Ms Anna Smith which of her constitutional rights may be at issue (5). (b) Apply the criteria laid down by the Constitutional Court in *Harksen v Lane NO* 1998 (1) SA 300 (CC) (10).

> [!note]- Self-check pointers (not an official memo)
> The equality right (s9) is the obvious candidate given the sex-based exclusion; consider also whether access-to-education-type considerations are relevant on these facts. For (b), verify the actual *Harksen* stages against your prescribed materials — this hub has no memo confirming which elements/emphasis the assessors expected. See [[Equality and the Harksen Test]] for what is and isn't confirmed by the source documents.

## Q(c) — NGO joining the proceedings (10 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Assume that an NGO advocating the rights of women would like to make submissions to the High Court on the issue of gender equality. (i) How would they enter these proceedings (1)? (ii) Which Constitutional Court Rule would be applicable (1)? (iii) List the submissions this NGO would have to make to succeed in its application to be heard (8).

> [!note]- Self-check pointers (not an official memo)
> This is the standard amicus curiae route — Rule 10 of the Constitutional Court Rules. See [[Amicus Curiae]] for the full requirements (description of interest, position to be adopted, submissions and their relevance/usefulness, consent-based or Chief-Justice-application-based admission).
