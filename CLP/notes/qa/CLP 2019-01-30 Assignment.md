# CLP — 30 January 2019 Assignment

Source question paper: [[CLP30Jan2019AssQ.docx]]
Source marking memo: [[CLP30Jan2019AssA.docx]]

Facts: Mr X owns and operates a nursery specialising in rare plants, trees and herbs. He has used cannabis daily for ten years and believes in its medicinal benefits. He plans to (1) grow a small amount of cannabis in his backyard for personal use, (2) write and sell a cannabis cookbook from his nursery from June 2019, with recipe ingredients also sold at the nursery, and (3) introduce a "cannabis section" at the nursery from April 2019, selling cannabis plants/species for on-site consumption or home cultivation. He wants a full legal opinion.

## Q — Full legal opinion on Mr X's cannabis plans (25)

> [!question] Question
> Write a full legal opinion for Mr X advising on the legality of his past actions and future plans. Deal with: (i) case law; (ii) his rights under the Bill of Rights; (iii) balance of rights; (iv) past use of cannabis; (v) use for personal consumption; (vi) the cultivation and sale of cannabis both at home and at the nursery.

> [!success]- Answer
> Governing authority: **Minister of Justice and Constitutional Development and Others v Prince; NDPP and Others v Rubin; NDPP and Others v Acton and Others** [2018] ZACC 30.
>
> **Right in issue — s14 (privacy):** everyone has the right to privacy, including the right not to have their person or home searched, property searched, possessions seized, or communications infringed.
>
> **Limitation — s36(1):** rights may be limited only in terms of law of general application, to the extent reasonable and justifiable, considering the nature of the right, the importance and purpose of the limitation, its nature and extent, the relation to its purpose, and less restrictive means.
>
> The infringement of privacy must be proportional to the laws criminalising cannabis use. The Court held that using cannabis **in private for personal consumption** is legal, and laws criminalising **cultivation** for that purpose unjustifiably limit the right to privacy (para 58). "Cultivation... in private by an adult for personal consumption in private" was given a **wide interpretation** — gardens and private dwellings, or any enclosure/room, qualify as long as they are genuinely private (para 85).
>
> **Applying this to Mr X:**
> - He **may** grow cannabis in his private backyard garden for his personal use, and he may smoke it in private.
> - He is **not** permitted to smoke it openly in public; if caught in possession, SAPS has discretion whether the quantity indicates personal consumption (para 111).
> - The **nursery is a public space** — cultivating cannabis there, or allowing it to be consumed on the premises, is **not** cultivation in a private dwelling and remains an offence.
> - **Selling** cannabis to the public at the nursery is expressly prohibited by the judgment; selling a cookbook that encourages cannabis use and sales can also be argued to be prohibited (para 88). Customers who buy from him would also be committing an offence (para 88).
> - The order is **not retrospective** (para 102) — it does not excuse his past ten years of use as lawful conduct at the time.
> - Although the declaration of invalidity was suspended for 24 months, the Court granted **interim relief**: Mr X will not commit an offence if he confines himself to private use and cultivation, for personal consumption, in private.
