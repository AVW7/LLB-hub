# CLP — 24 July 2010

Source question paper: [[CLP24Jul2010Q.doc]]
Source marking memo: [[CLP24Jul2010A.doc]]

Facts: your client James Brown is charged in the East London Regional Court with contravening s27 (read with s1) of the Films and Publications Act 65 of 1996 — possession of home-produced child pornography. s5 of the Act allows a "bona fide scientific, literary or documentary use" defence, but only to distributors/broadcasters, not to possessors like Brown. You are instructed to challenge s27's constitutionality; the case is adjourned to the Eastern Cape Division (ECD).

## Q1.1 — Locus standi to approach the ECD (5)

> [!question] Question
> Does your client have locus standi to approach the ECD?

> [!success]- Answer
> Yes. s38(a) of the Constitution provides that anyone who alleges a Bill of Rights infringement and has a personal interest in the matter may approach a competent court for relief. Since Brown is charged under s27 and alleges it is unconstitutional, he has sufficient personal interest to found locus standi.

Related: [[Locus Standi]]

## Q1.2 — Arguments to challenge s27's constitutionality (5)

> [!question] Question
> What arguments could be raised in the ECD to challenge the constitutionality of Section 27?

> [!success]- Answer
> s27 could be attacked as infringing: the **right to privacy** (the material is kept, produced and used in the privacy of his own home); the **right to freedom of expression** (it infringes on producing film/photographic matter); and the **right to equality** (possessors are denied the s5 defence that broadcasters and distributors are entitled to raise).

Related: [[Limitation of Rights]]

## Q1.3.1 — The State's counter-argument (5)

> [!question] Question
> Suppose the ECD found that Section 27 was in conflict with at least one right in the Bill of Rights. What counter-argument(s) may the State raise?

> [!success]- Answer
> Even if s27 impacts the rights to equality, privacy and freedom of expression, the State can argue the infringement is a justifiable limitation under s36 — including the constitutional need to protect children against abuse and to stamp out the market for child pornography.

Related: [[Limitation of Rights]]

## Q1.3.2 — Does an invalidity finding take immediate effect? (5)

> [!question] Question
> Briefly explain whether or not Section 27 could be invalid with immediate effect. Mention the applicable Rule of Court.

> [!success]- Answer
> No, s27 would not be invalid immediately. Once the ECD makes the declaration of invalidity, it must be referred to the Constitutional Court for confirmation before the invalidity takes effect — s172 of the Constitution, in accordance with **Rule 16**. Pending confirmation, a temporary interdict may be sought to regulate the position in the interim.

Related: [[Confirmation of Constitutional Invalidity]]

## Q1.4 — Why not the Magistrate's Court? (5)

> [!question] Question
> Explain, with reference to the relevant sections of the Constitution and applicable legislation, why it would not be possible for Brown to approach the Magistrate's Court for relief.

> [!success]- Answer
> The Magistrate's Court lacks the necessary jurisdiction to declare s27 unconstitutional. s170 of the Constitution: a Magistrate's Court may decide only matters determined by an Act of Parliament. s110 of the Magistrates' Courts Act confirms a Magistrate's Court may not decide on the constitutionality of any law — such challenges must be treated as though the law were constitutionally valid. Brown should be advised to approach the **High Court**.
