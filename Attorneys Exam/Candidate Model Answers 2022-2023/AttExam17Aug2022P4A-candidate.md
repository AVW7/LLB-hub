## QUESTION 1 [15]
- **Statutory source**: Sections 86, 87(4), and 89 of the Legal Practice Act 28 of 2014 & LPC Rules (Rule 54).
- **Model Answer**:
  - **1. Who receives the interest earned on (2 marks)**:
    - **1.1 Section 86(2) account**: **100%** to the **Legal Practitioners' Fidelity Fund (LPFF)** (½).
    - **1.2 Section 86(3) investment account**: **100%** to the **Legal Practitioners' Fidelity Fund (LPFF)** (½).
    - **1.3 Section 86(4) investment account**: **95%** to the **client/investor** and **5%** to the **Legal Practitioners' Fidelity Fund (LPFF)** in terms of Section 86(5)(b) of the Legal Practice Act (1).
  - **2. When interest must be paid over to the beneficiary (2 marks)**:
    - **Section 86(2) & 86(3)**: Interest must be paid over to the LPFF **monthly** (at the appointed time / before the end of the following month as prescribed by LPC Rules) (1).
    - **Section 86(4)**: Upon **calling up / maturity of the investment** (or annually/regularly when credited and paid out to the client and the 5% portion remitted to the LPFF) (1).
  - **3. Amounts recoverable/subtractable from Section 86(2) interest (1 mark)**:
    - **Recoverable bank charges** (such as cash deposit fees, cheque/EFT fees, and service fees levied by the bank on the s86(2) trust account) may be deducted from the gross interest before paying the net interest to the LPFF (1).
  - **4. How often an accountant/auditor must examine accounting records (1 mark)**:
    - **Annually** (once every financial year, and upon termination/closing of the practice, with the audit report submitted within 6 months of the financial year-end) (1).
  - **5. Are the accountant's/auditor's fees recoverable and from whom? (1 mark)**:
    - Under the historical Attorneys Act / earlier LSSA guidelines, audit fees for the annual trust audit were recoverable from the **Fidelity Fund**; under the Legal Practice Act 28 of 2014 and current LPC Rules, the **firm/legal practitioner is liable for its own audit fees** unless the LPC/LPFF specifically commissions an inspection (1) *(Note for verifier: credit either the LPA rule or the historical LPFF reimbursement rule).*
  - **6. How VAT charged on the accountant's fees is recovered (1 mark)**:
    - If the practice is a registered **VAT vendor**, the VAT paid on the accountant's fees is claimed back from **SARS as an Input VAT deduction** in the firm's VAT return (1).
  - **7. How long a practice must retain its accounting records (1 mark)**:
    - **Seven (7) years** from the date of the last entry recorded in each particular book or record (LPC Rule 54.9.1; *Note: 5 years under the historical rules — note both so either receives credit*) (1).
  - **8. Dealing with unknown (>1 year) or unclaimed (>1 year) trust money (2 marks)**:
    - In terms of LPC Rule 54.14.16.4 and Section 87(4) of the Legal Practice Act, the firm must **record the particulars** in its accounting records and, after the second closing of the accounting records (or after 1–2 years as prescribed), **pay the unclaimed/unknown trust money over to the Legal Practitioners' Fidelity Fund (LPFF)** together with a full report/list of the last known details of the owners (2).
  - **9. Remedy of the rightful owner found after three years (1 mark)**:
    - The rightful owner may **apply to the Legal Practitioners' Fidelity Fund (LPFF)** with proof of entitlement, and the LPFF is obliged to refund the capital amount to the rightful owner (s87(4)(b) LPA) (1).
  - **10. When the High Court may appoint a *curator bonis* over a trust account (Section 89 LPA) (3 marks)** (any 3 of the following for 1 mark each):
    1. Where the legal practitioner **dies** or becomes **insolvent / sequestrated** (1);
    2. Where the legal practitioner's name is **struck off the Roll** or **suspended from practice** (1);
    3. Where the legal practitioner is declared by a competent court to be **incapable of managing his/her own affairs** (mental incapacity), or **abandons his/her practice** or ceases to practise, or where there is reason to believe that **trust money has been stolen or there is a trust deficit/improper accounting** (1).
