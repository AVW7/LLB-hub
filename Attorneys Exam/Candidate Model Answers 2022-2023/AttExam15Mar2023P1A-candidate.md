## QUESTION 3 [10]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam17Mar2021P1A]] Q3.1 & Q4.
- **Model Answer**:
  - **3.1 Difference between General Damages and Special Damages (3 marks)**:
    - **General damages (non-pecuniary / non-patrimonial loss)** do not represent an exact out-of-pocket financial expense, cannot be mathematically calculated, and are awarded to compensate for **pain and suffering, shock, disfigurement, permanent disability, and loss of amenities of life** (subject to the serious injury threshold) (1½).
    - **Special damages (pecuniary / patrimonial loss)** represent actual, quantifiable financial or out-of-pocket losses to the claimant's estate (past or future), such as **past and future hospital/medical expenses and past and future loss of earnings/support**, which must be specifically pleaded and proved (1½).
  - **3.2 Timeframe and Options in respect of RAF Form 4 (Regulation 3(3)(dA)) (5 marks)**:
    - **Timeframe**: Within **90 days** from the date on which the Serious Injury Assessment Report (RAF Form 4) was sent by registered post or delivered by hand to the RAF (2).
    - **Three Options available to the RAF**:
      1. **Accept** the serious injury assessment report (1);
      2. **Reject** the serious injury assessment report (and furnish reasons) (1); or
      3. **Direct that the third party submit himself/herself to a further assessment** by a medical practitioner designated by the RAF at the RAF's cost (1).
  - **3.3 Section 17(4)(a) Undertaking (2 marks)**:
    - In terms of Section 17(4)(a) of the RAF Act, the RAF may issue an **undertaking** to compensate the third party in respect of **future accommodation in a hospital or nursing home, or future treatment of or rendering of a service or supplying of goods** to him/her arising out of the injuries (1), whereafter the RAF will pay the **agreed or proven costs (subject to the applicable statutory health tariff and the **80% apportionment** agreed on the merits)** directly as and when those costs are incurred and claimed in the future (1).

## QUESTION 4 [14]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam15Oct2018P1A]] Q11 & [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam4Nov2020P1A]] Q15.1 (Identify any 7 mistakes/omissions for 2 marks each = 14 marks).
- **Model Answer** (any 7 of the following for 2 marks each):
  1. **Plaintiff's citation (Paragraph 1)**: Fails to state the Plaintiff's **full first names** (only initials "P. J." are given, contrary to Uniform Rule 18(4)) (2).
  2. **First Defendant's citation (Heading & Paragraph 2)**: A trust is not a juristic person and cannot be sued in its own name ("The Jones Family Trust"); the **trustees** for the time being must be cited in their official capacities (**N.O.**) (2).
  3. **Ownership / *Locus Standi* (Paragraph 4)**: There is **no allegation that the Plaintiff is the owner** (or *bona fide* possessor bearing the risk) of the Mercedes Benz motor vehicle CA 12389 (merely driving it does not confer *locus standi* to claim property damage) (2).
  4. **Vicarious Liability — Course and Scope of Employment (Paragraphs 3–5)**: Although Paragraph 3 mentions that the Second Defendant is an employee of the First Defendant, there is **no allegation that at the time of the collision the Second Defendant was acting within the course and scope of her employment** with the First Defendant (2).
  5. **Grounds of Negligence (Paragraph 5)**: Paragraph 5 merely states a conclusion that the collision was caused by the "sole negligence" of the Second Defendant without pleading the **specific grounds/particulars of negligence** (e.g., failing to keep a proper lookout, excessive speed, failing to apply brakes) as required by Rule 18(4) (2).
  6. **Damages — Pre-collision value & necessity of repairs (Paragraph 6)**: A repair **invoice** alone is not the proper measure of delictual damages without alleging that the repairs were **necessary** as a result of the collision and that the repair costs did not exceed the diminution in market value (2).
  7. **Jurisdiction Averment**: There is **no paragraph alleging that the High Court (Western Cape Division, Cape Town) has jurisdiction** over the matter (especially since the First and Second Defendants reside/carry on business in Bloemfontein and Pretoria, outside the Western Cape, so jurisdiction must be specifically pleaded on the ground that the cause of action arose in Cape Town) (2).
  8. **Joint and Several Liability in the Prayer (Paragraph 8)**: The prayer fails to claim judgment against the First and Second Defendants **jointly and severally, the one paying the other to be absolved** (2).
  9. **Mora Interest Rate & Date (Paragraph 8.2)**: In an unliquidated delictual damages claim, *mora* interest runs from the **date of judgment** (or date of demand/summons under the Prescribed Rate of Interest Act), not from the date of the collision, and the prescribed rate was not 15,5% in February 2023 (2).
  10. **Attorney-and-Client Costs (Paragraph 8.3)**: Attorney-and-client costs are claimed in the prayer without pleading any **factual or contractual basis/grounds** justifying a punitive costs order (2).
  11. **Signature Block**: In the High Court, Combined Summons Particulars of Claim must be signed by ** both an Advocate (or Attorney with right of appearance in the High Court under s25(3) LPA) and the Plaintiff's Attorney**, and must include the Plaintiff's Attorney's physical service address within 15 km of the Registrar's office (Rule 17(3)/18(1)) (2).

## QUESTION 9 [5]
- **Statutory source**: Section 86(1) and (2) of the Criminal Procedure Act 51 of 1977 (Amendment of charge during trial).
- **Model Answer**:
  - Under **Section 86(1) of the Criminal Procedure Act**, where a charge is defective for want of an essential averment, or where there is a variance between the averment in the charge and the evidence offered, or where words have been omitted or unnecessarily inserted, the court may order that the charge be amended, **provided the court considers that the making of the amendment will not prejudice the accused in his defence** (2).
  - The primary consideration is **whether the accused will suffer prejudice in conducting his defence** (e.g., whether cross-examination or the defence strategy would have been conducted differently had the charge originally read as amended, and whether the amendment substitutes an entirely new offence rather than curing a defect) (2).
  - The court will also consider whether any potential prejudice to the accused can be cured by **adjourning/postponing the trial** for a reasonable period under Section 86(2) (as the court did here) to enable the accused to prepare for the amended charge and recall witnesses if necessary (1).
