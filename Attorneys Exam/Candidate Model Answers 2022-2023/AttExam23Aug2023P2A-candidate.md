## QUESTION 3 [13]
- **Verified against YouTube walkthrough**: `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LzLJMlF1WO4` (`23:55–26:15`).
- **Statutory source**: Recognition of Customary Marriages Act 120 of 1998 (s7(2)); Intestate Succession Act 81 of 1987 (s1(1)(c), s1(6)); common-law *nasciturus* fiction.
- **Model Answer**:
  - **1. Customary Marriage in Community of Property (3 marks)**:
    - Under **Section 7(2) of the Recognition of Customary Marriages Act 120 of 1998**, a monogamous customary marriage concluded in 2021 without an antenuptial contract is **automatically a marriage in community of property** (2).
    - Surviving wife **Khosi** is therefore first entitled to her **50% half-share of the R900 000,00 joint estate** = **R450 000,00**, leaving a **net intestate estate of R450 000,00** (1).
  - **2. Unborn Child (*Nasciturus* Fiction) & Minor Son Joshua's Purported Renunciation (4 marks)**:
    - **Unborn child**: Under the ***nasciturus* fiction** (*nasciturus pro iam nato habetur quotiens de commodo eius agitur*), because Khosi was 7 months pregnant with Justin's child at his death, the unborn child is deemed already born at the date of death provided the child is subsequently **born alive**, and therefore counts as a descendant (2).
    - **Minor son Joshua**: As a **minor**, Joshua lacks legal capacity to renounce (repudiate) an inheritance without the assistance of his guardian and the consent of the Master/High Court (see [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam19Oct2021P2A]] Q2: *"Elliot will inherit ... as he is a minor and has no capacity to renounce his rights of inheritance"*). Therefore, Joshua's purported renunciation is invalid and **Joshua remains an intestate heir** (2). *(Note: Even if a valid renunciation occurred, under s1(6) his share would accrue to surviving spouse Khosi).*
  - **3. Calculation of Shares under Section 1(1)(c) (4 marks)**:
    - A child's share of the R450 000,00 net intestate estate is divided by 2 children (Joshua + unborn child) + 1 spouse (Khosi) = **3** (`R450 000,00 ÷ 3 = R150 000,00`) (1).
    - Under Section 1(1)(c)(i), **Khosi** inherits a child's share (`R150 000,00`) or **R250 000,00**, whichever is the greater -> **Khosi inherits R250 000,00** from the intestate estate (giving Khosi a total of `R450 000 + R250 000` = **R700 000,00**) (1).
    - Under Section 1(1)(c)(ii), the remaining balance of **R200 000,00** (`R450 000 - R250 000`) is divided equally between Justin's two children:
      - **Joshua (minor son)**: **R100 000,00** (1);
      - **Unborn child (if born alive)**: **R100 000,00** (if not born alive, Joshua takes the full R200 000,00) (1).
  - **4. Persons Who Do Not Inherit as Heirs (2 marks)**:
    - **Solly (father)**: Excluded as an ascendant because Justin is survived by a spouse and descendants (though Solly has a **creditor's claim against the estate** for reimbursement of the reasonable funeral expenses he paid) (1).
    - **Martha (younger sister)**: Excluded as a collateral relative by the surviving spouse and descendants in the first *parentela* (1).

## QUESTION 4 [7]
- **Verified against YouTube walkthrough**: `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LzLJMlF1WO4` (`27:25–29:25`).
- **Statutory source**: Section 2(1)(b), Section 2(3), and Section 2A of the Wills Act 7 of 1953.
- **Model Answer**:
  - **1. Formal Invalidity of the Amendment under Section 2(1)(b) (3 marks)**:
    - Deleting Nelisa's name and writing Gloria's name above it constitutes an **amendment (alteration/interlineation)** made after the execution of the Will (1).
    - Under **Section 2(1)(b) of the Wills Act 7 of 1953**, any amendment made after execution is formally valid **only if** the alteration is identified by the **signature of the testator AND the signatures of two competent witnesses** present at the same time (1). Because Dickson signed next to the alteration **alone without two witnesses**, the substitution of Gloria is **formally invalid** on the face of the Will (and the Master cannot accept it *mero motu*) (1).
  - **2. Condonation by the High Court under Section 2(3) and Revocation under Section 2A (4 marks)**:
    - However, under **Section 2A(a)** of the Wills Act, the **High Court** is empowered to declare the bequest to Nelisa **revoked** if satisfied that Dickson made a written indication on his Will (crossing out Nelisa's name and signing next to it) by which he intended to revoke the bequest to Nelisa (2); and
    - Under **Section 2(3)** of the Wills Act, the **High Court** is empowered to order the Master to **accept the informal amendment in favour of Gloria** if satisfied that Dickson **personally drafted/executed** the alteration on the Will, has since died, and **intended that amendment to be an amendment to his Will** (1).
    - **Conclusion / Advice to Nelisa**: Unless only the Master's administrative process is followed without a court application, **Gloria** can apply to the High Court under Sections 2(3) and 2A, and because Dickson personally wrote Gloria's name and signed next to it, the High Court will likely condone the amendment and award the R600 000,00 legacy to **Gloria** (1).
