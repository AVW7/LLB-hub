## QUESTION 5 [7½]
- **Statutory source**: Sections 17, 19, 21, 22, and 25 of the Road Accident Fund Act 56 of 1996 (as amended by Act 19 of 2005).
- **Model Answer**:
  - **5.1** Under the amended RAF Act (post-1 August 2008), the former R25 000 passenger limitation was abolished. For Lora's claim to succeed (subject to the statutory annual cap on loss of income/support and the serious-injury threshold for general damages), she must prove that her bodily injuries were caused by or arose from the negligent driving of a motor vehicle (at least 1% negligence on the part of either driver) (1) and, to claim general damages, that her head trauma qualifies as a "serious injury" (30% Whole Person Impairment under the AMA Guides or the narrative test via RAF Form 4) (1). *(Note for verifier: under pre-amendment law or if testing common-law/statutory exclusions, a passenger had an unlimited claim unless conveyed for reward/in course of employment; under current law all claims are subject only to s17(1) and s17(4) caps).*
  - **5.2** The RAF steps into the shoes of the negligent wrongdoer(s) — here primarily **Mrs Malinga** (the insured driver whose negligent driving caused the collision) (1), and potentially also Mr Shabangu vis-à-vis his passengers if any contributory negligence was attributable to him (1).
  - **5.3** **RAF Form 3** (Statutory Accident Report Form under s22(1)) (1).
  - **5.4** **Yes** (½) — driving under the influence of alcohol by the wrongdoer does not exclude the RAF's liability to compensate an injured third party (it instead triggers the RAF's right of recourse against Mrs Malinga under s25).
  - **5.5** **Yes** (½) — in terms of Section 25(1) of the RAF Act, the RAF has a statutory right of recourse without having to take cession of the right of action.
  - **5.6** **No** (½) — following the 2005 Amendment Act deletion of s17(2), party-and-party costs are no longer automatically payable by statute on pre-summons settlements unless agreed as part of the settlement or ordered by a court.
  - **5.7** **Lora** (the Plaintiff/claimant alleging that she was conveyed) bears the onus of proof on a balance of probabilities (1).

## QUESTION 6 [6]
- **Statutory source**: Section 1 and Section 2 of the Apportionment of Damages Act 34 of 1956 + common law rules on accountability (*culpa incapax* / *doli incapax*).
- **Model Answer**:
  - **Tom (7 years old)**: A minor aged 7 years or under (an *infans*, and children under 7) is irrebuttably presumed to be *culpa incapax* (lacking capacity to be negligent or accountable in delict) (2). Because Tom cannot be legally negligent, he cannot be held delictually liable as a joint wrongdoer under Section 2 of the Apportionment of Damages Act, and any claim against him must fail (1).
  - **Sam**: Assuming Sam is over the age of accountability (or over 14, or between 7 and 14 with the capacity to distinguish right from wrong proven), Sam was negligent in taking his father's vehicle without permission and permitting an incompetent 7-year-old child to drive it on a public road (2). Sam is therefore solely liable for 100% of the owner's damages (assisted by his parents/guardians in the litigation), and cannot claim a contribution from Tom under the Apportionment of Damages Act because Tom is *culpa incapax* (1).

## QUESTION 7 [14]

- **Model Answer**:
  - **7.1** Deliver an **Interpleader Notice** (under Uniform Rule 58 in the High Court, as the amount of R1 000 000 exceeds the Regional Court limit of R400 000) accompanied by the client's affidavit, and pay the R1 000 000 into court (to the Registrar) to abide the court's decision (1).
  - **7.2** **Affidavit in terms of Rule 58(2)** (13 marks):
    - **Heading**: `IN THE HIGH COURT OF SOUTH AFRICA, [DIVISION] / In the interpleader proceedings between ANSHA SAYED (Applicant), CONTRA SUPPLIERS (PTY) LTD (First Claimant) and SECURITY CREDIT (PTY) LTD (Second Claimant) — APPLICANT'S AFFIDAVIT` (2).
    - **Deponent & Authority**: `I, the undersigned, ANSHA SAYED, do hereby make oath and state that: 1. I am an adult female businesswoman residing at [address] and the Applicant in these interpleader proceedings. The facts deposed to herein fall within my personal knowledge and are true and correct.` (2)
    - **Citation of Claimants**: `2. The First Claimant is Contra Suppliers (Pty) Ltd, a private company with principal place of business at [address]. 3. The Second Claimant is Security Credit (Pty) Ltd, a private company with principal place of business at [address].` (2)
    - **Background & Rival Claims**: `4. Pursuant to a written sale agreement concluded with the First Claimant, I purchased and received goods for a purchase price of R1 000 000,00, which amount is due and payable. 5. Both the First Claimant and the Second Claimant demand payment of the said sum of R1 000 000,00 from me: the Second Claimant relies on an alleged cession of book debts, whereas the First Claimant contends that the cession in securitatem debiti was duly cancelled. Copies of their respective demands are annexed marked "A" and "B".` (3)
    - **Mandatory Rule 58(2) Averments**:
      - `6. I claim no interest in the subject matter in dispute other than for charges and costs;` (1)
      - `7. I do not collude with either of the said Claimants;` (1)
      - `8. I am willing to deal with or act in regard to the subject matter of the dispute as the Court may direct, and I have paid the sum of R1 000 000,00 to the Registrar of this Honourable Court simultaneously with the delivery of the Interpleader Notice.` (1)
    - **Prayer & Signature**: Prayer for relief interpleading the claimants and awarding Applicant's costs out of the fund, followed by `DEPONENT` signature line (attestation clause omitted as instructed) (1).

  - **Corroborating Annexures**: `3. ## QUESTION 11 [7]

- **Model Answer**:

    1. The charge does not comply with the provisions of the Act relating to the essentials of a charge (s84) (1);
    2. The charge does not set out an essential element of the relevant offence (e.g., misrepresentation, prejudice, or intention to defraud) (1);

  - **11.2 Procedure under s85(1)–(2) CPA** (2 marks):

## QUESTION 12 [7]
- **Statutory source**: Section 105A of the Criminal Procedure Act 51 of 1977.
- **Model Answer**:
  - **12.1** The accused must be legally represented (1), and the prosecutor must be duly authorised in writing by the National Director of Public Prosecutions (and have consulted the investigating officer and complainant, taking into account the nature of the offence, personal circumstances, previous convictions, and community interests) (2).
  - **12.2** The **authorised prosecutor** (1) and the **accused's legal representative** (acting on the instructions of the accused) (1) (in consultation with the investigating officer and complainant).
  - **12.3** It must be in **writing** and signed by the prosecutor, the accused, and the accused's legal representative (1).
  - **12.4** The court plays **no part** in the negotiations itself (it only questions the accused and considers whether the sentence agreement is just after the agreement is disclosed in court) (1).

## QUESTION 17 [5]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam17Mar2021P1A]] Q12.2 (Magistrates' Courts Rule 18(6) tender where plaintiff fails to beat the tender).
- **Model Answer**:
  - **(i)** Because B failed to recover more than the R90 000 tendered by A under Rule 18 (recovering only R80 000), the court will amend its costs order to order **A to pay B's costs only up to the date of the tender** (1), and **B to pay A's costs incurred subsequent to the date of the tender** (2).
  - **(ii)** Judgment should still be given for the capital amount of **R80 000** proved at trial (since B rejected the R90 000 tender and proceeded to trial, where the court determined A's liability to be R80 000) (2).
