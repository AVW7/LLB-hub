## QUESTION 1 [25]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam10Feb2016P3A]] Q6.1, [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam20Oct2021P3A]] Sec A Q4.1, LPC Code of Conduct (touting, undercutting, competence, and conveyancing trust rules), and Scribd preview `986665878`.
- **Model Answer**:
  - **1.1 Charging a Reduced Bond Registration Fee for Volume Work (2 marks)**:
    - **Yes, subject to ethical limits** (1) — the LSSA/LPC conveyancing tariff is a **recommended guideline tariff** (not a mandatory fixed minimum tariff following Competition Commission rulings), so you may negotiate a reasonable reduced fee with your existing client Share Bank taking into account the volume of instructions, provided the fee remains fair and reasonable and does not compromise the quality of professional work or amount to touting/undercutting (1).
  - **1.2 Competing Panel Attorney Undercutting Fees to Solicit Work (3 marks)**:
    - **No** (1) — it is unethical and constitutes **improper touting / undercutting** for an attorney to approach a client or bank manager and offer to undercut a colleague's quoted fee specifically to induce the client to divert work away from the colleague (2).
  - **1.3.1 Acting for a Bank Employee on a Personal Divorce Matter Where You Lack Experience (9 marks)**:
    - **Conflict of interest check**: In principle, acting for an employee of Share Bank in her personal divorce does **not** create a conflict of interest with Share Bank (unless the bank is a party or its security is prejudiced) (1).
    - **Competence & Duty of Care**: However, an attorney may **not accept a mandate in a field in which he is not competent** unless he can acquire the necessary knowledge/skill without undue delay or cost to the client, or work alongside an experienced colleague/partner or brief Counsel (3).
    - **Options & Factors to Consider in Exercising the Mandate**:
      1. Since the divorce and maintenance enforcement matter is **not complicated**, you may accept the mandate provided you **study the applicable law and procedure** (Divorce Act 70 of 1979, Maintenance Act 99 of 1998, Regional/High Court Rules) (2);
      2. **Consult a senior colleague or partner** in the firm who has family-law experience (or brief Counsel if needed) to supervise and review the pleadings (2); or
      3. If you cannot handle the matter competently without risk of negligence, **decline the mandate or refer Mrs Unhappy** to a family-law practitioner (1).
  - **1.3.2 Acting *Pro Amico* for the Staff of Share Bank (3 marks)** — Precedent [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam10Feb2016P3A]] Q6.1 & [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam20Oct2021P3A]] Sec A Q4.1:
    - **No** (1). Acting *pro amico* (free of professional fees, recovering only disbursements) is reserved for a **close personal friend, relative, or colleague** (1). Acting free of charge for employees of a commercial client (Share Bank) to curry favour or retain the bank's panel work is an **improper inducement / touting**; however, because Mrs Unhappy's house is being sold, you may agree to **charge normal fees and defer payment against a written undertaking/authority to deduct your fees from the net proceeds of the sale of her house on registration of transfer** (1).
  - **1.3.3 Paying Estate Agent's Commission Prior to Registration of Transfer (4 marks)**:
    - **No, you may not pay the commission out of the deposit/trust funds prior to registration of transfer** (1), because:
      1. Unless the deed of sale expressly provides otherwise, estate agent's commission is only **earned and payable upon registration of transfer** of the property in the Deeds Registry (1);
      2. Until registration of transfer takes place, the purchase price / deposit held in your trust account remains **trust money held pending registration** (for the buyer/seller), and paying it out prematurely if the sale later falls through would cause a **trust shortfall/breach of trust** (1);
      3. It may only be paid early if **both the Seller (Mrs Unhappy) and Purchaser expressly authorise early payment in writing** out of unconditional funds belonging to the Seller (1).
  - **1.3.4 Factors the Court Considers in Determining Child Maintenance (4 marks)**:
    1. The **reasonable maintenance needs** of the two minor children (accommodation, food, clothing, medical care, and education) and the **standard of living** of the family prior to the divorce (2); and
    2. The **respective financial means, income, and earning capacities of both parents**, as both parents have a joint common-law and statutory duty under the Maintenance Act 99 of 1998 and Children's Act 38 of 2005 to support their children **pro rata according to their respective means** (2).

## QUESTION 4 [9]
- **Precedent grounding**: Standard Partnership Agreement Termination Clause.
- **Model Answer**:
  > **DURATION AND TERMINATION OF PARTNERSHIP**
  > 1. **Commencement and Indefinite Duration**: The partnership shall commence on the effective date and shall continue indefinitely until terminated in accordance with the provisions of this clause (1).
  > 2. **Termination by Written Notice**: Either Partner (`X` or `Y`) may terminate the partnership at any time by giving the other Partner not less than **three (3) calendar months' prior written notice** delivered to the other Partner's chosen *domicilium* (2).
  > 3. **Termination by Mutual Agreement**: The Partners may terminate the partnership at any time by **written agreement signed by both Partners** specifying the effective date and terms of dissolution (1).
  > 4. **Automatic Termination (*Ipso Iure*)**: The partnership shall terminate automatically upon the **death** or **final sequestration / insolvency** of either Partner (2).
  > 5. **Summary Termination for Material Breach / Incapacity**: Either Partner ("the aggrieved Partner") shall be entitled to terminate the partnership summarily by written notice to the other Partner ("the defaulting Partner") if the defaulting Partner:
  >    5.1 commits a **material breach** of this agreement or his fiduciary duty of good faith and fails to remedy such breach within **14 (fourteen) days** of receipt of written notice requiring him to do so (2);
  >    5.2 is convicted of any offence involving dishonesty (fraud or theft), or becomes permanently mentally or physically incapacitated from performing his duties as a partner (1).

## QUESTION 6 [16]
- **Statutory source**:
  - **6.1**: Section 84 of the Insolvency Act 24 of 1936 (Hypothec of credit provider under an instalment agreement).
  - **6.2**: Section 38 and Section 98A of the Insolvency Act 24 of 1936 (read with Section 339 of the Companies Act 61 of 1973) — Employees' claims upon liquidation of an employer company.

### 6.1 ABC Bank's Rights under Section 84 of the Insolvency Act [10]
- Under **Section 84(1) of the Insolvency Act 24 of 1936**, where a movable asset (the motor vehicle) was delivered to a debtor under an **instalment agreement** as defined in the National Credit Act 34 of 2005 (where ownership is reserved until the final instalment is paid) and the debtor is sequestrated, **ownership of the vehicle passes automatically to the insolvent estate**, and the credit provider (**ABC Bank**) **loses its reservation of ownership** (2).
- In return, **Section 84(1) automatically creates a statutory hypothec (real security right)** over the motor vehicle in favour of ABC Bank for the outstanding balance of **R70 000,00**, making ABC Bank a **secured creditor** in the insolvent estate (2).
- **Steps ABC Bank must take to obtain possession, realise the security, and recover the R70 000,00**:
  1. **Require the Trustee to deliver the vehicle**: ABC Bank may, by written notice, **require the trustee (or Master) to deliver possession of the motor vehicle to ABC Bank** under Section 84(1) (2);
  2. **Prove a secured claim & value the security (Section 44 & Section 83)**: ABC Bank must lodge and prove its claim of **R70 000,00** at a meeting of creditors by affidavit and supporting documents, **place a value on the vehicle held as security** under Section 83(1), and either realise the vehicle as required by Section 83 or allow the trustee to take it over at the agreed valuation (2);
  3. **Application of proceeds & concurrent claim for any shortfall**: The net proceeds of the vehicle (less costs of realisation and Master's/trustee's levy on that security) are applied to satisfy ABC Bank's secured claim up to the value of the vehicle (1), and if the net proceeds are less than R70 000,00, ABC Bank ranks as a **concurrent creditor** against the free residue for the remaining shortfall (1).

### 6.2 Tim Blank's Rights Upon Liquidation of His Employer [6]
- **1. Suspension and Termination of Employment Contract (Section 38 of the Insolvency Act) (2 marks)**:
  - Upon liquidation of the company, Tim Blank's employment contract is **automatically suspended** from the date of liquidation, and unless continued by the liquidator, **terminates automatically 45 days** after the appointment of the final liquidator (s38(1), (4), (9)) (1).
  - Under Section 38(10)–(11), Tim Blank has an **unsecured / concurrent claim for damages** suffered as a result of the premature termination of the remaining 2½ years of his 3-year fixed-term contract (including the **R15 000,00 relocation expenses** which the company had contractually undertaken to reimburse) (1).
- **2. Preferent Claim for Arrear Salary (Section 98A of the Insolvency Act) (3 marks)**:
  - Under **Section 98A(1)(a)(i)** of the Insolvency Act, Tim Blank has a **preferent claim** against the free residue of the company in liquidation for his **arrear salary/wages for a period not exceeding three (3) months** prior to liquidation, up to the statutory cap of **R12 000,00** (2).
  - Accordingly, out of his **R20 000,00** arrear salary (`2 months × R10 000,00`), **R12 000,00 ranks as a preferent claim** under s98A (and he does not even need to lodge a formal proof of claim affidavit at a meeting of creditors for his s98A salary preference unless required by the liquidator — s44(3) proviso) (1).
- **3. Concurrent Claim for the Balance (1 mark)**:
  - For the remaining **R8 000,00** of arrear salary exceeding the R12 000,00 statutory preference cap, plus the **R15 000,00** relocation expense reimbursement and breach-of-contract damages, Tim Blank must prove a **concurrent claim** against the free residue (1).

## QUESTION 8 [7]
- **Statutory source**: Schedule 8 (Code of Good Practice: Dismissal, Item 8 — Probation) to the Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995 & Section 37(1)(a) of the Basic Conditions of Employment Act 75 of 1997 (BCEA).
- **Model Answer**:
  - **1. May the Employer Dismiss Sally During Probation? (1 mark)**:
    - **Yes**, an employer may dismiss a probationary employee for poor work performance before or at the expiry of the probationary period, **provided** the dismissal is both **substantively and procedurally fair** in accordance with Item 8 of Schedule 8 to the LRA (probation is not a "free fire" period) (1).
  - **2. Basic Requirements to Ensure a Fair Dismissal for Poor Work Performance During Probation (Item 8 of Schedule 8 LRA) (4 marks)**:
    1. **Instruction, Training, Guidance & Evaluation**: The employer must have made the required performance standards clear to Sally and given her **reasonable evaluation, instruction, training, guidance, or counselling** needed to render satisfactory service (1);
    2. **Time to Improve & Warning**: Having already addressed her informally, the employer must have advised her of the specific respects in which she is failing to meet the standard and afforded her a **reasonable opportunity to improve** (and warned her that continued poor performance could lead to dismissal or extension of probation) (1);
    3. **Formal Inquiry / Opportunity to Make Representations (Procedural Fairness)**: Before deciding to dismiss Sally (or extend her probation), the employer **must invite Sally to make representations** at an inquiry/hearing and allow a **fellow employee or trade union representative** to assist and represent her (1);
    4. **Less Compelling Reasons**: While the reasons for dismissal during probation may be less compelling than for a permanent employee who has completed probation (Item 8(1)(j)), the employer must still consider her representations and alternatives before dismissing (1).
  - **3. Statutory Notice Period Required (Section 37 of the BCEA) (2 marks)**:
    - The employer **may NOT give 24-hour notice** (1).
    - Because the employment contract is silent on the notice period, **Section 37(1)(a) of the Basic Conditions of Employment Act 75 of 1997** applies: since Sally has been employed for **six (6) months or less** (since 1 April 2023), she is entitled to **not less than one (1) week's written notice** (or payment of one week's remuneration in lieu of notice under s38) (1).
