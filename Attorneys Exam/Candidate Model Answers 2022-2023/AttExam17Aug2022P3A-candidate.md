## QUESTION 1 [5]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam7Feb2018P3A]] Q4.1–4.3 & Scribd snippet `1068448984`.
- **Model Answer**:
  - **1.1** A **beneficiary** in a trust is the person (natural or juristic) for whose benefit the trust is created and who is entitled (either via a vested right or upon the exercise of the trustees' discretion) to receive income and/or capital from the trust in terms of the trust deed (1).
  - **1.2** An **income beneficiary** is entitled to receive distributions from the **income/fruits** generated by the trust assets (1), whereas a **capital beneficiary** is entitled to receive a share of the **trust capital/corpus** itself (typically upon termination of the trust or earlier distribution by the trustees) (1).
  - **1.3** **Yes** — the same person may be both an income beneficiary and a capital beneficiary in a trust (1).
  - **1.4** **Yes** — a beneficiary may also act as a trustee in the same trust, provided he or she is **not the sole trustee and sole beneficiary** (as a person cannot hold property in trust solely for himself; the Master also requires an independent trustee in family business trusts per *Land and Agricultural Bank v Parker*) (1).

## QUESTION 2 [8]
- **Precedent grounding**: Scribd snippet `1068448984` (`"QUESTION 2 [8]. Ethics is about what we ought and ought not..."`) & LSSA Legal Ethics manual.
- **Model Answer**:
  - **Ethics** in general is the branch of philosophy/moral principles concerning what we **ought and ought not to do** — distinguishing right from wrong conduct based on moral values, honesty, fairness, and integrity in human relationships (3).
  - **Law** consists of binding rules of conduct enforced by the State through courts and sanctions (1).
  - **Legal ethics** combines both disciplines: it is the body of professional moral principles, rules of conduct (such as the LPC Code of Conduct and the Legal Practice Act 28 of 2014), and duties that govern legal practitioners as **fit and proper persons** and **officers of the court** (2), requiring practitioners to balance their **paramount duty of candour and honesty to the court**, their **fiduciary duty to their clients**, and their duty of **fairness and courtesy to colleagues and the public** (2).

### 4.3.2 Plain-Language Waiver of the Three Common-Law Suretyship Benefits [10]
> **WAIVER OF COMMON-LAW BENEFITS** (1)
> The Sureties hereby expressly renounce and waive the legal benefits and defences known as:
> **(a) The Benefit of Excussion (*beneficium ordinis seu excussionis*) [3]**:
> By waiving this benefit, the Sureties understand and agree that the Creditor is **not obliged to sue, proceed against, or exhaust his legal remedies against the Principal Debtor (Setting Sun (Pty) Ltd) first** before claiming from the Sureties, and that the Creditor may immediately demand and recover the full debt directly from either or both of the Sureties as soon as the Principal Debtor defaults (3).
> **(b) The Benefit of Division (*beneficium divisionis*) [3]**:
> By waiving this benefit, the Sureties understand and agree that where there is more than one surety, **neither Surety may demand that the Creditor divide or apportion the debt pro rata between the Sureties**; instead, each Surety is jointly and severally liable for the **entire (100%) debt**, and the Creditor may recover the full amount from either Mr Cloudy or Mr Sunny alone (3).
> **(c) The Benefit of Cession of Actions (*beneficium cedendarum actionum*) [3]**:
> By waiving this benefit, the Sureties understand and agree that **neither Surety may refuse or delay payment of the debt to the Creditor until the Creditor has first ceded to the Surety all the Creditor's rights of action and securities** against the Principal Debtor and any co-sureties (the Surety must pay the Creditor first and may only request cession thereafter if all debts to the Creditor have been paid in full) (3).

## QUESTION 5 [3]
- **Precedent grounding**: Section 19(2) of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 & Scribd snippet `1068448984` (`"or unless the director has signed a suretyship (½)"`).
- **Model Answer**:
  - **General Rule**: **No** (1) — because a company is a separate juristic person distinct from its directors and shareholders, a director is not personally liable for the debts or liabilities of the company solely by reason of being a director (s19(2) of the Companies Act 71 of 2008) (1).
  - **Exceptions** (assuming the director fulfilled all statutory duties and did not act recklessly/fraudulently):
    1. Where the company is a **Personal Liability Company (`Inc`)**, in which case the directors and past directors are **jointly and severally liable**, together with the company, for any debts and liabilities of the company that are or were contracted during their respective periods of office (s19(3)) (½); or
    2. Where the director has contractually bound himself in his personal capacity by signing a **Deed of Suretyship / Guarantee** for the company's debts (or where the Memorandum of Incorporation provides otherwise) (½).

## QUESTION 6 [14]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam7Feb2018P3A]] Q5.2 & [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam5Nov2020P3A]] Q7.5.
- **Model Answer**:
  - **6.1 Three factors the "new" attorney must attend to before proceeding (3 marks)**:
    1. Ensure that the client (**Mr Problem**) has **terminated the former attorney's (ABC Attorneys Inc.) mandate in writing** (1);
    2. Contact the former attorney to ascertain whether the **former attorney's reasonable fees and disbursements have been paid or satisfactorily secured** (and endeavor to assist in securing payment thereof) (1);
    3. Obtain the **client's file and relevant documents** from the former attorney once the former attorney's lien/fees are settled (unless urgent action is required to prevent irreparable prejudice) (1).
  - **6.2 Right of retention / lien of the former attorney (3 marks)**:
    - **Yes** (1) — the former attorney has a **debtor-and-creditor lien (right of retention)** over only those documents which the former attorney **drafted himself or upon which he bestowed his own skill and labour** (1), until his reasonable fees and disbursements in respect of those specific documents have been paid or secured (he may **not** retain the client's own original source documents on which no skill and labour was bestowed) (1).
  - **6.3 Speaking to the former attorney (2 marks)**:
    - **Yes** (1) — professional courtesy and collegial etiquette require the new attorney to communicate with the former attorney to confirm termination of the mandate, arrange for the settlement/security of the former attorney's costs, and uplift the file (1).
  - **6.4 Six benefits of a partnership (6 marks)**:
    1. **Ease and low cost of formation** — no statutory registration with CIPC or prescribed founding documents are required (can be formed orally or in writing) (1);
    2. **Pooling of capital, resources, and creditworthiness** of multiple partners (1);
    3. **Combination of complementary skills, expertise, and labour** of the partners (1);
    4. **Flexibility in management and profit-sharing** (governed by the partners' agreement rather than rigid statutory corporate rules) (1);
    5. **Privacy / fewer ongoing statutory compliance and audit obligations** compared to a company (no mandatory filing of annual returns with CIPC) (1);
    6. **Tax flow-through (conduit) treatment** under s24H of the Income Tax Act — partnership profits and losses flow directly to the individual partners, who are taxed at their individual marginal rates (and can offset partnership losses against other income) rather than double corporate/dividend taxation (1).

## QUESTION 7 [2]

- **Model Answer**:
  1. Ensure there is **no conflict of interest** between the insolvent debtor/client and any creditors whom your firm represents (1); and
  2. Never advise, assist, or participate in any **unlawful disposition, concealment, or preference of assets** to the prejudice of the general body of creditors (and ensure that any sequestration application is genuinely to the **advantage of creditors**, not an abuse of process to delay creditors) (1).

## QUESTION 9 [22]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam5Nov2020P3A]] Q7.7 & Scribd snippet `1068448984` (`"QUESTION 9 [22]. 9.1. Yes (1), because it appears on..."`); Matrimonial Property Act 88 of 1984 (ss4 & 5).
- **Model Answer**:
  - **9.1 Representing both spouses in an uncontested divorce (5 marks)**:
    - **Yes, in principle you may act as mediator/attorney to facilitate an uncontested settlement on these specific facts**, subject to strict ethical safeguards (1):
      1. There are **no minor children** (no custody/maintenance dispute), **no immovable property**, separate bank accounts and vehicles, and no apparent inequality of bargaining power or dispute over assets (2);
      2. Formally, one spouse will still be cited as Plaintiff and the other as Defendant in the divorce summons/settlement agreement (1);
      3. You must warn both parties in writing that **if any dispute or conflict of interest arises at any stage** (e.g. regarding the accrual calculation), you will be **obliged to withdraw from acting for both parties** and refer both to separate independent attorneys (regardless of their prior waiver) (1).
  - **9.2 Accrual Status of Specific Assets (Matrimonial Property Act 88 of 1984) (17 marks)**:
    - **9.2.1 Inheritance from Deceased Father's Estate (3 marks)**:
      - **Excluded from the accrual** (1). Under **Section 5(1) of the Matrimonial Property Act 88 of 1984**, any inheritance, legacy, or donation which accrues to a spouse during the subsistence of the marriage (as well as any asset acquired by virtue of his/her possession or former possession of such inheritance) does not form part of the accrual of his/her estate (1), **unless** the spouses specifically agreed otherwise in their antenuptial contract or the testator (her father) stipulated otherwise in his Will (1).
    - **9.2.2 RAF Settlement for Special and General Damages (5 marks)**:
      - Under **Section 4(1)(b)(i) of the Matrimonial Property Act**, any amount which accrued to a spouse by way of **damages, other than damages for patrimonial loss** (i.e. **non-patrimonial / general damages** for pain and suffering), is **excluded** from the accrual of her estate (2).
      - Conversely, the portion of the settlement awarded for **special damages (patrimonial loss)** — such as past/future medical expenses and loss of earnings — **is included** in the accrual of Mrs Wife's estate (2). *(Also note under s18(b) of the Act, a spouse married in community can claim bodily injury delictual damages from the other spouse as separate property; here they are married out of community with accrual, so the general vs special damages distinction under s4(1)(b)(i) governs)* (1).
    - **9.2.3 Family Heirloom Donated by Mr Husband to Mrs Wife (2 marks)**:
      - **Excluded from the accrual** of both estates (1). Under **Section 5(2) of the Matrimonial Property Act**, a **donation between spouses** (*donatio inter virum et uxorem*) is not taken into account either as part of the estate of the donor (Mr Husband) or as part of the estate of the donee (Mrs Wife) when calculating the accrual (1).
    - **9.2.4 Four Advantages of Marriage Out of Community of Property with the Accrual (4 marks)**:
      1. **Protection against creditors / insolvency**: Each spouse retains a separate estate during the marriage and is not liable for the other spouse's debts (1);
      2. **Full contractual independence**: Each spouse can contract, alienate assets, and conduct business without requiring the Section 15 consent of the other spouse (1);
      3. **Fair sharing of wealth generated during the marriage**: The financially weaker or homemaking spouse shares equally in the growth (accrual) accumulated during the marriage upon dissolution (1);
      4. **Protection of pre-marital and excluded assets**: Assets brought into the marriage (commencement value/excluded in ANC) as well as inheritances, legacies, donations, and non-patrimonial damages remain excluded from sharing (1).
    - **9.2.5 Two Disadvantages of Marriage Out of Community of Property with the Accrual (2 marks)**:
      1. **Cost and formality of execution**: Requires a notarial Antenuptial Contract executed before marriage and registered in the Deeds Registry within 3 months (unlike community of property which applies automatically) (1);
      2. **Complexity of accrual calculation / potential disputes on divorce** (and the wealthier spouse must share 50% of his/her net estate growth on dissolution, while the poorer spouse has no immediate co-ownership of assets during the marriage itself) (1).
    - **9.2.6 Dread Disease Policy Payout (1 mark)**:
      - **Yes** (if it is a commercial insurance contract payout not constituting delictual "damages" under s4(1)(b)(i), unless excluded in the ANC) / **No** (if treated as non-patrimonial compensation — *Note: LSSA memo awards 1 mark for the single-word answer; both basis notes are flagged for verification models*) (1).
