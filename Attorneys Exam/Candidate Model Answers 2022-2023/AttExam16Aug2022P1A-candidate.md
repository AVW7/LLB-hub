## QUESTION 1 [5]
- **Legal principle**: Legality of income in RAF loss of earning capacity claims (*Dhlamini v Protea Assurance Co Ltd*; *Santam Insurance Ltd v Ferguson*; *amarasinghe* / tax evasion distinction).
- **Model Answer**:
  - **No, Mr Do Little's claim for future loss of earning capacity is not disqualified** (1).
  - Under South African delictual law, a claim for loss of earning capacity is only barred as *contra bonos mores* where the income-generating activity **itself** is inherently unlawful, criminal, or immoral (such as drug dealing or unlicensed illegal trade) (2).
  - Here, Mr Do Little's underlying business — running a magazine-subscription business — is a **lawful commercial activity** (1). The fact that he committed tax fraud/evasion does not render the business itself unlawful, although his loss of future earnings must be calculated on his **true net after-tax income** (after deducting the tax he was legally obliged to pay) (1).

## QUESTION 4 [12]

## QUESTION 5 [6]
- **Statutory source**: Uniform Rule 26 (and Rules 21(4), 22(1), 25(1)).
- **Model Answer**:
  1. **Failure to deliver a Declaration (Uniform Rule 20(1) & Rule 26)**: Where the plaintiff sued by way of simple summons and fails to deliver a declaration within **15 court days** after receipt of the defendant's notice of intention to defend, the defendant may serve a Notice of Bar requiring delivery within **5 court days** (2).
  2. **Failure to deliver a Plea (Uniform Rule 22(1) & Rule 26)**: Where the defendant fails to deliver a plea (or exception) within **20 court days** after delivery of his notice of intention to defend (in the case of a combined summons) or after delivery of the plaintiff's declaration, the plaintiff may serve a Notice of Bar requiring delivery within **5 court days** (2).
  3. **Failure to deliver a Plea to a Claim in Reconvention (Uniform Rule 25(1) & Rule 26)**: Where the plaintiff (defendant in reconvention) fails to deliver a plea to a counterclaim within **20 court days** after delivery of the counterclaim, the defendant may serve a Notice of Bar requiring delivery within **5 court days** *(Note: Uniform Rule 26 states that any party failing to deliver any replication or subsequent pleading within the 15-day time stated in Rule 25 shall be ipso facto barred without a Notice of Bar)* (2).

## QUESTION 10 [3]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[PLE/notes/topics/Conflict of Interest]] (Conflict between co-accused).
- **Model Answer**:
  - **10.1** Because a conflict of interest has arisen **during the initial consultation prior to trial** (before you have accepted the mandate to represent both at trial or obtained confidential disclosures from both independently), you must **decline to act for both** accused: you may continue to represent **one** of them (provided the other was not prejudiced by confidential disclosures) and advise the other to obtain separate legal representation, or withdraw from both if confidential information was already received from both (2).
  - **10.2** Because the conflict arises **during the course of the trial** after you have already consulted with and represented both co-accused, you have received confidential privileged information from both and **must withdraw from acting for both accused** (and request a postponement so both can secure independent legal representation) (1).

## QUESTION 11 [6]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam6Mar2019P1A]] Q9 (Fresh bail application on new facts under s65(2) / s60 CPA).
- **Model Answer**:
  - Bring a **fresh bail application based on new facts** before the **same Magistrate** who refused bail initially (2).
  - Place the **new facts** discovered in the docket before the court (by affidavit or *viva voce* evidence), namely the investigating officer's affidavit confirming that the complainant has emigrated and cannot be traced (2).
  - Argue that:
    1. The sole ground on which bail was previously refused (the risk of influencing the complainant under s60(4)(b)) **no longer exists** since the complainant has left the country (1); and
    2. The **strength of the State's case has been severely weakened** (with little prospect of conviction), and keeping the accused in custody for a further 6 months until trial would prejudice the interests of justice (1).

## QUESTION 14 [2]
- **Statutory source**: Capacity to be sued (*locus standi in iudicio* — Section 17 of the Children's Act 38 of 2005) and delictual accountability (*culpa capax*).
- **Model Answer**:
  - To be sued in their own names without the assistance of a parent or legal guardian (*locus standi in iudicio*), they must have attained the age of majority, which is **18 years of age** (as minors under 18, they must be sued assisted by their parents/guardians, or the parents/guardians must be cited in their representative capacities) (1).
  - Furthermore, for delictual liability (*culpa capax*), a child under **7 years** is irrebuttably *culpa incapax*, whereas a minor between **7 and 14 years** is rebuttably presumed *culpa incapax* until proven to have had the maturity to appreciate the wrongfulness/negligence of the act, and a minor of **14 years or older** has full delictual accountability (1).
