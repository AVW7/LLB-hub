## QUESTION 1 [10]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam4Nov2020P1A]] Q1 & Q2; [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam17Mar2021P1A]] Q2 (Section 17(1) of the Road Accident Fund Act 56 of 1996).
- **Model Answer**:
  - To succeed with a claim under Section 17(1) of the Road Accident Fund Act 56 of 1996, a claimant (third party) must establish the following essential elements:
    1. **Bodily injury or death**: The claim must be for loss or damage resulting from **bodily injury to the claimant himself/herself** or the **death of or bodily injury to any other person** (pure property/material damage to vehicles or goods is excluded) (2);
    2. **Caused by or arising out of the driving of a motor vehicle**: There must be a factual and legal **causal nexus** between the driving of the vehicle (including where moved by gravity under s20) and the bodily injury or death (2);
    3. **A "motor vehicle" as defined in Section 1**: The vehicle must be designed or adapted for propulsion or haulage on a road by means of fuel, gas, or electricity (2);
    4. **Unlawful and negligent (or other wrongful) driving**: The injury or death must be due to the **negligence or other unlawful act** of the driver or of the owner of the motor vehicle or his/her employee in the execution of his/her duties (at least 1% negligence for a passenger/dependant claim, or subject to apportionment for a driver/pedestrian) (2);
    5. **Within the territorial borders of South Africa & procedural/substantive compliance**: The accident must have occurred **within the Republic of South Africa** (1), and the claimant must have suffered **patrimonial loss** (past/future medical expenses, loss of earnings/support — subject to the statutory cap) and/or **non-patrimonial loss (general damages)** (which requires proving a **"serious injury"** via RAF Form 4) and complied with the statutory lodgement and prescription periods under ss23–24 (1).

## QUESTION 2 [15]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam22Aug2017P1A]] Q3 & [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam19Feb2020P1A]] Q1.3.
- **Model Answer**:
  - **2.1 Can you accept the RAF offer under the Power of Attorney after Mr Branson's death? (3 marks)**:
    - **No** (1). A mandate / **Power of Attorney terminates automatically (*ipso iure*) upon the death of the principal (Mr Branson)**, so you no longer have authority to accept the offer on his behalf (1).
    - Furthermore, because Mr Branson died **prior to the close of pleadings (*litis contestatio*)** and before the offer was accepted, his personal claim for **general damages (non-pecuniary loss of R250 000)** and **future medical expenses (R150 000)** lapsed/died with him (*actio personalis moritur cum persona*), and only his **patrimonial past losses** (past hospital expenses of R150 000 and past loss of earnings of R200 000) transmit to the **duly appointed Executor** of his deceased estate (1).
  - **2.2 Does minor child Paul have a claim against the RAF? (2 marks)**:
    - **Yes** (1) — because Mr Branson died as a result of the injuries sustained in the collision, his minor son Paul (to whom Mr Branson owed a legal duty of support) has an independent delictual claim against the RAF for **loss of support** (assisted by his mother and natural guardian, Mrs Branson) (1).
  - **2.3 When does prescription start running in respect of Paul's claim against the RAF? (3 marks)**:
    - Although a dependant's cause of action for loss of support arises on the **date of the breadwinner's death (15 April 2021)** (1), under **Section 23(2) of the RAF Act** prescription **does not run against a minor** while he is under the age of 18 years (1).
    - Prescription in respect of Paul's claim (where the truck driver's identity was established, so the 3-year identified period applies) will therefore only begin to run when Paul **attains majority (turns 18 years old) on 14 March 2029** (and will complete 3 years later on 13 March 2032) (1).
  - **2.4 Effect of Customary Marriage Followed by Civil Marriage with ANC on Paul's Claim (3 marks)**:
    - **Yes, Paul will still be able to claim from the RAF** (1).
    - First, a biological parent's **legal duty to support his minor child exists by operation of law** regardless of the parents' matrimonial property regime or the validity of their marriage (1).
    - Second, under **Section 10(1) of the Recognition of Customary Marriages Act 120 of 1998**, spouses in a monogamous customary marriage are expressly permitted to contract a civil marriage with each other (and execute an antenuptial contract prior to that civil marriage), so both the marriage and Paul's status are completely valid (1).
  - **2.5 Can the RAF Apply Apportionment to Paul's Claim Based on His Father's Negligence? (4 marks)**:
    - **No** (1).
    - A dependant's claim for loss of support is an **independent, original delictual action** (not a derivative claim through the deceased breadwinner's estate), and Paul is an **"innocent third party / plaintiff"** (1).
    - Under the **Apportionment of Damages Act 34 of 1956**, contributory negligence can only be apportioned against a plaintiff who was personally negligent; because the deceased father and the third-party truck driver were joint wrongdoers vis-à-vis the dependant Paul, **Paul is entitled to claim 100% of his loss of support from the RAF** without any apportionment for his late father's negligence (2).

### 5.2 Factors the Court Will Consider in Sentencing [6]
- **Model Answer**:
  - The court applies the **triad of sentencing factors in *S v Zinn* 1969 (2) SA 537 (A)** together with the **purposes/objectives of punishment** (*R v Karg*) and statutory minimum-sentence rules (Criminal Law Amendment Act 105 of 1997):
    1. **The Crime (Nature and Seriousness of the Offence)**: Murder is the most serious offence infringing the constitutional right to life (s11); the court considers the degree of moral blameworthiness, motive, provocation, weapon used, and prevalence of the offence (1½);
    2. **The Offender (Personal Circumstances of the Accused)**: Age (53), first offender status, family dependents, stable employment as a teacher, remorse, and prospects of rehabilitation (1½);
    3. **The Interests of Society**: Protection of the community, public confidence in the administration of justice, and avoiding sentences that are either too lenient or shockingly severe (1½);
    4. **The Four Objectives of Punishment** (**deterrence** [general and specific], **prevention**, **rehabilitation/reformation**, and **retribution**) balanced with **mercy**, as well as whether **substantial and compelling circumstances** exist under Section 51(3) of the Criminal Law Amendment Act 105 of 1997 to depart from the prescribed minimum sentence of 15 years' imprisonment for a first offender convicted of murder (1½).

### 5.3 Factors to Present in Mitigation of Sentence [8]
- **Model Answer** (any 8 points for 1 mark each):
  1. **Clean criminal record (first offender at age 53)**: He has lived a law-abiding, productive life for 53 years without a single previous conviction (1);
  2. **Gainful employment & contribution to society**: He is a dedicated high school teacher at Gelvandale High School in Bloemfontein educating the youth (1);
  3. **Family responsibilities & dependants**: He is married and is the breadwinner supporting his wife and **two minor children**, who will suffer severe financial and emotional hardship if he is incarcerated for a lengthy period (1);
  4. **Genuine remorse and co-operation with police**: He did not flee, co-operated fully with the Bloemfontein police from the outset, and shows genuine contrition (1);
  5. **Circumstances of the offence / diminished moral blameworthiness**: E.g., the offence was not premeditated or planned, occurred on the spur of the moment under severe provocation or exceeded the bounds of self-defence (1);
  6. **Excellent prospects of rehabilitation & zero risk of re-offending**: Given his age, stable family home at 1 Church Street, and career, he is not a danger to society and is an ideal candidate for rehabilitation (1);
  7. **Negative impact of long-term imprisonment**: A lengthy prison sentence will destroy his teaching career and family life and expose a 53-year-old first offender to hardened criminals (1);
  8. **Cumulative existence of "substantial and compelling circumstances" (s51(3) of Act 105 of 1997; *S v Malgas*)**: Taken cumulatively, the above factors constitute substantial and compelling circumstances justifying a departure from the prescribed 15-year minimum sentence in favour of a lesser sentence (1).
