## QUESTION 2 [11]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam19Feb2020P2A]] Q3.3 (Section 2B of the Wills Act 7 of 1953).
- **Model Answer**:
  - **2.1 (5 marks)**:
    - In terms of **Section 2B of the Wills Act 7 of 1953**, if a testator dies within **three (3) months** after his marriage was dissolved by divorce or annulment, any will executed by him prior to the dissolution of that marriage shall be implemented in the same manner as it would have been implemented if his previous spouse had died before the date of dissolution of the marriage, unless it appears from the will that the testator intended to benefit his previous spouse notwithstanding the divorce (2).
    - Barry divorced Maria on 12 March 2021 and died on 10 May 2021 — which is **within three months** of the divorce (1).
    - Maria is therefore deemed to have predeceased Barry and **does not inherit** under the will (1). As the will appointed no substitute heir, Barry's estate devolves **intestate** under Section 1(1)(a) of the Intestate Succession Act 81 of 1987 upon his surviving spouse **Emily** as sole heir (since Barry died childless) (1).
  - **2.2 (6 marks)**:
    - **Yes, the advice would differ** (1).
    - If Barry died on 17 July 2021, **more than three (3) months** had elapsed since his divorce from Maria on 12 March 2021 (1).
    - Because Barry failed to revoke or amend his will after the three-month grace period under Section 2B expired, he is deemed to have intended the provisions of his 13 January 2020 will in favour of Maria to remain in force (2).
    - Consequently, **Maria (the ex-wife) will inherit the entire estate** in terms of the valid will, and **Emily will not inherit** under the will (though Emily may lodge a claim for reasonable maintenance against the estate under the Maintenance of Surviving Spouses Act 27 of 1990 if she is unable to support herself from her own means and earnings) (2).

## QUESTION 4 [10]
- **Statutory source**: Section 1(1)(d)(ii), Section 1(4)(e), and Section 1(5) of the Intestate Succession Act 81 of 1987.
- **Model Answer**:
  - **4.1 Heirs and Distribution (7 marks)**:
    - Under Section 1(4)(e) and 1(5) of the Intestate Succession Act, an adopted child is deemed to be the descendant of his adoptive parent(s) (Henry) and the blood relation of his natural mother (Mavis, to whom Henry was married when Jane was born).
    - Joshua died without a surviving spouse or descendants. He is survived by one parent (**his mother Mavis**) and the descendants of his deceased adoptive father (**Henry**).
    - Under **Section 1(1)(d)(ii)** of the Intestate Succession Act:
      1. **Mavis** (surviving mother) inherits **one-half (50%)** of the estate = `R944 200,00 ÷ 2` = **R472 100,00** (2).
      2. The **other half (R472 100,00)** devolves equally upon the **four surviving children of his deceased father Henry** (`Merlin`, `Joseph`, `Sam`, and `Jane` — full-blood and half-blood collaterals through Henry share equally on Henry's side): each inherits `R472 100,00 ÷ 4` = **R118 025,00** (5):
         - **Jane** (full sister, child of Henry and Mavis): **R118 025,00** (1)
         - **Merlin** (half-sister through Henry and Susan): **R118 025,00** (1)
         - **Joseph** (half-brother through Henry and Dorah): **R118 025,00** (1)
         - **Sam** (half-brother through Henry and Dorah): **R118 025,00** (1)
         - *(Total distributed: `472 100 + (4 × 118 025) = R944 200,00` — 1 mark for method/division).*
  - **4.2 Persons Who Do Not Inherit & Reasons (3 marks)**:
    - **Leah (ex-wife)**: Divorce terminated the marriage; an ex-spouse is not a "spouse" under the Intestate Succession Act (1).
    - **Susan (stepmother / second wife of Henry)**: Step-relations are relatives by affinity (*affines*), not blood relations or adoptive parents, and cannot inherit intestate (1).
    - **Florah (maternal grandmother) and Uncle Peter**: Excluded by closer relatives in the second *parentela* (surviving parent Mavis and descendants of deceased parent Henry exclude grandparents and uncles/aunts under s1(1)(d)) (1).

## QUESTION 5 [10]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam19Feb2020P2A]] Q2 (Sections 31, 32, and 33 of the Administration of Estates Act 66 of 1965).
- **Model Answer**:
  - **Late Lodging of Claim (Section 31)** (4 marks):
    - A creditor who fails to lodge his claim within the period specified in the s29 notice does **not** forfeit his claim (1).
    - If the claim is lodged before distribution, the Executor must still include it in the account (or reframe the account if already lodged) (1).
    - However, unless the creditor satisfies the Master that he had a **reasonable excuse** for the delay, the creditor is personally liable for all costs payable out of the estate in connection with reframing the account (1), and if the estate has already been distributed, the creditor has no recourse against other creditors or heirs who were paid (save to claim from remaining unpaid assets) (1).
  - **Disputed Claim Procedure (Sections 32 and 33)** (6 marks):
    - Under **Section 32**, if the Executor disputes the claim, the Executor may require the claimant to lodge an **affidavit** setting out the details of the claim (1), and with the consent of the Master, may require the claimant or any other person to appear before the Master or a Magistrate nominated by the Master to be **examined under oath** (2).
    - Under **Section 33**, if the Executor still rejects the claim, the Executor must immediately notify the claimant in **writing by registered post** and state the **reasons** for the rejection (2).
    - The creditor may then institute civil action against the estate in a competent court (citing the Executor *nomine officio*) or object to the L&D Account under s35 (1).
