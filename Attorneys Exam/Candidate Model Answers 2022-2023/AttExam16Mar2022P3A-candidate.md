## PART A — QUESTION 1 [84]

### 1.1 Forms of companies under Companies Act 71 of 2008 [5]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam20Feb2020P3A]] Q1.2 (Section 8 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008).
- **Model Answer**:
  1. **Profit companies**, which comprise four types:
     - **(a) Private company** (`(Pty) Ltd`) (1);
     - **(b) Personal liability company** (`Inc`) (1);
     - **(c) Public company** (`Ltd`) (1);
     - **(d) State-owned company** (`SOC Ltd`) (1); and
  2. **Non-profit company** (`NPC` — incorporated for a public benefit or social/cultural object) (1).

### 1.2 Recommended company form for Mr A and his wife [2]
- **Model Answer**:
  - A **Private Company (`(Pty) Ltd`)** (1), because it is a profit company with only two shareholders/directors (Mr A and his wife), restricts the transferability of its securities, prohibits offers to the public, and confers limited liability with fewer regulatory burdens than a public company (1).

### 1.3 Four instances of standard of directors' conduct under Section 76 [4]
- **Statutory source**: Section 76(2) and 76(3) of the Companies Act 71 of 2008.
- **Model Answer**:
  - A director must exercise his powers and perform his functions:
    1. In **good faith and for a proper purpose** (s76(3)(a)) (1);
    2. In the **best interests of the company** (s76(3)(b)) (1);
    3. With the **degree of care, skill and diligence** that may reasonably be expected of a person carrying out the same functions and having the general knowledge, skill and experience of that director (s76(3)(c)) (1); and
    4. Must **not use the position of director, or any information obtained while acting in the capacity of a director**, to gain an advantage for the director or another person other than the company, or to knowingly cause harm to the company or a subsidiary (s76(2)(a)) (1).

### 1.4 VAT Going-Concern Clause in Sale of Business Agreement [5]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam7Feb2018P3A]] Q8 (Section 11(1)(e) of the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991).
- **Model Answer**:
  > **VALUE-ADDED TAX (GOING CONCERN)**
  > 1. The Seller and the Purchaser hereby reciprocally warrant to each other that they are, or will at the effective/signature date be, duly registered as VAT vendors in terms of the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991 (1).
  > 2. The parties agree and record in writing that:
  >    2.1 the business enterprise is disposed of as a going concern (1);
  >    2.2 the business enterprise will be an income-earning activity on the date of transfer thereof (1);
  >    2.3 all the assets necessary for carrying on the enterprise are disposed of by the Seller to the Purchaser in terms of this agreement, and the consideration agreed upon is inclusive of VAT at the rate of zero percent (0%) in terms of Section 11(1)(e) of the VAT Act (1); and
  >    2.4 should the Commissioner for the South African Revenue Service rule that VAT is payable on this transaction at a rate exceeding zero percent (0%), the Purchaser shall pay such VAT to the Seller in addition to the purchase price upon presentation of a valid tax invoice (1).

### 1.5 Effects of Section 197 of the Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995 [5]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam22Aug2019P3A]] Q2.
- **Model Answer** (any 5 for 1 mark each):
  1. The new employer (Mr A's company) is **automatically substituted** in the place of the old employer (Mr B) in respect of all contracts of employment in existence immediately before the date of transfer (1);
  2. All **rights and obligations** between the old employer and each employee at the time of transfer continue in force as if they had been rights and obligations between the new employer and each employee (1);
  3. **Anything done before the transfer** by or in relation to the old employer (including any dismissal, unfair labour practice, or collective agreement) is considered to have been done by or in relation to the new employer (1);
  4. The transfer **does not interrupt** an employee's continuity of employment, and an employee's contract continues with the new employer on terms and conditions that are on the whole **not less favourable** than those on which the employee was employed by the old employer (1);
  5. The old and new employers are **jointly and severally liable** for any claim concerning a term or condition of employment that arose prior to transfer (unless the old employer shows it complied with s197(7)-(8)), and any dismissal because of the transfer itself is **automatically unfair** under s187(1)(g) (1).

In order to protect the goodwill of the grocery business sold and the legitimate proprietary interest of the Purchaser therein, the Seller hereby undertakes in favour of the Purchaser that he shall not, for a period of **three (3) years** reckoned from the effective date (1), and within **Gauteng Province** [or within a radius of 20 kilometres from the premises] (1), whether as proprietor, partner, shareholder, director, member, employee, consultant, agent, financier, or in any other capacity whatsoever, directly or indirectly carry on, be engaged in, or hold any interest in any grocery store or business competing with the business sold (1½).

### 1.7 Section 34 of the Insolvency Act 24 of 1936 [16]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam23Aug2017P3A]] Q4.
- **Model Answer**:
  - **7.1 Requirements for the advertisement (5 marks)**:
    - The seller (trader) must publish a notice of the intended transfer of the business in the **Government Gazette** (1) and in **two issues of an Afrikaans newspaper and two issues of an English newspaper** (1) circulating in the **district in which the business is carried on** (1), not less than **30 days** (1) and not more than **60 days** before the date on which the transfer takes place (1).
  - **7.2 Reasons for the advertisement (4 marks)**:
    - To give notice to the seller's creditors of the impending alienation of the business outside the ordinary course of business (2) so that creditors have an opportunity to demand immediate payment of their claims and secure payment out of the business or purchase price before the business is transferred (2).
  - **7.3 What the proviso intends to prevent the Seller and Purchaser from doing (2 marks)**:
    - It prevents a trader in financial difficulty from selling his business, pocketing/dissipating the cash purchase price, and leaving creditors with no recourse while the purchaser takes over or liquidates the business assets free of the seller's debts (2).
  - **7.4 Effects of not advertising on the Purchaser (2 marks)**:
    - The transfer is **void as against the seller's creditors for a period of six (6) months** after such transfer (1), and **void as against the trustee** of the seller's insolvent estate if the seller is sequestrated at any time within that six-month period (meaning the trustee/creditors can attach the business assets in the purchaser's hands despite the purchaser having paid the purchase price) (1).
  - **7.5 Effects of advertising the Sale of Business (3 marks)**:
    - Upon the publication of the first such notice, **every liquidated liability** of the seller in connection with the said business which would become due at some future date **becomes due forthwith** if the creditor demands payment thereof (2), subject to a rebate of 6% per annum interest on any non-interest-bearing future debt (and the purchaser acquires clean title protected against the seller's creditors after transfer) (1).

### 1.9 Client's Offer to Lend Money to the Attorney [5]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam7Feb2018P3A]] Q5.1 & [[PLE/notes/topics/Borrowing From or Lending to Clients]].
- **Model Answer**:
  - **9.1** **No** (1) (as a general rule, an attorney should not borrow money from a client).
  - **9.2** Explain to Mr A that the ethical rules of the legal profession prohibit an attorney from borrowing money from a client because of the fiduciary relationship and inherent conflict of interest (1), **unless**:
    1. The client is in the business of lending money (e.g. a bank or registered money-lender) (1), **or**
    2. Full disclosure is made and the client first obtains **independent legal/financial advice** before advancing the loan (2). Polite decline is the safest ethical course unless Mr A is independently advised in writing.

### 1.10 Collection Matters (10.1–10.4) [32]
- **10.1 Suing on a Prescribed Claim (3 marks)** — Precedent: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam18Mar2021P3A]] Q6 & [[Attorneys Exam/notes/qa/AttExam 2017-02-15]] Q4.1:
  - **Yes**, you may issue summons on a prescribed claim (unless it falls under Section 126B of the National Credit Act, which prohibits collecting or issuing summons on a prescribed NCA credit agreement debt) (1).
  - Under the Prescription Act 68 of 1969, prescription must be raised by the debtor via a **special plea** and the court **may not take notice of prescription *mero motu*** (s17(1)) (1).
  - You must advise Mr A in writing of the risk that if the debtor raises a special plea of prescription, the claim will fail and Mr A will be liable for legal costs, so the action should be withdrawn immediately if the special plea is raised (1).
- **10.2 Partner Administering the Oath in Summary Judgment Affidavit (3 marks)** — Precedent: [[Attorneys Exam/notes/qa/AttExam 2017-02-15]] Q4.2:
  - **No** (1). In terms of Regulation 7(1) of the Regulations Governing the Administering of an Oath or Affirmation (GN R1258 under the Justices of the Peace and Commissioners of Oaths Act 16 of 1963), a Commissioner of Oaths may not administer an oath in any matter in which he or she **has an interest** (1). A partner in the law firm acting for the plaintiff in litigation is deemed to have an interest in the matter (1).
- **10.3 Conveyancing Matter Exception (2 marks)**:
  - **Yes, the position is different** (1). Under Regulation 7(2) (and the Schedule thereto), declarations/affidavits required for registration in a **Deeds Registry** (conveyancing matters) are expressly exempted from the "interest" prohibition, so a partner in the firm may administer the oath (1).

## PART B — QUESTION 1 [16]

### 1.1 Effect of Mr A's Sequestration on Mrs A's Property (Section 21 of the Insolvency Act 24 of 1936) [10]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam7Mar2019P3A]] Q4.2 & [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam20Oct2021P3A]] Section B Q4.1.
- **Model Answer**:
  - Under **Section 21(1) of the Insolvency Act 24 of 1936**, upon the sequestration of the estate of an insolvent spouse (Mr A), **all the property of the solvent spouse (Mrs A) vests first in the Master and, upon the appointment of a trustee, in the trustee** of the insolvent estate as if it were property of the sequestrated estate (2).
  - The risk is particularly acute here because the office furniture is in Mr A's possession at his office, so the Sheriff/trustee will attach or take control of it (1).
  - Under **Section 21(2)**, the trustee is obliged to **release** any property of the solvent spouse which is proved to have been:
    1. Acquired by the solvent spouse before her marriage to the insolvent (½);
    2. Acquired by her under a marriage settlement (½);
    3. Acquired by her during the marriage by a **title valid as against creditors of the insolvent** (here, inheritance from her late father's deceased estate) (1);
    4. Safeguarded under the Insurance Act (½); or
    5. Acquired with any such property or the income or proceeds thereof (½).
  - **Steps Mrs A must take to protect and enforce her rights**:
    - She must immediately compile and submit a **written application supported by affidavit and documentary proof** (such as the Liquidation and Distribution Account of her late father's estate and the Executor's award/receipt) to the trustee proving her valid title to the inherited office furniture and requesting its release under s21(2) (2).
    - If the trustee refuses or fails to release the furniture, Mrs A must bring an **application to the High Court under Section 21(4)** for an order declaring the property to be hers and directing the trustee to release it (and interdicting any sale by the trustee under s21(3)) (2).

### 1.2 May Consultation with Counsel Proceed in the Attorney's Absence? [5]
- **Statutory/Rule source**: LPC Code of Conduct (Rule 18 / Rule 33 / traditional referral rule for instructing Counsel).
- **Model Answer**:
  - As a general rule in referral practice, an instructing attorney (or a partner, professional assistant, or candidate attorney from the instructing attorney's firm) **must attend** all consultations between Counsel and the client to take instructions, keep notes, and handle financial/procedural matters (2).
  - However, in exceptional or urgent circumstances where the attorney is unavoidably prevented from attending, the attorney may arrange for a **colleague or candidate attorney from the firm** to attend in his stead (1); or, if no one from the firm is available, Counsel may proceed with the consultation **with the prior consent/arrangement of the instructing attorney** provided the consultation does not require the attorney to resolve a conflict or take trust funds, and Counsel subsequently reports back to the instructing attorney (2).

### 1.3 Sharing Fees with Counsel [1]
- **Precedent grounding**: [[Attorneys Exam/Official Memoranda 2015-2021/AttExam7Mar2019P3A]] Q7.5.3.
- **Model Answer**:
  - **No** — a legal practitioner (attorney) may **never share fees with Counsel (an advocate)** (1).
