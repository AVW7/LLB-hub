## Q1 — RAF claims arising from a taxi rear-end collision (15)

> [!question] Question
> June Mbatha was a passenger in a car driven by her boyfriend Joseph, with their 4-year-old daughter Silindile in the back. An unidentified minibus taxi driver rear-ended them. June suffered rib fractures and facial lacerations; Silindile a severe head injury; Joseph, who was in a coma, died of his injuries on 12 May 2016. June and Joseph had cohabited 5 years, were not married but had agreed lobola (half paid), and intended to marry once it was paid in full. Joseph's medical aid paid his medical expenses; June paid the funeral costs.
> 1.1 Does June have locus standi for Silindile's claim despite the differing surname? (2)
> 1.2 Who has locus standi for Joseph's funeral expenses? (2)
> 1.3 By what date must each claim be lodged: 1.3.1 June's personal injury claim (1); 1.3.2 Silindile's claim (1); 1.3.3 the funeral expenses claim (1)?
> 1.4 Can Joseph's pre-death medical expenses be claimed from the RAF? (2)
> 1.5 Is Silindile entitled to loss of support? (2)
> 1.6 Is June entitled to loss of support? (2)
> 1.7 If Joseph was intoxicated and solely responsible for losing control (no other vehicle involved), what effect on: 1.7.1 the personal injury claims (1); 1.7.2 the loss-of-support claims (1)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 Yes — she is Silindile's mother and natural guardian; the differing surname is legally irrelevant.
> 1.2 June — she personally incurred and paid the funeral expenses.
> 1.3.1 24 April 2018. 1.3.2 24 April 2018. 1.3.3 11 May 2018.
> 1.4 Yes — the medical aid payment is a collateral benefit which the RAF cannot deduct.
> 1.5 Yes — Joseph was legally obliged to, and did, support her.
> 1.6 Yes — the couple intended to marry, were cohabiting in a relationship with many marriage-like characteristics (joint upkeep of the home) — Paixão v Road Accident Fund.
> 1.7.1 Unaffected — Joseph's negligence being the sole cause doesn't defeat their own personal injury claims.
> 1.7.2 They cannot recover loss of support where their breadwinner's own negligence was the sole cause of the collision.

## Q2 — Go-cart, bus, and loss of a foetus (6)

> [!question] Question
> 2.1 Your client, 6 months pregnant, was run over by an intoxicated driver of a stolen go-cart.
> 2.1.1 Will she have an RAF claim for her injuries? (2)
> 2.1.2 Would the answer differ if a speeding bus hit her instead? (2)
> 2.1.3 Her only loss is the foetus — advise on a general-damages claim. (2)

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1.1 No — a go-cart, though fuel-propelled, is not designed or adapted for road propulsion, so it is not a "motor vehicle" under the Act.
> 2.1.2 Yes, she would have a claim — a bus is designed/adapted for road propulsion by fuel and is a "motor vehicle."
> 2.1.3 She has a claim for non-pecuniary loss under regulation 3(1)(b)(iii) — loss of a foetus is a listed exception qualifying for general damages even without 30%+ WPI.

## Q3 — RAF1 medical report when the treating doctor has died (4)

> [!question] Question
> Your client's RAF1 medical report was sent to the hospital for completion, but the treating doctor has since died. The claim prescribes within a month. Who else can complete the report?

> [!success]- Answer
> The Superintendent of the treating hospital (or their representative) may complete it. If the practitioner/Superintendent fails to complete it within a reasonable time and the claim risks prescribing, another medical practitioner who has fully satisfied themselves of the nature and treatment of the injuries may complete it.

## Q4 — Interlocutory application to compel further particulars (16)

> [!question] Question
> You act for the plaintiff in a High Court action set down for trial in six weeks. After close of pleadings you served a request for further particulars for trial preparation; the defendant's attorneys neither responded nor answered your compliance letter, and the notice period has expired. You are prejudiced in trial preparation.
> 4.1 What is an interlocutory application? (2) 4.2 Long form or short form notice of motion? (1) 4.3 What relief would you seek (not the full notice)? (3) 4.4 Draft the founding affidavit. (7) 4.5 If you succeed and the opponent still doesn't comply, what relief do you seek next? (3)

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 An interlocutory application seeks intermediate/procedural relief incidental to pending proceedings, not going to the merits.
> 4.2 The short form (Form 2A).
> 4.3 An order compelling the further particulars within 10 days of service of the order; leave to approach on the same papers if there's non-compliance (practice-dependent, no mark either way); costs of the application.
> 4.4 Deponent's capacity as the applicant's attorney; the parties' descriptions; the trial date and set-down annexed; the Rule 21 request made at least 20 court days before trial, annexed; the compliance letter demanding delivery within 7 days and warning of this application, annexed; prayer for an order per the notice of motion.
> 4.5 That the Defendant's defence be struck out, judgment be entered for the Plaintiff, with costs of the application and of the action/suit.

Related: [[Interlocutory Applications and Notices of Motion]]

## Q5 — Notice of appeal to the full bench (5)

> [!question] Question
> You act for a plaintiff whose damages claim was dismissed in the High Court. Leave to appeal to the full bench was granted by the presiding judge. Draft the Notice of Appeal, using your own facts where necessary (omit the heading).

> [!success]- Answer
> Notice that the Appellant appeals to the Honourable Court against the whole of the judgment and order handed down by the named judge, in the named court a quo, under the given case number — noting the appeal is with leave, granted on a stated date, with a certified copy of the leave order filed. Followed by the grounds of appeal (any two reasonable grounds accepted).

Related: [[Civil Appeal and Review Procedure]]

## Q6 — Grounds for review of an inferior court (4)

> [!question] Question
> Name the grounds on which proceedings of an inferior court may be brought under review in the High Court.

> [!success]- Answer
> Under s22 of the Superior Courts Act: absence of jurisdiction; the presiding officer having an interest in the case, being biased, malicious, or having committed an offence referred to in s22; gross irregularity in the proceedings; and the admission of inadmissible/incompetent evidence or the rejection of admissible/competent evidence.

Related: [[Civil Appeal and Review Procedure]]

## Q7 — Section 220 admissions statement, theft from an employer (8)

> [!question] Question
> Mr Long Fingers, a cashier, allegedly took R1 000 from the till at the end of his shift on 31 December 2015 and was found with the cash by security on leaving. He is charged with theft, intends to plead not guilty, but is prepared to make a s220 statement admitting all facts except that the money found on him belonged to his employer. Draft the statement.

> [!success]- Answer
> Statement in terms of s220 CPA: admits he was employed as cashier at the Sandton branch (within the court's jurisdiction), worked 6h00–18h00 on 31 December 2015, collected money from customers and placed it in the till, and was found with R1 000 in his pocket at the security check on leaving. Consents that these facts be recorded as formal admissions relieving the State of proving them. Signed and dated.

## Q8 — Docket access, s60(11B) disclosures, s60(4) bail grounds (8)

> [!question] Question
> 8.1 Is your client entitled, for bail purposes, to peruse the police docket? (1)
> 8.2 What must you, as legal representative, bring to the court's attention under s60(11B)(a)? (2)
> 8.3 The magistrate finds bail not in the interests of justice. List the grounds the court must consider. (5)

> [!success]- Answer
> 8.1 No.
> 8.2 Whether the accused has previous convictions, and whether there are pending charges and whether currently on bail for them.
> 8.3 Likelihood the accused will: endanger public/individual safety or commit a Schedule 1 offence; evade trial; influence/intimidate witnesses or conceal/destroy evidence; undermine or jeopardise the criminal justice system including the bail system; or (in exceptional circumstances) disturb public order/security.

Related: [[Bail Applications]]

## Q9 — Guidelines for a s112(2) statement (3)

> [!question] Question
> What are the guidelines for drafting a statement under s112(2) of the Criminal Procedure Act?

> [!success]- Answer
> It must: be in writing; admit all elements of the offence; and satisfy the court that the accused is guilty of the offence charged.

Related: [[Statements and Pleas in Criminal Trials]]

## Q10 — Application after a weak State case (2)

> [!question] Question
> After the State closes its case, there is little evidence implicating your client. What application would you bring, and on what grounds must the court decide it?

> [!success]- Answer
> An application for discharge under s174. If the court is of the opinion there is no evidence the accused committed the offence charged (or any competent alternative), it may return a verdict of not guilty.

Related: [[Discharge, Withdrawal and Stopping of Criminal Prosecution]]

## Q11 — Withdrawal vs stopping of a prosecution (4)

> [!question] Question
> At what stage can the State: 11.1 withdraw charges, and what is the effect? (2) 11.2 stop a prosecution, and what is the effect? (2)

> [!success]- Answer
> 11.1 Before the accused pleads — the accused may be charged with the same offence again.
> 11.2 After plea but before judgment — the accused must be acquitted of the charge.

Related: [[Discharge, Withdrawal and Stopping of Criminal Prosecution]]

## Q12 — Particulars of claim: abandoned building contract (AB Builders CC) (8)

> [!question] Question
> Your client contracts with AB Builders CC (represented by Anton Barnard) for R560 000 of home alterations, payable 50% on signature, R100 000 on foundation, balance on completion. Your client pays 50% and the R100 000 for the foundation. The contractor then fails to return to complete the work; your client validly cancels and hires a new contractor to finish for R240 000. Draft the particulars of claim including the prayer (omit headings).

> [!success]- Answer
> Plead the parties, the written agreement's material terms (price and payment schedule, annexed), the payments made, the abandonment, the valid cancellation (annexed), the completion contract at a fair and reasonable R240 000 (annexed), resulting damages of R60 000, demand and non-payment (annexed). Prayer: R60 000, interest at 10,5% from expiry of the letter of demand to date of payment, and costs of suit.

Related: [[Particulars of Claim Drafting]]

## Q14 — Notices to lead expert medical evidence (4)

> [!question] Question
> You are preparing for a Magistrate's Court trial where your client claims damages for a dog bite. What notices must you deliver to lead a medical specialist's evidence, and within what periods?

> [!success]- Answer
> File a notice at least 15 days before the hearing of intent to call an expert; file a further notice at least 10 days before the hearing summarising the expert's opinion and reasons, including their qualifications, that they examined the Plaintiff, and their findings.

Related: [[Trial Notices for Expert and Documentary Evidence]]

## Q15 — Remedy for withdrawal without a costs tender (2)

> [!question] Question
> The Plaintiff withdraws the action against your client without tendering costs. What legal remedy is available?

> [!success]- Answer
> An application to court for an order that the Plaintiff pay the costs of the action (together with the costs of bringing the application).

Related: [[Costs, Tenders and Withdrawal of Action]]

## Q16 — Continuing a defence after provisional sentence is granted (3)

> [!question] Question
> Your client, the Defendant in a provisional-sentence summons, delivers a notice of intention to oppose and an opposing affidavit; the Plaintiff replies; the Magistrate grants provisional sentence with costs. Your client still believes he has a good defence. Advise him on his next steps.

> [!success]- Answer
> The Defendant must call for security; the Plaintiff is then required to provide security for the judgment amount. If security is given, the Defendant must, within two months of the provisional sentence judgment, pay the claim with costs. If the Plaintiff cannot provide security, the Defendant need not pay and may simply defend the principal case — by giving notice of intention to defend and delivering a plea.

Related: [[Default Judgment and Provisional Sentence]]

> [!success]- Answer
