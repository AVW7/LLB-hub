## Q1 — Claim against a negligent driver directly (3)

> [!question] Question
> Does a claimant have a claim against the negligent driver and/or owner of a vehicle for bodily injuries, and if so, when?

> [!success]- Answer
> Not generally — except: (a) where the RAF or its agent is unable to pay compensation; or (b) for emotional shock suffered by a person who was not themselves a third party but who witnessed, observed, or was informed of another's bodily injury or death from the driving of a motor vehicle.

## Q2 — RAF and COIDA claims for security guards in a single-vehicle accident (14)

> [!question] Question
> Martha Mbhense (passenger) and Amos Khumalo (driver), both security guards, are injured on 27 July 2007 when Amos loses control of the vehicle and hits a tree while driving to a house-breaking scene. Martha sustains a fractured pelvis and clavicle; Amos's right leg is amputated at the hip. Neither has been compensated.
> 2.1 Does Martha have an RAF claim, and is it limited in any way? (4)
> 2.2 Does Amos have a claim, and against whom? (3)
> 2.3 Identify all heads of damages for Martha's RAF claim. (2½)
> 2.4 If Martha is assessed at 20% WPI and fails the narrative test, what effect on the heads of damage above? (½)
> 2.5 Identify any other claim Martha may have. (1)
> 2.6 If Martha cannot recover general damages from the RAF, can she recover them elsewhere? (3)

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 Yes. Under the RAF Act 56 of 1996 (in force at the time), her claim was capped at R25 000, but under the Transitional Provisions Act (claim not finalised before the TPA), the cap no longer applies — though the loss-of-earnings cap (as at 1 August 2008) and the general-damages threshold (30% WPI or the narrative test) still apply.
> 2.2 No RAF claim — the collision was caused by his own negligence. He has a claim against the Compensation Commissioner under COIDA (injured in the course and scope of employment); COIDA claims are based on strict liability, so his own negligence doesn't defeat it.
> 2.3 Past hospital/medical expenses; future hospital/medical expenses; past loss of earnings; future loss of earnings; general damages.
> 2.4 The general damages head falls away.
> 2.5 A COIDA claim against the Compensation Commissioner (injured on duty).
> 2.6 No — COIDA covers special damages only, and the RAF Act/TPA expressly exclude a general-damages claim against the wrongdoer in these circumstances.

## Q3 — Multi-party collision: driver, passenger, cyclist (8)

> [!question] Question
> Mr A is a passenger in a vehicle driven by Mr B. A collision occurs between B's vehicle and a cyclist, Mr C, who has a passenger, Mr D. A and D are seriously injured.
> 3.1 If C was exclusively negligent, will A have an RAF claim? (4)
> 3.2 If B's negligence contributed 20%, does the answer to 3.1 change? (2)
> 3.3 On the same 20%-contribution facts, will D have an RAF claim? (2)

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 No — a claim cannot be based on a cyclist's negligence, as a bicycle is not a "motor vehicle" (defined as a vehicle designed/adapted for propulsion on a road by fuel, gas or electricity, including trailers/caravans/agricultural implements drawn by such a vehicle).
> 3.2 Yes — A will now have a claim, based on B's negligent driving.
> 3.3 Yes — D will have a claim based on B's negligent driving.

## Q4 — Application after withdrawal of action without a costs tender (9)

> [!question] Question
> The Plaintiff, in a High Court action where you represent the Defendant, serves a notice of withdrawal without tendering costs.
> 4.1 What remedy does your client have? (1) 4.2 When must this step be taken? (1) 4.3 Draft the notice of motion (excluding heading/citation). (7)

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 Apply to court for an order that the plaintiff pay the costs of the action and of the application.
> 4.2 Within a reasonable time of receiving the notice of withdrawal (no fixed time limit).
> 4.3 Notice that application will be made for an order that: the plaintiff pay the costs of the withdrawn proceedings; the plaintiff pay the costs of this application; supported by the attached affidavit; addressed to the Registrar and to the Plaintiff's attorney.

## Q5 — Divorce jurisdiction and drafting the prayers (16)

> [!question] Question
> Mrs Mary Brand consults you in Bloemfontein. She married Pieter Brand out of community of property in Grahamstown on 12 December 1981. She is a housewife with no income. The parties lived in Pretoria where Mr Brand is permanently employed by the Department of Justice; all assets are his; he belongs to the Government Employees Pension Fund. Mrs Brand moved to a family member in Bloemfontein over the weekend, intending to stay permanently.
> 5.1 Which Division(s) of the High Court have jurisdiction? Give reasons. (5)
> 5.2 Advice on which court the summons should issue from? (2)
> 5.3 Draft only the prayers in the Particulars of Claim. (7)
> 5.4 How would the prayers differ if the parties married on 30 January 2001 out of community of property subject to accrual, with each party's commencement value R nil? Draft only the differing prayer(s). (2)

> [!success]- Answer
> 5.1 Free State High Court, Bloemfontein (s2(1)(b) Divorce Act — plaintiff ordinarily resident in the area at institution, and resident in the Republic for at least a year before that); and North Gauteng High Court, Pretoria (s2(1)(a) — parties domiciled there).
> 5.2 Free State High Court, Bloemfontein — most convenient and least costly.
> 5.3 Against First Defendant: a decree of divorce; maintenance of R5 000 p.m.; that 50% of the Defendant's assets (or such part as the court deems fit) be transferred to the Plaintiff; that 50% of the First Defendant's GEPF pension interest as at date of the court order vest in the Plaintiff when the benefit accrues, alternatively on date of divorce; costs; further/alternative relief. Against Second Defendant (the Fund): an endorsement that 50% (or such portion as directed) of the pension interest is payable to the Plaintiff, with proof of endorsement furnished to the Registrar within one month.
> 5.4 That 50% of the accrual in the First Defendant's estate from date of marriage to date of the order be transferred to the Plaintiff.

## Q6 — Conspiracy to murder: charges, bail, s204 witness, appeal grounds (15)

> [!question] Question
> A young woman is accosted and later found shot dead. Her husband (A) and two others are arrested. The State alleges A persuaded his employee (B) to find C to kill her; the three met to plan it; A handed B an unlicensed firearm which B passed to C, who forced her into a vehicle and killed her. Police found the firearm and 5 rounds on C. A applies for bail and it emerges he also holds a foreign passport.
> 6.1 List two possible charges against the three accused. (2)
> 6.2 What principle governs the court's bail decision? (1)
> 6.3 How do you deal with the multiple travel documents? (2)
> 6.4 You persuade the State to use B as a witness — describe the trial procedure and B's possible consequences. (5)
> 6.5 What could your role be as B's attorney at the trial? (1)
> 6.6 The court, relying on B's evidence, convicts A of premeditated murder and sentences him to life imprisonment. Give two possible grounds of appeal against: 6.6.1 conviction (2); 6.6.2 sentence (2).

> [!success]- Answer
> 6.1 Conspiracy to commit murder; murder; kidnapping; possession of an unlicensed firearm; possession of ammunition.
> 6.2 An accused in custody is entitled to release at any stage before conviction if the court is satisfied the interests of justice permit it, subject to exceptional circumstances given the nature of the offence.
> 6.3 Suggest surrender of all passports and regular reporting to a police station.
> 6.4 Section 204 CPA: the prosecutor informs the court the witness will be asked incriminating questions; the court informs the witness of the obligation to testify, that self-incriminating questions may be put, that they must be answered, and that answering frankly and honestly earns discharge from prosecution for the specified offence (and any competent verdict on it). The court records the discharge if satisfied the witness was frank and honest.
> 6.5 Hold a watching brief and argue at the end of trial that the witness is entitled to discharge from prosecution.
> 6.6.1 The court erred in accepting B's s204 evidence as frank, honest and reliable; erred in rejecting the accused's evidence as not reasonably possibly true; failed to apply the proper test for guilt (any reasonable answer accepted).
> 6.6.2 The court erred in not finding exceptional circumstances against the maximum sentence; failed to weigh personal circumstances; the sentence induces a sense of shock (any reasonable answer accepted).

## Q7 — Section 115 statement: fraudulent cheque defence (7)

> [!question] Question
> On 1 February 2015 in Port Elizabeth, John Smith hands SA Plumbing Suppliers a cheque dated 3 March 2015 for R25 000, knowing there are insufficient funds at that time but expecting payment from Sanctuary Developers by 21 February. Sanctuary Developers is provisionally liquidated on 20 February and Smith isn't paid. The cheque is presented and referred to drawer. Smith is charged with fraud and instructs a not-guilty plea. Draft his s115 statement setting out his defence in full (include heading).

> [!success]- Answer
> Admits drawing and handing over the cheque, and that it was referred to drawer for insufficient funds. Explains he had a reasonable expectation of receiving payment from a debtor before the cheque would be presented, and that the debtor's unexpected liquidation frustrated that expectation. Denies any intention to defraud SA Plumbing Suppliers when the cheque was handed over, and pleads not guilty.

## Q8 — Recovering a client's laptop held as an exhibit (3)

> [!question] Question
> During the 2015 xenophobic violence, your client (a Sudanese immigrant) had his shop looted. He is subpoenaed to testify against an accused found in possession of his laptop. What procedure do you follow to get the laptop back?

> [!success]- Answer
> Under s34 CPA, the presiding officer must, at the conclusion of the proceedings, order return of an exhibit article to any person lawfully entitled to possess it. Approach the prosecutor with the request; the prosecutor raises it with the presiding officer at the conclusion of the matter. The client must prove ownership (evidence-in-chief and/or documentary proof); the attorney may assist personally in court in making the request.

## Q9 — Particulars of claim: abandoned building contract (8)

> [!question] Question
> Emmanuel Mchunu enters a verbal contract with Improve-A-Home (Pty) Ltd for R320 000 of renovations at his Durban home (R100 000 on signature; R120 000 at 50% completion; R100 000 on completion, all payable in Durban). Emmanuel pays the initial R100 000 and advances R60 000 despite work not being 50% complete. The contractor abandons the work; Emmanuel cancels and employs James Builders to finish for R240 000. Draft the particulars of claim for the Durban District Magistrate's Court.

> [!success]- Answer
> Plead the parties, the verbal agreement's material terms (price, payment schedule, place of payment), the R160 000 paid, the Defendant's abandonment, the Plaintiff's cancellation (with the notice annexed), the completion contract with James Builders at R240 000, resulting damages of R80 000, demand and non-payment, and that the cause of action arose within the court's jurisdiction. Prayer: R80 000, interest at 9% p.a. from the date the letter of demand expired, and costs.

## Q10 — Interest rate applicable at trial after a rate change (2)

> [!question] Question
> Your client sued for goods sold and delivered, summons issued/served in 2013 claiming interest at 15,5% p.a. Judgment for the full amount is granted in December 2015. What interest rate applies?

> [!success]- Answer
> 15,5% — despite the statutory rate changing to 9% in August 2014, it is the rate applicable at the time the action was instituted that determines the rate that can be charged.

## Q12 — Third-party notice before close of pleadings (3)

> [!question] Question
> In a claim against your client arising from a collision, your client (the Defendant) admits the collision but alleges someone other than the Plaintiff caused it. What must the Defendant do before pleadings close?

> [!success]- Answer
> Issue a third party notice and have the Sheriff serve it on the party concerned; the third party must enter an appearance to defend; the Defendant then files a plea.

## Q13 — Correcting an error in the amount claimed (6)

> [!question] Question
> Your client instructs you to collect two months' rent totalling R50 000. You issue summons, then discover the rent was actually R20 000 per month.
> i) What procedure corrects the mistake? (2) ii) If the Defendant objects, what procedure must he follow (don't draft the document, just its contents)? (2) iii) If the Defendant's objection fails, what is the Plaintiff's next step? (2)

> [!success]- Answer
> i) The Plaintiff delivers a notice to amend.
> ii) The Defendant delivers a notice to object, setting out the grounds of objection.
> iii) The Plaintiff serves and files the corrected pages.

## Q14 — Notice to use trial photographs without further proof (4)

> [!question] Question
> You act for the Plaintiff in a motor collision matter set down for trial; your client has three colour photographs of the scene she wants to use. Draft the necessary notice (omit headings).

> [!success]- Answer
> Notice that the Plaintiff intends to use three colour photographs of the collision scene at trial, copies annexed, with the originals available for inspection at the Plaintiff's attorneys. The recipient has five days from receipt to raise any objection to the photographs being admitted without further proof, failing which they will be received in evidence on mere production.
