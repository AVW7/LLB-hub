## Q1 — Drafting an acknowledgement of debt (14)

> [!question] Question
> Mr A is asked to lend Mr B R100 000 at 12% p.a., repayable in 12 monthly instalments, with acceleration on default. Draft a suitable acknowledgement of debt, inventing additional facts/provisions as needed.

> [!success]- Answer
> Debtor acknowledges liability for the R100 000 principal debt lent and advanced. Interest at 12% p.a., calculated monthly in arrears and capitalised monthly. Repayable in 12 monthly instalments from signature. Acceleration clause on default — full balance becomes due and payable, summons may issue without further notice. Renunciation of the benefits of legal exceptions (revision of accounts, errore calculi, no value received). Consent to Magistrate's Court jurisdiction under s45. Acceleration on insolvency/act of insolvency. Debtor pays costs on the attorney-and-client scale plus 10% collection commission. Creditor may cede/pledge without Debtor's consent. Creditor may allocate payments as it sees fit. Full balance due on Debtor's death.

## Q3 — Lease clauses: option to purchase, sub-letting prohibition, option to renew (15)

> [!question] Question
> Draft the following clauses for a business-premises lease: 3.1 option to purchase the leased premises (5); 3.2 prohibition on sub-letting and similar dealings (6); 3.3 option to renew the lease (4).

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 Lessor grants Lessee an option to purchase for the lease duration at R500 000, payable against transfer, secured by a bank guarantee within 14 days of exercise, on standard terms in an annexure; exercised by written notice to the Lessor's domicilium.
> 3.2 Lessee may not, without the Lessor's prior written consent (not unreasonably withheld): sub-let the premises or any part; allow another person to occupy them; cede/assign the lease; or alienate shares/interest in the Lessee.
> 3.3 Provided the Lessee has complied with all lease terms, it may, not later than 3 months before expiry, renew for a further 5 years on the same terms (save the renewal right itself), by written notice to the Lessor's domicilium.

## Q4 — Correct forum for nine different disputes (16)

> [!success]- Answer

## Q5 — Without-prejudice letters and defamation (10)

> [!question] Question
> Attorney Z, acting for Mrs B, forwards her a copy of a "without prejudice" settlement letter from the opponent's attorney, which also contains defamatory statements about her. Explain the position, with reference to admissibility and whether the defamatory content can found a defamation action.

> [!success]- Answer
> Statements made expressly or impliedly without prejudice in bona fide settlement negotiations cannot be disclosed in evidence without both parties' consent — a public-policy protection to encourage settlement. The privilege applies only to statements that actually form part of the negotiation, not merely because the letter is headed "without prejudice." The reply is likewise inadmissible. Statements irrelevant to the dispute (e.g. a defamatory remark) fall outside the privilege and remain actionable — as does an acknowledgement of inability to pay debts (an act of insolvency) even if made without prejudice. If the settlement offer is accepted, both letters become admissible.

## Q6 — Protecting the client and yourself after a claim prescribes (8)

> [!question] Question
> An attorney allows a claim to prescribe. What steps must he take to: 6.1 protect his client (4); 6.2 protect himself (4)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 6.1 Advise the client in writing that the claim has prescribed, without admitting negligence (which could vitiate cover under the professional indemnity policy/AIIF master policy), and advise the client to consult another attorney regarding the original claim and the prescription.
> 6.2 Immediately notify his professional indemnity insurer and the Attorneys Indemnity Insurance Fund of the potential claim; hand over any formal demand or summons received to the insurer(s).

## Q9 — Requirements for re-admission after being struck off for dishonesty (5)

> [!question] Question
> An attorney struck off for dishonesty applies for re-admission after eight years. What must he satisfy the court of?

> [!success]- Answer
> On a balance of probabilities: a genuine, complete and permanent reformation; that the character defect leading to the strike-off no longer exists; that he would in future conduct himself as an honourable member of the profession; that he can be trusted to carry out an attorney's duties; and that the public will not be prejudiced by his re-admission.

Related: [[Professional Ethics and Conduct]]

## Q10 — What is not covered by privilege (5)

> [!question] Question
> To what documents/information in an attorney's possession does privilege not apply?

> [!success]- Answer
> Documents not otherwise privileged (handing them to the attorney doesn't create privilege); communications made to facilitate a crime or fraud; the client's name (which can be compelled); facts learned by the attorney's own senses; matters where a statute expressly or impliedly excludes privilege; and — where the confidentiality of oral evidence is destroyed by that evidence being given — the confidentiality of contemporaneous records is likewise destroyed.

Related: [[Professional Ethics and Conduct]]

## Q11 — Grounds for an attorney to withdraw from the mandate (5)

> [!question] Question
> What grounds entitle an attorney to withdraw as attorney and terminate the client's mandate?

> [!success]- Answer
> Improper conduct by the client; deliberate fraud by the client; a personality clash between attorney and client; the client rejecting advice and insisting on defending a matter the attorney strongly advises settling; failure to provide funds for the attorney to continue; failure to give instructions; illness of the attorney.
