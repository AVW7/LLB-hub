## Q1 — Timing of summons after an RAF claim (3)

> [!question] Question
> How soon after a claim has been lodged with the Road Accident Fund can summons be issued and/or under what circumstances?

> [!success]- Answer
> After the expiry of a period of 120 days from the date on which the claim was sent or delivered by hand to the RAF, unless the RAF repudiates in writing before the expiry of that period.

## Q2 — When general damages are recoverable from the RAF (6)

> [!question] Question
> When would a Plaintiff be entitled to compensation for general damages (non-pecuniary loss)? Motivate your answer fully.

> [!success]- Answer
> A RAF4 Serious Injury assessment form must have been filed, stating the injury resulted in 30% or more whole person impairment (AMA guidelines). An injury below 30% WPI may still qualify as "serious" if it: resulted in serious long-term impairment or loss of a body function; constitutes permanent serious disfigurement; resulted in severe long-term mental or behavioural disturbance; or resulted in loss of a foetus — provided the injury is not on the list of exclusions published in the Government Gazette.

## Q3 — RAF claim overlapping a COIDA award (6)

> [!question] Question
> Mr X was a passenger injured on 20 April 2014 while on duty, in a vehicle driven by a fellow employee. His RAF claim was duly lodged; his employer also lodged a COIDA claim. The Compensation Commissioner made a final award of R224 000 (R11 000 s47 temporary total disablement; R63 000 s49 permanent disablement; R150 000 medical/transport/hospital expenses under s73 read with ss72 and 76). The RAF accepts liability and quantifies the claim at R850 000 (R150 000 past medical/hospital; R70 000 past loss of earnings; R430 000 future loss of earnings; R200 000 general damages).
> 3.1 What amount should be paid out by the RAF to the claimant? Motivate. (2)
> 3.2 Assume the vehicle was driven by Mr X's employer instead. How would this affect his claims? Explain fully. (4)

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 R626 000 — the total COIDA award is deducted from the total RAF damages as agreed/ordered.
> 3.2 His RAF claim falls away entirely. Under s35(1) of COIDA an employee has no common-law claim against a negligent employer; under s19(a) of the RAF Act the RAF is not liable if the owner/driver is not liable. Mr X would then only have a claim against the Compensation Commissioner.

## Q4 — Dependants' claims after a parent's death in an RAF collision (10)

> [!question] Question
> Mrs Patience Ngubane's daughter Margaret died in a collision. Margaret is survived by three dependent children: Moses (18), Mandla (10), Thulile (8), all living with their grandmother since their father died and their mother moved for work. Margaret regularly sent money home for the children's education and maintenance. Mrs Ngubane now receives child support grants but has incurred considerable personal expenses.
> 4.1 Identify the children's claims arising from their mother's death. (2)
> 4.2 Can Mandla and Thulile lodge claims in their personal capacities (both parents deceased)? (2)
> 4.3 Is Mrs Ngubane entitled to lodge claims on the children's behalf? Motivate. (2)
> 4.4 Who may lodge claims on behalf of the three children? (2)
> 4.5 The RAF's settlement offer deducts the child support grants received. What advice do you give? (2)

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 All three are entitled to loss of support from the RAF — all three, including Moses (already a major), were dependent on their mother at her death.
> 4.2 No — both are minors under the Children's Act and lack the legal capacity to litigate personally.
> 4.3 No — a grandmother's status does not confer locus standi to act for a minor child.
> 4.4 Moses may claim in his personal capacity; a curator ad litem must be appointed for Mandla and Thulile (alternatively Mrs Ngubane could be appointed their guardian).
> 4.5 The RAF is not entitled to deduct child support grants (Coughlan N.O. v Road Accident Fund [2015] ZACC 9). Reject the offer, counter-propose reinstating the deducted amounts, or proceed to summons.

## Q5 — Particulars of claim: state vehicle collision (curator ad litem plaintiff) (15)

> [!question] Question
> You act for Advocate Richard Baloyi N.O., curator ad litem for Ben Botha, to sue the Minister of Police and Captain John Jones. On 1 April 2015 at the intersection of Black and Blue Streets, Pretoria, a SAPS vehicle driven by Capt Jones (on official duty) collided with Botha's Ferrari after Jones failed to stop at a red light. The Ferrari, valued at R712 000 pre-collision, was gutted by fire and written off. Draft the particulars of claim (heading may be omitted).

> [!success]- Answer
> Plaintiff cited as curator ad litem per court order annexure; First Defendant the Minister of Police (service at the State Attorney); Second Defendant Capt Jones, acting within course and scope of employment. Plead compliance with s3 of the Institution of Legal Proceedings against certain Organs of State Act 40 of 2002 and expiry of the prescribed period. Plead the collision, sole negligence of the Second Defendant (failing to stop at a red light; failing to keep a proper lookout; excessive speed; failing to have regard to other road users — max 2 marks for grounds). Damages of R712 000 as the fair and reasonable pre-collision value of the vehicle, not economically repairable. Despite demand, Defendants failed to pay. Prayer: judgment against First and Second Defendants jointly and severally, the one paying the other absolved, for R712 000 plus interest at the prevailing rate plus costs.

## Q6 — Interpleader notice (10)

> [!question] Question
> Your client Piet Makume faces adverse claims from Paul Moroke and Jan Monakedi, who both claim payment of R500 000 for goods sold and delivered. Makume admits owing the amount but, since negotiations were all telephonic, doesn't know who to pay. Draft the notice for the relief you recommend (omit heading, don't draft the affidavit).

> [!success]- Answer
> Interpleader notice under Rule 58: notice that the applicant has paid R500 000 into court, to which the first and second claimants make adverse claims. Calls on both claimants, in terms of Rule 58(3), to deliver particulars of their claims within 15 days of service. Notifies that the applicant will apply to court for a decision on liability/validity of the claims, at a date fixed by the Registrar, not less than 15 days from the date specified for delivery of claims. The applicant's supporting affidavit will be used. Addressed to the Registrar and to each claimant.

## Q7 — Bail affidavit, s112 plea, s115 statement in a murder charge (19)

> [!question] Question
> Skiet and Donder, childhood friends, attend a party. Donder argues with the bouncer, Frakas, who punches him. Believing Donder is in danger, Skiet draws his firearm and fatally shoots Frakas. Skiet is charged with murder in the Regional Court.
> 7.1 Draft the bail affidavit, inventing necessary facts, heading included. (7)
> 7.2 Skiet is advised to plead guilty to murder — draft his s112 plea with heading and admissions. (7)
> 7.3 If Skiet instead pleads not guilty, draft his s115 statement setting out his defence, admissions as needed, heading omitted. (5)

> [!success]- Answer
> 7.1 Affidavit format: deponent's personal circumstances (address, 20 years' residence, marriage, minor children), passport details and tender of travel document to the IO, undertaking not to interfere with witnesses/investigation, undertaking to report to the IO weekly, no previous convictions, personal circumstances supporting exceptional circumstances (business responsibilities, dependants, own ill-health) — concluding that exceptional circumstances for release on bail are established.
> 7.2 s112 plea: admits attending the party with Donder, possession of a licensed loaded firearm, firing a single shot that struck and killed Frakas, no intervening cause, intention to kill (dolus directus), knowledge of wrongfulness — pleads guilty to murder.
> 7.3 s115 statement: sets out the private-defence narrative — disparity in size/build between Frakas and Donder, belief that Donder's life was at risk, the shot fired only to protect Donder — denies acting unlawfully, pleads not guilty to murder.

Related: [[Bail Applications]], [[Statements and Pleas in Criminal Trials]]

## Q8 — Bail refusal, new facts, and use of bail evidence at trial (6)

> [!question] Question
> Radovan Karadzic is arrested for murder while facing other outstanding charges. Bail is refused as not in the interests of justice. He later discloses new facts, previously not before the court, favourable to him and bearing on the magistrate's grounds for refusal.
> 8.1 Can he appeal the magistrate's decision? Advise in detail. (3)
> 8.2 What information must you, as legal representative, bring to the court's attention during the bail application? (2)
> 8.3 May the State use bail-application evidence at the subsequent trial? (1)

> [!success]- Answer
> 8.1 No — an appeal should not be brought at this stage. A fresh bail application should be brought disclosing the new facts; only if the magistrate again refuses bail should the appeal procedure follow.
> 8.2 Whether the accused has previous convictions, pending charges, and/or has been released on bail in any other matter.
> 8.3 Yes.

## Q9 — Particulars of claim: goods sold on credit with suretyship (10)

> [!question] Question

> [!success]- Answer

Prayer: judgment against First and Second Defendants jointly and severally (the one paying, the other absolved) for R340 000, interest at 18% p.a. from 1 December 2014, and costs.

## Q11 — Allegations required to plead a cession (4)

> [!question] Question
> Your client has taken written cession of a claim and sues the debtor in the Magistrate's Court. What allegations must the summons contain regarding the cession (don't draft the cession itself)?

> [!success]- Answer

## Q12 — Which Magistrate's Court has jurisdiction over a branch (2)

> [!question] Question
> The Defendant company has its principal place of business in Johannesburg Central but a branch office in Pretoria. It bought goods from the Plaintiff in Pretoria, but delivery occurred in Johannesburg.
> 12.1 Out of which Magistrate's Court must summons issue? 12.2 Why?

> [!success]- Answer
> 12.1 Johannesburg Magistrate's Court.
> 12.2 In terms of s28(1)(a) the defendant carries on business in Johannesburg's jurisdiction; "place of business" is restricted to where central management or the registered address is — a branch does not found jurisdiction.

## Q13 — Interest where judgment omitted a prayer for it (1)

> [!question] Question
> Judgment was granted, but no prayer for interest was included in the application for judgment. Is your client entitled to any interest?

> [!success]- Answer
> Yes — mora interest from the date judgment was granted, at the rate prescribed under the Prescribed Rate of Interest Act (9% in the memo's example).

## Q16 — Tender to limit costs exposure on a disputed-liability claim (2)

> [!question] Question
> Your client is sued in the District Magistrate's Court for R60 000 arising from a motor collision. He believes the Plaintiff (the other driver) was 50% to blame. He has R30 000 available. What advice would you give him to curtail costs?

> [!success]- Answer
> Serve a without-prejudice tender in terms of Rule 18(2), offering payment of R30 000 plus costs to date.
