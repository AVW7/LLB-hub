# Estate Duty Calculations and Section 4A Rebate

Tested in **every Part 2 (Estates)** sitting — both inside Question 1's **Estate Duty Addendum** (typically 12–16 marks) and often as a standalone short calculation question on **Section 4A portable spousal rebate rollover** (5–6 marks). Governed by the Estate Duty Act 45 of 1955.

> [!important] Statutory Update (Current Law vs Historical Memos)
> - **Estate Duty Rate (s2)**: Since 1 March 2018, estate duty is levied at **20%** on the first R30 million of the dutiable amount, and **25%** on the dutiable amount exceeding R30 million. (Board exam scenarios below R30m use 20% throughout.)
> - **Section 4A Primary Rebate**: **R3 500 000**, with rollover of the unused portion from a predeceased spouse up to a combined maximum of **R7 000 000** (s4A(2)).

## 1. Property of the Deceased — Section 3(2)

Start with **Total Assets as per Liquidation Account**, then adjust:
- **Deduct** proceeds of domestic life insurance policies payable to the estate (because all domestic policies are brought in separately under **s3(3)(a) Deemed Property**).
- **Adjust private company shares**: Under s5(1)(f)bis, unlisted/private shares must be valued at their auditor/statutory valuation at date of death rather than their sale price if sold for a different figure — add or deduct the difference between the Liquidation Account figure and the s5 valuation.
- **Deduct 30% farming property allowance**: Where bona fide farming operations are carried on in the Republic (s1 "fair market value" definition), deduct **30%** of the land valuation (and adjust for any farm mortgage bond if applicable per memo method).
- **Deduct surviving spouse's ½ share**: In a marriage in community of property, deduct the surviving spouse's half-share of the adjusted net joint assets (or deduct ½ of assets and ½ of liabilities separately — markers accept either method if the net result matches).
- **Add ceasing fiduciary or usufructuary rights**: Capitalised at **12% per annum** over the life expectancy of the successor (`Annual value = Asset value × 12% × life expectancy factor`).

## 2. Property Deemed to be Property — Section 3(3)

- **s3(3)(a) Domestic Life Policies**: Include policies payable to the estate AND policies payable directly to third-party beneficiaries (e.g., spouse or children), **less** any premiums paid by the beneficiary who receives the proceeds plus **6% compound interest** per annum thereon. (Policies qualifying under s3(3)(a)(i) antenuptial/postnuptial settlement or s3(3)(a)(ii) key-man/buy-and-sell are exempt.)
- **s3(3)(cA) Accrual Claim**: Any accrual claim acquired by the deceased's estate against the surviving spouse under the Matrimonial Property Act 88 of 1984.

**Gross Value of the Estate** = `Adjusted s3(2) Property + s3(3) Deemed Property`.

## 3. Allowable Deductions — Section 4

- **s4(a)–(c)**: Funeral, tombstone and deathbed expenses (`s4(a)`), debts due in SA (`s4(b)`), and administration costs (`s4(c)`). (In a joint estate, only **½** of joint liabilities and administration costs are deductible, plus **100%** of funeral expenses.)
- **s4(lA)**: Any accrual claim payable by the deceased's estate to the surviving spouse.
- **s4(q) Surviving Spouse Deduction**: So much of any property or deemed property (including direct life policy proceeds and usufructs) as accrues to the **surviving spouse**.

**Net Value of the Estate** = `Gross Value - Total s4 Deductions`.

## 4. Section 4A Rebate & Spousal Rollover

- **Primary Rebate (s4A(1))**: Deduct **R3 500 000** from the Net Value to arrive at the **Dutiable Amount**.
- **Spousal Rollover (s4A(2)–(3))**: When the deceased was the surviving spouse of one or more predeceased spouses, the primary rebate is **R3 500 000 × 2 = R7 000 000**, less any amount previously deducted under s4A in the predeceased spouse's estate (provided the executor submits the predeceased spouse's estate duty return). If the predeceased spouse's net estate was R0 (e.g., due to a s4(q) bequest to the survivor), the full **R7 000 000** rebate is available!
- **Apportionment of Estate Duty (s11 & s13)**: Where estate duty is payable and a third party (not exempt under s4(q)) receives a deemed-property life policy, that beneficiary is liable for their proportionate share:
  $$\text{Beneficiary's share} = \frac{\text{Net deemed policy value}}{\text{Net value of estate}} \times \text{Total Estate Duty}$$
