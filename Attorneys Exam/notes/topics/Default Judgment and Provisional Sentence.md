# Default Judgment and Provisional Sentence

A single but dense question in this sample, covering three distinct end-of-trial/summary outcomes.

## Absolution from the instance

Where the only witnesses give **completely irreconcilable versions** and the court genuinely cannot decide which to believe, the correct outcome is **absolution from the instance**, with the plaintiff ordered to pay the costs — not a finding for either party on the merits.

## Documents required for default judgment

Where the defendant fails to enter an appearance to defend in the Magistrate's Court:

- the original summons;
- the original return of service;
- the original and one copy of the request for default judgment;
- a damages affidavit by an expert (where damages require expert quantification, e.g. vehicle repair costs).

## Provisional sentence

Available on liquid documents (e.g. a cheque). Sequence:

1. Plaintiff issues a provisional sentence summons.
2. Defendant delivers a notice of intention to oppose and an opposing affidavit; Plaintiff may reply.
3. If the Magistrate grants provisional sentence (with costs), the **Defendant may still call for security** — the Plaintiff must then provide security for the judgment amount.
4. If security **is** given, the Defendant must, within **two months** of the provisional sentence judgment, pay the Plaintiff the claim with costs.
5. If the Plaintiff **cannot** provide security, the Defendant need not pay, and may simply proceed to defend the principal case in the ordinary way — notice of intention to defend, then a plea.
