# Trust and Business Accounting, Transfers and Reconciliations

Tested in **every Part 4 (Legal Practitioners' Bookkeeping)** sitting across 3 to 4 questions totaling 60–70 marks. Covers trust vs business cash books and ledgers, s86 trust investments, transfer journal calculations, supplementary cash books, and bank/trust reconciliations.

> [!important] Statutory Update (Legal Practice Act 28 of 2014 vs Historical Memos)
> - **Statutory Section Renumbering (effective 1 November 2018)**:
>   - **Section 86(2) LPA** replaced **Section 78(1)** of the Attorneys Act 53 of 1979 (general trust banking account — interest vests 100% in the Legal Practitioners' Fidelity Fund).
>   - **Section 86(3) LPA** replaced **Section 78(2)(a)** (investment of surplus general trust funds not immediately required — interest vests 100% in the LPFF).
>   - **Section 86(4) LPA** replaced **Section 78(2A)** (separate trust savings/investment account on a client's **written instruction** — **5%** of the interest earned vests in the LPFF under **s86(5)(b)**, and **95%** accrues to the client!).
> - **VAT Rate**: **15%** since 1 April 2018 (memos from 2015–Feb 2018 use **14%**).

## 1. Fundamental Golden Rules of Trust Accounting

1. **No Trust Account May Ever Have a Debit Balance**: You may never pay out more from the Trust Cash Book for a specific client than the **cleared credit balance** standing to that client's credit in their Trust Ledger account.
2. **Cleared Funds Only**: Never draw against an uncleared cheque/deposit; if a client's trust cheque is dishonoured (`R/D`) after a trust disbursement was already made, an **immediate trust shortfall** arises and you must immediately transfer funds from the **Business Cash Book to the Trust Cash Book** (`Dr Trust Cash Book / Cr Business Cash Book`) to cure the deficit.
3. **Fees & Business Disbursements Belong in the Business Ledger**: Professional fees (and output VAT thereon) are **never** debited directly to the Trust Ledger. First debit the client in the **Business Ledger** via the **Fees Journal** (`Dr Client (B) / Cr Fees & Cr Output VAT`), then transfer the permissible amount from Trust to Business via the **Transfer Journal** and a corresponding bank transfer between the **Trust Cash Book** and **Business Cash Book**.

## 2. Trust-to-Business Transfer Calculation Schedule

When given a schedule of clients' Business Ledger (`Dr`/`Cr`) and Trust Ledger (`Dr`/`Cr`) balances:
- **Rule for Each Client**: You may only transfer where the client has **BOTH** a **Debit (`Dr`) balance in the Business Ledger** AND a **Credit (`Cr`) balance in the Trust Ledger**.
- **Amount Transferable**: The **lesser** of the client's Business `Dr` balance and Trust `Cr` balance.
- **Irregularities to Rectify First**:
  - A **Debit balance in the Trust Ledger** (`(Dr)` in Trust) is a serious trust deficit — transfer that exact amount from **Business to Trust** (`Dr Client (B) / Cr Client (T)` and `Cr Business Cash Book / Dr Trust Cash Book`).
  - A **Credit balance in the Business Ledger** where trust money was erroneously deposited into Business must be transferred from **Business to Trust**.

## 3. Section 86 Trust Investments (Formerly s78)

| Account Type | LPA Section | Old Act | Mandate Needed? | Who Gets the Interest? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| General Trust Current Account | **s86(2)** | s78(1) | No | 100% to LPFF |
| Surplus Trust Investment | **s86(3)** | s78(2)(a) | No (firm's discretion) | 100% to LPFF |
| Client-Instructed Investment | **s86(4)** | s78(2A) | **Yes (written client mandate)** | **95% to Client**, **5% to LPFF** (s86(5)(b)) |

- **Accounting for s86(4) Interest**: When the bank pays back capital + interest into the Trust Cash Book:
  - Credit the client's Trust Ledger with the client's share of interest (95% post-LPA; 100% in pre-LPA s78(2A) memos).
  - Credit the LPFF / Law Society (or pay directly) with the 5% statutory levy (or 100% of interest on a s86(3) / s78(2)(a) general investment).
- **Quarterly Trust Reconciliation**: Total of the **List of Trust Creditors** (every credit balance in the Trust Ledger) must equal **Total Trust Funds Available** (`Trust Cash Book Balance + s86(3) Investments + s86(4) Investments`), plus any transferable surplus not yet transferred.

## 4. Supplementary Cash Book & Bank Reconciliation Statement (15 marks)

Tested in almost every Paper 4 sitting:
1. **Step 1 — Supplementary Cash Book**: Start with the provisional Cash Book balance at month-end and post items appearing on the Bank Statement that have **not yet been recorded** in the Cash Book (bank charges, interest on overdraft/credit, debit/stop orders, dishonoured `R/D` cheques, direct EFT deposits, and **cash book errors** where the firm recorded the wrong amount). Calculate the **adjusted Cash Book balance**.
2. **Step 2 — Bank Reconciliation Statement**: Reconcile the **adjusted Cash Book balance** with the **Bank Statement balance** using items not yet reflected by the bank:
   - **Outstanding deposits** (deposited in Cash Book, not yet credited on Bank Statement).
   - **Unpresented cheques / outstanding EFTs** (paid in Cash Book, not yet debited on Bank Statement).
   - **Bank errors** (e.g., a trust deposit erroneously credited to the business bank statement by the bank, or vice versa).
