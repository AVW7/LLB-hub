# Intestate Succession and Representation

Tested in **every Part 2 (Estates)** sitting as a dedicated 8–20 mark family-tree distribution problem, and frequently integrated into Question 1's Distribution Account. Governed by the **Intestate Succession Act 81 of 1987** (as read with *Bhe v Magistrate, Khayelitsha* and *Hassam v Jacobs* for polygynous marriages, and *Bwanya v Master of the High Court* [2021] ZACC 51).

> [!important] Statutory Update (Current Law vs Historical Memos)
> - **Statutory Preferent Amount (s1(1)(c)(i))**: Fixed at **R250 000** since 24 November 2014 (GN 920 of 2014). Note that the **2015-02-10** sitting memo still used the older R125 000 figure; all subsequent sittings use **R250 000**.
> - **Permanent Life Partners (*Bwanya* [2021] ZACC 51)**: Historical memos prior to 2022 state that an unmarried cohabiting partner ("companion") inherits nothing on intestacy. Following *Bwanya v Master of the High Court* (and the Judicial Matters Amendment Act 15 of 2023), a surviving partner in a permanent life partnership in which the partners had undertaken reciprocal duties of support qualifies as a "spouse" under the Intestate Succession Act and the Maintenance of Surviving Spouses Act.

## Core Distribution Rules (Section 1)

1. **Preliminary Matrimonial Property Step**:
   - If married **in community of property**, the surviving spouse first takes **50% (½)** of the net joint estate by virtue of the marriage (after adding back funeral expenses, which are then deducted solely from the deceased's half-share).
   - If married **out of community with accrual**, adjust for the accrual claim (asset or liability) before distributing the net estate.
2. **Spouse Only, No Descendants (s1(1)(a))**: Surviving spouse inherits the **entire** intestate estate.
3. **Descendants Only, No Spouse (s1(1)(b))**: Descendants inherit the entire estate **per stirpes** by representation (s1(4)(a)).
4. **Spouse(s) AND Descendants (s1(1)(c))**:
   - **Calculating a Child's Share (s1(4)(f))**: Divide the net intestate estate by the number of children of the deceased who have either **survived** the deceased OR have **predeceased** the deceased leaving surviving descendants, **plus the number of surviving spouses** (every surviving spouse in a polygynous marriage counts as a separate unit in the divisor!).
   - **Surviving Spouse's Share (s1(1)(c)(i))**: Each surviving spouse inherits a **child's share or R250 000, whichever is the greater** (if the estate is insufficient to pay R250 000 to each spouse in a polygynous marriage, the estate is divided equally among the spouses).
   - **Residue to Descendants (s1(1)(c)(ii))**: The balance after deducting the spouse(s)' share(s) is divided equally among the deceased's descendants *per stirpes*.
5. **No Spouse and No Descendants (Parentelas)**:
   - **Both parents alive (s1(1)(d)(i))**: Parents inherit in equal shares (½ each).
   - **One parent alive + descendants of predeceased parent (s1(1)(d)(ii))**: Surviving parent takes **½**; descendants of the predeceased parent take the other **½** *per stirpes* (if no descendants of the predeceased parent, the surviving parent takes the whole estate).
   - **Both parents dead, leaving descendants of parents (s1(1)(e)(i))**: Estate splits into **½ maternal line (cloven)** and **½ paternal line**: full-blood siblings share in both halves; half-blood siblings share only in the half of their common parent.
   - **Nearest blood relatives (s1(1)(f))**: If no relatives in the first or second parentela exist, the nearest blood relatives inherit **per capita** in equal shares.

## Special Rules Heavily Tested in Exam Scenarios

- **Nasciturus Fiction**: An unborn child conceived before the deceased's death is counted as an heir **if born alive**. Always show **both** alternative calculations in the exam: (1) if born alive, and (2) if not born alive!
- **Renunciation by a Descendant when a Spouse Survives (s1(6))**:
  - If a **descendant** of the deceased (who together with the surviving spouse is entitled to inherit) **renounces** their right to inherit, that share **vests in the surviving spouse(s)** — NOT in the renouncing descendant's own children!
  - **Minors cannot renounce** an inheritance without High Court / Master consent, so an attempted renunciation by a minor child in an exam scenario is invalid and the minor still inherits their full share.
- **Disqualification / Unworthiness (s1(7) & *De bloedige hand erf niet*)**:
  - A person who unlawfully caused the death of the deceased is disqualified from inheriting.
  - Under **s1(7)**, the **descendants** of a disqualified heir inherit the share that the disqualified person would have received by representation (*per stirpes*), unlike s1(6) renunciation where a surviving spouse takes the share!
- **Adopted and Extra-Marital Children (s1(4)(e) & s1(2))**:
  - An **adopted child** is deemed to be the descendant of their adoptive parent(s) and **ceased** to be the descendant of their biological parents (except where adopted by the spouse of a biological parent).
  - Illegitimate / extra-marital status does **not** affect the capacity of a blood relation to inherit on intestacy (s1(2)).
