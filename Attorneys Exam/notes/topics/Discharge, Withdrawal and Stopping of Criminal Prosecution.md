# Discharge, Withdrawal and Stopping of Criminal Prosecution

Three distinct ways a criminal case can end before a verdict on the merits — tested together in one paper as short, precise definitional questions. Easy to confuse; the timing of each is the examinable distinction.

## s174 discharge at close of the State's case

Where, after the State closes its case, there is little or no evidence implicating the accused: apply for **discharge under s174**. The court must be of the opinion there is **no evidence** the accused committed the offence charged (or any competent alternative verdict) — if so, it **may return a verdict of not guilty**.

## Withdrawal of charges (before plea)

The State may **withdraw** charges against an accused **before the accused pleads**. Effect: the accused **may be charged again** with the same offence later — withdrawal does not bar re-prosecution.

## Stopping a prosecution (after plea, before judgment)

The State may **stop** a prosecution **after the accused has pleaded but before judgment is delivered**. Effect: the accused **must be acquitted** of the charge — stopping (unlike withdrawal) is final and bars re-prosecution on that charge.

## The three side by side

| Mechanism | Who applies | When | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| s174 discharge | Defence | After State closes its case | Verdict of not guilty (if no evidence) |
| Withdrawal | State | Before plea | Accused may be recharged later |
| Stopping | State | After plea, before judgment | Accused must be acquitted (final) |
