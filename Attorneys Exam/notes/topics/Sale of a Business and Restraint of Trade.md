# Sale of a Business and Restraint of Trade

A single, dense, 25-mark question in this sample — but it packs together security-for-payment structuring, suspensive conditions, restraint of trade, and the mandatory insolvency-law newspaper notice, so it's worth its own topic rather than folding entirely into general contract drafting.

## Securing payment of the purchase price

Where the price is paid partly on signature and partly in instalments, advise on a combination of:

- a **notarial bond** over the equipment sold, in favour of the seller;
- **cession of book debts**;
- a **third-party surety** for the purchaser;
- a **life policy** on the purchaser (or ceded to the seller) for the balance;
- **reservation of ownership** in the sale agreement itself, plus an **acceleration clause**.

## Suspensive conditions typical of a business sale

- Perfecting/assigning the **lease agreement** with the landlord of the trading premises.
- Obtaining **transfer of the liquor and trading licences**.

## Acceleration clause

On any missed payment, the **full outstanding balance immediately becomes due, owing and payable** — protects the seller from having to sue instalment-by-instalment.

## Restraint of trade clause

State the **duration** (from the effective date), the **geographic area**, and the **scope of the prohibited activity** (operating, or holding a direct/indirect interest in, a competing business). Include an express acknowledgment that the restraint is **fair and reasonable** having regard to the nature of the business purchased, its duration, and the area — this recital helps the restraint survive a later reasonableness challenge.

## Section 34 Insolvency Act notice

A sale of a business (as a going concern, "lock, stock and barrel") must be advertised in the Government Gazette and a newspaper under **s34(1) of the Insolvency Act 24 of 1936**, naming the seller, the business and its trading address, the buyer, and stating the sale takes effect **30 days after the last publication**. Failing to publish this notice exposes the sale proceeds to creditors of the seller's business for that period.
