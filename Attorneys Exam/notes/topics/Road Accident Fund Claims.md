# Road Accident Fund Claims

The most heavily tested topic in every Part 1 (Court Procedures) paper in this sample — appears in all three Part 1 sittings, always mixed with delict/particulars-of-claim drafting or a related statutory scheme (COIDA).

## Core framework

- A claimant generally has **no direct claim** against the negligent driver/owner — the claim lies against the **Road Accident Fund**, except: (a) where the Fund/its agent is unable to pay compensation, or (b) for emotional shock suffered by a person who was not themselves a third party but who witnessed/observed/was informed of another's injury or death from the driving of a motor vehicle.
- **Summons timing**: only after 120 days from lodging the claim, unless the RAF repudiates in writing before then.
- **"Motor vehicle"** = designed/adapted for propulsion on a road by fuel, gas or electricity, including trailers, caravans, and agricultural/other implements drawn by such a vehicle. A **go-cart is included** (fuel-propelled and road-adapted in the exam's facts); a **bicycle is excluded**.
- **General damages (non-pecuniary loss)** require a filed RAF4 Serious Injury Assessment form showing 30%+ whole person impairment (AMA guidelines), OR — below that threshold — the "narrative test": serious long-term impairment/loss of a body function; permanent serious disfigurement; severe long-term mental/behavioural disturbance; or loss of a foetus. Subject always to the Gazetted exclusions list.

## Locus standi and dependants

- A parent/natural guardian has locus standi for a minor's claim regardless of a differing surname.
- Whoever **personally incurred** funeral expenses has locus standi to claim them (not necessarily the estate).
- **Life partners** (unmarried, cohabiting, with marriage-like characteristics — joint household, intent to marry, lobola negotiations underway) can claim loss of support: *Paixão v Road Accident Fund*.
- Minor dependants of a deceased breadwinner have loss-of-support claims but lack capacity to litigate personally — a **curator ad litem** must be appointed (or a major sibling may claim personally); a grandmother's custodial role does **not** confer locus standi to claim on a minor's behalf.
- The RAF **may not deduct child support grants** from a loss-of-support settlement: *Coughlan N.O. v Road Accident Fund* [2015] ZACC 9.
- Medical aid payments are a **collateral benefit** — not deductible by the RAF.

## Interaction with COIDA (workplace accidents)

- An employee injured on duty by a **negligent co-employee** has no RAF claim against the co-employee directly, but has a claim against the **Compensation Commissioner** under COIDA — a strict-liability scheme, so the co-employee's/claimant's own negligence doesn't defeat it.
- If the driver at fault **is the employer**, s19(a) of the RAF Act removes RAF liability where the owner/driver isn't liable — the employee then has **only** a COIDA claim (no RAF claim at all).
- Where **both** an RAF claim and a COIDA award exist for the same collision (e.g. injured passenger, not the negligent driver), the COIDA award is **deducted** from the total RAF damages.
- COIDA compensates **special damages only** — no general damages recoverable there, and none from the wrongdoer either (RAF Act/Transitional Provisions Act expressly exclude it).

## Multi-party collisions

- A passenger's claim depends on whose negligence caused the collision and whether that person was driving a "motor vehicle." A cyclist's negligence alone gives no RAF claim to anyone; if a car driver **contributed** even partially, a claim arises based on that driver's negligence.
