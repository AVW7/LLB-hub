# Professional Ethics and Conduct

> [!important] Statutory Update (Legal Practice Act 28 of 2014)
> - Since **1 November 2018**, the **Legal Practice Act 28 of 2014 (LPA)**, the **Legal Practice Council (LPC)**, the **LPC Rules**, and the **LPC Code of Conduct** replaced the Attorneys Act 53 of 1979 and the provincial statutory Law Societies.
> - Where pre-2019 memos refer to the "Statutory Law Society", "Attorneys Fidelity Fund (AFF)", or "Attorneys Insurance Indemnity Fund (AIIF)", current answers should refer to the **Legal Practice Council (LPC)**, the **Legal Practitioners' Fidelity Fund (LPFF)**, and the **Legal Practitioners' Indemnity Insurance Fund (LPIIF)** (while noting historical equivalents if quoting older case law).

The dominant theme of both Part 3 (Attorney's Practice) papers in this sample — a grab-bag of discrete ethics/practice-management questions rather than one integrated scenario. High-yield: short, memorisable, list-based answers.

## Sources of the rules of professional conduct

The Attorneys Act 53 of 1979 (as amended) and its Regulations; the Rules of the various Law Societies; rulings of the Councils; the common law; South African textbooks; foreign influences; court judgments.

## Correct forum for a complaint or claim (know the full list)

| Situation | Forum |
|---|---|
| R14 000 damages claim | Small Claims Court |
| Constitutional challenge to a provision | High Court |
| Reinstatement after unfair dismissal | CCMA |
| Unfair pension benefit calculation | Pension Funds Adjudicator |
| Interrogating an insolvent hiding assets | Master of the High Court (who may appoint a magistrate) |
| Amend/enforce a High Court maintenance order | Maintenance/Family Court |
| Excessive attorney's fee, criminal trial | Statutory Law Society (Assessment Panel) |
| Trust money apparently stolen by an attorney | Attorneys Fidelity Fund and Statutory Law Society |
| Loss from a colleague's professional negligence | Sue the attorney directly; approach the AIIF and their indemnity insurers |

## Recusal of a presiding officer

If you discover a conflict (e.g. the magistrate is the complainant's relative), raise it with the magistrate **in chambers first**; if declined, apply for recusal **in open court**; if still refused, the trial proceeds without further review/appeal avenue at that point. An acquittal moots the issue; a conviction preserves review grounds.

## Privilege — what it does NOT cover

Documents not otherwise privileged (they don't become privileged merely by being handed to the attorney); communications made to facilitate a crime or fraud; the client's **name** (compellable); facts learned by the attorney's own senses; matters where a statute expressly/impliedly excludes privilege; and — where confidentiality of oral evidence is destroyed by that evidence being given — the confidentiality of contemporaneous records is likewise destroyed.

## Without-prejudice communications

Statements made expressly/impliedly without prejudice, in the course of bona fide settlement negotiations, cannot be disclosed in evidence without both parties' consent (public policy favouring settlement). Merely heading a letter "without prejudice" doesn't privilege content **irrelevant** to the negotiation — a defamatory remark inside such a letter remains actionable, and an acknowledgment of inability to pay debts remains an act of insolvency regardless of the heading. If a without-prejudice offer is **accepted**, both letters become admissible.

## Withdrawing from a mandate

Good cause includes: improper conduct by the client; the client's deliberate fraud; a personality clash; the client rejecting strong advice to settle and insisting on defending; failure to provide funds to continue; failure to give instructions; the attorney's own illness.

## Re-admission after being struck off

The onus is on the attorney (balance of probabilities) to show: a genuine, complete and permanent reformation; that the character defect leading to the strike-off no longer exists; that they would in future act as an honourable member of the profession; that they can be trusted with an attorney's duties; and that the public will not be prejudiced by re-admission.

## Commissioner of oaths — conflict of interest

An attorney may **not** administer the oath in a matter in which their own firm is involved (deemed to have an interest) — **except conveyancing matters**, which the Act and Regulations specifically exclude from the "interest" disqualification.
