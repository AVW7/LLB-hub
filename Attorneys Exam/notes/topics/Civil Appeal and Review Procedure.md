# Civil Appeal and Review Procedure

Tested once directly in this sample (both sub-questions in the same paper), but a core drafting skill. Distinct from the criminal bail-appeal procedure in [[Bail Applications]] and from the CCP module's criminal appeal/review notes — this is the civil-litigation version.

## Appeal to the full bench

Requirements before drafting a Notice of Appeal: leave to appeal must already have been **granted** by the presiding judge (or, failing that, by the Supreme Court of Appeal on petition — not tested directly here but implicit). The Notice of Appeal states:

- that the Appellant appeals to the (full bench of the) court;
- against the whole (or specified parts) of the judgment and order;
- identifying the judge, the court a quo, and the case number;
- noting the appeal is with leave, the date leave was granted, and attaching a certified copy of the leave order;
- the grounds of appeal (specific, not generic — attacking particular findings of fact or law).

## Grounds for review of an inferior court (s22, Superior Courts Act)

A lower court's proceedings may be brought under review in the High Court on the grounds of:

1. **Absence of jurisdiction** on the part of the court;
2. The **presiding officer having an interest** in the case, being **biased or malicious**, or having committed an offence referred to in s22;
3. **Gross irregularity** in the proceedings;
4. The **admission of inadmissible/incompetent evidence**, or the **rejection of admissible/competent evidence**.

## Appeal vs review — why it matters in drafting

Appeal challenges the **correctness** of the decision on the merits and requires leave; review challenges the **fairness/regularity of the process** by which the decision was reached and does not require leave to appeal (though its own procedural requirements apply). Choosing the wrong remedy for the facts given is a common trap — read for process defects (bias, jurisdiction, irregularity) versus substantive legal/factual error.
