# Trial Notices for Expert and Documentary Evidence

A narrow but recurring drafting skill: notices that let a party lead evidence at trial without the usual formal proof, or without ambushing the other side.

## Notice to call an expert witness

Two staged notices, with strict minimum periods before the hearing:

1. **At least 15 days before the hearing** — notice of intention to call an expert.
2. **At least 10 days before the hearing** — a further notice summarising the expert's opinion and the reasons for it, including qualifying them as an expert, confirming they examined the relevant party (e.g. the plaintiff), and stating their findings.

## Notice to use photographs without further proof

Where a party wants to use photographs (e.g. of a collision scene) at trial without calling a witness to formally prove them:

- state the photographs will be used at trial, with copies annexed and the originals available for inspection at the attorney's offices;
- give the recipient **five days** from receipt to raise any objection to their admission without proof;
- failing objection, the photographs are received in evidence on **mere production**, without further proof.

## Why these matter

Both notices exist to save trial time by converting what would otherwise require oral proof (an expert's qualifications and opinion; the authenticity of a photograph) into an unopposed formality — provided the notice periods are respected. Missing a notice period is a common way to lose the ability to rely on evidence without calling extra witnesses.
