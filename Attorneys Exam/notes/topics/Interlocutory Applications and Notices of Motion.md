# Interlocutory Applications and Notices of Motion

Tested in two of the three Part 1 papers sampled — a procedural application distinct from the main action, brought to unblock trial preparation or to enforce compliance.

## Core framework

- An **interlocutory application** seeks intermediate/procedural relief incidental to pending proceedings — it does not deal with the merits of the main case.
- **Short form (Form 2A)** notice of motion is used for interlocutory applications; the long form (Form 2) is reserved for applications that are themselves the principal relief sought.
- Typical structure of the founding affidavit: deponent's capacity, the parties, the underlying proceedings and their status (trial date, set-down annexed), the specific procedural default relied on (e.g. failure to answer a Rule 21 request for particulars within the required period, with the request and a compliance letter annexed), and the prayer for relief per the notice of motion.

## Compelling compliance and its escalation

1. Serve the request/notice requiring the step (e.g. Rule 21 further particulars, at least 20 court days before trial).
2. If ignored, send a letter demanding compliance within a stated period (commonly 7 days) and warning of an application to compel.
3. If still ignored, launch the interlocutory application to compel, with costs of the application.
4. If the court order is **still** not complied with, the next application seeks that the defaulting party's **defence be struck out** and judgment entered, with costs of both the application and the action.

## Related procedural relief: withdrawal without a costs tender

Where an opponent withdraws proceedings without tendering costs, the correct route is also an **application** (not an automatic entitlement) for an order that the withdrawing party pay the costs of the withdrawn proceedings and of the application itself — see [[Costs, Tenders and Withdrawal of Action]] for the full notice-of-motion content.
