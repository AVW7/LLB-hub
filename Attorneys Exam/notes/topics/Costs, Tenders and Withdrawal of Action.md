# Costs, Tenders and Withdrawal of Action

A recurring fact pattern — a plaintiff withdraws proceedings without tendering costs — appears near-verbatim in two separate sittings, alongside a related costs-limiting tender strategy.

## Withdrawal without a costs tender

Where a plaintiff serves a notice of withdrawal of the action **without tendering costs**, the defendant is not automatically entitled to costs — the remedy is an **application** for an order that the plaintiff pay:

- the costs of the withdrawn proceedings; and
- the costs of the application itself.

This step should be taken **within a reasonable time** of receiving the notice of withdrawal (no fixed statutory time limit). The notice of motion is supported by an affidavit and addressed to the Registrar and to the plaintiff's attorney.

> [!tip] Recurring fact pattern
> This exact scenario — "the Plaintiff withdraws the action without tendering costs; what is your client's remedy?" — is tested near-identically in [[AttExam 2016-08-16]] Q4 (defendant's side, full notice of motion required) and [[AttExam 2017-02-14]] Q15 (shorter version, remedy only). Worth memorising as a single reusable answer.

## Tendering payment to limit costs exposure

Where your own client (as defendant) accepts partial liability and wants to cap costs exposure, the tool is a **without-prejudice tender under Rule 18(2)** — offering payment of the amount you accept is owed, plus costs to date. If the plaintiff later recovers no more than the tendered amount at trial, this protects the defendant's costs position from that date forward.
