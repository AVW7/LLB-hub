# Execution of Judgments and Interpleader

Post-judgment enforcement mechanics — tested across two of the three Part 1 papers, always as short, discrete drafting/procedure questions rather than one large scenario.

## Enforcement tools against a judgment debtor

- **No attachable assets, but employed on a good salary** → a **s65 enquiry**, followed by an **emoluments attachment order (EAO)** requiring the employer to deduct a fixed monthly amount.
- **Employer fails to honour an existing EAO** despite the debtor remaining employed there → issue a **warrant of execution against the employer** for the arrear amount.
- **Interest on a judgment debt**: even where no prayer for interest was included in the application for judgment, the client is still entitled to **mora interest** from the date judgment was granted, at the rate under the Prescribed Rate of Interest Act.
- **Interest rate on the underlying claim**: where the statutory rate changes between issue of summons and judgment, it is the rate applicable **at the time the action was instituted** that governs, not the rate in force at judgment.

## Interpleader (Rule 58)

Used where two or more people make **adverse claims** to money/property in a party's possession (or attached under a judgment) and that party cannot determine who is entitled to it.

- The **notice** records that the sum/property has been paid into court (or is held), to which the claimants make adverse claims;
- calls on the claimants to deliver **particulars of their claims within 15 days** of service;
- notifies that the applicant will apply to court for a decision on liability/the claims' validity, at a date fixed by the Registrar not less than 15 days after the deadline for delivering claims;
- is supported by an affidavit;
- is addressed to the Registrar and to each claimant.

A common variant: a **third party (e.g. a spouse) claims ownership** of goods you've attached under a judgment — the correct procedure name is simply **interpleader**.
