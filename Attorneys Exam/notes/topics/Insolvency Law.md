# Insolvency Law

Appears twice in the same paper — once as a spousal-protection scenario, once as the mandatory newspaper notice accompanying a business sale (see [[Sale of a Business and Restraint of Trade]]).

## Effect of a spouse's sequestration (marriage out of community of property)

Where a husband holding most of the couple's assets is sequestrated, **those assets vest first in the Master, then in the trustee** — even though the wife is married out of community and the assets may in substance be hers or jointly used. The wife's remedy is to obtain **release of her assets** by proving her ownership, usually by affidavit to the trustee/Master, and — if that fails — by a **High Court application**.

## Section 34 sale-of-business notice

A business sold as a going concern must be advertised under **s34(1) of the Insolvency Act 24 of 1936** — see [[Sale of a Business and Restraint of Trade]] for the full notice content and the 30-day effective-date rule. The underlying policy: protecting the seller's creditors from a business being sold and its proceeds dissipated without notice.
