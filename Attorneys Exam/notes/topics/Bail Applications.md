# Bail Applications

Tested in every Part 1 sitting in this module — usually paired with a plea/statement question on the same fact pattern. This module's own notes stand alone; the same core CPA provisions (s60(4), s60(11B)) are also examined in the CCP module, but treat this as the self-contained Board Exam version.

## Core framework

- An accused in custody is entitled to release at any stage before conviction **if the court is satisfied the interests of justice permit it**, subject to exceptional circumstances given the nature of certain offences (e.g. Schedule 6).
- **Docket access**: an accused is **not** entitled, for bail purposes, to peruse the police docket.
- **s60(11B)(a) disclosures** — as legal representative you must bring to the court's attention: whether the accused has previous convictions; whether there are pending charges; and whether currently on bail in respect of those charges.
- **s60(4) grounds for refusing bail** — likelihood the accused, if released, will: endanger public/individual safety or commit a Schedule 1 offence; evade trial; influence/intimidate witnesses or conceal/destroy evidence; undermine or jeopardise the criminal justice system (including the bail system); or, in exceptional circumstances, disturb public order/security.
- **Multiple travel documents**: suggest surrender of all passports and regular reporting to a police station as bail conditions.

## Drafting the bail affidavit

Cover: personal circumstances (address, length of residence, ownership of property, marital/family status); travel documents and a tender to surrender them; undertakings not to interfere with witnesses/the investigation and to report periodically to the investigating officer; absence of previous convictions; ties to the community and dependants relying on the accused (business responsibilities, ill family members, childcare); the accused's own health if relevant. Conclude by submitting that exceptional circumstances (where required) or the interests of justice favour release.

## After a bail refusal

- If **new facts** emerge that weren't before the court and bear on the magistrate's grounds for refusal, the correct step is a **fresh bail application** disclosing those facts — **not** an immediate appeal. Only if the magistrate again refuses bail should the appeal procedure follow.
- Evidence given during a bail application **may** be used by the State at the subsequent trial.

## Section 204 witnesses in the same trial

Where the State needs a co-accused's testimony, s204 CPA lets the court warn the witness they must testify, that self-incriminating questions may be put, and that answering frankly and honestly earns discharge from prosecution for the specified offence. The witness's own attorney may hold a watching brief and argue for that discharge at the trial's end.
