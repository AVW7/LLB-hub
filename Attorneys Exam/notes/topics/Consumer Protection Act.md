# Consumer Protection Act

A single but substantial (15-mark) question in this sample — the implied-warranty regime for defective goods.

## Implied warranty (ss55–56, CPA)

Every transaction/agreement carries an implied warranty from the **producer/importer, distributor, and retailer** that the goods:

1. are reasonably suitable for their generally intended purpose;
2. are of good quality, in good working order, and free of defects;
3. will be reliable and durable for a reasonable period; and
4. comply with the Standards Act.

## Remedies where goods fail within 6 months of delivery

The consumer may return the goods **without penalty**, at the **supplier's risk and expense**, and the supplier must either:

- **repair or replace** the goods; or
- **refund** the price paid.

If a **repair** is attempted and, within **3 months** of that repair, the same failure/defect/unsafe feature recurs, the supplier must then **replace the goods or refund the purchase price** — the consumer isn't stuck in a repeat-repair cycle.

## Voetstoots clauses don't help the supplier

A supplier **cannot contractually exclude** the CPA's implied warranty — a voetstoots ("as is") clause in the sale agreement does not defeat a claim under ss55–56 where the CPA applies to the transaction.
