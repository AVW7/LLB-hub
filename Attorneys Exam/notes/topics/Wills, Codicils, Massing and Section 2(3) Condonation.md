# Wills, Codicils, Massing and Section 2(3) Condonation

Tested in **every Part 2 (Estates)** sitting through practical drafting (joint wills, single wills, codicils, s2(1)(a)(v) certificates, adiation certificates, redistribution agreements) and short problem questions on the **Wills Act 7 of 1953**.

## 1. Execution Formalities & Section 2(1)(a)(v) Certificates

- **Testamentary Capacity (s4)**: Every person aged **16 years or older** unless mentally incapable of appreciating the nature and effect of their act.
- **Competent Witness (s1)**: Any person aged **14 years or older** who at the time of witnessing is not incompetent to give evidence in a court of law.
- **Standard Execution (s2(1)(a)(i)–(iv))**:
  - Signed at the **end** by the testator (or by an amanuensis in the testator's presence and by their direction).
  - Signature made or acknowledged in the presence of **two or more competent witnesses** present at the same time.
  - Witnesses attest and sign the will in the presence of the testator and of each other (witnesses only need to sign the **last page**; the testator must sign every page other than the last page anywhere on the page).
- **Execution by a Mark, Thumbprint, or Amanuensis (s2(1)(a)(v))**:
  - Where the testator signs by making a **mark** (or thumbprint) or asks another person to sign on their behalf, a **Commissioner of Oaths** must append a certificate and sign **every other page** of the will (anywhere on the page).
  - **Essential wording of the s2(1)(a)(v) Certificate**:
    > I, [Full Name and Address], in my capacity as Commissioner of Oaths, certify that I have satisfied myself as to the identity of the testator [Full Name], and that the accompanying Will is the Will of the testator.
  - Timing: Must be appended as soon as possible after the will is signed (and may be appended even after the testator dies, per s2(1)(a)(v)(bb)).

## 2. Amendments (s2(1)(b)), Condonation (s2(3)), Divorce (s2B) & Disqualification (s4A)

- **Amendments to a Will (s2(1)(b))**: Any deletion, addition, alteration, or interlineation made after execution must be identified by the signature of the testator and two witnesses (plus a s2(1)(a)(v) certificate if made by mark/amanuensis).
- **Court Condonation Rescue Clause (s2(3))**: If a court is satisfied that a document (or amendment) was **drafted or executed** by a person who has died since, and that the deceased **intended** that document (or amendment) to be their will (or an amendment to their will), the High Court **shall** order the Master to accept it despite non-compliance with s2(1) formalities.
- **Effect of Divorce or Annulment (s2B)**: If a testator dies **within three months** after their marriage is dissolved by divorce or annulment, a will executed prior to that dissolution takes effect as if the **previous spouse had died before the dissolution**, unless it appears from the will that the testator intended to benefit the ex-spouse notwithstanding the divorce. Once **more than 3 months** have elapsed without the testator altering the will, the ex-spouse regains their status as beneficiary!
- **Disqualification of Witness, Writer, or Signer (s4A)**:
  - Anyone who attests and signs a will as a witness, or writes out the will (or any part thereof) in their own handwriting, or signs for the testator, and **their spouse at the time of execution**, is disqualified from receiving any **benefit** under that will (including nomination as **executor**, trustee, or guardian!).
  - **Exceptions (s4A(2))**:
    1. A High Court declares the person competent because it is satisfied they **did not defraud or unduly influence** the testator (`s4A(2)(a)`).
    2. The person would have been an **intestate heir**, in which case they may inherit under the will up to the value of their **intestate share** (`s4A(2)(b)`).
    3. At least two other competent, non-benefiting witnesses attested the will (`s4A(2)(c)`).

## 3. Joint Wills, Massing (s37) & Adiation / Repudiation

- **Massing (s37 Administration of Estates Act)**: Occurs when two or more persons consolidate their separate estates (or their half-shares of a joint estate) into a single massed unit on the death of the first-dying, disposing of the massed estate after the death of the first-dying while conferring a **limited interest** (such as a **usufruct** or fiduciary interest) on the survivor.
- **Election (Adiation vs Repudiation)**:
  - **Adiation**: The survivor accepts the benefit (e.g., lifelong usufruct over the whole massed estate) and surrenders their own estate/half-share to the ultimate heirs; they can no longer revoke or alter the disposition of the massed estate. Drafted via an **Adiation Certificate** attested by two witnesses and an attorney's certificate explaining the consequences.
  - **Repudiation**: The survivor rejects all benefits under the joint will and retains their own separate estate (or half-share of the joint estate) free of the will's terms.

## 4. Redistribution Agreements (s14(1)(b)(iii) Deeds Registries Act)

Used where heirs (and/or the surviving spouse) agree to reshuffle specific assets rather than taking undivided shares:
- Cite all parties with capacity (if a minor is involved, the Master must consent under s80/s94).
- Recite (`WHEREAS`) the deceased's death, the original testate/intestate entitlement, and the parties' desire to redistribute.
- Set out the exact assets and cash equalisation awarded to each heir.
- Include the mandatory clause that the agreement is **subject to confirmation/acceptance by the Master of the High Court**.
