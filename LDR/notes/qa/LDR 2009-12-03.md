## Q1 — Incapacity dismissal for ill health (8)

> [!question] Question
> Mike is employed by X Computer Company. He is chronically absent from work and his absence is affecting the flow of work at the company. After each absence he hands in a medical certificate to the employer.
> 1.1 Is X Company entitled to terminate Mike's employment? Explain (2). 1.2 Assume X Company is entitled to terminate — describe in detail the procedure the company must follow (5). 1.3 Explain the source where the above procedure is found (1).

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 Incapacity on ground of ill-health is a valid reason for dismissal. However this is subject to a fair procedure/investigation being followed.
> 1.2 The employer must hold an investigation. The investigation shall look at whether the illness is temporary or permanent. If temporary, the extent of the incapacity should be investigated. If the employee is likely to be absent unreasonably long, the employer should investigate all the alternatives short of dismissal. When alternatives are considered, relevant factors include the nature of the job, the period of absence, seriousness of the injury and the possibility of securing a temporary replacement. In cases of permanent incapacity, the employer could consider another post for the employee or adapting the employee's duties or circumstances to accommodate the disability. During the investigation the employee should be assisted by a trade union representative or fellow employee. The employee should be permitted to state a case in response. The degree and/or cause of the incapacity is relevant. In certain kinds of incapacity, e.g. alcoholism or drug abuse, counselling and rehabilitation may be appropriate steps to consider. The duty on an employer to accommodate an employee who was injured at work is more onerous. (Any five answers will do.)
> 1.3 Schedule 8, Code of Good Practice: Dismissal, Labour Relations Act, 66 of 1995.

## Q2 — Discrimination: sexual orientation and a medical scheme (4)

> [!question] Question
> John, a homosexual employee of Brookes Hill company, wishes to register his friend with whom he was living for six years as a dependant member of his medical scheme. The rules of the fund do not make provision for such an inclusion and the employer refuses to change the rules to make it possible.
> 4.1 To which body must he refer the dispute (1)? 4.2 Is it possible for his request to be successful? Motivate your answer (3).

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 CCMA.
> 4.2 Yes — unfair discrimination on the ground of sexual orientation.

## Q3 — Protected vs unprotected strike (9)

> [!question] Question
> The employees of Spar Supermarket refuse to continue to work because the employer will not grant their demand of 15% salary increase. This refusal to work takes place while salary negotiations between the employer and the trade union are still in progress. No prior notice of the strike was given to the employer. The refusal to work is resulting in thousands of rands damage to Spar Supermarket every day.
> 3.1 Is the strike a protected strike? Motivate your answer (3). 3.2 May the employer dismiss the employees on strike (1)? 3.3 May the employer employ replacement labour (1)? 3.4 Is it possible for the employees to strike if the employer had unilaterally amended conditions of employment and refuses to restore the status quo prior to negotiations (3)? 3.5 May damages be awarded to the employer (1)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 No — does not comply with the requirements for a protected strike: refer the dispute to the CCMA — if no resolution, or 30 days expire without resolution, then 48 hours' notice to the employer of intention to strike is required.
> 3.2 Yes — Sec 67.
> 3.3 Yes — but not if the whole or part of the undertaking has been designated a maintenance service.
> 3.4 Yes — if the employer fails to comply with the request within 48 hours of service of the referral notice on him, the workers may strike. The strike will be a protected strike although it does not comply with general procedures.
> 3.5 Yes — the Labour Court may award just and equitable compensation for any loss attributed to the strike.

## Q4 — Collective bargaining defined (4)

> [!question] Question
> Explain collective bargaining and the purpose thereof.

> [!success]- Answer
> A voluntary process by means of which employees in an organised relationship negotiate with their employers, or employers in an organised relationship, with regard to employment conditions or disputes arising therefrom, with the object of reaching an agreement about these matters. Purpose: the regulation of terms and conditions of employment and to resolve disputes.
