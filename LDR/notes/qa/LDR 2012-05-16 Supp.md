## Q1 — Full s189 retrenchment consultation process (15)

> [!question] Question
> AIM Insurance (Pty) Ltd's HR director advises that due to the worldwide recession over the past 18 months, the company must downsize — policies lapsed at a high rate and income declined dramatically. 15 of 45 employees, spread over various departments, will likely need to be dismissed for operational reasons. Some loyal employees have 10+ years' service. AIMO is the registered trade union at the workplace with 10 employee members. The company has a financial advisory department and an in-house psychologist who could assist.
> 1.1 Draft the first notice/letter required by the LRA to inform employees of the contemplated dismissals (8). 1.2 What issues must be consulted on during the first meeting, in terms of the law (4)? 1.3 The employer fails to agree with employees on selection criteria — what selection criteria would you suggest, and how should the client implement them (3)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 Letter headed "Consultation in terms of Section 189 of the Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995 regarding the possible retrenchment of staff based on operational requirements," addressed to All Staff Members **and** to AIMO (the trade union) — explains the reason (recession, declining income); states that **no final decision has been made** and all options/alternatives, including employee input, will be considered; notes the letter is addressed to AIMO as the union representing 10 members; invites constructive engagement; gives notice of a meeting under s189(3) to discuss: the reasons for possible retrenchment; alternatives considered and reasons for rejecting them; the number of employees likely affected (15 of 45); fair and objective selection criteria (skills, experience, years of service, LIFO); the timing of dismissals; how retrenchment could be avoided; how the number of affected employees/adverse effects can be minimised and mitigated; voluntary retrenchment (by agreement only); payment during the notice period; severance pay (minimum one week's wage per completed year of service); assistance to affected employees (UIF forms, service certificates/references, free financial advice, free counselling); and the possibility of future re-employment. Notes no operational-requirements retrenchments in the preceding 12 months, and current headcount of 45.
> 1.2 Section 189(2) issues (any four): appropriate measures to avoid dismissals; appropriate measures to minimise dismissals (eliminating overtime, moratorium on new appointments, voluntary retrenchment, early retirement, short time, temporary lay-off); changing the timing of the dismissals; mitigating the adverse effects of dismissal (e.g. agreements on re-employment of retrenched workers); the method for selecting employees to be dismissed; severance pay (regulated by s41 BCEA — minimum one week's pay per completed year of service; forfeited if an employee unreasonably refuses alternative employment).
> 1.3 Section 189(7): the employer must select employees according to criteria agreed by the consulting parties, or, if none agreed, criteria that are fair and objective. Here no agreement was reached, so following a fair and sufficient consultation process the employer may decide on the selection criteria itself — e.g. skills, experience, years of service, LIFO (last in, first out).

## Q2 — Employee vs independent contractor (4)

> [!question] Question
> 2.1 Your attorney firm acquires a personal assistant from a Temporary Employment Service to assist a senior attorney for 4 months while her permanent PA is on maternity leave. Is the new PA an employee of your firm? Motivate (2). 2.2 Colin trades as "IT Safe & Secure" and is contracted by Astro Brokers CC to upgrade its IT systems over 6 months, billing for his time. Is Colin an employee of Astro Brokers CC? Motivate (2).

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 No. The employee is employed by the Temporary Employment Service — section 198 refers.
> 2.2 No. Colin has his own business and is an independent contractor. No control is exercised by Astro Brokers CC — the dominant impression test points to independent contractor status. He does not earn a salary; he bills/invoices Astro Brokers CC.

## Q3 — Poor work performance dismissal, on probation (6)

> [!question] Question
> Mike is employed by X Computer Company and is still within his probation period. Despite proper training, he simply cannot perform as agreed contractually (per his job description). The company doesn't know what to do, as the workplace situation is unbearable.
> 3.1 Is X Computer Company entitled to terminate Mike's employment in these circumstances — could this be a fair reason? Explain briefly (1). 3.2 Assume it is entitled to terminate — describe the procedure the company must follow (5).

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 Yes — incapacity due to poor work performance can be a valid and substantive reason to dismiss, whether within or outside the probation period.
> 3.2 The Code of Good Practice: Dismissal (Schedule 8, Items 4, 8 and 9) summarises the essential elements: after probation an employee should not be dismissed for unsatisfactory performance unless the employer has given appropriate evaluation, instruction, training, guidance or counselling, and after a reasonable period for improvement the employee continues to perform unsatisfactorily. The procedure should include an investigation into the reasons for unsatisfactory performance, and the employer should consider ways short of dismissal to remedy the matter. The employee has the right to be heard and to be assisted by a trade union representative or fellow employee. The employee must be informed that performance doesn't meet the required standard and told the consequences of non-improvement, and be given an opportunity to improve before dismissal is considered. It is the employer's responsibility to supply proper support, guidance, supervision and training. A hearing is required before dismissal for poor work performance, at which the employee may state a case and prove mitigating circumstances. (Item 9 applies more directly post-probation; Item 8 provides for a less formal and strict process during probation.)
