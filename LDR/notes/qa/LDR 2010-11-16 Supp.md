## Q1 — Right to join a trade union (5)

> [!question] Question
> Your client, Themba Matiso, has been employed by ABC Electrical since June 1988. He wants to join FUW union, but his employment contract stipulates that he does not have the right to join any union.
> 1.1 Is this provision in his contract valid (2)? 1.2 Briefly explain all steps your client may take to resolve a dispute over this matter with his employer (3).

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 The provision in the contract is invalid. Every employee has a right to join a trade union.
> 1.2 Refer the dispute in writing to the Bargaining Council or CCMA for conciliation. If not successful, refer to the Labour Court for adjudication.

## Q2 — Automatically unfair dismissal: pregnancy (7)

> [!question] Question
> M Momberg, your client, was employed on probation with a view to entering into articles of clerkship with a partner of Louw Attorneys. The firm terminated her services without any hearing on the ground that she was deceitful in not disclosing her pregnancy, and thus tried to lure her intended principal into registering her articles. She did not know at the time that she was pregnant.
> 2.1 Is her dismissal fair? Motivate (4). 2.2 Is it possible to reinstate the employee (1)? 2.3 What amount of compensation could be awarded if the court finds she was in fact unfairly dismissed (2)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 Yes [dismissal is unfair] — not substantively or procedurally fair. The dismissal will be automatically unfair because she was dismissed on the grounds of pregnancy.
> 2.2 Yes — one of the remedies available to the employee.
> 2.3 Compensation must be just and equitable, but it may not exceed the equivalent of 24 months' remuneration, calculated at the employee's rate of remuneration on the date of dismissal.

Related: [[Automatically Unfair Dismissal on Pregnancy]]

## Q3 — Protected vs unprotected strike (9)

> [!question] Question
> The employees of Spar Supermarket refuse to continue to work because the employer will not grant their demand of 15% salary increase. This refusal to work takes place while salary negotiations between the employer and the trade union are still in progress. No prior notice of the strike was given to the employer. The refusal to work is resulting in thousands of rands damage to Spar Supermarket every day.
> 3.1 Is the strike a protected strike? Motivate (3). 3.2 May the employer dismiss the employees on strike (1)? 3.3 May the employer employ replacement labour (1)? 3.4 Is it possible for the employees to strike if the employer had unilaterally amended conditions of employment and refuses to restore the status quo prior to negotiations (3)? 3.5 May damages be awarded to the employer (1)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 No — does not comply with the requirements for a protected strike: refer the dispute to the CCMA — if no resolution, or 30 days expire without resolution, then 48 hours' notice to the employer of intention to strike is required.
> 3.2 Yes — Sec 67.
> 3.3 Yes — but not if the whole or part of the undertaking has been designated a maintenance service.
> 3.4 Yes — if the employer fails to comply with the request within 48 hours of service of the referral notice on him, the workers may strike. The strike will be a protected strike although it does not comply with general procedures.
> 3.5 Yes — the Labour Court may award just and equitable compensation for any loss attributed to the strike.

Related: [[Protected and Unprotected Strikes]]

## Q4 — Discrimination: sexual orientation and a medical scheme (4)

> [!question] Question
> John, a homosexual employee of Brookes Hill company, wishes to register his friend with whom he was living for six years as a dependant member of his medical scheme. The rules of the fund do not make provision for such an inclusion and the employer refuses to change the rules to make it possible.
> 4.1 To which body must he refer the dispute (1)? 4.2 Is it possible for his request to be successful? Motivate your answer (3).

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 CCMA.
> 4.2 Yes — unfair discrimination on the ground of sexual orientation.
