## Q1 — Drafting a pre-dismissal ultimatum letter for an unprotected strike (8)

> [!question] Question
> Your client's business faces an unprotected strike (does not comply with the LRA) — the majority union has been informed in writing, employees have been striking since 2 May 2012, and are intimidating non-striking employees with violence and damaging property. The union and employees demand a 26% salary increase; the employer has acted in good faith, called meetings to conciliate, and offered 14% (roughly double inflation), but the union has failed to respond or attend meetings. The client is suffering irreparable financial loss (cancelled orders). Advise on approaching the Labour Court for an interdict, and on substantive and procedural fairness under Schedule 8. Client instructs you to draft a letter to the employees and union about the untenable situation, requiring them to report for work, failing which dismissal may follow.

> [!success]- Answer
> **Notice to all employees (pre-dismissal) involved in unprotected strike:**
> Since 2 May 2012 you have been participating in an unprotected strike. You have also been intimidating fellow employees who are not participating by threatening them with violence, and there is evidence of damage caused to company property. Your actions are unprotected as they do not comply with the provisions of the Labour Relations Act — legal procedures are available to you to resolve your dispute. You are also in breach of your employment contract; you will not be paid while you do not work.
> The company has throughout acted in good faith, called meetings to conciliate, and offered a very high increase of 14% (double the current inflation rate); the union has failed to respond to these offers or attend the meetings. The company is suffering irreparable financial loss — customers have cancelled orders and approached other companies. The union has been informed of your actions.
> You must cease your participation in this unprotected strike immediately. If you fail to do so, you may be dismissed. An interdict may also be obtained from the Labour Court. The company reserves the right to claim from you all damages it has and may further suffer as a result of your actions, including legal costs.
> Should you believe there are special circumstances applying to you, advise your union official, shop steward, or the company's management by [date] 2012 so the company may take them into account.
> Signed for the company, with a line for the employee to acknowledge receipt.

## Q2 — Drafting a notice of disciplinary enquiry (8)

> [!question] Question
> XYZ Company instructs you to draft a notice of a disciplinary enquiry. Employee Dan Smit is to be charged with unauthorised possession of company property — on 3 May 2012 he had, without permission, one box of chocolates (36 units of 250g, valued R180 total) in his possession passing through the security area to his vehicle — conduct that could lead to summary dismissal under the client's code of conduct. Advise on the substantive and procedural requirements, the audi alteram partem rule, and the onus of proof. The hearing will be chaired by Susan Owen, HR Manager. Draft the notice.

> [!success]- Answer
> **Notification of a hearing:** notifies the employee of the disciplinary hearing to consider the charge — unauthorised possession of company property in that on 3 May 2012 he had, without permission, one box of chocolates (36 units of 250g, valued R180 total) in his possession while proceeding through the security area on his way to leave the premises.
> States the date, time and place of the hearing, chaired by Susan Owen, HR Manager. Sets out the employee's rights: to be represented by a fellow employee/union representative; to call witnesses; to question management's witnesses; to an interpreter (on advance request). States the employee has the right to be present, and that the hearing will proceed in his absence if he fails to attend without good cause. Signed for the company, with a line for the employee to acknowledge receipt.

## Q3 — Legal representation at an internal hearing (3)

> [!question] Question
> Your client works for a private company and is not a member of any trade union. A few days ago she was charged with poor work performance. The hearing is in 4 days. Are you, as an admitted attorney, entitled to represent an employee at a disciplinary hearing? Explain in detail and motivate.

> [!success]- Answer
> No. In law one does not have a right to be represented by a legal practitioner in these internal proceedings. Item 4 of Schedule 8 provides for representation or assistance by a fellow employee or trade union representative only. However, the employment contract, employer policies, and/or precedent as to how this aspect was dealt with previously by the employer may allow for it, in which case one may enforce this right. Notwithstanding the above, one can always file an application to the chairperson to be represented and assisted by a legal practitioner.

## Q4 — Rescission after relocation, wrong address used (6)

> [!question] Question
> ABC Electronics CC, represented by Sonja Wilson, has operated from 6 Church Street, Bellville since 1 May 2012 (prior to that, from 40 Keerom Street, Cape Town). It uses a postal address for correspondence; no physical post box exists at either address. It dismissed an employee about six months ago following a disciplinary hearing for stealing stock worth R200 000 over 7 months. Yesterday the employee arrived at the new address and handed Ms Wilson an Arbitration Award ordering reinstatement (effective 6 months ago) with full benefits, six months' remuneration, and R30 000 compensation. The client never received any document or notice from the CCMA or the employee. The dismissal followed a fair hearing (ample opportunity for evidence, two postponements at the employee's request, legal representation allowed) with a fair reason and fair process; the chairperson recommended dismissal after considering mitigating circumstances.
> 4.1 Advise on possible steps under the LRA and CCMA Rules; list only the document(s) to be drafted and delivered within the prescribed period (2). 4.2 You discover all documents were sent by registered mail to the client's old address, 6 Church Street [sic — old address was Keerom Street; documents sent to the then-current/old address per facts]. Draft the clauses dealing with reasons for non-appearance and prospects of success (4).

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 Apply for Rescission of the Arbitration Award in terms of s144 of the LRA and Rules 31 and 32 of the CCMA Rules. Document: Application/Notice of Motion and supporting affidavit(s).
> 4.2 **Reasons for non-appearance:** business had moved; uses postal address only; no post box outside; for 6 months heard nothing and received no document or notice whatsoever; would have opposed and attended the CCMA; phoned the CCMA and established that registered mail was sent to the incorrect address.
> **Prospects of success:** had a proper hearing; fair procedure followed; outside/objective chairperson; serious offence of theft of R200 000; proved on a balance of probabilities.
