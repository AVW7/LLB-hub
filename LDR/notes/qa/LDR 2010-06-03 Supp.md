## Q1 — Non-appearance at arbitration, rescission (12)

> [!question] Question
> Mr Riaan Burger, a plumber in Bloemfontein trading as Burger Plumbers, uses a postal address for correspondence — no physical post box exists at the business premises. He dismissed employee Tau Mthembu three months ago after a disciplinary hearing for assaulting a client with a set-spanner. Mr Mtembu arrived today and handed Mr Burger an arbitration award ordering payment of R24 000 as compensation for unfair dismissal. Your client received no notice whatsoever from the CCMA.
> 1.1 Which documents was your client supposed to receive from the CCMA (3)? 1.2 Advise your client on possible steps that can be taken (1). 1.3 You contact the CCMA and discover all notices were sent by registered mail to 100 Kelner Street. Draft the clauses dealing with the reason for non-appearance and the prospect of success (8).

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 Notice of referral to CCMA (LRA Form 7.11) and notice of set down of conciliation; notice of referral to CCMA for arbitration (Form 7.13) and notice of set down for arbitration; the arbitration award.
> 1.2 Bring an application for rescission of the arbitration award at the CCMA.
> 1.3 "I submit that I was not in wilful default of the CCMA because I was never the employer of the respondent and did not dismiss the respondent. I did not receive a notice that the matter was referred to the CCMA for conciliation. I did not get a notice of set down from the CCMA for conciliation. I did not receive notice that the matter was referred for arbitration and I did not get a notice of set down for arbitration from the CCMA. I did not receive the arbitration award from the CCMA; it only came to my attention when Mr Mtembu showed up at the business today and handed the award to me. According to the documents I received from Mr Mtembu, all documents from the CCMA were sent to 100 Kelner Street, Bloemfontein, 9300. This is not my postal address. No physical post box exists at the business premises and I only use my postal address: PO Box 31270, Fichardtpark, Bloemfontein, 9317.
> I believe that the Commissioner should rescind the award because I have a good prospect of succeeding in my claim against the respondent, because we had a proper disciplinary hearing and different eye witnesses testified against the respondent. He was found guilty on the charge of misconduct for assaulting a client and was dismissed for serious misconduct."

## Q2 — Retrenchment: severance pay (3)

> [!question] Question
> ABC Manufacturing (Pty) Limited's HR director advises that due to a downturn, the company must downsize, and 15 employees will likely need to be dismissed for operational reasons. It appears probable the company will secure reasonable alternative employment for the majority of affected employees with another company (Company B). Assume s189A does not apply.
> Advise on whether the employees, including those for whom the company secures reasonable alternative employment, will be entitled to severance pay, and how it should be calculated to comply with minimum legal requirements.

> [!success]- Answer
> Employees who do not get reasonable alternative employment with Company B will receive severance pay. Employees offered reasonable alternative employment will not receive severance pay if they unreasonably refuse it. Minimum severance pay = one week's remuneration for every completed year of service.

## Q3 — Incapacity dismissal for ill health (8)

> [!question] Question
> Mike is employed by X Computer Company. He is chronically absent from work and his absence is affecting the flow of work at the company. After each absence he hands in a medical certificate to the employer.
> 3.1 Is X Company entitled to terminate Mike's employment? Explain (2). 3.2 Assume X Company is entitled to terminate — describe in detail the procedure the company must follow (5). 3.3 Explain the source where the above procedure is found (1).

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 Incapacity on ground of ill-health is a valid reason for dismissal, subject to a fair procedure/investigation being followed.
> 3.2 The employer must hold an investigation into whether the illness is temporary or permanent. If temporary, investigate the extent of the incapacity. If the employee is likely to be absent unreasonably long, investigate all alternatives short of dismissal, considering the nature of the job, period of absence, seriousness of the injury, and possibility of a temporary replacement. In cases of permanent incapacity, consider another post or adapting duties/circumstances to accommodate the disability. The employee should be assisted by a union representative or fellow employee and permitted to state a case in response. The degree and/or cause of incapacity is relevant; for alcoholism or drug abuse, counselling and rehabilitation may be appropriate. The duty to accommodate an employee injured at work is more onerous.
> 3.3 Schedule 8, Code of Good Practice: Dismissal, Labour Relations Act, 66 of 1995.

## Q4 — CCMA award, no notice of arbitration date (2)

> [!question] Question
> Company Y receives an arbitration award from the CCMA that it must pay Thomas, an ex-employee, R120 000. The company is surprised, as it was not notified of the date of the arbitration. Advise the company.

> [!success]- Answer
> The company may apply for rescission of the award in terms of section 144 of the Labour Relations Act.
