# Employee vs Independent Contractor Status

Threshold question before any LRA remedy is available — no dispute can be referred under the Act unless the claimant is an "employee".

## s200A presumption of employment

A person is **presumed to be an employee**, until the contrary is proved, if any **one or more** of the following factors is present (and the person earns **below the earnings threshold** determined by the Minister of Labour under s200A(2) — at the time of these papers, just over R149 000/year):

(a) the manner in which the person works is subject to the control or direction of another person;
(b) the person's hours of work are subject to the control or direction of another person;
(c) in the case of a person who works for an organisation, the person is a part of that organisation;
(d) the person has worked for that other person for an average of at least **40 hours per month** over the last three months;
(e) the person is **economically dependent** on the other person for whom they work or render services;
(f) the person is provided with **tools of trade or work equipment** by the other person;
(g) the person only works for or renders services to **one person**.

## Common-law tests (used to argue either way)

- **Control test** — degree of control the employer exercises over how work is done.
- **Dominant impression test** — weighs all indicators together to form an overall impression of the relationship.
- **Organisation/integration test** — whether the person's work is integrated into the organisation's structure.

## Independent contractor indicators

- Runs their own business and **bills/invoices** for time or project work rather than earning a salary;
- Uses their **own tools and equipment**;
- Not subject to the client's control over how the work gets done;
- No income tax/UIF deducted at source.

## Temporary Employment Services (labour brokers)

- **s198 LRA**: an employee placed by a Temporary Employment Service (TES) with a client is deemed to be the **employee of the TES**, not the client, for most purposes.
