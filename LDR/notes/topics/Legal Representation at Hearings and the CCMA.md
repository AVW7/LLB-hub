# Legal Representation at Hearings and the CCMA

Tested three times, always contrasting the **internal disciplinary hearing** with **CCMA arbitration**.

## Internal disciplinary hearing (within the business)

- **No legal right** to be represented by a legal practitioner.
- **Item 4 of Schedule 8** (Code of Good Practice: Dismissal) only provides for representation/assistance by a **fellow employee or trade union representative**.
- **However**: the employment contract, the employer's policies, or precedent (how the employer has dealt with this before) may create such a right, in which case it can be enforced.
- Regardless, the employee can always **apply to the chairperson** (not the employer, who is a party) to be represented/assisted by a legal practitioner — the chairperson decides at the start of the hearing.

## CCMA arbitration

- Governed by **Rule 25** of the Rules for the Conduct of Proceedings before the CCMA.
- **General rule**: a legal practitioner **may** appear on behalf of a party at arbitration.
- **Exception**: where the dispute concerns the **fairness of a dismissal** and the reason for dismissal relates to the employee's **conduct or capacity** (misconduct or incapacity — including poor work performance) — then there is **no automatic right** to legal representation.
- Within that exception, representation is still possible if:
  - the **commissioner and all parties agree**; or
  - the party **applies** to be represented, addressing the factors in **Rule 25(1)(c)(2)** (e.g. nature of the questions of law raised, complexity of the dispute, public interest, and the comparative ability of the parties to represent themselves).

## Practical advice to a client refused representation

- Client is not automatically entitled to legal representation where dismissal was for misconduct/capacity.
- Bring a formal application addressing the Rule 25 factors; alternatively, prepare and coach the client thoroughly to present their own case if the application fails.
