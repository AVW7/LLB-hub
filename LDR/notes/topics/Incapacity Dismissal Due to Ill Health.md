# Incapacity Dismissal Due to Ill Health

Appears almost word-for-word in three papers — the "Mike, chronically absent, hands in medical certificates" fact pattern.

## Is dismissal permitted?

**Yes** — incapacity on the ground of ill health is a valid (substantively fair) reason for dismissal. **However**, this is always subject to a **fair procedure/investigation** being followed.

## The required procedure

1. The employer must hold an **investigation**.
2. The investigation must establish whether the illness is **temporary or permanent**.
3. If temporary, investigate the **extent of the incapacity**.
4. If the employee is likely to be absent unreasonably long, the employer must investigate **all alternatives short of dismissal**.
5. Relevant factors when weighing alternatives: nature of the job, period of absence, seriousness of the injury/illness, possibility of securing a temporary replacement.
6. In cases of **permanent** incapacity, the employer could consider: another post for the employee, or adapting the employee's duties/circumstances to accommodate the disability.
7. During the investigation the employee should be **assisted by a trade union representative or fellow employee**.
8. The employee must be **permitted to state a case in response**.
9. The **degree and/or cause** of the incapacity is relevant.
10. For certain kinds of incapacity (e.g. **alcoholism or drug abuse**), counselling and rehabilitation may be the appropriate steps rather than dismissal.
11. The duty on an employer to **accommodate an employee injured at work** is more onerous than the general duty.

*(Memo note: "any five answers will do" for the 5-mark procedure sub-question — this is a checklist, not all eleven points are needed to score full marks.)*

## Source

**Schedule 8, Code of Good Practice: Dismissal**, Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995.
