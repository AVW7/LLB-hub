# Rescission of CCMA Arbitration Awards

The single most heavily tested LDR topic — a near-identical fact pattern (small-business owner never receives CCMA notices, judgment/award handed to him by the ex-employee) recurs in four of the eight past papers.

## The fact pattern

A sole proprietor/small business operates from a street address but uses a postal address for correspondence, with **no physical post box** at the business premises. An employee dismissed months earlier arrives unannounced and hands the employer an **arbitration award** — ordering reinstatement/back pay/compensation. The employer says he never received any notice from the CCMA at any stage.

## What the employer should have received

1. Notice of referral to the CCMA for **conciliation** (LRA Form 7.11) + notice of set down for conciliation;
2. Notice of referral to the CCMA for **arbitration** (LRA Form 7.13) + notice of set down for arbitration;
3. The **arbitration award** itself.

## The remedy

- Apply for **rescission of the arbitration award** in terms of **s144 of the LRA**, read with **Rule 31 and Rule 32** of the Rules for the Conduct of Proceedings before the CCMA.
- Vehicle: an **Application/Notice of Motion supported by an affidavit**.

## Drafting the affidavit — two limbs

**1. Reasons for non-appearance / not in wilful default**
- Never received any document or notice from the CCMA at any stage;
- Business uses a postal address only — no physical post box exists at the premises;
- All CCMA notices were in fact sent by registered mail to the **street address**, not the postal address used for correspondence;
- Would have opposed and attended had proper notice been received;
- Phoned the CCMA and confirmed the misdirected registered mail; phoned the Post Office and confirmed no collection was ever made.

**2. Prospects of success**
- A proper disciplinary hearing was held with a fair procedure and an objective/outside chairperson;
- The offence was serious (e.g. theft, assault of a client) and proved on a balance of probabilities;
- The chairperson recommended dismissal and the employer acted on that recommendation.
- Note: the **CCMA may not award general damages** — a ground to attack the quantum of an award as well.

## Related but distinct scenario

Where the employer *did* receive proper notice at the correct address but simply moved premises without updating the CCMA/employee, the same s144 rescission application applies but the "reasons for non-appearance" limb changes accordingly (see [[LDR 2012-05-05]] Q4 — registered mail sent to the *old* address after the business relocated).
