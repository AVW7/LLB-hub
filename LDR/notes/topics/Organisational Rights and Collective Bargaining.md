# Organisational Rights and Collective Bargaining

Covers the right to join a union, a union's organisational rights at the workplace, and the definition of collective bargaining itself.

## Right to join a trade union

- Every employee has a **right to join a trade union of choice** (s4 LRA).
- A clause in an employment contract purporting to **deny** this right is **invalid**.
- Dispute route: refer the dispute **in writing** to the relevant **bargaining council** (or the **CCMA** if none) for **conciliation**; if unresolved, refer to the **Labour Court** for **adjudication**.

## Organisational rights (s21/s22 LRA)

Facts pattern: a registered trade union serves written notice on an employer seeking to exercise organisational rights (e.g. access to the workplace, stop-order facilities, leave for union activities) and/or recognition, claiming to be "sufficiently representative" though not representing a majority. The employer refuses to engage.

- **s21 route**: the union gives written notice of the rights it seeks to exercise; the employer must meet to try to conclude a **collective agreement**; if no agreement is reached, either party may refer the dispute to the **CCMA for conciliation**; if still unresolved, the union may **refer the matter for arbitration** (or, depending on the right in dispute, resort to protected strike action) — see Flow Diagram 2, Schedule 4 of the LRA.
- **s22 alternative framing**: refer dispute to CCMA for conciliation; if unresolved, may request **arbitration** at the CCMA.
- A trade union does **not** need to represent an absolute majority to seek to exercise organisational rights — "sufficiently representative" is the relevant threshold for certain rights.

## Collective bargaining — definition

> A voluntary process by means of which employees in an organised relationship negotiate with their employers, or employers in an organised relationship, with regard to employment conditions or disputes arising therefrom, with the object of reaching agreement about these matters.

Purpose: to **regulate the terms and conditions of employment** and to **resolve disputes** between the bargaining parties.
