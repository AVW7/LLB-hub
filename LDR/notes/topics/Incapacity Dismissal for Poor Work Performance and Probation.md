# Incapacity Dismissal for Poor Work Performance and Probation

Distinct from ill-health incapacity — this is the "cannot meet the required standard despite training" scenario, tested both **within** and **after** probation.

## Is dismissal permitted?

**Yes.** Incapacity due to poor work performance can be a valid, substantively fair reason to dismiss — whether within or outside the probation period.

## The procedure — Code of Good Practice: Dismissal, Items 8 and 9

1. If the employer determines performance is below standard, the employer must **advise the employee** of the respects in which performance fails to meet the required standard (or in which the employee is considered incompetent).
2. The employee must be given **appropriate evaluation, instruction, training, guidance or counselling**.
3. After a **reasonable period of time** to improve, if performance remains unsatisfactory, the employer may proceed.
4. The employer must **investigate** to establish the reasons for the unsatisfactory performance and consider other ways, **short of dismissal**, to remedy the matter.
5. The employee is entitled to be **heard** — to state a case and prove mitigating circumstances — before a decision to dismiss is taken, and to be **assisted by a trade union representative or fellow employee** (who may also make representations on the employee's behalf).
6. The employer may only decide to dismiss **after** inviting and considering the employee's representations.
7. If dismissing, the employer must advise the employee of the **right to refer a dispute** to a bargaining council or the CCMA.
8. **Notice**: at least **one week's written notice** must be given (where the contract is silent on notice).

## Probation is treated less strictly

- **Item 8** (probation) allows a **less formal and stricter process is not required** than Item 9 (post-probation) — a decision-maker "ought to accept reasons that may be less compelling than would be the case in dismissals effected after completion of probation."
- The purpose of probation is to give the employer the opportunity to evaluate performance before confirming employment — so the threshold to justify dismissal during probation is lower, but a fair procedure (evaluation, guidance, a chance to improve, a hearing) is still required.
