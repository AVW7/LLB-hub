# Disciplinary Hearing Procedure

General procedural fairness for a **misconduct** dismissal — the drafting-heavy counterpart to the incapacity topics.

## Drafting a notice of disciplinary enquiry

A proper notice to the employee must contain:
1. A clear **heading/caption** identifying it as a notification of a hearing;
2. The **charge** — stated with enough factual detail to allow the employee to prepare (date, what happened, why it's a breach of the code of conduct);
3. The **date, time and place** of the hearing;
4. Who will **chair** the hearing (should be someone reasonably independent of the matter where possible);
5. A statement of the employee's rights at the hearing:
   - to be **represented** by a fellow employee or trade union representative;
   - to **call witnesses**;
   - to **question (cross-examine)** management's witnesses;
   - to an **interpreter**, on request in advance;
6. A statement that the employee has the **right to be present**, and that the hearing will proceed **in absentia** if the employee fails to attend without good cause;
7. A signature line for the employee to **acknowledge receipt** of the notice.

## Underlying principles

- **Audi alteram partem** — the employee must be given a fair opportunity to state their case before a decision is taken.
- The **onus** rests on the employer to prove the misconduct occurred and that dismissal is an appropriate sanction.
- These requirements track **Item 4, Schedule 8** (Code of Good Practice: Dismissal) — the same source underlying representation rights at internal hearings (see [[Legal Representation at Hearings and the CCMA]]).
