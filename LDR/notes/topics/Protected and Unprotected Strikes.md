# Protected and Unprotected Strikes

The "Spar Supermarket" 15%-increase fact pattern is near-verbatim across two papers; a third paper tests the employer-side response (ultimatum letter) to an unprotected strike.

## Requirements for a protected strike

A strike is **not protected** unless:
1. The dispute has been **referred to the CCMA (or bargaining council) for conciliation**; and
2. Either a **certificate of non-resolution** is issued, or **30 days** have lapsed since the referral without resolution; and
3. At least **48 hours' notice** of intention to strike has been given to the employer (in writing).

If salary negotiations are still in progress and no prior notice was given, and the dispute was never referred for conciliation, the strike is **unprotected**.

## Consequences of an unprotected strike

- The employer **may dismiss** employees participating (Sec 67 referenced in the source memo — because the strike does not enjoy s67 protection).
- The employer **may employ replacement labour** — **unless** the whole or part of the undertaking has been designated a **maintenance service**.
- The **Labour Court may award just and equitable compensation** for any loss attributable to the (unprotected) strike.

## The unilateral-change exception

Even without following the general s64 procedure, a strike can still be **protected** where the employer has **unilaterally changed terms and conditions of employment** and, after being served with a written referral notice requiring restoration of the status quo, **fails to comply within 48 hours**. In that scenario the employees may strike immediately and the strike remains protected despite not following the general notice procedure.

## Employer's response: the pre-dismissal ultimatum letter

Where employees are on an unprotected strike (intimidating non-strikers, damaging property), before dismissing, the employer should issue a written ultimatum covering:
- The strike is **unprotected** (does not comply with the LRA) and employees are in **breach of contract** — no pay for time not worked;
- Any misconduct during the strike (intimidation, property damage) is noted;
- The employer's good-faith conduct in negotiations (meetings called, reasonable offer made) and the union's failure to respond/attend;
- The financial harm being suffered;
- A clear demand to **return to work by a stated deadline**, failing which **dismissal** may follow, an **interdict** may be sought from the **Labour Court**, and **damages** (including legal costs) may be claimed;
- An invitation to raise **special circumstances** by a stated date;
- A line for the employee to **sign acknowledgement of receipt**.
