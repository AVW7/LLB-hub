## Frequently referenced provisions

> [!quote] s34 — Access to courts
> "Everyone has the right to have any dispute that can be resolved by the application of law decided in a fair public hearing before a court or, where appropriate, another independent and impartial tribunal or forum."

Relevant across the civil-procedure modules (CLP, HCP, MCP) whenever a topic note discusses a litigant's right to have a matter adjudicated, and in CCP when discussing the right to a fair hearing.

> [!quote] s35(1) — Rights on arrest
> Everyone arrested has the right to remain silent; to be informed promptly of that right and the consequences of not remaining silent; not to be compelled to make a confession or admission; to be brought before a court within 48 hours (or the end of the next court day if the 48 hours expire outside ordinary court hours/days); to be charged or informed of the reason for continued detention, or released, at first appearance; and to be released if the interests of justice permit.

> [!quote] s35(2) — Rights while detained
> Includes the right to be informed promptly of the reason for detention, to consult a legal practitioner (and to a state-funded one if substantial injustice would otherwise result), to challenge the lawfulness of detention before a court, to humane conditions of detention, and to communicate with a spouse/partner, next of kin, religious counsellor and medical practitioner.

> [!quote] s35(3) — Right to a fair trial
> Every accused person's fair trial right includes: to be informed of the charge with sufficient detail; adequate time and facilities to prepare a defence; a public trial before an ordinary court; trial without unreasonable delay; to be present when tried; to choose (or be assigned) legal representation; to be presumed innocent and remain silent; to adduce and challenge evidence; not to be compelled to give self-incriminating evidence; to an interpreter if needed; protection against retrospective offences and double jeopardy; the benefit of the least severe prescribed punishment; and the right of appeal or review.

> [!quote] s35(5) — Exclusion of unconstitutionally obtained evidence
> "Evidence obtained in a manner that violates any right in the Bill of Rights must be excluded if the admission of that evidence would render the trial unfair or otherwise be detrimental to the administration of justice."

s35 as a whole is directly relevant to CCP's rights-of-arrested-and-accused-persons topic, the police-interrogation and confession-admissibility material, and the docket-access/disclosure topics — s35(3)(b) (adequate time and facilities to prepare a defence) underpins the accused's right to access the police docket.

> [!quote] s38 — Enforcement of rights
> Anyone alleging that a right in the Bill of Rights has been infringed or threatened may approach a competent court, including: (a) anyone acting in their own interest; (b) anyone acting on behalf of another who cannot act in their own name; (c) anyone acting as a member of, or in the interest of, a group or class of persons; (d) anyone acting in the public interest; and (e) an association acting in the interest of its members.

Relevant to standing/*locus standi* discussions in the civil procedure modules (CLP/HCP/MCP) — s38 is the constitutional broadening of standing beyond the traditional direct-and-substantial-interest test.

**Chapter 8 (Courts and Administration of Justice, ss165–180)** vests judicial authority in the courts, protects judicial independence, and establishes the court hierarchy (Constitutional Court, Supreme Court of Appeal, High Courts, Magistrates' Courts and other courts). Relevant background whenever a HCP or MCP topic note discusses court structure or jurisdiction.
