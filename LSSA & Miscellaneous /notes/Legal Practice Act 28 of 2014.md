# Legal Practice Act 28 of 2014

Full text: [[Legal Practice Act 28 of 2014.pdf/markdown.md|Legal Practice Act 28 of 2014 — full text]]

The Act that unified the attorneys' and advocates' professions under a single South African Legal Practice Council. Use the chapter/section table below to jump to the right part of the source document, and "Frequently referenced provisions" for quick-check quotes on the topics most likely to come up in exam prep (fees, trust accounts, misconduct, the Fidelity Fund).

## Frequently referenced provisions

> [!quote] s35 — Fees in respect of legal services
> "(1) ... fees in respect of litigious and non-litigious legal services rendered by legal practitioners ... must be in accordance with the tariffs made by the Rules Board for Courts of Law ... (2) The Rules Board ... must ... take into account — (a) the importance, significance, complexity and expertise of the legal services required; (b) the seniority and experience of the legal practitioner concerned ...; (c) the volume of work required and time spent ...; and (d) the financial implications of the matter at hand. (3) ... nothing in this section precludes any user ... from agreeing with a legal practitioner in writing, to pay fees ... in excess of or below any tariffs ..."

Relevant to any topic note on costs, fee agreements, or contingency-fee arrangements (e.g. FBE, PCT, or CLP costs topics).

> [!quote] s38 — Procedure for dealing with complaints of misconduct
> "(1) The Council must make rules to determine the procedure to be followed by disciplinary bodies established in terms of section 37 for dealing with all complaints of misconduct relating to legal practitioners, whether practising as an advocate, an attorney, a candidate legal practitioner or a juristic entity, and such complaints must be lodged in writing with the Council."

Relevant to PLE/professional-conduct topic notes on the disciplinary process, and to the Legal Practice Act Rules Part X (Disciplinary) which sets out the detailed procedure under this section.

> [!quote] s57(1) — Purpose and application of the Fidelity Fund
> Lists the purposes the Fund must be used for, including: "(a) Meeting the liability of the Fund referred to in section 55; (b) paying expenses incurred in operating the Board and the Fund ...; (c) paying expenses incurred by the Board in investigating and establishing the validity of claims ...; (d) paying all expenses and legal costs incurred by the Board for the purpose of recovering money from the persons whose wrongful conduct gave rise to the claim; (e) refunding the costs ... incurred by a claimant in establishing a claim ...; (f) paying legal expenses incurred in defending a claim made against the Fund ..."

The Fidelity Fund reimburses clients who lose money through a legal practitioner's theft or dishonesty in handling trust funds — relevant to any PLE/professional-conduct topic on client protection.

> [!quote] s84 — Obligations relating to trust monies
> "(1) Every attorney or any advocate referred to in section 34(2)(b) ... who practises ... (a) for his or her own account either alone or in partnership; or (b) as a director of a practice which is a juristic entity, must be in possession of a Fidelity Fund certificate. (2) No legal practitioner referred to in subsection (1) or person employed or supervised by that legal practitioner may receive or hold funds or property belonging to any person unless the legal practitioner concerned is in possession of a Fidelity Fund certificate."

> [!quote] s86(1)–(2) — Trust accounts
> "(1) Every legal practitioner referred to in section 84(1) must operate a trust account. (2) Every trust account practice must keep a trust account at a bank with which the Fund has made an arrangement ... and must deposit therein, as soon as possible after receipt thereof, money held by such practice on behalf of any person."

s84 and s86 are the core trust-account obligations tested in PLE and PCT topics on handling client money; see also the Legal Practice Act Rules Part XII (Accounting Rules) for the detailed mechanics of trust withdrawals, interest, and reporting.

> [!quote] s93(1) — Offences and penalties (illustrative)
> "Any person who, in a practice, without the written consent of the Council, employs in any capacity any person who has been struck off the Roll or suspended from practice, while that person remains struck off or suspended, commits an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine or imprisonment for a period not exceeding one year."

Relevant to PLE topics on the consequences of practising while struck off or unenrolled.
