# Mortgage Bonds — Registration and Cancellation

Definitions, causae, registration documents and cancellation mechanics — tested in almost every sitting, usually as a mix of definition, list and true/false questions.

## Core definitions

- **Mortgagor** — the owner of the property who gives (binds) the property as security for fulfilment of a financial obligation. The mortgagor is the **borrower**.
- **Mortgagee** — the person who grants the loan (or other benefit); in return the borrower gives the property up as security. The mortgagee is the **lender**.
- A **mortgage bond** is a document recording that the owner of a property owes a debt to the lender, with the property given as security for that debt.

## Effect of a mortgage bond

- The owner/debtor **cannot transfer** the property to someone else unless the bond is cancelled — this requires the **bondholder's consent**.
- The Deeds Office will usually only cancel a bond if the bondholder **consents in writing** (typically once the debt is repaid in full).
- On default, the bondholder may (after due legal process) **attach and sell** the property by public auction, using proceeds to settle the debt.
- On the debtor's **insolvency**, the bondholder has a **secured claim** — paid first out of the proceeds of the property before ordinary creditors.

## Documents lodged for registration of a mortgage bond

1. Power of Attorney to Pass Bond
2. Draft Mortgage Bond
3. Original Title Deed

## Causae (reasons) for registration of a mortgage bond (any 5)

- Loan of money for existing debt
- Loan of money for future debt
- Loan of money securing existing and future debt
- Goods sold and delivered
- Balance of purchase price for a property (**kustingsbrief**)
- Indemnity agreement
- Collateral security
- Giving effect to a suretyship agreement

## Types of bond — true/false traps

- A **collateral bond** is registered by the **same** mortgagor (as an additional bond over other property, same debtor). — True
- A **surety bond** is registered by a **different** person (the surety), not the same mortgagor. — False if the question says "same mortgagor"

## Bond cancellation

- **Consent to cancel the bond** — used where the bond is being cancelled entirely at the Deeds Office, e.g. the mortgagor settled the loan in full or sold the property and the mortgagee (bank) signs consent to cancel.
- **Consent to release** — used where the bond is *not* being cancelled but a specific property or person is being **released** from the operation of the bond (e.g. a portion is released from a bond covering multiple properties).
- Who signs consent to cancel: a person appointed by the bondholder (bank) — e.g. a director/financial manager, or someone in the bond cancellation attorney's office holding a registered Power of Attorney from the bank.

### Reasons for cancellation of a mortgage bond (examples)

- The mortgagor sold the property and wishes to transfer to the purchaser.
- The mortgagor settled the loan owing to the bank.
- The mortgagor is switching finance to another bank.

## Capital sum vs additional sum in a bond

- **Capital sum** — the value of the primary obligation, i.e. the actual money loaned.
- **Additional sum** — a "budget" amount covering costs or other sums the borrower may become liable for on breach, over and above the capital sum and interest.
