# Marital Property Regimes and Conveyancing

The marital regime of a party affects who must sign, and whether consent is needed, in a conveyancing transaction. Tested mainly as true/false.

## Community of property — signing requirements

- Where spouses are married **in community of property (ICOP)** and purchase immovable property, **both must sign**, or one may sign with the **written consent** of the other spouse.
- It is **not** sufficient for only one spouse to sign a deed of sale for the transfer of immovable property without the other's consent.
- A wife married ICOP **can ratify a deed of sale** signed by her husband, provided it is within a reasonable time — True.
- A wife married ICOP **cannot ratify a suretyship** signed by her husband (suretyships require prior written consent, not later ratification) — False.

## Documents to establish marital status

- Marriage certificate
- Antenuptial contract (ANC)
- Divorce order and settlement agreement
- Affidavit, if never married before

## Other true/false traps

- The Matrimonial Property Act 88 of 1984 **does** apply to same-sex marriages/partnerships — True.
- A legal opinion on the consequences of a **foreign marriage** is **not** required *every single time* the owner alienates property — False (only where uncertainty exists).
- Parties married under the Customary Unions Act after 15 November 1998 are **not automatically** regarded as married in community of property — False (depends on requirements under the applicable legislation).
