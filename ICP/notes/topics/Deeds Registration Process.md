# Deeds Registration Process

The pipeline a deed follows once lodged at the Deeds Office — and the errors that get it rejected. Tested in nearly every sitting.

## The pipeline (lodgement to delivery)

1. Lodgement
2. Data-Prep
3. Distribution Room
4. Junior Examination
5. Senior Examination
6. Assistant Registrar of Deeds / Monitor
   - **If rejected** → Delivery (back to conveyancer, with notes)
   - **If passed** → Prep
7. Execution (registration)
8. Numbering (endorsements, Registrar's seal embossing)
9. Data-capture
10. Scanning / microfilming
11. Delivery of the registered deed to the conveyancing firm

A shorter version of the same sequence, as tested: 1st Examination → Monitored, then passed → On prep → Registration → Data capture → Delivery of registered deed.

## What "on prep" means

- Deeds are "**on prep**" once they have been **passed by the examiners**; any examiners' notes have been attended to and removed; and the finances and everything else pertaining to the transaction are in order.
- "On prep" sits in the process **after (senior) examination has passed the deed, and before registration/execution**.

> [!note] Watch for inconsistent memo wording
> One memo describes "on prep" as being positioned "post registration, before registration/execution" (likely a typo for "post-examination"); a near-identical recycled paper's memo says "post examination/, before registration/execution". Treat "on prep" as: **after the examiners have passed the deed, before execution/registration.**

## Once notes have been raised and attended to — stages before delivery (any 5)

1. Notes attended to; final interdict check by the Deeds Office; transferring attorney confirms finances are in order.
2. Registration (execution).
3. Numbering, endorsements, Registrar's seal embossing.
4. Data capturing.
5. Microfilming.
6. Delivery of the registered deeds.

## Lodgement sequence of a linked transaction

Where a transfer is linked to a bond cancellation and a new bond, the deeds/documents are lodged and registered **simultaneously**, in a fixed sequence — tested as: **T / BC / BC / B** (Transfer, then the linked bond cancellations, then the new Bond).

## Errors that could lead to rejection (any 5)

- Incorrect description of the transferor
- An attachment noted over the property (not yet uplifted)
- Mortgage bond not lodged
- Property description incorrect
- Extending clause incorrect
- Conditions omitted from the conditions clause
- Prep clause not signed by the conveyancer / pages not initialled
- Transferor described as married ICOP in the old deed but OCOP in the new deed (or vice versa) — a mismatch
- A document that should have been lodged has been omitted

Worked example: a deed reciting that the appearer's principal "truly and legally sold" the property to a trust, where the **trust was only established after the date of that sale** — this is a rejectable error, since the trust did not exist (and had no capacity to be a party) at the date of the transaction.
