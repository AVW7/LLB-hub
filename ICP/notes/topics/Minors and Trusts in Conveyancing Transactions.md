# Minors and Trusts in Conveyancing Transactions

Two recurring capacity issues: minors dealing in immovable property, and trusts that were not properly constituted at the time of the transaction.

## Minors buying/selling/mortgaging property

- A minor requires the **assistance of a guardian** to sell or mortgage immovable property.
- Depending on the **value** of the property, further consent is required in addition to the guardian's assistance:
  - Where the value is **below the statutory threshold**, the **written consent of the Master of the High Court** is required in addition to the guardian's assistance.
  - Where the value is **above the statutory threshold**, a **High Court order** authorising the transaction is required in addition to the guardian's assistance.

> [!warning] Threshold has appeared differently across sittings
> One older paper gives the threshold as **R100 000** (minor may purchase without the Master's consent if the value is below this); a later paper frames the guardian/Master's-consent-vs-High-Court-order split at **R250 000**. This may reflect a change in the applicable monetary threshold over time, or a purchase/sale-mortgage distinction — check the current Administration of Estates Act threshold rather than relying on either figure blindly.

## Trusts — ratification is not possible

- Facts pattern: a trust is created by trust deed on one date; the *trust* (via a person purporting to act for it) buys property **before** a trustee has been formally appointed by Letters of Authority from the Master; the offer to purchase contains a clause allowing the "trustee" to ratify the sale later, failing which they are personally bound.
- **This cannot be done.** Section 6 of the **Trust Property Control Act** requires the trustee to be appointed **at the time of the transaction** — a trustee cannot ratify a transaction entered into before their appointment.
- Practical consequence: if no valid ratification is possible, the person who signed is personally bound to the contract.
