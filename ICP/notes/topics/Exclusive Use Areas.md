# Exclusive Use Areas

One of the most heavily tested single concepts in ICP — some form of Exclusive Use Area (EUA) question appears in almost every sitting.

## Definition

An exclusive use area is an area that forms part of the **common property** of a sectional title scheme but which is, in terms of the law, reserved for the **exclusive use** of the owner of a particular section. Examples: gardens, balconies, patios, parking bays, storerooms, outdoor entertainment areas.

## Two ways to reserve/register an EUA (Sectional Titles Act 95 of 1986)

| Route | Section | Nature of right | How created |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **s27** | **Real right** | Indicated on the sectional plan and reserved by a **Certificate of Real Right of Exclusive Use Area** |
| 2 | **s27A** | **Personal right** | Reserved in the **conduct/management rules** of the scheme (not on the plan) |

## Can an EUA be mortgaged?

Yes — a real right EUA created under s27 (Certificate of Real Right) can be mortgaged, in the same way as any other real right in immovable property.

## Transfer/cession of an EUA

Transferred by **Notarial Deed of Cession of Exclusive Use Area**.

## Real right of extension — non-disclosure

Where a developer, on opening the sectional title register, reserves a **real right of extension** but this is not disclosed to a purchaser, the purchaser (once advised by the conveyancer) may:
1. **Annul (rescind) the agreement**, or
2. **Proceed with the transaction** and **waive in writing** the right to annul.

Disclosure of any registered real right of extension is one of the certifications a conveyancer gives in the **Conveyancer's Certificate under s15B(3)** — see [[Body Corporate — Management and Conduct Rules]].

## Practice notes

- An EUA is *not* the same as ownership of a section — it is a use right over common property.
- Watch for questions asking you to name examples (garage, parking bay, garden, storeroom) — a garage demarcated as such on the sectional plan is a textbook example.
