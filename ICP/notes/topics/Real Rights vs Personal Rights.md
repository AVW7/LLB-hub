# Real Rights vs Personal Rights

Foundational distinction, tested as a straight definition-plus-example question.

## Definitions

- **Real right** — a strong right in relation to a *thing*, generally enforceable against the whole world (not just one or two individuals). Example: ownership of a thing, or a mortgagee's rights under a mortgage bond.
- **Personal right** — a right enforceable against another person (or persons); it may or may not relate to a particular thing. Example: an employee's right to demand payment of salary from the employer.

## Why it matters in conveyancing

The real right/personal right distinction underlies several other ICP topics:

- An exclusive use area can be created as a **real right** (s27 certificate) or a **personal right** (s27A conduct rule) — see [[Exclusive Use Areas]].
- A mortgage bond gives the mortgagee a **real right** in the mortgaged property, enforceable against successors in title, not just the original mortgagor — see [[Mortgage Bonds — Registration and Cancellation]].
