# Transfer of Land — Role Players and Process

The conveyancing transaction itself: who is involved, what gets lodged, and where the law comes from. Tested constantly, usually as list-style questions.

## Examples of conveyancing transactions (any 5)

- Transfer of ownership in land
- Registration of mortgage bonds
- Cancellation of mortgage bonds
- Township creations
- Creation of sectional title schemes
- Subdivision or consolidation of properties
- Creation of servitudes

## Role players in a linked transaction

A sale with a bond to be cancelled and a new bond to be registered typically involves **three firms**, coordinated by the **transferring attorney**:

- **Transferring attorney** — attends to the transfer itself; coordinates the whole transaction.
- **Bond attorney** — attends to registration of the purchaser's new mortgage bond (instructed by the purchaser's bank).
- **Bond cancellation attorney** — attends to cancellation of the seller's existing bond (instructed by the seller's bank).

Coordination steps carried out by the transferring attorney (any 10):
1. Contact seller, purchaser and estate agent, confirm receipt of instruction.
2. Request the bond cancellation attorney's cancellation guarantee requirements.
3. Inform the bond attorney of the transferring attorney's guarantee requirements.
4. Draft/prepare the transfer documents.
5. Schedule signing consultations with seller and purchaser.
6. Apply for clearance figures.
7. Apply for a transfer duty receipt (or exemption).
8. Comply with FICA — see [[FICA Compliance in Conveyancing]].
9. Draft the deed of transfer.
10. Arrange lodgement once all three firms are ready.
11. Check finances are in order/on file.
12. Arrange registration once on prep.
13. Pay the bond cancellation fee.
14. Prepare the final statement of account — see [[Trust Account, Guarantees and Undertakings]].
15. Pay the seller and any other person entitled thereto.

Note: an **instruction from an estate agent** to attend to a transfer does **not** make the estate agent your client — the client is the transferor/transferee.

## Documents lodged for a normal transfer

- Title deed
- Deed of transfer
- Power of attorney
- Transfer duty receipt
- Clearance certificate

## Documents for a Close Corporation as a party (any 5)

- CK1 and CK2
- Resolution by members (or extract from minutes of members' meeting)
- Identity document of the member authorised to act on the CC's behalf
- Proof of residence of the authorised representative
- Proof of business address of the CC
- Identity documents of CC members
- Affidavit by a member regarding the affairs of the CC
- Accounting officer's report

## Deeds office printout — purpose (any 4)

- Verify the name of the owner
- Verify the identity number of the seller
- Confirm marital status and solvency of the seller
- Verify the property description
- Establish which local authority has jurisdiction
- Check for registered conditions or servitudes
- Check for interdicts/attachments
- Ascertain registered mortgage bonds
- Check for registered exclusive use areas or a real right of extension

## Certificate of Title vs Deed of Transfer

| | Certificate of Title | Deed of Transfer |
|---|---|---|
| Change of ownership | No | Yes |
| Transfer duty/clearance certificate required | No | Yes (usually) |
| Typical use | Subdivision, consolidation, co-owner obtaining separate title for their share | Usually the whole property, transfer to a new owner |

## Prep clause / responsibility for correctness (Deeds Registries Act 47 of 1937)

- The **conveyancer** takes responsibility for the correctness of a deed of transfer or certificate of title (s15 and regs 43/44 of the Deeds Registries Act 47 of 1937).
- The conveyancer signs the **prep clause** to accept this responsibility.
- A **notary** can also sign this clause (in an appropriate matter).
- The clause does **not** appear in a deed transferring/ceding an exclusive use area (a notarial deed of cession, not a deed of transfer).
- The appearer authorised under the Power of Attorney to Transfer, and the person signing the prep clause, **can** be one and the same person.

## Essential clauses in a deed of sale (any 5)

Seller · Purchaser · Purchase price · Property (description) · Agent's commission · Date of sale · Deposit amount and due date · Bond clause · Guarantee due date · Transfer duty or VAT · Date of possession · Date of occupation · Occupational interest · Date of transfer.

## Property description / extending clause

An extending clause typically contains three facts:
1. First/extending title deed number.
2. General plan/diagram number.
3. Current/holding title number.

## Disadvantages of a partnership (any 4)

No limited liability · No perpetual succession · Risk of unauthorised contract binding the partnership · Limitation on number of partners.
