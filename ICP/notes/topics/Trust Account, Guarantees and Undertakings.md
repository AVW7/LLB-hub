# Trust Account, Guarantees and Undertakings

How conveyancers hold and account for clients' money, and the two instruments used to secure payment of the purchase price.

## Why conveyancers must have trust accounts

By law, conveyancers must keep clients' money in trust accounts, **separate** from the conveyancer's own money. There is a risk that clients' money could be used up and lost if it is not kept aside separately.

## Investing the purchaser's funds

- The purchaser should be advised to authorise **investment of the funds at a registered bank**.
- Advantage: the purchaser is entitled to the **interest earned** on the investment, at a competitive rate.
- Interest / the invested amount is normally released to the seller **on the day of registration of transfer**.

## Paying the seller before registration

Can the conveyancer pay the seller before registration/transfer using the purchaser's yet-to-be-released trust funds? **No.** Instead, the conveyancer can:

1. Call for payment directly from the seller.
2. Make the payment on behalf of the seller from the conveyancer's **business account**.
3. Arrange **bridging finance** for the seller.

## Guarantee vs undertaking

Both instruments fulfil the same function — securing a promise to pay — but differ in who issues them:

| | Guarantee | Undertaking |
|---|---|---|
| Issued by | A **financial institution** (bank) — an irrevocable undertaking to pay a specified amount to a specified person on fulfilment of specified conditions (e.g. registration of transfer) | A **firm of attorneys**, on their letterhead, undertaking to pay a specified amount on fulfilment of specified conditions |
| Typical use | Purchaser has secured a bank loan — the bond registration attorneys issue a guarantee for the purchase price before registration | Purchaser paid cash, held in the firm's trust/investment account — the firm holding the money issues the undertaking |

## Statement of account (seller's account) — worked example

A seller's account typically nets purchase price plus occupational rental against deductions (mortgage settlement, agents' commission) to arrive at the amount due to the seller:

```
Sellers Account
To Purchase Price                    1 000 000
To Occupational Rental                  10 000
Less Mortgage Settlement    330 000
Less Estate Agents Commission 60 000
Cheque herewith/Due to you             620 000
```
