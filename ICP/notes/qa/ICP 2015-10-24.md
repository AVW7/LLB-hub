## Q1 — Unimproved/improved land; real vs personal rights; who may register (5)

> [!success]- Answer (no question wording available)
> 1.1.1 Unimproved land is land that is vacant, with no buildings on it. 1.1.2 Improved land is land with buildings on it. 1.1.3 A real right is a strong right in relation to a thing, generally enforceable against the whole world, as opposed to just one or two individuals. 1.1.4 A personal right is a right against another person/persons (may or may not relate to a particular thing). 1.2 A practising conveyancer.

## Q3 — Trust account, investing purchaser's funds, paying the seller early (10)

> [!success]- Answer (no question wording available)
> 3.1 Trust current account. 3.2 The purchaser should be advised to authorise investment of the funds at a registered bank; the advantage is that the purchaser is entitled to the interest earned, at a competitive rate. 3.3 No — the conveyancer can call for payment directly from the seller, make the payment on behalf of the seller from the conveyancer's business account, or arrange bridging finance for the seller. 3.4 On the day of registration of transfer. 3.5 By law conveyancers must have trust accounts to keep clients' money separate from their own; there is a risk that clients' money may be used up and lost if not kept separate.

## Q5 — Exclusive use area (5)

> [!success]- Answer (no question wording available)
> 5.1 An exclusive use area is an area that forms part of the common property but which is, in terms of the law, reserved for the exclusive use of the owner of a particular section — e.g. gardens, balconies, patios, parking bays, storerooms, outdoor entertainment areas. 5.2 No. 5.3 The body corporate of the sectional scheme. 5.4 Notarial deed of cession of exclusive use area.

Related: [[Exclusive Use Areas]]

## Q6 — Capital sum vs additional sum; guarantee vs undertaking (5)

> [!success]- Answer (no question wording available)
> 6.1 Capital sum — the value of the primary obligation, the actual money loaned. Additional sum — a "budget" covering costs or other sums the borrower may become liable for on breach, over and above the capital sum and interest.
> 6.2 Both are documents "securing" a promise to pay. A bank guarantee is issued/signed by the bank, or a conveyancing bond attorney authorised by the bank, usually limited in Rand value. A letter of undertaking is issued/signed by the conveyancing bond attorney (or other staff member) per the firm's general policy.
