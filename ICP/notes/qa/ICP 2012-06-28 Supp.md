## Q1 — Close Corporation documents (5)

> [!question] Question
> List 5 documents that need to be obtained by a conveyancer where a Close Corporation is a party in a transfer.

> [!success]- Answer
> Any 5 of: CK1; CK2; resolution by members/extract from minutes of members' meeting; identity document of the authorised member; proof of residence of the authorised representative; proof of business address of the CC; identity documents of CC members; affidavit by a member regarding the affairs of the CC; accounting officer's report.

## Q2 — Guarantee vs undertaking (4)

> [!question] Question
> 2.1 What is the difference between a guarantee and an undertaking? (2) 2.2 Give a practical example where you would require each of these instruments (2).

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 A guarantee is an official letter issued by a financial institution (e.g. a bank) making an irrevocable undertaking to pay a certain amount to a specified person on fulfilment of specified conditions (e.g. registration of transfer). An undertaking is a letter of promise issued by a firm of attorneys on their letterhead, undertaking to pay a certain amount on fulfilment of specified conditions.
> 2.2 A guarantee is required where the purchaser has secured a bank loan and the bond registration attorneys must issue a guarantee for the purchase price prior to registration. An undertaking is issued where the purchaser paid cash, held in a firm's trust/investment account on the client's behalf.

Related: [[Trust Account, Guarantees and Undertakings]]

## Q4 — True/false (6)

> [!question] Question
> True or false: 4.1 The property owner who gives his property as security for a loan under a bond is known as the mortgagee. 4.2 A shareholder, by virtue of holding shares in a Share-Block company that owns immovable property, is entitled to ownership of that property. 4.3 Subdivision is when two properties are combined to form one property. 4.4 Rules of the Body Corporate are divided into two categories, management and conduct rules. 4.5 One of the key role players in a conveyancing transaction is the purchaser. 4.6 If parties are married in community of property and purchase immovable property, both must sign, or one can sign with the other spouse's consent.

> 4.1 False. 4.2 False. 4.3 False. 4.4 True. 4.5 True. 4.6 True.

## Q5 — Purpose of a Deeds Office printout (4)

> [!question] Question
> What is the purpose of obtaining a Deeds Office printout — mention any 4?

> [!success]- Answer
> Any 4 of: verify the name of the owner; verify the seller's identity number; confirm marital status and solvency of the seller; verify the property description; establish which local authority has jurisdiction; check for registered conditions or servitudes; check for interdicts/attachments; ascertain registered mortgage bonds; check for registered exclusive use areas or a real right of extension.

## Q7 — EUA and "transfer" defined (2)

> [!question] Question
> 7.1 What is an Exclusive Use Area? 7.2 Explain the term "transfer".

> [!success]- Answer
> 7.1 That part of the common property reserved for the exclusive use of the owner of a particular section. 7.2 The change in ownership of immovable property from one person to another.

## Q8 — Stages after notes raised on prep (5)

> [!question] Question

## Q9 — Consent to cancel vs consent to release; cancellation reasons (10)

> [!question] Question
> 9.1 What is the difference between consent to cancel the bond and consent to release? (4) 9.2 Mention 3 reasons for the cancellation of mortgage bonds (6).

> [!success]- Answer
> 9.1 A consent to cancel the bond applies where the bond is being cancelled entirely at the Deeds Office (e.g. the mortgagor settled the loan in full, or sold the property, and the mortgagee signs consent to cancel). A consent to release applies where the bond is not being cancelled, but a specific property or person is released from the operation of the bond.
> 9.2 The mortgagor sold the property and wishes to transfer to the purchaser; the mortgagor settled the loan owing to the bank; the mortgagor is switching finance to another bank.

Related: [[Mortgage Bonds — Registration and Cancellation]]

## Q10 — Types of ownership differentiated (4)

> [!question] Question
> Differentiate between: 10.1 Freehold; 10.2 Leasehold; 10.3 Sectional Title; 10.4 Time-share.

> [!success]- Answer
> 10.1 Freehold — full ownership, owner free to deal with the property as he pleases; owns the land and structures on it. 10.2 Leasehold — qualified ownership for a limited period, after which the property reverts to the original owner. 10.3 Sectional title — similar to freehold, but the owner owns the building or part of it, separate from the land, usually alongside other owners. 10.4 Time-share — a number of people acquire a right to occupy a property for a specified period each.
