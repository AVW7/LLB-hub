## Q1 — True/false (15)

> [!question] Question
> 1.1 Deeds can be registered with the Master of the High Court. 1.2 Property in Durban can be registered in the Pretoria Deeds Office. 1.3 Sectional Title Schemes can be opened and registered on farm property. 1.4 Sectional title units not sharing a common boundary can be consolidated. 1.5 An attorney can sign a mortgage bond. 1.6 An attorney can sign an Application for a Certificate of Consolidated Title. 1.7 A clearance certificate for the transfer of a sectional title unit must be lodged with every transfer. 1.8 A collateral bond is registered by the same mortgagor. 1.9 A surety bond is registered by the same mortgagor. 1.10 Vesting clause: "Special du Toit, 050505 5153 086, minor, duly assisted by his parents Spanner du Toit 480302 5034 086 and Wetwipe du Toit 640725 0054 084, married in community of property to each other" — correct? 1.11 The transferor pays transfer duty if the transferee is registered for VAT. 1.12 An instruction from an estate agent to attend to a transfer means the estate agent is your client. 1.13 The bond attorney acts on behalf of the mortgagor. 1.14 You can transfer a property if you are not the registered owner thereof. 1.15 A SARS document older than 4 months is acceptable for FICA purposes.

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 False. 1.2 False. 1.3 True. 1.4 True. 1.5 False. 1.6 True. 1.7 True. 1.8 True. 1.9 False. 1.10 False. 1.11 False. 1.12 False. 1.13 False. 1.14 True. 1.15 False.

## Q3 — Ways to register/reserve an EUA (4)

> [!question] Question
> Mention 2 ways in which Exclusive Use Areas can be registered/reserved and explain.

> [!success]- Answer
> Section 27, Act 95/1986 — indicated on the sectional title plan and reserved in terms of a Certificate of Real Right of Exclusive Use Area (real right). Section 27A, Act 95/1986 — reserved in the rules (personal right).

## Q5 — Zero-rated transaction requirements (4)

> [!question] Question
> What requirements have to be met for a transaction to be regarded as a Zero-Rated transaction?

> [!success]- Answer
> Seller must be a VAT vendor; purchaser must be a VAT vendor; written agreement that the property is sold as a going concern; at time of sale it must be an income-generating business.

Related: [[Zero-Rated VAT Transactions]]

## Q6 — Trust ratification (2)

> [!question] Question
> A trust was created by trust deed on 20 January 2010. On 22 January 2010 the trust bought Erf 123 Suspectville Township. The trustee was only appointed by Letter of Appointment on 13 February 2010. The offer to purchase said the trust must ratify the sale, failing which the trustee is personally bound. Can this be done? Give reasons.

> [!success]- Answer
> Not possible. Section 6 of the Trust Property Control Act requires the trustee to be appointed at the time of the transaction — it cannot be ratified after the fact.

Related: [[Minors and Trusts in Conveyancing Transactions]]

## Q7 — "On prep" (4)

> [!question] Question
> 7.1 What is meant by "my deeds are on Prep" and what is normally done when deeds are on prep? 7.2 Where in the registration process would your deeds be if they are "on Prep"?

> [!success]- Answer
> 7.1 The deeds have been passed by the examiners; examiners' notes can be attended to and removed; finances and everything else pertaining to the transaction are in order. 7.2 Post-examination, before registration/execution.

Related: [[Deeds Registration Process]]

## Q8 — Certificate of Title vs Deed of Transfer (4)

> [!question] Question
> 8.1 Explain a Certificate of Title. 8.2 Explain a Deed of Transfer.

> [!success]- Answer
> 8.1 No change of ownership; no transfer duty or clearance certificates; used for subdivision, consolidation and a co-owner needing a separate title for his share. 8.2 Change of ownership; transfer duty and clearance certificates applicable; usually no subdivision, but transfer of the whole property.

## Q10 — 15B(3) vs Section 10 certificates (6)

> [!question] Question
> List the differences between a s15B(3) certificate and a Section 10 certificate (Sectional Titles Act 95/1986).

> [!success]- Answer
> s15B(3): conveyancer signs; a certificate; lodged with every transfer; refers to the s25 real right of extension and levies paid. Section 10: developer signs; an affidavit; only lodged where the CRST is still held by the developer; refers to s10/renting of the unit.
