## Q1 — Real vs personal rights; management vs conduct rules (8)

> [!question] Question
> 1.1 What is the difference between real rights and personal rights? Give examples (4). 1.2 Differentiate between management and conduct rules of the body corporate (4).

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 A real right is a right in relation to a thing, with universal application — enforceable against the whole world (e.g. ownership, or a mortgagee's rights under a bond). A personal right is enforceable against another person (e.g. an employee's right to demand payment of salary from the employer).
> 1.2 Management rules deal with management of the scheme (e.g. appointment of trustees, meetings, determination/collection of levies). Conduct rules deal with the conduct of occupiers of the sections — what is acceptable/unacceptable (e.g. hanging laundry from windows, littering).

Related: [[Real Rights vs Personal Rights]], [[Body Corporate — Management and Conduct Rules]]

## Q2 — Mortgagor/mortgagee; bond registration documents (5)

> [!question] Question
> 2.1 Explain the terms "mortgagor" and "mortgagee" (2). 2.2 Mention 3 documents lodged for registration of a mortgage bond (3).

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 Mortgagor — the owner of a property who gives it as security for fulfilment of a financial obligation under a bond; the borrower. Mortgagee — the person who grants the loan/benefit in return for the property as security; the lender.
> 2.2 Power of Attorney to Pass Bond; Draft Mortgage Bond; Original Title Deed.

> [!question] Question

## Q5 — Undisclosed real right of extension (4)

> [!question] Question
> The purchaser bought a unit in a Sectional Title Scheme. The developer, on opening the register, reserved a right to extend the scheme, but this was not disclosed to the purchaser. The conveyancer advised the purchaser of this. What options are available to the purchaser?

> [!success]- Answer
> The purchaser may choose to annul the agreement, or proceed with the transaction and waive in writing the right to annul.

## Q9 — Bond cancellation and the Chief Registrar (4)

> [!question] Question
> 9.1 Who signs a Consent to Cancellation of a bond? 9.2 Who is responsible for overseeing the operation of all Deeds Registries in the country?

> [!success]- Answer
> 9.1 A person appointed by the bondholder (bank) — e.g. a director/financial manager, or a person in the bond cancellation attorney's office holding a registered Power of Attorney from the bank. 9.2 The Chief Registrar of Deeds.
