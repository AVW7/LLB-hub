## Q1 — Causes of transfer; mortgage bond definition and consequences (11)

> [!success]- Answer (no question wording available)
> 1.1 Sale; donation; inheritance; divorce settlement.
> 1.2 A mortgage bond is a document stating that the owner of a property owes a debt to the lender, and that the property is given as security for the debt.
> 1.3 The owner cannot transfer the property unless the bond is cancelled, which requires the bondholder's consent; the Deeds Office will usually only cancel a bond if the bondholder consents in writing (usually once the debt is repaid in full); on default, the bondholder may (after due process) attach and sell the property by public auction to settle the debt; on the debtor's insolvency, the bondholder has a secured claim, paid first from the proceeds of the property before ordinary creditors.
> 1.4 [Named party in the fact pattern] is the owner.

## Q2 — Freehold vs leasehold; sectional title vs freehold (8)

> [!success]- Answer (no question wording available)
> 2.1 Freehold property is where the land and buildings are privately owned by the registered owner; leasehold property is where the occupier "owns" the land and buildings for a limited duration only, reverting to the owner (usually the authorities) after that period.
> 2.2 Leasehold is a type of land right involving qualified ownership; a lease is a rental agreement between the land owner and a tenant.
> 2.3 Yes — in most areas where these rights were granted, land was initially not surveyed and no diagrams/general plans exist for individual stands; these areas must first be surveyed and general plans drawn up and approved, which takes time and money.
> 2.4 Sectional title ownership involves ownership of a building or part of a building, separate from the land (e.g. a flat/section); freehold involves ownership of the whole building(s) and the land on which it is situated.

Related: [[Types of Property Ownership]]
