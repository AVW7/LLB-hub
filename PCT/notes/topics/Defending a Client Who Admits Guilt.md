# Defending a Client Who Admits Guilt

## The core position

An attorney **is permitted** to act for a client who privately admits guilt (e.g. to murder or armed robbery) but instructs a **not-guilty plea**. Determining guilt or innocence is not the attorney's function — doing so would **usurp the function of the court**.

## Ethical limits on how the defence may be conducted

The attorney **may not**:
- lead evidence contrary to what the client has admitted;
- put a version to a witness that the attorney knows to be false (e.g. a false alibi defence);
- mislead the court, or mislead a witness by making submissions known to be untrue;
- formulate or advance an affirmative defence known to be untrue.

The attorney **may**:
- test/attack the State's case — cross-examine on the **credibility or reliability** of witnesses (e.g. deficiencies in identification evidence);
- challenge the **admissibility and relevancy** of evidence;
- raise **technical defences** — jurisdiction of the court, correctness of the charge(s) as framed.

Keep in mind throughout that the plea can still be **changed to guilty** as the matter proceeds.
