# Trust Account Theft and the Attorneys Fidelity Fund

The single most heavily tested PCT topic — a trust-theft scenario (usually a bookkeeper or employee stealing trust money) appears in some form in most sittings, almost always paired with [[Prescribed Claims and the Attorneys Insurance Indemnity Fund]].

## Prevention / early detection

- Extract a list of trust creditors **every quarter** and compare it against the bank statement and investments — this is the routine check that should surface a shortfall early.

## Steps once theft is discovered

1. Inform the **Law Society**.
2. Have the firm's accountant conduct a **complete investigation** of the shortfall.
3. **Pay in** any determined shortfall.
4. Inform the **trust creditors**.
5. Undertake to make good the shortfall as soon as the amount is determined.
6. Reassure creditors regarding the **Fidelity Fund as fund of last resort**.
7. Invite creditors to take independent advice from the Law Society or another attorney.

## The claim mechanism

- A theft claim is lodged (via the statutory Law Society) against the **Attorneys Fidelity Fund**, which indemnifies persons who suffer loss from the theft of money or property entrusted to a practitioner **in the course of practice as such**.
- The Fund covers theft committed by the **practitioner personally, or by an employee/candidate attorney** acting in the course of the practice (e.g. a bookkeeper).
- The Fund may require the attorney to be **excussed** before entertaining the client's claim, and **time limits apply**.
- **Not all trust money is protected** — exclusions apply under **s47(1)(g)** of the Act.
- The attorney is **primarily liable** to the client for the loss, independently of the client's Fidelity Fund claim.

## Fund income and other benefits

- Two main sources of Fidelity Fund income: **interest on invested trust money**, and **income from the Fund's own investments**.
- Practitioners contribute to the Fund by paying the interest accumulated on trust funds invested (in terms of **s78(2)(a)**) to the Fund.
- The Fund makes discretionary payments that benefit **all practitioners directly**: refunding **bank charges and audit fees** on practitioners' trust banking accounts, and paying the premium for practitioners' **Professional Indemnity Insurance** cover.

## A related but separate fund

The **Attorneys Development Fund** — its purpose is to promote the sustainability and growth of newly established and deserving attorneys' firms, and to provide skills development and skills transfer programmes to build a body of qualified, competent practitioners. Do not confuse it with the Fidelity Fund.
