# Prescribed Claims and the Attorneys Insurance Indemnity Fund

Recurring companion scenario to [[Trust Account Theft and the Attorneys Fidelity Fund]] — usually the same fact pattern tests both: an employee steals trust money AND a client's claim is discovered to have become prescribed through the firm's neglect.

## Where the prescription is the attorney's own fault

- Advise the client to consult **another, independent attorney** to pursue a negligence claim against the original (negligent) attorney.
- The negligent attorney must **not admit liability** to the client — an admission risks the indemnity fund **repudiating** the claim.
- The independent attorney lodges the claim, which is then referred to/indemnified by the **Attorneys Insurance Indemnity Fund (AIIF)** (or the practitioner's own professional indemnity insurer, if any).
- The Law Society need not be proactively informed of the prescribed claim, but the practitioner must respond to any complaint that is raised.
- In both the trust-theft and the prescription scenario, the **attorney is primarily liable** to the client.

## Where the claim was already prescribed before you received it

- Not every prescribed claim is the current attorney's fault — sometimes a claim is handed over already dead.
- Advise the client that **prescription is a complete defence** available to the defendant.
- If the client nevertheless wants to proceed, accepting the risk of **wasted costs** in the hope prescription won't be pleaded, the attorney may proceed on that basis.
