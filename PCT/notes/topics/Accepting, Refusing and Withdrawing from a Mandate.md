# Accepting, Refusing and Withdrawing from a Mandate

## General rule

A practitioner is entitled to **accept or refuse any work offered**, unless a special reason creates an obligation to accept or refuse it. The practitioner is free to choose between competing mandates — but **once a mandate is accepted it must be carried through to conclusion**.

## Grounds to refuse a new mandate (any three)

1. A conflict of interest exists.
2. Animosity between attorney and client.
3. The attorney is not competent to handle that type of matter.
4. The attorney would not be available.
5. The client wants the attorney to break the law or commit fraud.

## Grounds to withdraw from an accepted mandate (any four)

1. A conflict of interest arises.
2. The client requires an illegal or improper action.
3. The client fails to give instructions.
4. The client fails to pay an agreed deposit.
5. A personality clash / breakdown of trust occurs.
6. The attorney cannot perform due to illness.

## Procedure for withdrawal

- Where appropriate, **place the client on terms first** — make clear that unless (e.g.) a deposit is paid by a stated date, you will withdraw.
- Withdraw **timeously / at an opportune time**.
- Ensure the client **still has time to obtain other representation**.

## Special case: attorney becomes a potential witness

If, at the time of taking instructions or during the trial, it appears the attorney may be called as a witness, the attorney is ethically obliged either to **decline the instructions at the outset**, or (if it arises during trial) to **withdraw from the matter** — unless the case has proceeded so far that withdrawal would be **unconscionably prejudicial** to the client.

## Special case: client relationship breaks down mid-consultation

If a client becomes heated and storms out: decline to act further, explain to the client the reason for the breakdown in the relationship, and refer the client to a colleague or another attorney.

## Special case: choosing between two competing mandates

Where time constraints mean only one of two mandates can be accepted, the practitioner is **free to choose either** — the fact that one produces a larger profit does not create an obligation to prefer it, or to prefer whichever was offered first.
