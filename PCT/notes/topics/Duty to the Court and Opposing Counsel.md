# Duty to the Court and Opposing Counsel

## Adverse authority discovered after argument

If, at the end of a civil trial, the attorney becomes aware of new (e.g. SCA) judgments **adverse** to the client's case: the attorney is bound to bring the adverse authority to the notice of the **court**, but need **not** inform the opposing colleague, other than in the ordinary course of argument.

## Judicial recusal (e.g. magistrate is a party's relative)

Sequence, in order:
1. Before proceedings start, approach the magistrate **in chambers** to point out the apparent conflict of interest, and request recusal.
2. If the magistrate does not accede in chambers, make a **formal application in open court**.
3. If the formal application also fails, **proceed with the case** — and, if the client is convicted, take the matter on **review or appeal**.

## Interviewing an opposing party's witness

Once a matter has been **set down for trial** (signalled, e.g., by the other side issuing a subpoena for a specific witness), a practitioner who wants to interview that witness must give the **opponent notice** — by whatever means are reasonable in the circumstances — of the intention to conduct the interview. The opponent's **consent is not required**, only notice.

## Inability to attend court or a consultation with counsel

- Inability to attend a **postponed trial**: arrange a postponement/adjournment with the prosecutor if possible, or ask a colleague to appear, or proceed — the client's **best interests** are the determining factor.
- Inability to attend a **consultation with counsel**: postpone, adjourn, cancel, or send a candidate attorney/colleague instead. Counsel **may not see the client without the instructing practitioner present.**
