# Borrowing Money From or Investing With a Client

## Core rule

It is **improper conduct** for an attorney to borrow money from a client **unless**:
- the client is **independently represented** in the money-lending transaction, **or**
- lending money is the **client's usual business**.

## Extension to investments

It is likewise improper conduct for an attorney to obtain an investment in a business the attorney owns or has an interest in, **unless**:
- the attorney makes **full disclosure** to the client of the nature and extent of the attorney's interest in that business, **and**
- if it is **not** the client's usual business to make such investments, the client must also be **independently represented** in the transaction.

## Practical framing (from the memos)

Not advisable to borrow from a client unless the client is in the business of money-lending. If the client is not in that business and the attorney decides to proceed anyway, the client must obtain independent legal advice on the money-lending transaction — the underlying risk being an undisclosed conflict of interest.
