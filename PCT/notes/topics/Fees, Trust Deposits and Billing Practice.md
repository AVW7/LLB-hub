# Fees, Trust Deposits and Billing Practice

## Attorney's lien

An attorney may retain documents drawn by that attorney until costs have been paid. A 1992 (Natal) judgment extended the lien to documents on which the attorney has bestowed **skill and labour**, not only documents drawn by the attorney personally.

## Advising a client on costs risk before proceeding with a weak case

Having fully advised the client on the prospects of success, an attorney may still act at the client's insistence even where the prospects are remote — **provided** the attorney advises at the outset that the client will be responsible for costs on the applicable scale/tariff. If unsuccessful, the court is likely to grant an adverse costs order against the client, possibly on a **punitive scale**.

## When the client disputes the bill after a hopeless case

Where a client pursued a weak case against advice, lost, and then disputes the account: the attorney is entitled to draw a **Bill of Costs** on the applicable scale of fees and have it **taxed** — by the Clerk of the Court in the Magistrate's Court, or the **Taxing Master** in the High Court.

## Fee deposits taken in advance

- The deposit **remains the client's property**, not the practitioner's, until earned.
- It must be deposited into the **trust account**.
- It may only be treated/utilised as fees once the matter is **complete** and a **proper statement of account** has been rendered to the client — unless interim fees were agreed in advance.
- An attorney **may not** transfer an estimated fee amount from the trust account to the business account mid-matter; doing so is **unprofessional conduct** and exposes the attorney to a charge of **theft of trust monies**.
- Bank charges incurred on a client's **credit-card fee payment** may **not** be debited to the client or the trust account.
