## Q1.1 — Negligence vs misconduct (2)

> [!note] Editorial gloss (not sourced)
> Likely question: "When does negligence amount to professional misconduct?"

> [!success] Memo content
> Negligence will only constitute misconduct if it involves impropriety. (p36)

Related: [[Fit and Proper Person and Striking Off the Roll]]

## Q1.2 — Confidentiality vs privilege (2)

> [!success] Memo content
> Attorney-and-client privilege arises from the rules of evidence to encourage clients to be frank with attorneys. Confidentiality arises from the contract. (p39)

Related: [[Confidentiality, Privilege and Protected Communications]]

## Q1.3 — Conflicting interest (5)

> [!success] Memo content
> A conflicting interest is one which is likely to influence the judgment of a lawyer on behalf of or loyalty to a client. (examples pp42/4)

Related: [[Conflict of Interest]]

## Q1.4 — Attorney's lien (3)

> [!success] Memo content
> An attorney may retain documents drawn by such attorney until the costs have been paid. A 1992 (N) judgment extends the lien to documents on which the attorney has bestowed skill and labour. (p44)

Related: [[Fees, Trust Deposits and Billing Practice]]

## Q2.1 — Borrowing money from / investing with a client (6)

> [!success] Memo content
> It is improper conduct for an attorney to borrow monies from a client unless the client is independently represented in the money lending transaction or it is the client's usual business to lend monies.
>
> It is also improper conduct for an attorney to obtain an investment in a business in which the attorney either owns or has an interest unless the attorney makes a full disclosure to the client of the nature and extent of his interest in the said business. If it is not the client's usual business to make such investments then the client must also be independently represented in the transaction.

Related: [[Borrowing Money From or Investing With a Client]]

## Q2.2 — Attorney called as a witness (2)

> [!success] Memo content
> If at the time of your instructions or during the trial it appears that the attorney may be called in as a witness then the attorney is ethically obliged either not to accept those instructions or, if during the trial, to withdraw from the matter unless the matter has proceeded to such an extent that it would be unconscionable in relation to the prejudice to be suffered by the client, to withdraw.

Related: [[Accepting, Refusing and Withdrawing from a Mandate]]

## Q2.3 — Advising on costs risk in a weak case (3)

> [!success] Memo content
> Having advised the client fully on the prospect of success an attorney may at the instance of the client act in a matter even where the prospects of success are remote provided that the attorney advises the client at the inception that the client would be responsible for the payment of his costs on the scale or tariff of fees on which such costs are to be charged. In the likely event of the client being unsuccessful, the court would most likely make an adverse order of costs against the client and possibly on a punitive scale.

Related: [[Fees, Trust Deposits and Billing Practice]]

## Q3 — Fit and proper person (2)

> [!success] Memo content
> A practitioner is a fit and proper person if his/her personal qualities reflect integrity and reliability.

Related: [[Fit and Proper Person and Striking Off the Roll]]
