## Q1 — Client admits guilt, wants a not-guilty plea (5)

> [!question] Question
> You are approached by Joe Soap who has been charged with armed robbery. During your consultation he advises you that he committed the offence but wants to plead not guilty.
> 1.1 May you represent him under the circumstances? (1)
> 1.2 If so, subject to what ethical limits may you undertake the mandate and in what manner would you conduct the case? (4)

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 Yes.
> 1.2 You may not lead evidence contrary to what the client has told you. You may not put a version to the witnesses, e.g. an alibi defence. You may only test the evidence of the State against your client — e.g. cross-examine as to deficiencies in the identity evidence against the accused.

Related: [[Defending a Client Who Admits Guilt]]

## Q2 — Trust theft and prescribed claims (6)

> [!question] Question
> During the course of an audit of your trust account it appears that your bookkeeper has stolen a large sum of trust monies belonging to various clients of your firm. Upon further investigation it is discovered that the files of these clients are missing and a number of claims have become prescribed.
> Explain how you would go about addressing the issue of compensating the clients for the theft of the trust monies and the claims that have become prescribed.

> [!success]- Answer
> In respect of the stolen trust monies, a claim would have to be lodged with the Attorneys Fidelity Fund, which indemnifies clients for loss suffered as a result of theft by employees in the course of business.
>
> With regard to the prescription of claims, the client must be advised to immediately consult with another legal practitioner, who could lodge a claim against the original attorney, who would thereafter have to lodge a claim against the Attorneys Fidelity Fund. It is also advisable never to admit any liability until the matter has been properly investigated — such an admission is not recommended.

Related: [[Trust Account Theft and the Attorneys Fidelity Fund]], [[Prescribed Claims and the Attorneys Insurance Indemnity Fund]]

## Q3 — Grounds to refuse a mandate (3)

> [!question] Question
> Name three situations in which an attorney would be obliged to refuse a mandate to act on behalf of a client.

> [!success]- Answer
> Any of: where there exists a conflict of interest; animosity between attorney and client; where the attorney is not competent to handle a matter; where the client wishes the attorney to assist in breaking the law.

Related: [[Accepting, Refusing and Withdrawing from a Mandate]]

## Q4 — Contingency fee agreement (6)

> [!question] Question
> A client, Mr T Stark, approaches you to institute legal action against the police for an incident in which he was viciously assaulted by members of the South African Police Services. He advises that he cannot afford to pay your fees up front and has enquired whether you would be willing to enter into an agreement to the effect that you settle your account upon the successful finalisation of the claim. You assess the claim as having merit.
> 4.1 Are you permitted to enter into such an agreement with the client? (2)
> 4.2 If so, set out the prescribed formalities. (2)
> 4.3 What would be the maximum fee you would be entitled to charge? (2)

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 An attorney is permitted to enter into a contingency fee agreement with the client.
> 4.2 The agreement must be in writing and in a form prescribed by the Contingency Fees Act.
> 4.3 A legal practitioner is entitled to charge double his/her usual fee but not exceeding 25% of the judgment amount, whichever is the lesser of the two.

Related: [[Contingency Fee Agreements]]

## Q5 — Difficult client / referral outside expertise (4)

> [!question] Question
> What would you do as an attorney in the following situations?
> 5.1 During consultation with a client things become heated between yourselves and the client storms out of your offices. (2)
> 5.2 You are approached by a client who has been advised that he wishes for one of his latest inventions to be patented (this is an area of law you know nothing about). (2)

> [!success]- Answer
> 5.1 Decline to act further for the client, explain to the client the reason for the breakdown in the relationship between yourself and the client, and refer the client to a colleague or another attorney.
> 5.2 Decline to act and refer the client to another attorney who could possibly assist him in this regard.

Related: [[Accepting, Refusing and Withdrawing from a Mandate]], [[Declining Work Outside Your Competence]]

## Q6 — Conflict of interest (1)

> [!question] Question
> Give your understanding of what amounts to a conflict of interest.

> [!success]- Answer
> A situation arising when, as a result of any number of factors, the judgment of an attorney adversely affects his loyalty or commitment towards his client or his cause.
