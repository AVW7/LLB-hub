# PCT — 19 May 2012

Source question paper: [[PCT19May2012Q.doc]]
Source marking memo: [[PCT19May2012A.doc]]

A single integrated scenario paper — bookkeeper absconded, files untouched for three years, clients complaining of missing funds, plus a same-day consultation with counsel and a postponed criminal trial to attend.

## Q — Integrated trust-crisis scenario (25)

> [!question] Question
> You arrive at your office and find:
> 1. Your bookkeeper has absconded and left town.
> 2. In her office are several files on which no work has been done for three years.
> 3. Clients complain that they have not received funds collected on their behalf.
> 4. At 09h00 you and Mr A have a consultation with counsel.
> 5. At 11h15 you must appear in a postponed criminal trial.
>
> You fear that trust money may have been stolen and claims were allowed to prescribe. What do you do regarding the following:
> (a) Checking urgently whether there appears to be a shortfall (2)
> (b) A finding by you that funds have in fact been stolen (6)
> (c) The discovery that a number of claims have become prescribed (6)
> (d) Inability to attend the consultation with counsel (3)
> (e) Inability to attend at court (3)
> (f) A claim which had in fact prescribed before it was handed to you — brief advice to the client (3)
> (g) Uncertainty about the correct action regarding an ethical problem arising from the circumstances (2)

> [!success]- Answer
> (a) Since you are required to extract a list of trust creditors every quarter, you could easily compare the latest one with your bank statement and investments.
>
> (b) If trust funds have been stolen you should: inform your Law Society; have your accountant do a complete investigation of the shortfall; pay in any determined shortfall; inform trust creditors; undertake to make good shortfalls as soon as the amount is determined; reassure creditors regarding the Fidelity Fund as fund of last resort; invite creditors to take advice from the Law Society/another attorney.
>
> (c) Inform clients whose claims have prescribed, refer them to independent advice on their rights, do not admit liability, inform the AIIF and your own indemnity insurer (if any) of the probable claims. You need not inform the Law Society but must respond to any complaints.
>
> (d) Arrange a postponement/adjournment with counsel if possible, or ask a colleague to attend, or even proceed — the client's best interests being the determining factor.
>
> (e) Postpone, adjourn, cancel, or send a candidate or colleague instead. Counsel may not see the client without the practitioner present. Promote the client's best interest.
>
> (f) Inform the client that prescription would be a complete defence, but if the client wishes to accept the risk of wasted costs in the hope it will not be pleaded, you may proceed.
>
> (g) If you are uncertain, read up on the authority, then consult a senior colleague, or finally seek guidance from the Law Society.
