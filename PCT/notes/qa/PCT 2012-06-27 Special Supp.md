## Q3 — Commissioner of oaths within the firm (4)

> [!question] Question
> Your partner has drawn an affidavit in an urgent High Court application. No other commissioner of oaths is available.
> 3.1 Your partner requests you to administer the oath in a conveyancing matter. May you? (2)
> 3.2 May you administer the oath if it is not a conveyancing matter? (2)

> [!note]- Self-check pointers (not an official memo)
> See [[Commissioner of Oaths Conflicts]] — general rule bars anyone in the firm from commissioning an oath in a matter the firm is handling; conveyancing transactions are an exception.

Related: [[Commissioner of Oaths Conflicts]]

## Q4 — Trust theft and a prescribed claim (9)

> [!question] Question
> You discover that there has been theft of R100 000,00 from your firm's trust account in a particular file by your secretary. While you are busy resolving this problem you discover that a large personal injury claim that your partner was instructed to attend to has prescribed.
> 4.1 Can your client institute a claim against you or your firm for the stolen money and can you or your firm be held liable? (2)
> 4.2 With whom can your client lodge a claim in respect of the theft if you and your firm are financially unable to repay him? Within how many months must he lodge his claim? (2)
> 4.3 What is your professional duty towards your client with regard to the prescribed claim? (2)
> 4.4 From whom can you or your firm recover some or all of the money you had to pay to the client? (1)
> 4.5 Can you admit liability if you want to succeed with your claim in 4.4 above? (1)
> 4.6 Would the position be different if a candidate attorney or a professional assistant had stolen the trust funds? (1)

> [!note]- Self-check pointers (not an official memo)
> See [[Trust Account Theft and the Attorneys Fidelity Fund]] and [[Prescribed Claims and the Attorneys Insurance Indemnity Fund]] — theft claims go via the Attorneys Fidelity Fund (statutory time limits apply); prescribed-claim negligence must not be admitted to the client. 4.6 is not directly answered by any memo in this archive — reason from the fact that the Fidelity Fund covers theft by the practitioner, an employee, or a candidate attorney acting in the course of the practice.

Related: [[Trust Account Theft and the Attorneys Fidelity Fund]], [[Prescribed Claims and the Attorneys Insurance Indemnity Fund]]
