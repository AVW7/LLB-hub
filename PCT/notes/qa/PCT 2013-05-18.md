## Q1 — Adverse authority and judicial recusal (8)

> [!question] Question
> Discuss briefly how an attorney should act in the following circumstances:
> 1.1 At the end of a civil trial the attorney becomes aware that there are new SCA judgments which are adverse to the client's argument. What obligations in relation to the court and the colleague/opponent apply? (3)
> 1.2 Just before the start of a criminal trial before magistrate X, it appears that the complainant in the case is the magistrate's son. How do you act in applying the ethical rules? (5)

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 An attorney is bound to bring any adverse authority to the notice of the court but need not inform the colleague other than in the course of argument.
> 1.2 The magistrate should be requested to recuse himself. Before the proceedings start approach the magistrate in chambers to point out the apparent conflict of interest. Should the magistrate not accede to the request in chambers, proceed to make a formal application in court. Should the formal application still be unsuccessful, proceed with the case and take the matter on review or appeal in the event of the client being convicted.

Related: [[Duty to the Court and Opposing Counsel]]

## Q2 — Commissioner of oaths within the firm (5)

> [!question] Question
> You have just completed an affidavit for your client in an urgent application being brought in the High Court. It is ready for attestation. The only commissioner of oaths close by is your partner. The affidavit needs to be signed and served urgently.
> 2.1 May your partner act as the commissioner of oaths? Give reasons. (3)
> 2.2 Are there any exceptions to the above? (2)

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 No. All clients are deemed to be represented by the firm and thus by everyone in the firm. In terms of legislation, no one with an interest in the matter may act as commissioner of oaths — hence as the partner is deemed to have an interest, he/she may not act as commissioner, no matter how urgent the matter is.
> 2.2 Conveyancing transactions, formal affidavits for government records.

Related: [[Commissioner of Oaths Conflicts]]

## Q3 — Refusing a mandate and confidentiality exceptions (5)

> [!question] Question
> 3.1 Name two (2) circumstances when you are obliged to refuse a mandate. (2)
> 3.2 Name three (3) instances when attorney/client confidentiality does not apply. (3)

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 Conflict of interest; when the client wants you to do something illegal.
> 3.2 Legislation (FICA); the client informs you that he/she is going to commit a crime; the client's name; you have knowledge of an asset in a deceased client's estate of which the executor is unaware.

Related: [[Accepting, Refusing and Withdrawing from a Mandate]], [[Confidentiality, Privilege and Protected Communications]]

## Q4 — Choosing between two competing mandates (4)

> [!question] Question
> You are approached by a client who requests you to do some work for him. Before you accept the mandate, another client approaches you with instructions which will produce a larger profit for you than the work offered by the first client. Because of time constraints, you cannot accept both mandates.
> Must you accept the first mandate or are you free to choose either of the two mandates? Give reasons.

> [!success]- Answer
> The general rule is that a practitioner is entitled to accept or refuse any work offered to him/her, unless for special reason(s) there is some obligation to accept or refuse work. The practitioner is free to accept whichever mandate he/she chooses. However, once a mandate is accepted, it must be carried through to conclusion.

Related: [[Accepting, Refusing and Withdrawing from a Mandate]]

## Q5 — Borrowing money from a client (3)

> [!question] Question
> May you borrow money from a client? Give reasons.

> [!success]- Answer
> Not advisable unless the client is in the business of money lending. If not in the business and you decide to continue, the client should obtain legal advice on the money lending transaction — there is a potential conflict of interest.

Related: [[Borrowing Money From or Investing With a Client]], [[Conflict of Interest]]
