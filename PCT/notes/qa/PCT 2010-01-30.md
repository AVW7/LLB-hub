## Q1 — Contingency fee agreement (5)

> [!question] Question
> You are approached by a Plaintiff client to sue a Defendant for R1 million in respect of a money lending transaction. The client requests that the issue of your fees be dealt with on the basis that you will be entitled to remuneration only if the claim is successful. You wish to accept the mandate.
> 1.1 Are you permitted to enter into such an agreement with the client? (1)
> 1.2 If the answer to 1.1 is in the affirmative, what are the prescribed formalities for such an agreement? (2)
> 1.3 What fee would you be entitled to charge? (2)

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 An attorney is permitted to enter into a contingency fee agreement with the client.
> 1.2 The agreement must be in writing and in the form prescribed by the Contingency Fees Act.
> 1.3 A practitioner is entitled to charge up to twice the amount of his usual fees but not exceeding 25% of the amount of the award of judgment.
>
> Note to markers: contingency fees are not uniform nationally — the Law Society of the Northern Provinces accepted members may choose between a Contingency Fees Act agreement or a common-law contingency fee agreement. Apply the rules applicable to your region.

## Q2 — Trust theft and prescribed claims (9)

> [!question] Question
> During the course of an audit of your trust account it appears that your former bookkeeper has stolen a large sum of trust monies belonging to various clients of your firm. Upon further investigation it is discovered that the files of the clients are missing and a number of claims have become prescribed.
> Explain how the clients would go about recovering their losses arising from (a) the theft of the trust funds; and (b) the claims which have become prescribed; and (c) how are you affected?

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 In respect of the theft of the trust funds, a claim must be lodged via the statutory law society to the Attorneys Fidelity Fund, which is charged with the indemnification of clients who have suffered loss arising from the theft of money entrusted to the attorney in the course of his practice as such. The Attorneys Fidelity Fund may require the attorney to be excussed before they will entertain the client's claim. Time limits apply.
> 2.2 In the case of the prescription of claims, the client must be advised to consult another attorney to lodge a claim against the negligent attorney, who will then refer the claim to the AIIF. The attorney may not admit liability to the client in respect of the prescribed claim, as the AIIF can then repudiate the claim.
> 2.3 In both cases the attorney is primarily liable.

## Q3 — Adverse authority and judicial recusal (8)

> [!question] Question
> Discuss briefly how an attorney should act in the following circumstances:
> 3.1 At the end of a civil trial the attorney becomes aware that there are new SCA judgments which are adverse to the client's case. What obligations in relation to the court and the colleague/opponent apply? (3)
> 3.2 Just before the start of a criminal trial before magistrate X, it appears that the complainant in the case is the magistrate's son. How are the ethical rules exercised? (5)

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 An attorney is bound to bring any adverse authority to the notice of the court, but need not inform the colleague other than in the course of argument.
> 3.2 The magistrate should be requested to recuse himself. Before the proceedings start, approach the magistrate in chambers to point out the apparent conflict of interest. Should the magistrate not accede to the request in chambers, proceed to make a formal application in court. Should the formal application still be unsuccessful, proceed with the case and take the matter on review or appeal in the event of the client being convicted.

Related: [[Duty to the Court and Opposing Counsel]]

## Q4 — Grounds to refuse a mandate (3)

> [!question] Question
> Name three situations in which an attorney would be obliged to refuse a mandate to act on behalf of a client.

> [!success]- Answer
> Any three of: where a conflict of interest exists; animosity between the attorney and the client; where the attorney is not competent to handle the type of case; where the attorney would not be available; where the client wants the attorney to break the law or commit fraud.
