## Q1 — Notice of intention to defend (10 marks)

> [!question] Question
> John Brown sues Paul Naidoo; Naidoo has a good defence. 1.1 What notice would you serve? 1.2 Days of grace? 1.3 Draft the notice (omit heading/endings). 1.4 What if delivered late?

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 Notice of intention to defend.
> 1.2 10 court days.
> 1.3 "NOTICE OF INTENTION TO DEFEND — PLEASE TAKE NOTICE that the defendant of [address] hereby gives notice of his intention to defend this action. AND TAKE FURTHER NOTICE that, in accordance with Rule 19(3), the defendant appoints the office of his attorney set out below as the address at which he will accept service of all notices, process and documents in this action."
> 1.4 A late-delivered NID may not be ignored, provided default judgment has not yet been granted.

## Q2 — Summary judgment (8 marks)

> [!question] Question
> 2.1 Rule 32(1) lists four types of claim for which summary judgment is competent — what are they? 2.2 The affidavit must express an opinion on two issues — what are they? 2.3 Per Corbett JA in *Maharaj v Barclays National Ltd* 1976 (1) SA 418 (A) at 426, the court enquires into two matters before refusing summary judgment — what are they?

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 Claims on a liquid document; for a liquidated amount in money; for delivery of specified movable property; for ejectment.
> 2.2 That the defendant has no bona fide defence; and that the NID was delivered solely for the purpose of delay.
> 2.3 (i) Whether the defendant has fully disclosed the nature and grounds of the defence and the material facts founding it. (ii) Whether, on the facts disclosed, the defendant appears to have a defence — as to the whole or part of the claim — that is both bona fide and good in law.

Related: [[Rule 32 Judgment Procedure]]

## Q3 — Action vs application: six differences (6 marks)

> [!question] Question
> List 6 differences between the action and application procedure.

> [!success]- Answer
> Parties: plaintiff/defendant vs applicant/respondent. Factual disputes: action deals with substantial factual disputes; application only where no such dispute. Commencement: summons vs notice of motion + founding affidavit. Further process: further pleadings exchanged vs further affidavits exchanged. Trial prep: action has a "preparation for trial" stage; application does not. Conclusion: action ends in the trial court with oral evidence; application ends in motion court with no oral evidence/testimony in principle.

Related: [[Application Procedure and Notice of Motion]]

## Q4 — Urgent interdict against publication (12 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Mr Moon learns the Sunshine Daily will publish a defamatory, untrue article about him tomorrow. He instructs you to get a court order to stop them. 4.1 Draft the notice of motion (relief only). 4.2 List, in point form, what should be in the founding affidavit.

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 Order sought: (i) that non-compliance with the rules be condoned; (ii) that the application be treated as urgent; (iii) that Respondent be interdicted from publishing the defamatory information in the Sunshine Daily Newspaper; (iv) costs; (v) further and/or alternative relief.
> 4.2 Applicant's details; Respondent's details; that the court has jurisdiction; that Respondent is about to publish defamatory information about Applicant; that publication will cause irreparable harm; that Applicant has no other remedy but to approach the court; that the matter is urgent.

Related: [[Interdicts]], [[Application Procedure and Notice of Motion]]

## Q5 — Citation of parties under Rule 17 (9 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Cite, per Rule 17: 5.1 Daniel Steyn, born 1 December 2000, as plaintiff. 5.2 The Minister for Safety and Security as defendant. 5.3 The John Brown Trust (IT2000/08), trustees Gary Brown and Ian Long.

> [!success]- Answer
> 5.1 "The plaintiff is Daniel Steyn, a minor male scholar born on 1 December 2000, at [address], herein assisted by his father and natural guardian Pete Steyn, an adult attorney of the same address."
> 5.2 "The defendant is the Minister for Safety and Security, sued in his capacity as Minister, [Parliament address], whose address for these proceedings is that of the State Attorney, [address]."
> 5.3 "Gary Brown and Ian Long, in their capacities as trustees of the joint trust (IT2000/08)."

## Q6 — Special plea of prescription (5 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Plaintiff's combined summons claims motor vehicle damages from a collision on 7 March 2005; summons issued 18 August 2008, served 22 August 2008. Draft the special plea (wording only, no heading/ending, do not plead over the merits).

> [!success]- Answer
> "1. The cause of action sued upon arose on 7 March 2005. 2. The plaintiff's claim constitutes a debt as defined by the Prescription Act 68 of 1969. 3. The summons in this action was served on the defendant on 22 August 2008, that is more than three years after the claim arose. 4. In the premises, and by virtue of section 11 of the Prescription Act 68 of 1969, the plaintiff's claim has become prescribed. WHEREFORE the defendant prays for judgment in the defendant's favour with costs."
