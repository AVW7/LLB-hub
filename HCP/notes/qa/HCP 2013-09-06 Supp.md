## Q1 — Claim against a police officer and the Minister of Police (15 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Client wants to sue a police officer and the Minister of Police for damages from an unlawful assault. 1.1 Advise on preliminary statutory requirements (time periods, contents). 1.2 What must be alleged to hold the Minister liable? 1.3 Days for the Minister to enter appearance? 1.4 A part of the claim is for personal injury damages — what must be specified (Rule 18(10))? 1.5 Draft the preamble to the prayers.

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 No legal proceedings against an organ of state unless prior notice given or consent obtained (s3(1)–(2), Institution of Legal Proceedings against Certain Organs of State Act 40/2002); notice within 6 months of the debt arising; must set out the facts giving rise to the debt and particulars of it.
> 1.2 That the police officer acted within the course and scope of his employment.
> 1.3 20 court days.
> 1.4 Rule 18(10): date of birth; nature/extent of injuries; nature/effects/duration of disability; separately: medical/hospital/similar expenses; pain and suffering (temporary/permanent, injuries causing it); disability re earning of income (earnings lost to date and how made up, future estimated loss, future work capacity); enjoyment of amenities of life (temporary/permanent); disfigurement (full description, temporary/permanent).
> 1.5 Jointly and severally, the one to pay the other to be absolved.

## Q2 — Special plea of prescription (5 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Summons served 14 February 2013; cause of action (motor collision) arose 10 January 2010. Draft the special plea (wording only).

> [!success]- Answer
> The plaintiff's claim is based on a motor vehicle collision on 10 January 2010, which became due and payable then; the summons was served on 14 February 2013, more than 3 years after the collision/claim became due; the claim has therefore prescribed under the Prescription Act. Wherefore the defendant prays that the claim be dismissed with costs.

## Q3 — Summary judgment (12 marks)

> [!question] Question
> You act for the plaintiff; defendant enters appearance and you apply for summary judgment. 3.1 For which liquidated demands may summary judgment be applied? 3.2 Draft the applicant's affidavit (own facts, omit headings/attestation). 3.3 Briefly discuss defendant's two avenues to resist.

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 Claims based on liquid documents, liquidated amounts, delivery of specified movables, eviction/ejectment.
> 3.2 "I, the undersigned, [name], do hereby make oath and say: I am the plaintiff in this case. I have personal knowledge of the facts contained in the summons. I confirm the cause of action and the amount claimed in the summons. I verily believe the defendant has no bona fide defence to the action and has delivered notice of intention to defend solely for purposes of delay." (No additional evidence may be tendered.)
> 3.3 File an opposing affidavit setting out a bona fide defence; or furnish security for any order the court may make.

## Q4 — Exception (9 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Summons alleges plaintiff drove a Porsche; a collision occurred with defendant's BMW; plaintiff suffered R310,000 damages. Parties, jurisdiction and quantum may be assumed correctly pleaded. 4.1 On which two grounds can an exception be raised? 4.2 Draft the exception.

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 Vague and embarrassing; and failure to disclose a cause of action.
> 4.2 "NOTICE OF EXCEPTION — Please take notice that defendant hereby raises an exception against the plaintiff's particulars of claim in that it: fails to indicate that the plaintiff has locus standi, as there are no allegations that the plaintiff is the owner of the vehicle and thus personally sustained the damages claimed; it is not alleged that the damages were caused by the negligence of the defendant. WHEREFORE the plaintiff [sic — defendant] prays that the exception be upheld, that the plaintiff's particulars of claim be set aside with costs, and that the plaintiff be given leave, if so advised, to file amended particulars of claim within one month."

## Q5 — Interdict: action vs application, interim/final requirements (9 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Client Palmer bought Smith's farm; a week before occupation Smith's attorney refuses occupation, arguing occupational rental is a material/essential term missing from the contract. Palmer wants an interdict directing Smith to honour the agreement and allow occupation. 5.1 Action or application procedure — motivate. 5.2 Distinguish interim vs final interdict requirements. 5.3 Court finds a real, unforeseeable dispute of fact — what orders can it make? 5.4 Court finds the dispute of fact should have been foreseen — what does it order?

> [!success]- Answer
> 5.1 Both parties agree nothing was said about occupational rental; the dispute is whether occupational rental is (legally) a material/essential term — a legal dispute, so application procedure is used as quicker and cheaper.
> 5.2 Interim interdict: prima facie right; irreparable harm; balance of convenience favours applicant; no alternative remedy. Final interdict: a clear right; actual injury or threatened violation of rights; no alternative remedy.
> 5.3 If the dispute is simple, refer the matter for oral evidence; if comprehensive/complicated, refer to trial with appropriate directions on the exchange of pleadings.
> 5.4 The court may dismiss the application.
