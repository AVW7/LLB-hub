## Q1 — Six mistakes in a drafted summons (12 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Attorney X issues a High Court summons from North Gauteng (Pretoria) for client A (resides Pretoria) against P, a peregrinus (resides Durban), claiming vehicle damage and bodily injury damages from a collision in Durban. B, A's brother, owns the vehicle. P entered appearance to defend to delay, without a bona fide defence. X served/filed a declaration, then applied for summary judgment, and also required P to furnish security for costs. Identify 6 mistakes.

> [!success]- Answer
> 1. Locus standi (plaintiff) — A cannot claim for vehicle damage; he isn't the owner (B is).
> 2. Locus standi (defendant) — A cannot sue P for bodily injuries; must claim from the Road Accident Fund.
> 3. Jurisdiction — North Gauteng has no jurisdiction; defendant resides in Durban and the cause of action arose in Durban.
> 4. Irregular proceeding — wrong summons used: a simple summons + declaration for a damages claim, which should be a combined summons.
> 5. Irregular proceeding — summary judgment is not competent for a damages (unliquidated) claim.
> 6. A plaintiff cannot require a defendant to furnish security for costs.
>
> (Note to examiner: also accept — application for summary judgment made before the declaration should even be filed.)

## Q2 — Interlocutory application to compel discovery (23 marks)

> [!question] Question
> You act for the defendant in a defended High Court action, trial in 2 months. You served a Rule 35(1) notice on plaintiff's attorneys; no response, and a follow-up letter also unanswered. You advise your client to launch an interlocutory application.
> 2.1 What is an interlocutory application? 2.2 Short (Form 2) or long (Form 2A) notice of motion? 2.3 Who serves it? 2.4 On whom is it served? 2.5 Draft the form of order (omit heading/preamble/ending). 2.6 When may a party call for a specified document/tape after NID? 2.7 When are pleadings considered closed?

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 An interlocutory application does not deal with the merits, but a procedural aspect adjunct to the case.
> 2.2 Short Form (Form 2).
> 2.3 Defendant/Applicant's attorney.
> 2.4 On Plaintiff/Respondent's attorney (not on the Respondent/Plaintiff personally).
> 2.5 Order sought: (1) Plaintiff/Respondent directed to furnish the reply to the Rule 35(1) notice served/filed on [date], within 5 days of service of this order; (2) if non-compliant, Defendant/Applicant given leave to apply on these papers (supplemented as necessary), on 5 days' notice, for an order dismissing the claim, with costs; (3) Plaintiff/Respondent to pay the costs of this application; (4) further/alternative relief.
> Founding affidavit: attorney of record confirms personal knowledge; the Rule 35(1) Notice (Annexure A) was served/filed on stated dates; no response; a follow-up letter (Annexure B) also unanswered; the defendant is prejudiced by non-delivery of the discovery affidavit.
> 2.6 For purposes of drafting a plea.
> 2.7 Pleadings are closed under Rule 29: (a) if parties join issue without alleging new matter or adding pleadings; (b) if the last day for filing a replication/subsequent pleading has elapsed and it has not been filed; (c) if the parties agree in writing and file the agreement with the registrar.

## Q3 — Provisional sentence on a dishonoured cheque (15 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Client holds a cheque dated 12/2/2008 drawn by Smith Brothers on Standard Bank Bloemfontein for R180,000, deposited 13/2/2008, dishonoured 14/2/2008 for lack of funds. You decide to use provisional sentence.
> 3.1 What other summons/es could be used? 3.2 Defendant files an opposing affidavit — can you file a replying affidavit? 3.3 Define a liquid document. 3.4 Draft the cause of action. 3.5 Onus of proof in provisional sentence proceedings — discuss.

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 Simple summons or combined summons.
> 3.2 Yes.
> 3.3 A document evidencing unconditional liability for payment of a fixed or easily ascertainable amount of money, signed by the debtor or his agent.
> 3.4 R180,000 together with interest at 15,5% per annum a tempore morae, claimed as an amount due and payable by Defendant to Plaintiff by virtue of a cheque dated 12/2/2008 drawn by Defendant on Standard Bank of South Africa, Bloemfontein, in favour of Plaintiff, of which Plaintiff is the holder. The cheque was duly presented for payment and dishonoured for lack of funds. The cheque remains unpaid and in Plaintiff's possession. A copy is annexed marked "A".
> 3.5 If the defendant denies the liquidity of the document (e.g. authenticity of signature, or agent's authority), onus rests on the plaintiff. If the defendant relies on an underlying (merits) defence, onus rests on the defendant.
