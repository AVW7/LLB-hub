## Q1 — Full integrated scenario: contract, drafting, default judgment (42 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Mr Moeletsi Mahlomola contracts with Baagi Construction (Pty) Ltd to build his R950,000 house, paying R450,000 upfront. Four months pass with no real construction; Mahlomola cancels and demands his R450,000 back. A letter of demand (7 days, urgent sheriff delivery) goes unanswered.
> 1.1 Draft a complete particulars of claim for the summons against Baagi Construction. [20]
> 1.2 Days for Baagi Construction to file its NID? [1]
> 1.3 Days to file its plea after that? [1]
> 1.4 Draft a complete NID on behalf of Baagi Construction. [4]
> 1.5 Baagi Construction misses the plea deadline and is sent a notice. 1.5.1 What is this notice, and which rule allows it? [2] 1.5.2 Draft the notice (with citation and signature). [3] 1.5.3 What can Baagi Construction do to be allowed to plead, with the relevant rule number? [2]
> 1.6 Baagi Construction still fails to plead. Draft the application for default judgment. [9]

> [!note]- Self-check pointers (not an official memo)
> - 1.1 → [[Particulars of Claim and Rule 18 Requirements]] (breach of a building/construction contract — Rule 18(6) contract averments: written/oral, when/where/by whom concluded, copy attached; plead the breach, cancellation, and the restitutionary claim for the R450,000 paid)
> - 1.2 / 1.3 / 1.4 → [[Notice of Intention to Defend]] (standard dies: 10 court days for NID if within area; plea typically 20 days after NID; use the standard NID drafting checklist)
> - 1.5.1–1.5.3 → [[Plea, Special Plea, Replication and Close of Pleadings]] (notice of bar under Rule 26, 5 days; removal of the bar under Rule 27, on good cause)
> - 1.6 → [[Default Judgment and Rescission of Judgment]] (Rule 31 application — liquidated claim via the registrar, or set down before court if not liquidated; this claim, being for repayment of a fixed sum, should be capable of registrar's default judgment)

Related: [[Particulars of Claim and Rule 18 Requirements]], [[Notice of Intention to Defend]], [[Default Judgment and Rescission of Judgment]]

## Q2 — Acknowledgement of debt and summary judgment (26 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Baagi Construction signs an acknowledgement of debt for the R450,000, payable in two instalments; both are missed. Summons issued (with the AOD attached) is defended, though you consider it a delaying tactic. Summons served 16 April 2018.
> 2.1 What advice to obtain a speedy remedy and prevent delay? [2]
> 2.2 What is a liquid document, and is an AOD one? [2]
> 2.3 What is a liquidated claim, and does this claim qualify? [2]
> 2.4 Draft the application for summary judgment (notice of motion and affidavit). [20]

> [!note]- Self-check pointers
> - 2.1/2.4 → [[Rule 32 Judgment Procedure]] (summary judgment is the speedy remedy; use the standard affidavit template — deponent confirms personal knowledge, cause of action/amount, and that the defendant has no bona fide defence and defended solely to delay)
> - 2.2 → [[Provisional Sentence Procedure]] (a liquid document evidences unconditional liability for a fixed/easily ascertainable sum, signed by the debtor; an acknowledgement of debt qualifies)
> - 2.3 → a liquidated claim is one for a fixed or readily ascertainable sum of money; the R450,000 AOD claim qualifies, supporting summary judgment under Rule 32(1)

Related: [[Rule 32 Judgment Procedure]], [[Provisional Sentence Procedure]]
