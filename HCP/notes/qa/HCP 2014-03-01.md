## Q1 — Rescission of judgment following a sale-in-execution notice (10 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Bafana Builders CC's managing director shows you a newspaper extract with a notice of sale in execution of their immovable property, set for two months' time. Investigation shows all documents were served at their accountants' offices. Draft the notice of motion for rescission of judgment (own facts where necessary).

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 Notice of Motion: "KINDLY TAKE NOTICE that application will be made ... for an Order in the following terms: 1. That the late filing of this application be condoned; 2. That the judgment entered against the Applicant/Defendant on or about [date] be rescinded; 3. That the Applicant/Defendant [opposing party] be ordered to pay the costs of this application in the event they oppose; 4. Further and/or alternative relief. BE FURTHER PLEASED TO TAKE NOTICE that the affidavit by [deponent] is attached in support of the application."
> 1.2 Circumstances for default judgment (context): where the defendant is in default with the NID; where the defendant failed to plead after a notice of bar; where the defendant failed to deliver a declaration; where the plaintiff or defendant failed to appear at the hearing.

Related: [[Default Judgment and Rescission of Judgment]]

## Q2 — Provisional sentence summons on a dishonoured cheque (15 marks)

> [!question] Question
> XYZ Builders is owed R350,000 by Mala Constructions CC — a cheque was returned "refer to drawer" and dishonoured. 2.1 Draft the provisional sentence summons for issuing by the Registrar. 2.2 List documents for Counsel's brief on an opposed claim for provisional sentence.

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 "INFORM Mala Construction CC ... (defendant) that they are hereby called upon immediately to pay XYZ Builders ... (plaintiff) R350,000 together with interest at 15,5% per annum from [date], claimed for a cheque drawn by the plaintiff [sic, likely defendant] in favour of the defendant which was returned by the bank marked 'refer to the drawer', for supply of goods, copy annexed; that failing such payment they are called upon to appear before this Court personally or represented by an advocate or an attorney with right of appearance, at [date/time], to admit or deny liability; that if they deny liability, they shall not later than noon on [date] file an affidavit with the registrar and serve a copy on plaintiff's attorney, setting forth the grounds of their defence, stating whether they admit or deny the claim. AND INFORM the defendant further that in the event of non-payment and failure to file such affidavit and appear, provisional sentence may be granted against them with costs and interest."
> 2.2 Documents for counsel's brief: copy of the summons + supporting documents; copy of the return of service; the original liquid document; copy of the defendant's answering affidavit; copy of the plaintiff's affidavit; copy of the notice of set down.

## Q3 — Rule 35(1) discovery notice and close of pleadings (8 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Trial is set down, pleadings closed. 3.1 Draft a Rule 35(1) notice (own facts). 3.2 When are pleadings considered closed?

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 "PLAINTIFF'S NOTICE IN TERMS OF RULE 35(1) — TAKE NOTICE that in terms of Rule 35(1) the Plaintiff requires the Defendant to make discovery on oath within TWENTY (20) days of service hereof, of all documents and tape recordings relating to any matter in question in this action (whether arising between Plaintiff and Defendant or not), which are or have at any time been in the Defendant's possession or control."
> 3.2 Rule 29: (1) if parties join issue without alleging new matter or adding pleadings; (2) if the last day for a replication/subsequent pleading has lapsed unfiled; (3) if the parties agree in writing, filed with the registrar; (4) if the parties cannot agree and the court, on application, declares pleadings closed.

Related: [[Discovery]], [[Plea, Special Plea, Replication and Close of Pleadings]]

## Q4 — Options after a stalled simple summons; appeal vs review; special pleas; Rule 4 true/false (18 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Defendant was served with a simple summons and entered an NID; plaintiff neither delivered a declaration nor applied for summary judgment. 4.1 What options does the defendant have? 4.2 Explain the difference between appeal and review. 4.3 Identify six instances when a special plea would be appropriate. 4.4 True/false, Rule 4 service: 4.4.1 at the chosen domicilium; 4.4.2 on a duly authorised agent; 4.4.3 at the Mayor's residential address where a municipality is defendant; 4.4.4 at a company director's residential address where the company has a registered address elsewhere; 4.4.5 on a partner at his/her residential address, partnership has no registered address; 4.4.6 personally on each trustee of an inter vivos trust at their business addresses. 4.5 When is the combined summons used?

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 The defendant may serve a notice of bar requiring delivery of the declaration within 5 days; or may apply for absolution from the instance, or, after adducing evidence, apply for judgment.
> 4.2 An appeal is (subject to court orders) normally a rehearing on the merits, limited to the evidence/information on which the decision was given. Review concerns decisions of lower courts/tribunals brought into question in higher courts where grave irregularities are found in those proceedings.
> 4.3 Lis pendens; arbitration; non-joinder; prescription; res judicata; absence of jurisdiction.
> 4.4 4.4.1 True. 4.4.2 True. 4.4.3 False. 4.4.4 False. 4.4.5 True. 4.4.6 True.
> 4.5 A combined summons is used in all cases where the action procedure is appropriate (i.e. not a liquidated/debt claim suited to a simple summons).
