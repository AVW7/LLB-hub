## Q1 — Dies to enter appearance to defend (5 marks)

> [!question] Question
> X's North Gauteng summons is served on Y. State the dies for Y to serve/file NID: 1.1 Y resides in the North Gauteng area. 1.2 Y resides outside the area but within 160km of the seat. 1.3 Distinguish how the two periods are calculated. 1.4 Y is cited in his official capacity as Minister of Justice.

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 10 days / 10 court days. 1.2 14 days / 14 calendar days. 1.3 Court days (1.1) exclude Saturdays, Sundays, public holidays (per the Rules); calendar days (1.2) are calculated per the Interpretation Act and include weekends/public holidays. 1.4 20 days / 20 court days.

## Q2 — Notice to organs of state (5 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Under the Institution of Legal Proceedings against Certain State Organs Act 40/2002: 2.1 Within what period from the debt arising must notice be given? 2.2 What must the notice set out? 2.3 How many days must lapse after the notice before process may be served? 2.4 Does the Act apply to contractual debts?

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 6 months. 2.2 The facts giving rise to the debt, and such particulars of the debt as are within the creditor's knowledge. 2.3 30 days. 2.4 Yes.

## Q3 — Summary judgment (12 marks)

> [!question] Question
> You act for the plaintiff; defendant enters appearance; you apply for summary judgment. 3.1 For which liquidated demands may summary judgment be applied for? 3.2 Draft the applicant's supporting affidavit (own facts, omit headings/attestation). 3.3 Briefly discuss the defendant's two avenues to resist.

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 Claims based on liquid documents, liquidated amounts, delivery of specified movables, and eviction/ejectment.
> 3.2 "I, the undersigned, [name], do hereby make oath and say: I am the plaintiff in this case. I have personal knowledge of the facts contained in the summons. I confirm the cause of action and the amount claimed in the summons. I verily believe the defendant has no bona fide defence to the action and has delivered notice of intention to defend solely for purposes of delay." (Note: no additional evidence may be tendered.)
> 3.3 File an opposing affidavit setting out a bona fide defence; or furnish security for any order the court may make (e.g. amount claimed plus costs).

Related: [[Rule 32 Judgment Procedure]]

## Q4 — Rule 18 pleading requirements (9 marks)

> [!question] Question
> 4.1 Improve "The parties entered into a written agreement" per Rule 18's contract-averment requirements. 4.2 What necessary detail must be pleaded for a delict claim under Rule 18?

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 "On [date] and at [place] the plaintiff (acting personally) and the defendant (duly represented by [name]) entered into a written agreement, a copy of which is attached hereto marked Annexure 'A'."
> 4.2 The delict complained of; the date when, the place where, and the manner in which the delict was committed; and the amount of damages claimed.

Related: [[Particulars of Claim and Rule 18 Requirements]]

## Q5 — Anton Piller order averments (5 marks)

> [!question] Question
> An Anton Piller order is obtained by rule nisi on an ex parte application to preserve evidence. What must the applicant's affidavit aver?

> [!success]- Answer
> That the applicant has a cause of action it wishes to pursue against the respondent; that the respondent possesses specific and specified documents/things constituting vital evidence; and that there is a real and well-founded fear that this evidence may be hidden, destroyed or lost before the matter reaches court, or the discovery stage.

Related: [[Interdicts]]

## Q6 — Special plea of prescription (5 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Summons served 14 February 2011; cause of action (motor collision) arose 10 January 2008. Draft the special plea (omit heading/plaintiff-defendant attorney particulars).

> [!success]- Answer
> The plaintiff's claim is based on a motor vehicle collision on 10 January 2008 and became due and payable then; the summons was served on 14 February 2011, more than 3 years after the collision/claim became due; the claim has therefore prescribed in terms of the Prescription Act. Wherefore the defendant prays that the claim be dismissed with costs.

## Q7 — Formal objection to a defective summons (9 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Combined summons alleges plaintiff drove a Porsche, a collision occurred with defendant's BMW, and plaintiff suffered R310,000 damages. Parties, jurisdiction and quantum may be assumed correctly pleaded. 7.1 Can defendant raise a formal objection? Explain briefly. 7.2 Draft the necessary notice/application/pleading.

> [!success]- Answer
> 7.1 Yes — an exception can be raised on the basis that the particulars of claim do not disclose a cause of action.
> 7.2 "NOTICE OF EXCEPTION — Please take notice that defendant hereby raises an exception against the plaintiff's particulars of claim in that it: fails to indicate that the plaintiff has locus standi, as there are no allegations that the plaintiff is the owner of the vehicle and thus personally sustained the damages claimed; it is not alleged that the damages were caused by the negligence of the defendant. WHEREFORE the plaintiff [sic — defendant] prays that the exception be upheld, that the plaintiff's particulars of claim be set aside with costs, and that the plaintiff be given leave, if so advised, to file amended particulars of claim within one month."

Related: [[Exception, Irregular Proceedings and Striking Out]]
