# Joinder, Third Party Procedure and Interpleader

## Joinder — Rule 10

- All parties with a **direct and substantial interest** in the relief that may be granted must be joined.
- Multiple plaintiffs may sue one defendant even on **different causes of action**.
- Multiple defendants may be joined where the same question of law or fact arises — relief may be sought jointly, jointly and severally, separately, or in the alternative.
- **Non-joinder**: if a necessary party was *not* joined, raise it by **special plea** — see [[Plea, Special Plea, Replication and Close of Pleadings]].
- **Misjoinder**: if a party was joined when they should not have been, raise it as a **point in limine**.
- Costs consequences: an unsuccessfully joined plaintiff may have to pay the other plaintiff's and defendant's costs; where a defendant is unsuccessfully joined, the successful defendant's costs are generally borne pro rata by the unsuccessful parties.

## Intervention — Rule 12

Any person entitled to be joined as a plaintiff, or liable to be joined as a defendant, may apply to **intervene** in existing proceedings (e.g. a major creditor intervening in an insolvency matter where existing creditors gain no advantage from the current process). Notice to all parties required.

## Third party procedure — Rule 13

Used where:
1. A **third party is or may be liable to contribute to**, or **indemnify**, a party to the action (Rule 13(1)(a)); or
2. A **substantially similar question or issue** has arisen between a party and the third party as between the existing parties (Rule 13(1)(b)).

Mechanics:
- **Third Party Notice** (Form 7) — must set out the nature of the claim, the grounds of claim, the issue to be determined, and the relief sought; the rules on pleadings apply to it.
- Copies of all pleadings delivered to date must be served on the third party (with the notice); if pleadings have already closed, the court's consent is required to deliver a third party notice.
- The third party, if it intends to contest, must deliver its own **notice of intention to defend**.
- **Default judgment is not possible against a third party** — *Steyn v P Steyn* Case No 3761/05 (WLD).
- The third party may plead, except, or otherwise contest; may counterclaim against the party who issued the notice (but not against any other party); may apply for separation of trials.
- The **lis** between the party issuing the notice and the third party is distinct from the lis between the original parties; where the third party's liability turns only on apportionment of fault, the remedy is limited to a **declaratory order** (not a money judgment) — read with s2 of the Apportionment of Damages Act 34 of 1956.
- **Alternative**: instead of third party procedure, a defendant may institute **separate proceedings** against the third party and apply for **consolidation** — *MCC Contracts (Pty) Ltd v Coertzen* 1998 (4) SA 1046 (SCA).

### Drafting checklist — Third Party Notice (structure, from the memos)

> On [date], plaintiff sued defendant for R[x] for damages arising from [cause of action, e.g. a motor collision]. A copy of the summons and particulars of claim is attached, marked "A".
>
> On [date], defendant filed a plea denying sole responsibility. A copy of the plea is attached, marked "B".
>
> The collision/loss was caused by the sole negligence of the Third Party, who [set out grounds, e.g. failed to keep a proper lookout; failed to observe a red traffic light].
>
> WHEREFORE defendant claims against the third party an order that, in the event of plaintiff succeeding against defendant, the third party indemnify defendant in such amount as defendant is ordered to pay, together with the costs of defending the action.

## Interpleader — Rule 58

Used where a stakeholder holds money/property claimed adversely by two or more people and wishes the court to decide who is entitled to it.

### Drafting checklist — Interpleader Notice (structure, from the memos)

> Please take notice that [Applicant/stakeholder] has today paid into court, in terms of Rule 58, an amount of R[x] to which First and Second Claimants are making adverse claims.
>
> The applicant calls upon the First and Second Claimants to deliver particulars of their claims within 15 days of service of these papers on them.
>
> The applicant will apply to court for a decision on liability/validity of the respective claims on a date to be fixed by the registrar, not being less than 15 days from the date for delivery of claims.

The supporting affidavit must aver: the applicant has **no interest** in the subject matter of the dispute (beyond charges/costs); does **not collude** with any claimant; is **willing to act** as the court directs; and has **paid the money to the registrar**.
