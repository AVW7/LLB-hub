# Exception, Irregular Proceedings and Striking Out

Three distinct procedural attacks on a pleading — the exam frequently expects candidates to identify *which* remedy fits the defect described.

## Exception — Rule 23

- Attacks a pleading as **inherently defective** — either (a) **vague and embarrassing**, or (b) **fails to disclose a cause of action / defence**.
- Purpose: to bring the matter to an end (or force an amendment) without going to trial.
- If the complaint is that the pleading is **vague and embarrassing**, the excipient must first give **15 days' notice** to remove the cause of complaint before excepting.
- Procedure: opposed motion.

### Drafting checklist — Notice of Exception (from the memos)

> Please take notice that defendant hereby raises an exception against the plaintiff's particulars of claim in that it:
> - [ground 1, e.g. fails to indicate that plaintiff has locus standi as there are no allegations that plaintiff is the owner of the vehicle and thus personally sustained the damages claimed]
> - [ground 2, e.g. it is not alleged that the damages were caused by the negligence of the defendant]
>
> WHEREFORE the defendant prays that the exception be upheld, that the plaintiff's particulars of claim be set aside with costs, and that plaintiff be given leave, if so advised, to file amended particulars of claim within one month.

Two recurring grounds tested together: **no locus standi pleaded** (ownership of the damaged property not alleged) and **no negligence pleaded** (delict claim lacking the fault element).

## Irregular proceeding / irregular step — Rule 30

- Any **procedural** irregularity (as opposed to a substantive defect in the pleading itself) — e.g. non-compliance with Rule 18, premature enrolment, irregular notice of bar, an unserved summons, non-compliance with time limits (Rule 30A).
- Procedure: within **10 days** of becoming aware of the irregular step, and **before taking any further step** in the proceedings, give written notice affording the opponent an opportunity to remove the cause of complaint. If not removed, apply to court (5 days' further notice) for an order — typically striking out the offending step.
- **Rule 30A**: failure to comply with rules/time limits generally (e.g. failure to deliver further particulars, failure to discover) — give 10 days' notice to compel; if not complied with, apply to court for appropriate relief (which can include dismissal of the claim or defence).

## Notice to strike out — Rule 23(2)

- Attacks only **certain allegations** within an otherwise valid pleading — allegations that are **scandalous, vexatious or irrelevant**.
- Applicant must show **prejudice**.
- By application, within the period allowed for the next pleading.

## Comparative summary

| Remedy | Target | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| Exception (Rule 23) | Whole pleading | Vague/embarrassing, or no cause of action/defence |
| Notice to strike out (Rule 23(2)) | Specific allegations | Scandalous, vexatious, irrelevant (+ prejudice) |
| Rule 30 irregular step | A procedural step | Rules 17/18 not complied with, premature enrolment, bad notice of bar, unserved process |
| Rule 30A | Non-compliance generally | Failure to comply with rules/time limits (further particulars, discovery) |
