# Summons Types and the Essentialia

Picking the *wrong* summons is the single most common "spot the mistake" trap in this module.

## The three types

| Type | Used for | Form | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple summons** | Debt/liquidated demand only | Form 9 | Rule 17(2)(b) — in the High Court you **must** ("shall") choose the simple summons for liquidated claims (Magistrates' Court "may") |
| **Combined summons** | Everything else (unliquidated/damages claims, delict, etc.) | Form 10 | Particulars of claim attached at issue — no separate declaration stage |
| **Provisional sentence summons** | Claim on a **liquid document** | — | Fast-tracked procedure; see [[Provisional Sentence Procedure]] |

- A **simple summons** is followed, if defended, by a **declaration** (Rule 20) — not by "particulars of claim" attached at the outset.
- Simple summons is competent only for: payment of a debt, delivery, transfer, eviction, cancellation of a contract. **Not competent** for divorce or a declaratory order.
- Classic drafting error tested repeatedly: using a simple summons + declaration for a **damages claim** (delict) — this must be a **combined summons** from the start, because damages are not a liquidated demand. See [[HCP 2010-03-06]] Q1 and [[HCP 2010-03-18 Supp]] Q1.

## Essentialia of a summons/particulars of claim

Every summons/particulars of claim must, at minimum, plead:
1. **Parties** — correctly cited (see [[Parties, Citation and Legal Capacity to Litigate]]).
2. **Cause of action** — all factual allegations necessary to found the claim; an allegation not pleaded cannot be relied on at trial.
3. **Relief claimed** (the prayer).
4. **Jurisdiction** — the basis on which the court has jurisdiction.

If any allegation is not made in the summons it cannot be considered at trial — plead **all** legal requirements and necessary factual allegations.

## Cause-of-action drafting patterns (from the memos)

- **Cheque/liquid document claim**: "R[x] together with interest thereon at [rate]% per annum a tempore morae, claimed by the Plaintiff as being an amount due and payable by Defendant to Plaintiff by virtue of a cheque dated [date] drawn by Defendant on [bank] in favour of the Plaintiff and of which Plaintiff is the holder. The said cheque was duly presented for payment ... where same was dishonoured by non-payment as a result of lack of funds ... A copy of the said cheque is annexed hereto marked 'A'."
- **Sale of goods claim**: "payment of the sum of R[x], being the purchase price that is due, owing and payable by the defendant to the plaintiff for certain goods sold and delivered by the plaintiff to the defendant during or about [date] in terms of an agreement concluded between the parties within this Honourable Court's jurisdiction during or about [date] and which sum, despite demand, remains unpaid."
- **Preamble to the prayer where multiple defendants sued jointly** (delict, joint wrongdoers): "Plaintiff claims against First and Second Defendant, jointly and severally, the one to pay the other to be absolved, payment of: [amount] / interest at [x]% per annum a tempore morae / costs / further and alternative relief."

## Formal requirements for documents filed (practice-directive level, but examined)

- Typed/printed in black ink on white A4 paper, double spacing, one side only, font ≥12pt, margin ≥35mm.
- Left-hand lower corner of the first page: name, telephone number, email of the legal representative (or litigant in person).
- Non-A4 documents must be gummed to A4.
- Registrar may refuse non-compliant documents.
