# Service of Process and Edictal Citation

## Ordinary service — Rule 4

- Effected **by the sheriff**.
- Between **7am and 7pm**; only a warrant of arrest may be served on a Sunday.
- If the sheriff cannot effect service, apply to court for assistance/directions on an alternative mode of service.
- **Rule 4A** — service of documents *after* the summons (i.e. once the attorney of record is on the record) may be by email or fax, or hand delivery/registered post.
- True/false traps tested in the exams (from [[HCP 2014-03-01]] Q4.4):
  - Service at the defendant's **chosen domicilium** — **true**.
  - Service on a duly authorised agent (written authority to accept service) — **true**.
  - Service at the **residential address of the Mayor** where a municipality is defendant — **false** (must be on the municipality itself/its registered address).
  - Service at the **residential address of a director** where the company has a registered address elsewhere — **false** (serve at the registered address).
  - Service on **a partner** at his/her residential address, where the partnership has no registered address — **true**.
  - Personal service on **each trustee** of an inter vivos trust at their respective business addresses — **true**.

## Days of grace to enter appearance (dies)

- Defendant resident within the court's area: **10 court days**.
- Defendant resident outside the area but within 150km of the seat of the court: **14 calendar days** (calculated per the Interpretation Act — includes weekends/public holidays, unlike "court days" which exclude them).
- Defendant cited in an **official capacity** (e.g. a Minister): **20 (court) days**.
- Defendant is **the State or a Province**: **20 court days** (Rule 19(2)).
- Defendant more than 150km from the seat of the court: **one month**; less than 150km: **two weeks** — "normal days" (Rule 24 old numbering — confirm against current Uniform Rules of Court text in use).
- **Dies non**: 16 December – 15 January does not count.

## Substituted service and edictal citation

- Where the defendant's whereabouts are **unknown**, or the defendant is **outside South Africa**, ordinary personal service is not possible — the plaintiff applies to court (Rule 5) for leave to serve by an alternative method.
- **Edictal citation** (service **outside** the Republic): court must be satisfied of the grounds for jurisdiction, informed of the defendant's likely whereabouts, and directs the manner of service — often via the Department of International Relations to the SA embassy in the relevant country, with the summons translated into the local language; some countries permit direct service by a local attorney.
- **Substituted service** (defendant untraceable, but believed to be **within** South Africa): applicant must show attempts made to trace the defendant; court directs a manner of service (e.g. publication in a specified newspaper) reasonably likely to bring it to the defendant's attention — an unusual modern example accepted was service via Facebook (*CMC Woodworking Machinery / Pieter Odendaal Kitchens*).
- The application (Form 1-type affidavit) must set out: nature and extent of the claim, grounds of claim, grounds for jurisdiction, the suggested manner of service, and information on the defendant's whereabouts (or last known whereabouts and enquiries made).
- The court's order must state the manner of service and the time allowed for the defendant to respond.

## Pre-litigation notice to organs of state

- **Institution of Legal Proceedings against Certain Organs of State Act 40 of 2002**: no legal proceedings may be instituted against an organ of state unless (a) prior written notice has been given, or (b) the organ of state has consented in writing.
- Notice must be given **within 6 months** of the debt arising.
- Notice must set out the **facts giving rise to the debt** and particulars of the debt.
- Once given, a further period (commonly cited as **30 days**) must elapse before process may be served.
- Applies even to **contractual** debts against the state.
