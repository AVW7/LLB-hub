# Parties, Citation and Legal Capacity to Litigate

Rule 17 governs how parties are described (cited) in a summons/pleading. Recurring drafting question — same handful of scenarios (minor, state functionary, trust, CC, company) tested near-verbatim across sittings.

## General citation requirements

- Name or initials and surname of plaintiff and defendant.
- Address of residence or work.
- Occupation, if known.
- Sex; if female, marital status.
- If suing in a representative capacity, state that capacity.
- If the action is based on a contract — state oral or written, when, where and by whom concluded, copy attached.

## Standard citation formulas (memorised from the memos)

- **Minor**: "The plaintiff is [Name], a minor [male/female] [occupation e.g. scholar] born on [DOB], at [address], herein assisted by his/her [father/mother/guardian] and natural guardian [Name], an adult [occupation] of the same address."
- **State functionary sued in official capacity**: "The defendant is the Minister for Safety and Security, sued in his capacity as Minister, [Department address], and whose address for purposes of these proceedings is that of the State Attorney, [address]."
- **Trust**: cite the **trustees** in their capacities as trustees of the named trust (with its IT reference number) — not the trust itself: "[Trustee A] and [Trustee B], in their capacities as trustees of the [Name] Trust (IT[xxxx]/[yy])."
- **Close corporation**: "[Name] CC, CK[xx/xxxx], a close corporation duly registered in terms of the Close Corporations Act, with registered address at [address]."
- **Company**: "[Name] (Pty) Ltd, a company duly registered and incorporated in accordance with the company laws of the Republic of South Africa, carrying on business as [trade name] with its principal place of business within this Honourable Court's jurisdiction at [address]."
- **Sole proprietor trading under a name**: cite the natural person, "carrying on business as [trade name]".

## Capacity to litigate (locus standi in judicio) — who can/can't sue or be sued

- General test: **capacity to sue or be sued**, plus a **direct and substantial interest** in the right that is the subject matter of the litigation.
- **Judges of the High Court** — cannot be sued for judicial acts (subject to narrow exceptions).
- **Members/officers of Parliament** — may only be *sued* in Cape Town; may *sue* anywhere.
- **Diplomats** — immune from civil and criminal proceedings.
- **Fugitives from justice** — cannot sue (subject to exceptions: between spouses, in their profession, for delictual damages); can be sued.
- **Alien enemies** — cannot sue while resident in an enemy country; can be sued.
- **Married persons** — since the Matrimonial Property Act, spouses generally litigate independently; written consent of the other spouse required only in limited circumstances.
- **Minors** — 18 years is majority; represented by a guardian, or by a **curator ad litem** in litigation where appointed (common for legal practitioners to be appointed curator ad litem and then conduct the litigation themselves; the Legal Aid Board can act as curator ad litem for children it represents) — see *Legal Aid Board v Four Children* (2011) ZASCA 39.
- **Curator bonis** — administers a person's property/affairs generally (mentally disordered person, interdicted prodigal).
- **Unrehabilitated insolvent** — diminished locus standi.

## Rule 17 signature/address requirements for the summons itself

- Physical address within **15 km** of the court, plus email and fax.
- Signed by the attorney or the plaintiff personally.
- If a **combined summons**, also signed by an advocate, or an attorney with right of appearance in the High Court.
- An attorney's right to sign pleadings **qua attorney** is limited to the division in which admitted/enrolled; the right of appearance certificate extends the right to sign **qua advocate** in all divisions — *Zeda Car Leasing v Pillay* 2007 (3) SA 89 D and CLD; *Liberty Group Ltd v Singh* 2012 (5) SA 526 (KZD).
