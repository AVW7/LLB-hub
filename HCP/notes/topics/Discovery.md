# Discovery — Rule 35

## The notice to discover — Rule 35(1)

- Available **after close of pleadings**.
- Calls on the other party to make discovery on oath **within 20 days**.
- Reply must be by **affidavit**, in the prescribed form (Form 11).

### Drafting checklist — Rule 35(1) notice (from the memos)

> TAKE NOTICE that in terms of Rule 35(1) the Plaintiff requires the Defendant to make discovery on oath within TWENTY (20) days of service hereof, of all documents and tape recordings relating to any matter in question in this action (whether such matter is one arising between the Plaintiff and Defendant or not), which are or have at any time been in the possession or control of the Defendant.

## If discovery is inadequate or ignored

- **Rule 35(3)** — notice for **specific documents** believed to be in the other party's possession but not discovered; the other party must make them available for inspection, or state on oath (within 10 days) that the documents are not in its possession (and, if known, their whereabouts).
- **Rule 35(4)** — a party who fails to discover after receiving notice may **not use** the undiscovered document at trial.
- **Rule 35(7)** — if a party still does not discover, the other party may apply to **compel** discovery, and, if non-compliance continues, apply to have the claim or defence **dismissed/struck out**.
- **Rule 35(8)** — notice to specify particulars of dates/parties to a document within 15 days.
- **Rule 35(9)** — notice to admit a document (properly executed, what it purports to be) within 10 days — if not admitted, the document must be proved at trial, and the non-admitting party can be ordered to pay the costs of proof.
- **Rule 35(10)** — notice to produce the original at trial, given 5 days before trial (no witness necessary).
- **Rule 35(12)** — notice (Form 15) to inspect/copy any document or tape referred to in an opponent's pleadings/affidavits; non-compliance again means the document may not be used.

### Application to compel discovery — structure

Where a Rule 35(1) notice is served and ignored, the aggrieved party applies (typically by way of an **interlocutory application** — see [[Application Procedure and Notice of Motion]]) for an order:
1. directing the defaulting party to comply with the Rule 35(1) notice within a stated period (e.g. 5 days of the court order);
2. providing that, on continued non-compliance, the applicant may apply — on the same papers, supplemented if necessary, and on 5 days' notice — for an order dismissing the claim/striking the defence, with costs;
3. costs of the application against the defaulting party.

## Rule 36 — experts, plans, photographs, medical examination

- **Expert witness**: notice not later than **15 days** before the hearing of the intention to call the expert; not later than **10 days** before the hearing, supply a **summary** of the expert's proposed evidence. (These two notices may be combined.)
- **Medical examination**: a party may be required, on notice, to submit to a medical examination by a practitioner nominated by the other party — used where the integrity/independence of the opponent's own medical expert is in doubt.
- **Photographs/plans**: notice not less than **10 days** before the hearing of intention to use them at trial.

## What counts as a "document" for discovery

High Court Rule 35 covers documents and **tape recordings**; note that the Magistrates' Court rule is broader on its face (documents and tape, electronic, digital or other recordings including sound track, film, magnetic tape, record) — worth knowing the distinction is sometimes tested. Also be aware of **metadata, residual data and replicant data** as categories of electronically stored information potentially discoverable.
