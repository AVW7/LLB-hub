# Default Judgment and Rescission of Judgment

## When default judgment is applied for — Rule 31

- No Notice of Intention to Defend delivered.
- Failed to deliver a plea (after being barred).
- Failed to deliver a declaration (after being barred).
- Plaintiff or defendant failed to appear at the hearing.

## Procedure

- The **Registrar** grants default judgment where the debt is **liquidated** (administrative process — Form A application, Form B draft order).
- Where the amount is **not liquidated**, the matter must be **brought before the court** (a set-down notice is required; no need to serve the set-down if no NID was filed).
- Under the National Credit Act, an affidavit addressing compliance with s130(3) may be required with the application for judgment (see relevant Practice Notes).

## Rescission of default judgment — Rule 31(2)(b) and Rule 42

- A defendant against whom default judgment was granted may apply to have it **set aside**, within (commonly) **20 days of acquiring knowledge of the judgment**.
- Must show **good cause** — typically: a reasonable explanation for the default, a bona fide defence, and that the application is made in good faith (not to delay).

### Drafting checklist — Notice of Motion for rescission (from the memos, condonation + rescission combined)

> KINDLY TAKE NOTICE that application will be made ... for an Order in the following terms:
> 1. That the late filing of this application be condoned;
> 2. That the judgment as entered against the Applicant/Defendant on or about [date] be rescinded;
> 3. That the Applicant/Defendant [opposing party] be ordered to pay the costs of this application in the event they oppose;
> 4. Further and/or alternative relief.
>
> BE FURTHER PLEASED TO TAKE NOTICE that the affidavit by [deponent] is attached in support of the application.

- Common trigger fact pattern tested: the defendant only learns of the default judgment (and an imminent **sale in execution**) from a newspaper notice of sale — because the summons/judgment documents were served at an outdated address (e.g. former accountants). This grounds both the *condonation* for late rescission application and the *rescission* itself.

## Judgment where declaration not delivered

- **Notice of bar (Rule 26)**: give 5 days' notice of intention to apply for judgment if the declaration is not delivered.
- If the amount is not liquidated, the matter must still be brought before the court — a set-down notice is required.

## Reconsideration of a judgment

- Any party dissatisfied with a judgment obtained by default/in the party's absence may, within 20 days, set the matter down for **reconsideration by the court** (Rule 31(5)(d) — confirm number against the Rules in force at the time of the exam year).

## Related: warrant of execution / sale in execution (trigger for many rescission scenarios)

- A **warrant of execution** must fully describe the property (situation/address).
- Must be served on the owner, the Registrar of Deeds, and the occupier (Rule 4).
- **Notice of sale**: obtained from the sheriff; may not be less than **one month** after the warrant was served; must describe the property, situation, street address, time/place of sale, and note that conditions of sale may be inspected at the sheriff's office.
- The **conditions of sale** (Form 21) bind the purchaser and protect the execution creditor, sheriff, auctioneer and the auctioneer's attorney.
- Advertising: execution creditor must advertise in a local newspaper **not less than 15 days** before the sale (High Court Rule 45(7)(a)) — this newspaper notice is exactly what alerts an unaware judgment debtor in the recurring rescission fact pattern above.
