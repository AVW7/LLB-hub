# Particulars of Claim and Rule 18 Requirements

The most heavily and repeatedly tested drafting topic in the module — appears in nearly every sitting in some form (full drafting, spot-the-defect, or an exception grounded in non-compliance).

## Rule 18 general requirements

- Pleadings must be **divided into paragraphs** (including sub-paragraphs where convenient); each distinct averment gets its **own paragraph**.
- Must contain a **clear and concise statement of the material facts** relied on, in sufficient detail for the opponent to plead.
- **Rule 18(5)** — a denial must not be evasive; the pleader must answer the point of substance.
- **Rule 18(6)** — reliance on a **contract**: state whether written or oral; when, where and by whom concluded; if written, attach a copy.
- **Rule 18(9)** — a party claiming a division/transfer/forfeiture in divorce must give details of the grounds.
- **Rule 18(10)** — a plaintiff suing for **damages for personal injury or death** must specify:
  - date of birth (of the deceased, if a death claim);
  - nature and extent of the injuries;
  - nature, effects and duration of the disability;
  - and **separately quantify**: medical/hospital/similar expenses; pain and suffering (state temporary/permanent); disability re earning of income (earnings lost to date, how the amount is made up, future estimated loss); loss of amenities of life; disfigurement (full description, temporary/permanent).
- **Rule 18(11)** — damages for death of another: must state the deceased's date of birth.
- **Rule 18(12)** — non-compliance with Rule 17 or 18 is deemed an **irregular step**, actionable under **Rule 30**.

## Consequence of non-compliance

A summons/pleading that doesn't comply with Rule 18 is an **irregular proceeding** (Rule 30). The aggrieved party must, within 10 days of becoming aware of it (and before taking any further step), give written notice affording the opponent an opportunity to remove the cause of complaint; if not remedied within a further 10 days (some memos say 15 days for particulars-of-claim defects specifically — check the applicable time limit against current Rules), the complainant applies to court to have the pleading struck out as an irregular step.

### Drafting checklist — Notice in terms of Rule 30 (from the memos)

> BE PLEASED TO TAKE NOTICE that Application will be made to the above Honourable Court on [date] at 10:00 or so soon thereafter as Applicant's Counsel may be heard for an Order in the following terms:
> 1. Striking out the Plaintiff's Summons as an irregular proceeding in terms of the provisions of Rule 30.
> 2. Costs of this Application.
> 3. Further and/or alternative relief.
> KINDLY ENROL the matter accordingly.

## What a declaration (following a simple summons) must set out

1. The nature of the claim.
2. The conclusions of law the plaintiff is entitled to deduce from the facts stated.
3. A prayer for the relief claimed.

## Pre-litigation notice requirements folded into drafting

Before suing an **organ of state**, statutory notice under the Institution of Legal Proceedings against Certain Organs of State Act must be given and pleaded as complied with — see [[Service of Process and Edictal Citation]]. Similarly, claims under the National Credit Act must allege compliance with sections 129–131.

## Common defects tested (compiled from marking memos)

- No allegation of negligence in a delict claim (no cause of action).
- No allegation that the plaintiff *suffered* damages.
- Insufficient particulars to found vicarious liability (employment relationship not pleaded).
- Ownership of the damaged property not pleaded — exposes a **locus standi** defect (see [[Jurisdiction of the High Court]]).
- Wrong interest rate pleaded, or interest running from an implausible date.
- Attorney-and-client costs claimed without basis.
- Wrong preamble to the prayer (should be "jointly and severally, the one paying the other to be absolved" for joint wrongdoers).
- Wrong form of summons/pleading used (declaration instead of particulars of claim, or vice versa) — see [[Summons Types and the Essentialia]].
