# Application Procedure and Notice of Motion

## Action vs application — six differences (as tested, verbatim structure)

| Action | Application |
|---|---|
| Parties are plaintiff and defendant | Parties are applicant and respondent |
| Deals with substantial factual disputes | No substantial factual dispute that cannot be dealt with "on the papers" |
| Commences with a summons | Commences with a notice of motion and supporting founding affidavit |
| Further pleadings are exchanged | Further affidavits are exchanged |
| A "preparation for trial" stage follows pleadings | No written "preparation for trial" stage |
| Ends in the trial court, mainly oral evidence | Ends in motion court; in principle no oral evidence, parties don't testify |

When to use an application: the relief is **prescribed** by the Rules to be sought that way, or there is **no real factual dispute** and the matter can be decided on the papers.

Certain relief **must** be sought by application (motion procedure only) — e.g. **divorce is excluded from simple summons** but note some relief cannot be obtained by application at all; conversely, matters like **summary judgment (Rule 32)** and **Rule 43 relief** (maintenance pendente lite, contribution to costs, interim custody/access) are themselves interlocutory applications within an action.

## Types of applications

1. **Ex parte applications** — no respondent cited; may be heard in chambers. Competent only where notice would defeat the purpose of the application (e.g. Anton Piller order, attachment to found jurisdiction) or would not affect another person (e.g. appointment of a curator ad litem).
2. **Applications by notice** (ordinary opposed/unopposed motion) — Form 2 (short form) or Form 2A (long form).
3. **Urgent applications** — Rule 6(12): must give reasons for urgency; can request the court to condone non-compliance with the ordinary time periods/forms of service; matter can be placed for reconsideration.
4. **Specific applications** (e.g. summary judgment, Rule 43).
5. **Interlocutory applications** — see below.
6. **Chamber Book applications** — set down before noon on the court day but one.

## Notice of motion forms

- **Short form (Form 2)** — used for most standard applications, including interlocutory applications and ex parte applications.
- **Long form (Form 2A)** — where the respondent is expected to oppose on affidavit; sets out time periods for the respondent to (a) give notice of intention to oppose (commonly **5 days**), (b) deliver an opposing affidavit (commonly **10 days**, or **15 days** if the respondent is government), and (c) deliver a replying affidavit (commonly **10 days**).
- Application must be served **by the sheriff**.

## Interlocutory applications

- Do **not** deal with the merits of the case, but with a procedural aspect adjunct to it (e.g. compelling discovery, security for costs disputes).
- Brought by the party needing the relief (e.g. the defendant compelling the plaintiff to discover), served on the opposing party's attorney.
- Short form notice of motion used.

### Drafting checklist — interlocutory application founding affidavit (e.g. to compel discovery)

> I, the undersigned, [name], do hereby make oath and say that:
> 1. I am a major [male/female] attorney practising under the name and style of [firm].
> 2. The facts herein are within my personal knowledge and belief, unless stated to the contrary, and are true and correct.
> 3. I am the attorney of record for the [Defendant/Applicant] herein.
> 4. The attached Notice in terms of Rule 35(1), Annexure "A", was served on [date] and filed on [date].
> 5. No response was received; I addressed a letter dated [date], Annexure "B".
> 6. Again no response was received.
> 7. The [Defendant/Applicant] is being prejudiced by the non-delivery of the [Plaintiff's/Respondent's] discovery affidavit.

### Draft order sought (interlocutory to compel discovery)

> 1. The Plaintiff/Respondent is directed to furnish the reply to the Notice to discover in terms of Rule 35(1) served on [date], within 5 days of service of this order.
> 2. If the Plaintiff/Respondent fails to comply, the Defendant/Applicant is given leave to apply on these papers, duly supplemented, and on 5 days' notice, for an order dismissing the claim, with costs.
> 3. Costs of this application against the Plaintiff/Respondent.
> 4. Further or alternative relief.

## The founding affidavit generally — what it must contain (Rule 6 application)

- Full names/status/address of applicant and respondent.
- That the court has jurisdiction.
- The facts grounding the cause of action / relief sought (attach a pro forma particulars of claim where relevant, e.g. an attachment-to-found-jurisdiction application).
- The urgency (if applicable) and why no alternative remedy exists.
- The relief sought, mirrored in the notice of motion.

## Evaluating opposed applications — the Plascon-Evans rule

Where there's a genuine, bona fide dispute of fact on the papers, the court grants relief on the facts as stated by the **respondent**, together with the facts admitted by the respondent in the applicant's affidavit — unless the respondent's version is **so far-fetched or clearly untenable** that it can be rejected on the papers. *Plascon-Evans Paints v Van Riebeeck Paints* 1984 (3) SA 623 (A); *National Scrap Metal (Cape Town) v Murray & Roberts* (SCA).

## Ex parte application to found/confirm jurisdiction (attachment)

Where a foreign (peregrinus) defendant owes no local presence but a **local debtor owes the peregrinus money**, the applicant may bring an ex parte application (short form notice of motion, signed by an attorney) for an order calling on the local debtor to show cause why the debt should not be **attached** to found jurisdiction for the applicant's intended action. The founding affidavit must set out: full details of applicant and (intended) defendant; that the defendant is a peregrinus; a prima facie cause of action (attach pro forma particulars of claim); that the local debtor's debt to the defendant is due, payable and attachable; and that the applicant intends to institute action.
