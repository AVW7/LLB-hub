# Plea, Special Plea, Replication and Close of Pleadings

## The pleading sequence

Summons → NID → (declaration, if simple summons) → **Plea** → (Replication, if needed) → (Rejoinder) → Close of pleadings.

## Plea on the merits

Responding to each allegation, the defendant may:
- **Admit** an allegation.
- **Deny** an allegation.
- **Admit but add justification** ("confess and avoid").
- **Elect not to admit** a fact not within the defendant's knowledge, and state the effect of that.
- Any allegation **neither admitted nor denied is deemed admitted** — Rule 22(3) [numbered as R30(3) in one memo — confirm current rule number].

## Special plea

- Filed **together with** the plea on the merits, but does **not** address the merits.
- Two categories:
  - **Dilatory** (delaying) — e.g. *lis pendens*, arbitration, non-joinder.
  - **Declinatory** (final, dispositive) — e.g. prescription, *res judicata*, absence of jurisdiction.

### Drafting checklist — special plea of prescription (recurring fact pattern: cause of action/collision date vs date summons served, gap >3 years)

> 1. The cause of action sued upon arose on [date].
> 2. The plaintiff's claim constitutes a debt as defined by the Prescription Act 68 of 1969.
> 3. The summons in this action was served on the defendant on [date], that is more than three years after the claim arose.
> 4. In the premises and by virtue of section 11 of the Prescription Act 68 of 1969 the plaintiff's claim has become prescribed.
>
> WHEREFORE the defendant prays for judgment in the defendant's favour with costs.

### Drafting checklist — special plea of no jurisdiction

> Defendant denies that this Honourable Court has jurisdiction to hear plaintiff's claim for damages as:
> 1. the delict complained of allegedly occurred at [place], which is outside the area of jurisdiction of the court; and
> 2. the defendant resides at [address], an address which is outside the jurisdiction of the court.
>
> WHEREFORE defendant prays that plaintiff's claim be dismissed with costs.

### Six recognised instances for a special plea (as tested)

Lis pendens · arbitration · non-joinder · prescription · res judicata · absence of jurisdiction.

## Replication and rejoinder

- **Replication** — plaintiff's reply to the defendant's plea, delivered within (memos say) 15 days of the plea; same requirements as a declaration.
- If a **plea in reconvention** (counterclaim) is delivered, the plaintiff's response is a **plea in reconvention answer / replication to plea in reconvention**, generally within 10 days.
- **Rejoinder** — defendant's reply to the replication, ~10 days after replication.
- A **counterclaim** must be delivered **together with the plea** (i.e., at the same time); if delivered later, the defendant must obtain the plaintiff's written consent to the late delivery, failing which must apply to court for leave.

## Close of pleadings — Rule 29

Pleadings are deemed closed when:
1. The parties have joined issue without adding any further pleading; or
2. The last day allowed for a replication/subsequent pleading has lapsed and it has not been filed; or
3. The parties agree in writing that pleadings are closed, and the agreement is filed with the registrar; or
4. The parties are unable to agree, and the court, on application, declares the pleadings closed.

## Notice of bar — Rule 26

- Where a party is in default of a pleading (e.g. plaintiff fails to deliver a declaration, or a party fails to plead), the other party serves a **notice of bar** — giving the defaulting party **5 days'** notice to deliver the outstanding pleading, failing which that party is **automatically barred**.
- **Removing a bar** (Rule 27): the barred party applies to court, on good cause shown, for the bar to be lifted.
- Once barred, no further pleading may be delivered without the bar being removed.

## Defendant's options if the plaintiff stalls after an NID (no declaration, no summary judgment application)

1. Serve a **notice of bar** on the plaintiff requiring delivery of the declaration within 5 days.
2. Apply for **absolution from the instance**, or, after adducing evidence, apply for judgment.
