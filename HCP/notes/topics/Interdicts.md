# Interdicts

## Interim interdict — requirements

1. A **prima facie right** (even if open to some doubt).
2. **Irreparable harm** if the interdict is not granted.
3. The **balance of convenience** favours the applicant.
4. **No alternative (satisfactory) remedy**.

## Final interdict — requirements

1. A **clear right**.
2. An **actual injury**, or at least a **reasonably apprehended/threatened** violation of that right.
3. **No alternative remedy**.

(Note the shift from "prima facie" to "clear" right, and from "irreparable harm" to "actual/threatened injury", between the interim and final tests.)

## Choosing action vs application procedure for interdict relief

Interdicts can be sought via either the action or application procedure, depending on whether a real, foreseeable dispute of fact exists:
- If the true dispute is a **legal** question (e.g. whether an unstated term is "material and essential" to a contract) rather than a **factual** one, use the **application** procedure — quicker and cheaper.
- If the court finds at the hearing that there **is** a real dispute of fact that the applicant could not have foreseen, the court may (a) refer the matter for **oral evidence** on the disputed issue (if simple), or (b) refer the whole matter to **trial**, with directions on the exchange of pleadings (if complex).
- If the dispute of fact **should have been foreseen** by the applicant, the court may simply **dismiss the application**.

## Urgent interdict applications (e.g. to restrain publication)

### Drafting checklist — notice of motion, relief sought

> 1. That the court condone the non-compliance with the rules of court;
> 2. That the court treat the application as urgent;
> 3. That the Respondent be interdicted from [publishing the defamatory information / other specified conduct];
> 4. Costs;
> 5. Further and/or alternative relief.

### Founding affidavit — points to cover

- Applicant's and respondent's details.
- That the court has jurisdiction.
- That the respondent is about to commit the act complained of (e.g. publish defamatory material).
- That the act will cause irreparable harm.
- That the applicant has no other remedy but to approach the court.
- That the matter is urgent (and why).

## Anton Piller orders (a species of interim interdict, obtained ex parte by rule nisi)

Aim: to **preserve evidence** the respondent might otherwise hide or destroy. Founding affidavit must aver:
1. The applicant has a cause of action it wishes to pursue against the respondent.
2. The respondent possesses specific and specified documents/things constituting vital evidence for that cause of action.
3. There is a real and well-founded fear that this evidence may be hidden, destroyed or lost before the matter reaches court (or the discovery stage). See *Frangos v Corpcapital* 2004 (2) SA 643 (T).

## Rule nisi interdicts against the state

Under the **General Law Amendment Act 62 of 1955**, an interdict against the state generally requires **72 hours' notice**.
