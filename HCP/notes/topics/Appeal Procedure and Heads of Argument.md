# Appeal Procedure and Heads of Argument

## Leave to appeal

- s16 of the Superior Courts Act: leave to appeal must first be sought.
- Apply **immediately** to the trial judge, or, if not raised then, within (commonly) **15 days** of the judgment or of reasons being furnished.
- Normally heard by the judge who presided at the trial.
- Test: **reasonable prospects that another court would come to a different conclusion** (or some other compelling reason).
- Leave may be sought to the **full court** of the division or to the **Supreme Court of Appeal**.

## If leave is refused by the trial court

- May petition the **Supreme Court of Appeal** (via the Chief Justice) — within **one month** of the refusal.
- The petition/application: 2 copies, does not attach the full record, served on the respondent; must attach the order appealed against, the order refusing leave, the judgment of the court a quo, and the judgment refusing leave.
- Heard by **two judges** of the SCA, who may grant, refuse, or refer the petition to open court.
- If leave is **granted**, notice of appeal must be filed within **20 days**.

## Rule 49 — the appeal timeline (High Court to full court/SCA)

| Step | Rule | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Application for leave to appeal | Rule 49(1) | Within 15 days of judgment/reasons |
| Arrange hearing date with the judge | Rule 49(1) / practice notes | Within 10 days, attorneys jointly approach the judge |
| Notice of appeal (if leave granted) | Rule 49(2) | Within 20 days |
| Security for costs of appeal | Rule 49(6)(a) | Before lodging copies of record |
| Apply for a hearing date to the registrar | Rule 49(6)(b) | — |
| 3 copies of the record + index to registrar | Rule 49(7)(b) | — |
| 2 copies of record to respondent | Rule 49(7)(c) | — |
| Cross-appeal | Rule 49(7)(d) | 10 days after notice of appeal |
| Appellant's heads of argument + list of authorities (3 copies to registrar) | Rule 49(15) | At least 15 days before hearing |
| Respondent's heads of argument + list of authorities (3 copies to registrar) | Rule 49(15) | At least 10 days before hearing |

Formatting of heads: clearly typed A4, double spacing, every 10th line numbered, left margin 35mm.

## Appeal to the Supreme Court of Appeal (own timeline where leave was granted directly)

Application for leave (21 days of judgment/reasons) → notice of appeal (1 month, if leave granted) → respondent's reply (1 month) → counter-appeal/answer (10 days) (SCA Rules 6–7).

## Zweni test — what is appealable

A decision must be: (1) **final** in effect, (2) **definitive of the rights of the parties**, and (3) have the effect of **disposing of at least a substantial portion of the relief claimed** in the main proceedings — *Zweni v Minister of Law and Order* 1993 (1) SA 523 (A). If the order has no practical effect, an appeal may be dismissed on that basis alone.

## Operation of the order pending appeal

Noting an appeal generally **suspends** the operation and execution of the order under appeal (s18 of the Superior Courts Act), subject to the court's power to order otherwise.

## Notice of Appeal — structure (from the precedent template)

A Notice of Appeal must identify: the court and case number, the parties, the order/judgment appealed against (judge, date), whether leave is to the SCA or the full bench, and then set out numbered **grounds of appeal** — each ground identifying the specific misdirection alleged (finding of fact or ruling of law), followed by the correct position contended for. End with a request that the matter be placed on the roll, signature block, and address for the registrar and opposing attorneys. See the precedent [[HCP NoticeofAppeal.doc]] (Minister of Water Affairs v Exxaro Coal) for a full worked example — the grounds systematically track each misdirection alleged in the judgment a quo, point by point.

## Heads of argument — structure (from the precedent template)

Structure demonstrated in [[HCP Example - Heads of argument.pdf]] (S v Penxa, respondent's heads on an application for leave to appeal + condonation):
- Heading identifying court, case number, parties, and "[Respondent's/Appellant's] Heads of Argument".
- **Introduction** — states what the application is (e.g. leave to appeal + condonation for late filing).
- **Ad Condonation** — deal with the condonation application first if relevant (oppose or concede, with reasons).
- **Ad [substantive ground, e.g. Leave to Appeal]** — numbered paragraphs, each making one legal submission, supported by:
  - references to the trial record (e.g. "Record p.3 line 8–10");
  - case authority quoted and cited in full (e.g. *S v Halgryn* 2002 (2) SACR 211 (SCA) at 216h–j);
  - the argument built cumulatively, paragraph by paragraph, toward a conclusion.
- Close with the party's submission on the ultimate relief sought (e.g. that leave to appeal be refused / condonation be granted).

## Practical/etiquette notes from practice materials

- Proper dress for attorneys: black gown, white shirt/blouse closed at the neck, white lace jabot, dark pants/skirt, black/dark closed shoes.
- Never hand up original documents to the court — always copies; confirm fees, give time limits.
- Arrange with your opponent for a formal introduction to the presiding judge(s) before the hearing (senior counsel/attorney introduces).
- If briefing counsel, provide the complete, finally numbered and paginated record well in advance.
