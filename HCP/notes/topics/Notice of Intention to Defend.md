# Notice of Intention to Defend

The recurring "John Brown v Paul Naidoo" fact pattern — near word-for-word the same question across multiple sittings.

## Core facts

- A defendant who wishes to defend an action must, within the applicable **dies** (see [[Service of Process and Edictal Citation]]), deliver a **Notice of Intention to Defend (NID)**.
- If the NID is delivered **late** but before default judgment has been granted, it **may not be ignored** — the plaintiff must accept it (subject to the plaintiff's right to have already applied for judgment if the dies had already expired and no NID was yet filed).

## Drafting checklist — Notice of Intention to Defend (verbatim from the memos)

> **NOTICE OF INTENTION TO DEFEND**
>
> PLEASE TAKE NOTICE that the defendant of (set out a residential or business address) hereby gives notice of his intention to defend this action.
>
> AND TAKE FURTHER NOTICE that, in accordance with Rule 19(3) of the rules of this Honourable Court, the defendant appoints the office of his attorney set out below as the address at which he will accept service of all notices, process and documents in this action.
>
> [Attorney's details]

Key elements the memos mark separately: address for service under **Rule 19(3)**, and the appointment of the attorney's address as the address for further service.

## What happens next

- Once an NID is filed, the plaintiff must (for a **simple summons**) deliver a **declaration** — see [[Particulars of Claim and Rule 18 Requirements]].
- If a **combined summons**, pleadings proceed straight to plea.
- If the plaintiff fails to progress the matter after an NID (no declaration, no summary judgment application), the defendant may serve a **notice of bar** compelling the plaintiff to act — see [[Plea, Special Plea, Replication and Close of Pleadings]].
