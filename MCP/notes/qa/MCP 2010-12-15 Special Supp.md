## Q1 — "What will you do if…" procedural drill (10)

> [!question] Question
> Answer briefly (no rule citations needed). What will you do if: a. your client's claim is for R110,000 in the Magistrate's Court? b. your client's claim is R110,000 but he admits owing the defendant R15,000? c. the defendant fails to deliver a Plea in time? d. having entered an appearance to defend, you realise the cause of action has prescribed? e. the Plea served on you is vague and embarrassing? f. you want to use an expert witness at trial? g. your request for further particulars gets no response? h. in a non-damages action, the Defendant fails to enter an appearance to defend? i. on the MVA damages trial date you realise the presiding magistrate is the same one who convicted your client of negligent driving in the related criminal case? j. the plaintiff wrongfully fails to respond to your rule 15(1) notice?

> [!success]- Answer
> a. Abandon the excess of R10,000 (over the jurisdictional limit implied). b. Deduct the R15,000 from the total claim (deal with any set-off claim in your reply). c. Deliver a Notice of Bar (give a conventional warning first if required). d. Raise a **Special Plea**. e. Notify the Defendant of the defect and require rectification within 10 days. f. Deliver a Notice of Expert Witness and a summary of the evidence. g. Apply to court to compel the furnishing of further particulars, with costs. h. Request default judgment and file a damages affidavit if necessary. i. Request the magistrate to **recuse** himself. j. Apply for dismissal of the claim with costs.

## Q2 — Rule 24(10) photograph notice (12)

> [!question] Question
> Thomas Brooks' BMW is damaged in a collision with a Department of Safety and Security (Limpopo) vehicle driven by an on-duty policeman. Damage is R15,500. 2.1 The Plaintiff has 4 colour photographs for trial — draft the necessary notice to the other party (10). 2.2 What is the time limit for giving the notice (1)? 2.3 Your opponent files a notice objecting to the photographs — what will you do (1)?

> [!success]- Answer
> **2.1** A **Notice in terms of Rule 24(10)**, addressed to the Clerk of the Court and the Defendant's attorneys, giving notice that the Plaintiff intends using the 4 colour photographs (copies attached, marked A–D) at trial; that the Defendant may inspect them at the Plaintiff's attorneys on weekdays 8h00–16h00; and that unless the Defendant objects to their authenticity within 5 days of delivery, they will be admitted into evidence without further proof.
> **2.2** At least 10 days before the trial.
> **2.3** Lead evidence to prove the authenticity of the photographs.

Related: [[Expert Witness Notices (Rule 24)]]

## Q3 — Security for costs (15)

> [!question] Question
> Your client is sued by Jeffreys Manufacturers CC for R5,800 for goods sold and delivered; you are instructed to defend. 3.1 What is the time limit within which you may request security for costs (1)? 3.2 Your client is also a CC — can the Plaintiff request security for costs if your client counter-claims? Motivate (3). 3.3 Jeffreys Manufacturers CC fails to furnish security — what are your client's options (2)? 3.4 Draft the application to exercise those options (headings and citation omitted) (9).

> [!success]- Answer
> **3.1** Before close of pleadings.
> **3.2** No — security can only be asked of a Plaintiff **in convention**.
> **3.3** Apply to court for dismissal of the action, alternatively a stay of proceedings until security is furnished (noting that under the Close Corporations Act, the party seeking the order against a CC must satisfy the court there is good reason).
> **3.4** A **Notice in terms of Rule 62(2)**: reciting that the Plaintiff, having been duly requested by notice dated (date) to furnish security in a form satisfactory to the Defendant, has failed to do so; notice that application will be made for an order (a) dismissing the Plaintiff's action with costs, alternatively (b) staying proceedings until security is furnished, (c) granting costs of this application, (d) further/alternative relief.

Related: [[Security for Costs]]

## Q4 — Summary judgment scope and pleadings mechanics (13)

> [!question] Question
> 4.1 On what types of claims may a plaintiff apply for summary judgment (4)? 4.2 Name two ways a defendant may respond to a summary judgment application (2). 4.3 True or false: a plaintiff may file a replying affidavit in a summary judgment application (1)? 4.4 True or false: once pleadings are deemed closed under rule 21, neither party may amend pleadings already filed (1)? 4.5 In what instances does a summons lapse (2)? 4.6 Name three instances when you may raise a special plea (3).

> [!success]- Answer
> **4.1** Liquid document; liquidated amount of money; delivery of specified movables; ejectment (also matters subject to the Prevention of Illegal Eviction Act).
> **4.2** Pay into court the amount claimed; or file and serve an opposing affidavit setting out a bona fide defence or bona fide counterclaim.
> **4.3** False.
> **4.4** False.
> **4.5** If not served within 12 months after issue by the clerk of the court; or if the plaintiff takes no further action within 2 months after service by the sheriff.
> **4.6** Any 3 of: prescription; lis pendens; res judicata; non-joinder; misjoinder (list not exhaustive).
