## Q1 — Loan + suretyship POC (15)

> [!question] Question
> Client A. Brown made an oral loan of R30,000 to Red Leather CC on 25 October 2009, repayable in full by 31 January 2010 — unpaid. On the same date, the CC's sole member signed a written Deed of Suretyship containing all usual terms. The surety has also not paid. Draft full particulars of claim.

> [!success]- Answer
> POC: Plaintiff Anton Brown, adult male businessman; First Defendant Red Leather CC; Second Defendant James Smith (surety), full particulars otherwise unknown to Plaintiff. First Defendant indebted for R30,000 loaned and advanced at its special instance and request on 25 October 2009 under an oral agreement, due 31 January 2010. Second Defendant executed a Deed of Suretyship the same date, binding himself as surety and co-principal debtor (annexed as "A"). Neither Defendant has paid. Prayer: judgment against both jointly and severally, one paying the other absolved, for R30,000 + interest at 15.5% p.a. from 1 February 2010 + costs of suit.

## Q2 — MVA POC: jurisdiction, default judgment, costs, tender, summary judgment, expert notice (25)

> [!question] Question
> [POC facts: Li Na (Plaintiff, resident Harare, Zimbabwe) v Koos Smith (Defendant, employed Johannesburg, resides Durban); collision 1 March 2008 in Plein Street, Cape Town; sole negligence of Defendant; R20,000 vehicle damage; demand unmet.]
> 2.1 Which court(s) have jurisdiction and why (6)? 2.2 If the Defendant fails to enter an Appearance to Defend, what documents must the Plaintiff file for Default Judgment (4)? 2.3 The Defendant is worried about recovering his costs from the Plaintiff if successful — what action can he take (2)? 2.4 The Defendant believes both parties were equally to blame and has R10,000 available — what should he do to prevent further costs (4)? 2.5 After an Appearance to Defend, can the Plaintiff apply for Summary Judgment? Give reasons (3). 2.6 After pleadings close and the Defendant disputes the vehicle damages, what notice(s) must the Plaintiff's attorneys file before trial, and what must they contain (6)?

> [!success]- Answer
> **2.1** May sue in Durban MC (Defendant resides there); Johannesburg MC (Defendant employed there); or Cape Town MC (whole cause of action arose there) — any of the three.
> **2.2** Original and copy of the Request for Default Judgment; original Return of Service; original Summons; Damages Affidavit.
> **2.3** Ask for **security for costs**, since the Plaintiff is a peregrinus (foreigner).
> **2.4** Make a **"without prejudice" offer** of R10,000 plus costs to date, in settlement of the Plaintiff's claim.
> **2.5** No — the damages are an **illiquid claim**, so summary judgment is unavailable.
> **2.6** An **Expert Notice** not less than 15 days before the hearing of the intention to use the expert, and not less than 10 days before the hearing a summary of the expert's opinion and reasons — the expert should confirm having examined the vehicle, the damage found, and the fair and reasonable cost of repair.

Related: [[Jurisdiction of the Magistrate's Court]], [[Default Judgment]], [[Security for Costs]], [[Rule 14 Judgment|Summary Judgment (Rule 14)]], [[Expert Witness Notices (Rule 24)]]

## Q3 — Deed of settlement (10)

> [!question] Question
> Action for damages from an unlawful assault; pleadings have closed. Defendant's attorney conveys an offer of R8,000 in settlement plus interest at the legal rate and party-and-party costs to date; acceptable to your client. Defendant will pay in monthly instalments of R500. Draft the deed of settlement to present to the Court.

> [!success]- Answer
> A Deed of Settlement in which the Defendant agrees to pay R8,000 plus costs and interest; agrees to pay the Plaintiff's taxed party-and-party costs and attorney-and-client costs from the deed's date until final payment; interest at 15.5% p.a. on the R8,000 from the date of the deed and on the costs from date of taxation; instalments of R500/month starting/continuing on stated dates; on default, the full balance becomes immediately due and the Plaintiff may proceed to execution without further application; payments go to the Plaintiff's attorneys, applied first to attorney-and-client costs, then taxed costs, then interest, then capital; the parties request the Court to make the agreement an order of court.
