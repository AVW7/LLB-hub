## Q1 — Rule 24(9) expert witness notice (8)

> [!question] Question
> Plaintiff sues Defendant for vehicle damage from a collision; the matter is defended and set down for trial. The defendant needs to call Monty Naicker, a panelbeater, to give evidence supporting his damages claim. Draft the necessary Notice.

> [!success]- Answer
> A **Notice in terms of Rule 24(9)(a)(b)**, addressed to the Clerk of the Court and the Plaintiff's attorneys, giving notice that the Defendant intends calling Monty Naicker (a panelbeater with 20 years' experience) as an expert witness, who will testify: that he inspected the vehicle on a stated date; found it damaged as set out in an annexed schedule; that the reasonable and necessary cost of repair was R45,000; and that the damage is consistent with a motor collision.

Related: [[Expert Witness Notices (Rule 24)]]

## Q2 — Notice of Bar for inaction after appearance to defend (9)

> [!question] Question
> Plaintiff sues Defendant for MVA damages; Defendant enters an Appearance to Defend; two months pass with no further steps taken by the Defendant. 2.1 What action is available to the plaintiff (1)? 2.2 Draft the necessary Notice (8).

> [!success]- Answer
> **2.1** A **Notice of Bar**.
> **2.2** Addressed to the Clerk of the Court and the Defendant's attorneys, giving notice that the Defendant is required to file his plea within 5 days of receipt hereof, failing which he shall be ipso facto barred (alternatively: failing which the Plaintiff will apply for Default Judgment without further notice).

Related: [[Notice of Bar]]

## Q3 — Provisional sentence vs action, judgment, defendant's options (14)

> [!question] Question
> Plaintiff (office furniture manufacturer) sold desks to Defendant for R100,000; Defendant took delivery and paid by cheque, then immediately stopped payment. 3.1 Advise on Provisional Sentence vs Action, with reasons (4). 3.2 Provisional Sentence Summons issued; Defendant opposes, filing an affidavit alleging the desks are defective (wood-borer infested), no other papers filed. Plaintiff's Replying Affidavit attaches a certificate from the wood supplier confirming the wood was tested and found free of wood-borer. As Magistrate, what judgment would you give, and why (5)? 3.3 Assuming Provisional Sentence is granted but the Defendant still believes he has a valid defence — what options does he have (5)?

> [!success]- Answer
> **3.1** Proceed by **Provisional Sentence** — the onus falls on the Defendant to show, on a balance of probabilities, that he would succeed in a defended trial, which is a harder onus than in an action (where a defended claim only exposes the Defendant to Summary Judgment if he can show a mere bona fide defence).
> **3.2** Grant **Provisional Sentence** for the Plaintiff (R100,000 plus costs) — the Defendant has only made a bare allegation, unsupported by independent evidence, while the Plaintiff has put up a certificate showing the wood was free of wood-borer. The result might differ if the Defendant had put up an independent inspector's affidavit confirming the infestation.
> **3.3** Within 2 months of judgment, pay the Plaintiff the claim amount plus interest plus costs; the Plaintiff must then furnish security for the amount paid; the matter then proceeds as an action, with the Provisional Sentence Summons standing as the Summons, and the Defendant in due course filing his plea.

Related: [[Provisional Sentence]], [[Rule 14 Judgment|Summary Judgment (Rule 14)]]

## Q4 — Jurisdiction-consent clauses (4)

> [!question] Question
> 4.1 A contract clause reads: "The parties consent to the jurisdiction of the Port Elizabeth Magistrate's Court for the determination of any dispute arising from the contract." Defendant breaches, resides in Port Elizabeth, claim is R500,000 — can the Plaintiff sue in the Port Elizabeth MC (2)? 4.2 Same facts, but the clause instead reads: "The parties consent to the jurisdiction of the Magistrate's Court…" — can the Plaintiff sue in the Port Elizabeth MC (2)?

> [!success]- Answer
> **4.1** No — a clause naming **one specific** Magistrate's Court invalidates the entire clause; the action must go to the High Court.
> **4.2** Yes — since no specific Magistrate's Court is named, the generic consent clause is valid and the action can be brought in the Port Elizabeth MC.

Related: [[Jurisdiction of the Magistrate's Court]]

## Q5 — Building contract + suretyship POC (15)

> [!question] Question
> Billy Bob contracted with Construct-a-Home (Pty) Ltd for R85,000 of home alterations (R35,000 on signature, R25,000 on completion of foundations, R25,000 on completion). Sam Samson (a director) bound himself as surety and co-principal debtor. Billy paid the initial R35,000 and a further R25,000 although the foundations weren't complete; Construct-a-Home then abandoned the work. Billy cancelled and had Bay Construction complete the work for R50,000. Draft the Particulars of Claim to recover Billy's damages (omit headings).

> [!success]- Answer
> POC pleads: Plaintiff (Billy Bob); First Defendant (Construct-a-Home (Pty) Ltd); Second Defendant (Sam Samson, surety); the contract's material terms (price R85,000, payment schedule) with the contract annexed; the suretyship; Plaintiff's payments (R35,000 + R25,000 = R60,000 of the R85,000); First Defendant's failure to complete and Plaintiff's resultant cancellation; completion by Bay Construction at R50,000; resulting damages of R25,000 (R60,000 already paid + R50,000 to complete = R110,000 total cost, R25,000 more than the original R85,000 contract price). Prayer: judgment against First and Second Defendants jointly and severally, one paying the other absolved, for R25,000 + interest at 15.5% p.a. a tempore morae + costs of suit.

Related: [[Summons and Particulars of Claim Drafting]]
