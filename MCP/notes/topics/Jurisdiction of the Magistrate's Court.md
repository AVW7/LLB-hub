# Jurisdiction of the Magistrate's Court

The Magistrate's Court is a creature of statute — the **Magistrates' Courts Act 32 of 1944** — and has no jurisdiction beyond what the Act grants. It consists of district courts and regional courts (regional division).

## Monetary limits

Per the archive's 2019 webinar notes: up to **R200,000** for the District Court; above R200,000 up to **R400,000** for the Regional Courts.

## Core statutory bases

- **s28** — jurisdiction in respect of persons.
- **s29** — jurisdiction in respect of causes of action.
- **s46** — matters entirely excluded from MC jurisdiction: interpretation of a will; the status of a person as regards mental capacity; specific performance without an alternative of payment of damages (subject to exceptions).
- **s45** — jurisdiction by consent: parties may consent in writing to a MC otherwise beyond its monetary jurisdiction; only a court that already has territorial jurisdiction under s28 can be the beneficiary of consent; the consent must be pleaded specifically. A clause consenting to "the jurisdiction of **the** Magistrate's Court" (generic) is valid and lets the plaintiff sue in whichever MC would otherwise have jurisdiction. A clause naming **one specific** MC (e.g. "Port Elizabeth Magistrate's Court") where the parties don't otherwise fall within its jurisdiction is invalid and the **whole clause fails**, forcing the matter to the High Court.
- **s40** — splitting of claims disallowed, where the parties and the point at issue in all the split actions would be the same.
- **s43** — cumulative jurisdiction: where 2+ claims each based on a different cause of action are combined in one summons, the court has the same jurisdiction over each as if each were the sole subject of a separate action.
- Concurrent jurisdiction — the High Court retains concurrent jurisdiction with the MC unless a statute provides otherwise.
- **s30bis** — attachment to found (*ad fundandam*) or confirm (*ad confirmandam*) jurisdiction over a **peregrinus** defendant. Requirements: a prima facie cause of action; the defendant is a peregrinus; and property in which the defendant has a beneficial interest is within the court's jurisdiction. Must be brought **before** summons is issued — cannot be brought after appearance to defend or after close of pleadings; if peregrinus status is discovered after summons has issued, withdraw and start over.
- Objection to jurisdiction is raised by **special plea**, briefly stating the facts relied on.
- **s65A** proceedings — an exception to normal jurisdiction rules: only the court in whose area the debtor resides, is employed, or carries on business (or, for a juristic person, where its main place of business/registered office is) has jurisdiction.

## Personal jurisdiction bases (s28) in practice

The recurring "Li Na v Koos Smith" fact pattern tests three independent, alternative bases for personal jurisdiction: the defendant **resides** in Durban; is **employed** in Johannesburg; or the **whole cause of action arose** in Cape Town — any one of the three grounds jurisdiction in that court.
