# Provisional Sentence

Available where the claim is founded on a **liquid document** (e.g. a cheque).

## Procedure

- Once a provisional sentence summons is issued, the defendant may file a notice of intention to oppose and an affidavit setting up a defence.
- The **onus is on the defendant** to show, on a balance of probabilities, that he would succeed if the matter proceeded to trial — heavier than the bona fide defence standard in [[Rule 14 Judgment|Summary Judgment (Rule 14)]]. A bare, unsupported allegation (e.g. that goods were defective, without an independent inspector's affidavit) will not discharge it. [[MCP 2012 Special Supp]] Q3.1–3.2
- If provisional sentence is granted, the defendant may, within **2 months** of judgment, pay the plaintiff the amount claimed plus interest plus costs; the plaintiff must then furnish security for the amount paid; the matter proceeds as an ordinary action with the provisional sentence summons standing as the summons, and the defendant in due course files a plea. [[MCP 2012 Special Supp]] Q3.3

## Choice of procedure

Provisional sentence favours a plaintiff holding a liquid document because of the heavier onus it places on the defendant to resist judgment, compared to an ordinary action where a defended claim only exposes the defendant to a summary judgment application (bona fide defence standard). [[MCP 2012 Special Supp]] Q3.1
