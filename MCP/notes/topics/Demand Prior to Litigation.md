# Demand Prior to Litigation

## General rule

A demand is **ordinarily not** a condition precedent to issuing summons — a plaintiff can have a complete cause of action without ever demanding payment first, though sending a letter of demand remains advisable practice.

## Exceptions — where a demand IS necessary to complete the cause of action

- A promissory note or acknowledgment of debt payable "on demand."
- A **s129(1)(a)** or **s86(10) National Credit Act 34 of 2005** notice.
- A notice to pay arrears before cancellation/acceleration under an **Alienation of Land Act 68 of 1998** transaction.
- A **s3 notice to an organ of state** under the Institution of Legal Proceedings against Certain Organs of State Act 40 of 2002.
- Notice required by the underlying agreement itself before cancellation.
- Where a contract has **no stipulated time for performance**, a demand is needed to place the debtor in mora.

Where demand IS a necessary part of the cause of action, it must be **specifically pleaded**.

## Form and content

- May be written or oral.
- A **personal demand by the plaintiff** (not necessarily via an attorney) suffices.
- Must give the debtor enough detail to know the basis of the claim.
- Merely rendering an account is not enough for a money claim, but any wording that notifies the debtor the debt is due and payment is required will do.
- No need to threaten legal proceedings.

## Effect of omission

Where no demand was legally required, failing to make one is **not a defence**.

## Applied fact patterns

- SAPS vehicle collision claim — requires the s3 Institution of Legal Proceedings notice to the Minister before suing an organ of state. [[MCP 2010-03-06]] Q1
- No demand needed before summons where the contract already fixed a payment date (15 days after statement) — the debtor was already in mora by the fixed date. [[MCP 2010-03-18 Supp]] Q1.1
