# Rule 14 Judgment (Summary Judgment)

Available only after the defendant has entered an appearance to defend, and only for claims that are: a liquid document; a liquidated amount of money; delivery of specified movable property; or ejectment (the Dec 2010 memo also notes claims subject to the Prevention of Illegal Eviction Act).

Not available for illiquid/unliquidated damages claims (e.g. motor vehicle accident damages) — confirmed identically in two separate memos: [[MCP 2010-09-07 Supp]] Q2.5, [[MCP 2012-08-30 Supp]] Q2.5.

## Procedure steps

Within 15 days of delivery of the notice of intention to defend, the plaintiff delivers a notice of application for summary judgment plus a supporting affidavit (by the plaintiff or someone who can swear positively to the facts).

The affidavit must: verify the cause of action and the amount claimed; state that in the deponent's opinion there is no bona fide defence; and state that the notice of intention to defend was delivered solely to delay. If the claim is founded on a liquid document, attach a copy.

The notice must set the hearing down not less than 10 days from delivery.

Only the plaintiff's and defendant's affidavits are considered; the defendant may supplement with oral evidence with the court's leave.

No replying affidavit for the plaintiff is permitted (the memo confirms this proposition is false).

## Defendant's options to resist

1. Pay into court the amount claimed; or
2. File and serve an opposing affidavit disclosing a bona fide defence or bona fide counterclaim.

## Contrast with provisional sentence

In [[Provisional Sentence]], the onus sits on the defendant to show, on a balance of probabilities, that he would succeed at trial — a heavier onus than merely showing a bona fide defence under summary judgment. [[MCP 2012 Special Supp]] Q3.1.
