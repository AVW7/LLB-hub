# Parties and Locus Standi

**Locus standi in iudicio** — the capacity to institute or defend legal proceedings — attaches to natural persons (unless under a legal disability) and juristic persons.

## Juristic persons with locus standi

- Companies and close corporations.
- Public higher education institutions (**Higher Education Act 101 of 1997**, s20(4)).
- Public schools (**South African Schools Act 84 of 1996**, s15).

## Trusts — the oddity

A trust is **not** a juristic person. It cannot sue or be sued in its own name — the **trustees** must be joined in their official capacities. A trust is also not an "association, partnership or firm" for Rule 54 purposes.

## Rule 54 — partnerships, firms, syndicates, unincorporated associations

- These entities have no legal personality of their own.
- Rule 54 permits citing the entity in its own trading name in the summons.
- Service under **Rule 9(7)(a)** may be effected at the firm's place of business or on any partner — not every partner individually.
- Any party may demand (by notice) a statement of the partners' names/addresses at the time the cause of action arose; must be delivered within **10 days** of the notice.
- Once disclosed, the action proceeds as if the partners had been named, but all proceedings continue in the firm's name.

## Married persons

Marital power of husbands over wives has been abolished. A person married in community of property may need the other spouse's written consent to litigate regarding the joint estate (**Matrimonial Property Act 88 of 1984**, ss15–17(1)).

## Minors

Lack locus standi. Options: guardian sues on the minor's behalf in a representative capacity; minor sues in his/her own name duly assisted by the guardian; or the court appoints a **curator ad litem** on application.

## Mental incapacity

Also lacks locus standi — a curator ad litem must be appointed (application under **s33 Magistrates' Courts Act**, brought in the MC where proceedings are to be instituted, not on an ex parte basis). Only the **High Court** may declare a person incapable of managing their own affairs.
