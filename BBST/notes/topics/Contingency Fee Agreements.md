# Contingency Fee Agreements

## Core framework

- **Contingency Fees Act 66 of 1997, s2(1)–(5):** contingency fee agreements are permitted only within the Act's limits — a success fee capped at double the normal fee, and, for claims sounding in money, the total success fee further capped at **25% of the amount awarded, excluding costs**. Any agreement concluded outside these limits is unlawful and invalid (*Price Waterhouse Coopers Inc*, SCA).
- **Constitutional challenge:** applying the *Harksen v Lane* two-stage s9 test, the Act differentiates between legal practitioners/clients and other contracting parties, but this differentiation is rationally connected to a legitimate purpose (balancing access to justice against the historic vices of speculative litigation funding) and will probably not be found to amount to unfair discrimination under s9(3).
- **Common law position:** contingency fee agreements were *prohibited* at common law before the Act commenced (23 April 1999) — see *De La Guerre v Ronald Bobroff and Partners Inc*; absent the Act, a legal practitioner is only entitled to a reasonable fee for work actually done. The common law has **not** developed to recognise contingency agreements concluded outside the Act's prescripts (*Mofokeng v RAF* and companion cases, ZAGPJHC) — an attorney cannot deliberately contract outside the Act and then fall back on a common-law contingency claim.

## Recognising the fact pattern

Watch for a fee agreement that was *deliberately* structured outside the Act (e.g. on the stated view that the Act is unconstitutional, or simply ignoring the 25% cap) — this is the trigger to work through both the constitutional argument (likely to fail) and the common-law fallback (also unavailable), leaving the agreement unenforceable as pleaded.
