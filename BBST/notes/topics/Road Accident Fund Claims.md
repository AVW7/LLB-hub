# Road Accident Fund Claims

## Core framework

- **Who can claim:** the injured person personally; a surviving spouse/dependant for loss of support following a fatal collision (acting in her personal capacity and, where there are minor children, also as their guardian/representative); an insurer that has indemnified an owner steps into the insured's shoes by **subrogation** but the action is still brought in the insured's name (no direct contractual link between the wrongdoer and the insurer, and the insured cannot recover the same loss twice).
- **What can be claimed:** past and future medical expenses (may be paid directly to the provider under the amended RAF Act); loss of earnings/support, calculated per Rule 18 with the relevant factors (income before/after, dependency, remaining life/working expectancy) identified and quantified; general damages — since the post-2008 amendment, only recoverable where a **serious injury assessment report** is lodged and the injury is assessed against a prescribed tariff; a passenger conveyed for reward whose "conveyed for reward" status is unproven is treated as an ordinary passenger, with special damages capped at R25 000.
- **The pre-/post-2008 amendment split matters:** the amendment date changes what is recoverable and how — always check the accident date against the amendment before answering (the archive tests this exact distinction more than once).
- **Procedure:** lodgment and service-of-summons deadlines run from the RAF Act's prescription periods; claims may be lodged at the RAF's Pretoria office or (for jurisdiction) wherever the collision occurred; if the RAF neither offers nor repudiates within the prescribed period, summons may be issued; a claim accepted/settled before service of summons may still allow recovery of legal costs depending on when the offer was made and accepted.

## Particulars of claim — recurring structure

Correct citation of the plaintiff personally and (where relevant) in a representative capacity for a minor child; correct citation of the RAF as defendant; date/time/place and description of the collision, including driver and vehicle detail; jurisdiction; description of the defendant driver's negligence; description of the result of the collision (injuries/death); damages set out separately for the plaintiff personally and representatively, properly calculated with the calculation method and relevant factors stated (and any statutory caps applied); allegation of compliance with the RAF Act (lodgment within time, no offer/repudiation); full and correct prayers (capital, interest, costs); clear, unambiguous drafting throughout.

## First-consultation checklist (merits + quantum)

Merits: how/where the collision occurred; the driver's and vehicle's particulars; description of the negligence; whether the police attended/any report exists; any related criminal case. Quantum: income before and after the accident; whether the claimant was on duty at the time; the nature and extent of injuries; medical/hospital particulars; degree of permanent disability; future loss of earnings.

## Related to vicarious liability

Where the wrongdoer was driving an official/employer-owned vehicle (e.g. a police vehicle, or an employee "on the clock"), evaluate whether the employer is vicariously liable in addition to — or instead of — a direct RAF claim (see [[Delictual Claims and Vicarious Liability]]).
