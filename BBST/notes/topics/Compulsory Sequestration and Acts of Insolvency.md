# Compulsory Sequestration and Acts of Insolvency

The single most heavily tested BBST topic — a sequestration/liquidation thread runs through nearly every sitting in the archive, usually bolted onto a matrimonial or CC-liability scenario.

## Core framework

- Under s10–12 of the Insolvency Act 24 of 1936, a court sequestrates a debtor's estate only if satisfied that: (a) the petitioning creditor has a **liquidated claim** of at least R100; (b) the debtor has committed an **act of insolvency** or is factually insolvent; and (c) there is **reason to believe sequestration will be to the advantage of creditors**. Failing any of these the court dismisses the petition, sets aside a provisional order, or requires further proof and postpones (not sine die).
- "Liquidated claim" = an amount agreed upon or capable of speedy and prompt ascertainment (mere matter of calculation) — this is the exact same test used for whether attorney's fees can be set off (see [[Attorneys Fees and Fee Agreements]]).
- **Acts of insolvency (s8):** s8(g) — a *written* notice to a creditor of inability to pay debts, tested by how a reasonable recipient would understand it, and requiring genuine inability (not mere unwillingness) to pay; s8(h) — Gazette publication under s34 that all liabilities are due, where the trader is then actually unable to pay.
- **Advantage to creditors** is assessed by (a) allowing for the anticipated costs of sequestration, then (b) determining whether there is a reasonable prospect of an actual payment to proving creditors — the onus stays on the sequestrating creditor throughout, even once an act of insolvency is proved.

## Founding affidavit — recurring structure

Correct citation and description of the parties; jurisdiction; notice to the Master (and proof of service/publication where the affidavit is opposed or final); the cause of the claim (liquidated debt); the act of insolvency or factual insolvency; a submission that a free residue will likely remain for concurrent creditors after preferent claims are paid; a submission that sequestration is to the advantage of creditors.

## Rehabilitation, CC liability and Debt Review — how they interact

- **Debt Review under the National Credit Act is itself an act of insolvency** and does not bar or stay a sequestration application — a creditor need not first comply with s129 NCA notice requirements before proceeding to sequestrate (*Naidoo v Absa Bank Ltd* 2010 (4) SA 567 (SCA); *FirstRand Bank Ltd v Evans* 2011 (4) SA 597 (KZD)). See [[BBST 2012-06-09]] Q6.2.
- Where a debtor is the sole member of a CC that fails, personal liability under the Close Corporations Act (see [[Personal Liability of CC Members]]) is usually pleaded *together with* CC liquidation and personal sequestration in one application.
- Employees' contracts of service are automatically **suspended** on sequestration (Insolvency Act s38) — no work, no pay, no accrual of benefits during suspension; UIF benefits become available from the date of suspension; the trustee must consult the relevant union/employee representatives before terminating contracts, aimed at reaching consensus on saving the business; absent agreement, suspended contracts lapse 45 days after the trustee's appointment; employees may then claim compensation and severance benefits from the estate.
- A pending sequestration is often timed against a **simultaneous or preceding divorce** — where an application was *served* before a divorce but only *granted* after it, the joint estate as it stood before the divorce is what gets sequestrated; a suspiciously-timed, undisclosed divorce points toward mala fides on a later rescission application (see [[Rescission of Judgment]]).
- **Rehabilitation** of an already-sequestrated estate is a distinct process — see [[Rehabilitation of an Insolvent Estate]].
