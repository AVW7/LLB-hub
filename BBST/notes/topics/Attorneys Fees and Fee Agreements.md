# Attorneys Fees and Fee Agreements

## Core framework

- **A disputed, untaxed bill of costs is not a "liquidated" debt.** An attorney's fee only becomes capable of "speedy and easy proof" (i.e. liquidated) once **taxed** — not merely once invoiced or agreed in principle (*Blakes Maphanga Inc v Outsurance*; *Tredoux v Kellerman*). An attorney therefore **cannot lawfully set off** disputed, untaxed fees against monies collected on the client's behalf; taxation must happen first. This is the same "liquidated claim" concept tested in sequestration applications — see [[Compulsory Sequestration and Acts of Insolvency]].
- **Once taxed**, the taxed bill of costs becomes the cause of action for a simple summons claim; interest runs at the legal/prescribed mora rate; costs are ordinarily sought on the scale appropriate to an undefended claim (party-and-party, absent an attorney-and-own-client agreement).
- **Reasonable fee criteria** (law societies' rules): the amount and importance of the work; the complexity of the matter; the skill, labour and expertise required; the volume/importance of documents perused; the place of rendering services; time expended; the value of the claim; the attorney's experience; the relevant law society tariff (a guide only, not binding); any tariff prescribed under s69(d) of the Attorneys Act; whether fees were incurred through the attorney's own over-caution, negligence or mistake (excludable from the bill); and the importance of the matter to the client.
- **What a proper fee agreement should cover** (to avoid disputes): the parties and their domicilium; the mandate and its scope; the fee basis (attorney-and-own-client) and how it differs from party-and-party costs; disbursements (defined and illustrated); authority to recover/receive/deduct fees and disbursements from capital collected before paying the client the balance; billing frequency; payment terms and interest on overdue accounts; the hourly/tariff rate (excluding VAT/disbursements) and any annual escalation; a fixed window (e.g. 10 days) for the client to dispute a bill, after which it is deemed correct; deposit requirements and consequences of non-payment (including withdrawal); dispute resolution (taxation vs referral to a professional body); a retaining lien on the file for unpaid fees on withdrawal; and personal liability of a signing director/member where the client is a company or CC.
- **Set-off requires two liquidated, mutually-due debts** — an attorney who unilaterally deducts disputed fees from funds collected for a client without first taxing the account has acted unlawfully, exposing the firm to a claim for the improperly withheld amount.
