# Domestic Violence Protection Orders

## Core framework

- **Domestic Violence Act 116 of 1998** provides a protection order remedy for anyone in a **"domestic relationship"** with the abuser — this expressly includes unmarried cohabiting partners, not just spouses, so an abusive ex-partner or a current abusive cohabitant both qualify as respondents.
- **Jurisdiction:** the Magistrates' Court.
- **Interim relief:** an interim protection order is available **ex parte** (without notice to the respondent) where there is a risk of harm to the applicant — appropriate wherever the facts show a real, immediate threat (prior break-ins, assaults, explicit threats to kill, stalking to the applicant's workplace).
- **Procedure in outline:** the applicant applies to the Magistrates' Court for a protection order; if the court is satisfied there is prima facie evidence of domestic violence and undue hardship would result from delay, it issues an interim order together with a warrant of arrest suspended pending non-compliance; the interim order is served on the respondent with a return date for a final hearing at which the respondent may oppose.

## Recognising the fact pattern

Watch for: (1) an *unmarried* cohabiting relationship — students sometimes wrongly assume only spouses qualify, but the Act's "domestic relationship" definition covers cohabitants; (2) escalating conduct (prior incidents, explicit threats, access to a weapon or professional fighting skill) supporting urgency and an ex parte interim order; (3) the abuser following the applicant to their workplace or other locations, relevant to the scope of relief sought.
