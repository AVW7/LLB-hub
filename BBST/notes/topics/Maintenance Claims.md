# Maintenance Claims

## Core framework

- **Child maintenance (within a divorce):** the court weighs the child's previous lifestyle, the child's current needs, and both parents' income/means — both parents are liable for maintenance in proportion to their respective means, and the child's best interests take priority over either parent's preference. See [[Divorce Actions and Settlement Agreements]].
- **Maintenance of Surviving Spouses Act 27 of 1990:** on a literal reading, "survivor" means a spouse to a marriage dissolved *by death* — an unmarried life partner does not qualify. In *Volks v Robinson* the Constitutional Court held it is not unfair discrimination for the Act to distinguish between married and unmarried survivors, because marriage creates *ex lege* reciprocal duties of support that mere cohabitation does not. An unmarried cohabitant's maintenance claim against the estate therefore depends on proving a **de facto reciprocal duty of support actually existed** between the parties (sustained cohabitation, financial support given/received, dependence) — see also *Van der Merwe v RAF* on the s9(1) rationality/equality analysis. This is a fact-intensive enquiry, not an automatic entitlement.

## Recognising the fact pattern

Maintenance questions appear either (a) folded into a divorce particulars of claim (routine, formulaic), or (b) as a standalone "unmarried life partner claims against the estate" scenario testing *Volks v Robinson* — watch for long cohabitation, one partner financially dependent, and no marriage ever concluded.
