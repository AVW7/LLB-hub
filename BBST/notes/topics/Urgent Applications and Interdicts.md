# Urgent Applications and Interdicts

## Core framework

- **Elements of an interdict** (recurring drafting checklist): parties; jurisdiction; locus standi; the undisputed facts; a clear right (or, for an interim interdict, a prima facie right); harm/infringement of that right; absence of another adequate remedy; balance of convenience favouring the applicant; the relief sought; urgency; and costs.
- **Urgent Notice of Motion structure:** standard paragraphs dispensing with the ordinary forms and time periods for service under the Rules; provision for the respondent to give notice of intention to oppose and to file answering/replying affidavits by stated (compressed) dates; relief often split into **Part A** (urgent interim relief, e.g. restraining conduct pending finalisation) and **Part B** (final relief argued later on the ordinary roll).
- **The supporting affidavit** must be based on the applicant's own personal knowledge and must positively establish each interdict element above — a bare assertion of urgency or right is insufficient.
- **A confirmatory affidavit** from a corroborating witness is commonly required/expected alongside the founding affidavit where the founding deponent did not personally witness every fact alleged.
- **Proportionality of the relief sought matters:** the interdict must be targeted at the specific conduct causing harm, not drafted so broadly that it stops an entire lawful activity or demands an impossible guarantee (e.g. interdicting only a specific hazardous element of an event, not the whole event; interdicting only further publication/circulation of specific defamatory material, not all speech about the applicant).
- **Interdicts pair naturally with**: defamation (restraining publication or compelling retraction — see [[Defamation]]); domestic violence/harassment (see [[Domestic Violence Protection Orders]]); and employment/contractual disputes (restoring a relationship pending final resolution).
