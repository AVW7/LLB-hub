# Matrimonial Property Systems

## Core framework — the three systems

- **In community of property** — the default absent an antenuptial contract (ANC); one joint estate; spouses have largely equal powers of administration but need the other's consent for specified transactions (e.g. suretyship — see [[Suretyship and the Matrimonial Property Act]]); special rules apply on divorce and death; spouses remain jointly liable, even after divorce, for debts incurred *during* the marriage with the requisite lawful consent of both (*BP Southern Africa (Pty) Ltd v Viljoen en 'n Ander* 2002 (5) SA 630 (O)) — no claim lies against the joint estate for debts incurred without the other spouse's consent.
- **Accrual system** — requires an ANC; each spouse's estate is kept separate and administered independently during the marriage; on dissolution, the growth ("accrual") of each estate over its value at the start of the marriage is calculated, and the spouse with the smaller accrual has a claim for half the difference against the spouse with the larger accrual; parties may exclude specific assets from the accrual calculation by agreement.
- **Out of community of property (no accrual)** — requires an ANC excluding accrual; estates are kept fully separate throughout and on dissolution; each spouse acts wholly independently and has no claim on the other's estate growth.

## Changing the matrimonial property system during marriage

Spouses may jointly apply to court, by way of an ex parte application, for leave to change their matrimonial property system. The court may grant the order only if satisfied that: there are sound reasons for the change; every creditor owed more than R500 has been given sufficient notice; and no other person will be prejudiced by the change. Procedure: application served on the Registrar of Deeds at least two weeks before the hearing; a notice published in the Government Gazette at least two weeks before the hearing; a rule nisi sent to all creditors; service and publication confirmed by affidavit on the return date; a draft of the proposed antenuptial contract attached to the application.

## Recognising the fact pattern

Matrimonial property questions are rarely tested alone — they are almost always the backdrop to a divorce action (see [[Divorce Actions and Settlement Agreements]]), a sequestration/CC-liability scenario (spouse's consent to suretyship, or a suspiciously-timed divorce ahead of a creditor's claim — see [[Compulsory Sequestration and Acts of Insolvency]]), or an accrual claim in reconvention.
