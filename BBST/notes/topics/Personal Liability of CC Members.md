# Personal Liability of CC Members

## Core framework

The Close Corporations Act enforces compliance largely through self-regulation, backed by personal-liability sanctions for members (and certain other persons) where core statutory duties are breached — limited liability is a benefit of incorporation forfeited on serious breach.

- **s63** — joint and several personal liability for every debt incurred while an accounting officer position is vacant for more than 6 months.
- **s64** — liability for reckless or fraudulent trading.
- **s65** — abuse of the corporation's separate juristic personality: the court may declare the CC deemed not to be a juristic person as regards specified rights/obligations/liabilities, making the relevant conduct a personal venture of the members.
- New Companies Act s129(7) (by analogy, referenced in the memos): where directors/members have reasonable grounds to believe the entity is in financial distress, they must place it under business rescue, liquidate it, or notify shareholders/creditors/employees/unions of the financial difficulty and their reasons for not doing either.

## The leading case — *Airport Cold Storage (Pty) Ltd v Ebrahim*

A CC bought goods on credit, failed to pay, and was liquidated with nothing left for the unpaid creditor. The creditor then sued the CC's sole member and a non-member manager/officer personally, relying on ss63, 64 and 65. The court found: the CC's business was run informally and interchangeably with the defendants' other businesses (shared premises, bank accounts, staff, phone numbers); no proper books were kept as required by s56; invoices didn't identify the CC; income wasn't banked in the CC's own account; VAT was collected from customers but not remitted to SARS. Held: the defendants took the *benefits* of incorporation without complying with its *burdens*, so they could not hide behind the corporate veil — the court granted a s65 declaratory order and held them personally liable for the CC's debt plus interest and costs. This was the first case granting relief of this nature under s65.

## Recognising the fact pattern

The archive reuses a near-identical "red flag" checklist across sittings to signal that personal liability is in play: no accounting officer appointed; no formal accounting records; VAT collected from customers but not paid to SARS; all income paid into a *member's personal* bank account rather than the CC's own; assets sold to the members themselves (or their associates) below market value shortly before a creditor moves against the CC; and a conveniently-timed divorce or other disposal of personal assets by the member facing exposure.
