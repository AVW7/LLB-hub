# Rehabilitation of an Insolvent Estate

## Core framework — rehabilitation timelines

An insolvent may become rehabilitated:
- **Automatically after 10 years** from the date of sequestration;
- Possibly from **12 months** after confirmation of the account (via a formal application);
- **3 years** if the debtor was previously sequestrated;
- **5 years** where the debtor was convicted of an insolvency-related offence;
- **6 months** where there are no outstanding proved claims, no prior sequestration, and no fraud.

## Application for rehabilitation — affidavit structure

Correct heading for an ex parte High Court application; the affidavit made personally by the applicant, with full particulars and confirmation the facts are within personal knowledge; reference to the applicant's own sequestration order; confirmation of no prior sequestration and no insolvency-related conviction; background of the insolvency consistent with the facts, with the final liquidation and distribution account attached; an allegation of complete surrender of the estate; current employment and income; current assets (the "new" post-sequestration estate) with an account of income and expenses; current liabilities; an allegation that security was lodged (s125 of the Insolvency Act); allegations of adequate notice to the Master and to the trustee (with proof attached); an allegation that notice was published in the Government Gazette (with proof); the prayer that the court rehabilitate the applicant under s124(4); the deponent's signature and a Commissioner of Oaths certificate.

## Practice notes

- Rehabilitation only makes sense once sequestration has already occurred — always confirm which stage of the insolvency lifecycle a question is testing (sequestration itself vs rehabilitation afterward).
- The archive tests the *same* rehabilitation question, near-verbatim, twice with slightly different "further information" facts (a fresh salary and asset position) — always re-apply the timeline list to the new numbers given rather than assuming the answer repeats unchanged.
