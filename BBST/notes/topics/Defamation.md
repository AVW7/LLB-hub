# Defamation

## Core framework

- **Particulars of claim — recurring structure:** citation of the Plaintiff and the Defendant(s) (potentially joined jointly and severally, e.g. the individual publisher and their employer vicariously — "the one paying, the other absolved"); jurisdiction; the specific defamatory statement or image and its defamatory meaning; publication (how and to whom it was communicated — broadcast, SMS, social media); the resulting damages; the letter of demand and the defendant's failure to respond; and prayers for judgment for capital, interest at the legal rate *a tempore morae*, costs, and further/alternative relief.
- **Prior-restraint interdicts against publication** (stopping a story before it runs) are a serious remedy courts grant sparingly, because of freedom-of-expression considerations — the supporting affidavit must establish urgency, a clear right, the (alleged) falsity of the statements, that damages after the fact would be an inadequate remedy, and that the balance of convenience favours the applicant. See [[Urgent Applications and Interdicts]].
- **Interdicts after the fact** (compelling removal/retraction of already-published defamatory material) follow the same interdict elements: a prima facie or clear right, harm, absence of another adequate remedy, and balance of convenience — commonly sought alongside damages, not instead of them.

## Recognising the fact pattern

Defamation questions test either (a) a straightforward particulars-of-claim drafting exercise after a demand goes unanswered, or (b) an urgent interdict to stop or reverse publication (before or after the fact) — always check whether vicarious liability of an employer/broadcaster is also in play.
