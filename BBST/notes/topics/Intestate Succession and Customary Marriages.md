# Intestate Succession and Customary Marriages

## Core framework

- **Validity of a customary marriage — s3(1) Recognition of Customary Marriages Act 120 of 1998:** both spouses must be 18 or older, both must consent, and the marriage must be negotiated, entered into and celebrated in accordance with customary law.
- **Further customary marriages — s7(6)/(8):** a husband who wishes to enter a further customary marriage after an existing one must apply to court for an order approving a written contract regulating the matrimonial property system of all his marriages, joining the existing and prospective wives to that application. A "marriage" concluded without this court process — even where lobolo negotiations occurred and produced a written record — is **invalid**.
- **Registration is not validity — s4(9):** failure to register a customary marriage does not affect its validity (*Wormald NO v Kambule*). **s4(7)** allows an application to court to register a marriage, and late registration of an otherwise valid marriage may be condoned even after the death of a spouse.
- **Intestate succession:** the Intestate Succession Act 81 of 1987 governs devolution where there is no valid will; a valid customary marriage and its children qualify as intestate heirs; an *invalid* customary "marriage" does not create heirship for the putative spouse personally, though a child born of that relationship may still qualify as the deceased's descendant. Reference should also be made to *Bhe v Magistrate, Khayelitsha*, the Recognition of Customary Marriages Act and the Reform of Customary Law of Succession Act.
- **Unregistered domestic partners:** under the (draft) Domestic Partnerships Bill framework, an unregistered domestic partner may apply to court for a share of the intestate estate equal to a child's share (or a statutory cap, whichever is greater); where an unregistered domestic partner's claim competes with a valid customary spouse's claim, the court decides what is just and equitable on the facts.

## Recognising the fact pattern

These questions typically present a deceased with **multiple overlapping relationships** — a registered or unregistered customary marriage, an unmarried life partner, and/or a "second wife" whose lobolo negotiations were never taken to court — and ask the candidate to rank who qualifies as an heir. Work through each relationship's validity separately before applying the Intestate Succession Act.
