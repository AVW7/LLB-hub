# Divorce Actions and Settlement Agreements

## Core framework

- **Jurisdiction:** pleaded under s2(1)(a) of the Divorce Act 70 of 1979 (a South African court has jurisdiction if either spouse is domiciled or ordinarily resident in its area on the date the action is instituted).
- **Particulars of claim — recurring structure:** correct citation of Plaintiff and Defendant; jurisdiction under the Divorce Act; particulars of the marriage (date, place, regime) and of any children; best-interests-of-the-child averments — typically that both parties retain full parental responsibilities and rights of guardianship, that one party retains care and primary residence, that the other's contact rights are defined (or limited, with reasons, where warranted), and a stated maintenance contribution; the **Regulation 2 affidavit** under the Mediation in Certain Divorce Matters Act 24 of 1987 attached as an annexure whenever minor children are involved; pleaded reasons for the breakdown of the marriage; division of the joint estate (or, where a settlement exists, incorporation of the settlement agreement as an order of court); correct prayers.
- **Settlement agreements** reduce the parties' informal agreement (asset division, maintenance, contact) to a document the court is asked to make an order of court alongside the decree.
- **Maintenance for children:** the court weighs the child's previous lifestyle, the child's needs, and both parents' income/means (both parents are liable for maintenance in proportion to their means) — the child's best interests come first. See [[Maintenance Claims]].
- **Accrual claims** in reconvention: where the marriage is subject to the accrual system, a financially weaker spouse may claim 50% of the amount by which the other's accrual exceeds her own — see [[Matrimonial Property Systems]].

## Recognising the fact pattern

The "amicable divorce with an informal settlement to be drafted up" scenario recurs with different names but an identical skeleton: citation → jurisdiction → marriage/children particulars → best-interests averments → Regulation 2 affidavit → division of assets → prayers. Watch for two variants that change the analysis: (1) a **suspiciously-timed** divorce used to shield a joint estate from a creditor (cross-reference [[Compulsory Sequestration and Acts of Insolvency]]); and (2) a spouse who has **resigned or become destitute** because of the marriage, raising maintenance pendente lite / accrual arguments.
