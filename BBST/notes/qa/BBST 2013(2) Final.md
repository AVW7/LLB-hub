# BBST — 2013(2) Final

Source Statement of Facts: [[BBST2013(2)Statement of Facts.doc]]
Source question paper: [[BBST2013(2)FinalQ.doc]]
Source marking memo: [[BBST2013(2)Q&A.doc]] (titled "FINAL MEMORANDUM")

## Statement of Facts

You are a candidate attorney in Cape Town, advising Robin Radebe, executor of the late Pieter Radebe's estate.

**Clients/parties:** Robin Radebe (42, artist); Pieter Radebe (58, business analyst at Vodacom, died October 2012 by suicide, resided with Robin); two children, Jason (8) and Emma (6).

**Legal Issue 1 (fees):** Pen n Pencil Attorneys ("PnP") took Pieter's personal injury claim (traumatic brain injury, loss of use of arms/legs) against Nigel Mansell (TV presenter, admitted speeding) on a contingency fee of 25% of any settlement/judgment plus expenses — the agreement was concluded *outside* the Contingency Fees Act 66 of 1997, on PnP's view that the Act is unconstitutional. The claim settled for R5,2 million plus party-and-party costs; PnP now wants R1,3 million plus costs as its fee.

**Legal Issue 2 (life partnership maintenance):** Pieter and Robin were unmarried life partners since 1994 ("living together as man and wife"); Pieter supported Robin financially and via his medical aid; Robin cared for the children. Pieter never executed a will (he died intestate as to Robin — see Q5). Robin wants to claim maintenance from the estate under the Maintenance of Surviving Spouses Act 27 of 1990.

**Legal Issue 3 (customary marriages):** Pieter also had a customary marriage to Jane Moeketse (concluded 1996, certificate issued 2002, two children) and, separately, entered a relationship with Brenda Mbeki in 2001 (one child, Lucas). In 2002 lobolo negotiations for Brenda as a "second wife" produced a handwritten letter, but this second customary marriage was never registered.

**Legal Issue 4 (suretyship):** Pieter, a former director of Kyoto Petroleum (wound up, owing ~R50 million to Protocol Petroleum), signed an unlimited deed of suretyship in favour of Protocol on 15 December 2009 without informing Robin, Jane or Brenda. Protocol now moves against the estate; the estate wants to oppose.

**Legal Issue 5 (vehicle damage):** Pieter's vehicle was damaged (R582 000) in the same accident that injured him; Nigel signed a written acknowledgement of debt for R400 000 (16 monthly instalments of R25 000 from 1 March 2010) but has made no payments and no action was taken despite clear instructions given on 2 November 2010.

## Q1 — Contingency fee agreement: constitutionality and common law (20)

> [!question] Question
> Robin is unhappy to pay R1,3 million plus costs as PnP's contingency fee. 1.1 Advise on the constitutionality, legality and enforceability of the fee agreement under the Contingency Fees Act (10). 1.2 Advise on its legality and enforceability under the common law (10).

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 The Act (s2(1)) permits contingency fee agreements only within the limits of s2(2)–5 (success fee capped at double the normal fee, and — for money claims — the total success fee capped at 25% of the amount awarded, excluding costs). Any agreement outside those limits is unlawful and invalid (*Price Waterhouse Coopers Inc*, SCA). On the constitutional challenge: applying the *Harksen v Lane* two-stage test under s9, the Act differentiates between legal practitioners/clients and others, but this differentiation is rationally connected to a legitimate purpose (balancing access to justice against the historic vices of contingency fees) and probably does not amount to unfair discrimination under s9(3).
> 1.2 Contingency fee agreements were prohibited at common law before the Act came into force (23 April 1999) — *De La Guerre v Ronald Bobroff and Partners Inc*; a legal practitioner was only entitled to a reasonable fee for work actually done. The common law has not developed to recognise contingency agreements outside the Act's prescripts (*Mofokeng v RAF* and companion cases, ZAGPJHC). PnP's agreement, concluded deliberately outside the Act, is therefore unlawful and unenforceable against the estate.

## Q2 — Maintenance of Surviving Spouses Act: does a life partner qualify? (10)

> [!question] Question
> Does Robin qualify as a "spouse" under the Maintenance of Surviving Spouses Act? Evaluate her prospects of a maintenance claim against the estate.

> [!success]- Answer
> A literal reading of the Act (defining "survivor" as a spouse to a marriage dissolved by death) excludes Robin, since she and Pieter never married. In *Volks v Robinson* the Constitutional Court held it was not unfair discrimination for the Act to distinguish between married and unmarried survivors, because marriage creates ex lege obligations that cohabitation does not — an unmarried cohabitant's claim depends on proof of an actual reciprocal duty of support having existed. The decisive question is therefore whether such a reciprocal duty of support existed in fact between Robin and Pieter (drawing also on *Van der Merwe v RAF* on rationality/equality analysis under s9(1)). On these facts (17+ years of cohabitation, Pieter's ongoing financial support, Robin's dependence and childcare role), a court may be persuaded a de facto reciprocal duty of support existed, strengthening — but not guaranteeing — Robin's claim.

## Q3 — Customary marriage validity and late registration (15)

> [!question] Question
> 3.1 Did a valid customary marriage exist between Pieter and Brenda, and what is its status (10)? 3.2 Could that marriage be registered after Pieter's death (5)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 Section 3(1) of the Recognition of Customary Marriages Act 120 of 1998 requires both spouses to be 18+, both to consent, and the marriage to be negotiated/entered into/celebrated in accordance with customary law. Section 7(6) requires a husband entering a further customary marriage to obtain a court order approving a written contract regulating the matrimonial property system of his marriages, joining the existing and prospective wives (s7(8)). On these facts, no such court application was made and the marriage was not negotiated/celebrated in accordance with customary law — the second customary marriage to Brenda is therefore invalid, though the parties' good-faith conduct and the lobolo negotiations are relevant considerations.
> 3.2 Section 4(9): failure to register a customary marriage does not affect its validity (*Wormald NO v Kambule*). Section 4(7) allows an application to court to register the marriage; late registration of a valid customary marriage may be condoned even after death.

Related: [[Intestate Succession and Customary Marriages]]

## Q4 — Suretyship and spousal consent under the Matrimonial Property Act (15)

> [!question] Question
> Was Pieter required to obtain Robin's/Jane's/Brenda's written consent to the deed of suretyship under the Matrimonial Property Act 88 of 1984 for it to be valid?

> [!success]- Answer
> Section 15(2)(h) of the Matrimonial Property Act requires a spouse married in community of property to obtain the other spouse's written consent before binding themselves as surety. Section 15(6) exempts transactions entered in the ordinary course of the surety's profession, trade or business (*Amalgamated Banks of SA Bpk v De Goede*). Robin and Pieter were never married (no community of property arises); Pieter's customary marriage to Jane, depending on its property regime, may or may not attract s15 at all (s7 of the Recognition Act governs proprietary consequences of customary marriages separately); and the invalid "marriage" to Brenda creates no consent requirement. A creditor relying on a suretyship need only show it was executed by someone with full contractual capacity — it is not part of the creditor's cause of action to prove the surety was unmarried or that consent was given or unnecessary (s15(9)).

## Q5 — Intestate succession (20)

> [!question] Question
> Pieter died intestate. Jane wants an opinion on the potential intestate beneficiaries.

> [!success]- Answer
> Under the (draft) Domestic Partnerships Bill framework discussed in the memo, an unregistered domestic partner may apply to court for a share of the intestate estate equal to a child's share (or a statutory cap, whichever is greater); where there is a dispute between an unregistered domestic partner and a customary spouse, the court decides what is just and equitable. Reference should also be made to *Bhe v Magistrate, Khayelitsha*, the Recognition of Customary Marriages Act and the Reform of Customary Law of Succession Act — the valid customary marriage to Jane and her children qualify as intestate heirs under the Intestate Succession Act 81 of 1987; Robin's position depends on the outcome of the Q2 maintenance analysis and any recognised domestic partnership claim; the invalid Brenda "marriage" does not create intestate heirship for Brenda personally, though her child Lucas may still qualify as Pieter's descendant.

Related: [[Intestate Succession and Customary Marriages]]

## Q6 — Recovering the vehicle damage; ethical duty on the excluded claim (20)

> [!question] Question
> 6.1 Draft only the Statement of Facts/Particulars of Claim (with citations, not the summons) to recover the amount due for the damage to Pieter's vehicle (15). 6.2 Advise Robin on recovering any amount you cannot include in the action against Nigel, addressing your ethical and legal duty and the reason for the exclusion (5).

> [!success]- Answer
> 6.1 Citation: Plaintiff — the Estate of Pieter Radebe; Defendant — Nigel Mansell. Plead jurisdiction; that the claim is based on the written acknowledgement of debt (not the collision itself); quantum R400 000 (noting part may have prescribed); interest at the prescribed/legal rate; costs.
> 6.2 The instruction to sue was given on 2 November 2010 but no action was taken — part of the claim on the acknowledgement of debt has since prescribed. This raises a professional negligence issue: the attorney must not conceal the negligence from the client, must advise the client to seek independent advice/refer the matter to another attorney, and should point Robin toward a possible claim against the Attorneys Fidelity Fund/professional indemnity cover rather than acknowledge negligence directly.
