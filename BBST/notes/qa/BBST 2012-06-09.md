## Q1 — Changing matrimonial property system during marriage (10)

> [!question] Question
> Mr and Mrs X, married by customary union in 1998, want to change their matrimonial property system before Mr X starts a business, to protect Mrs X's property. Explain whether this is possible, the requirements, and the procedure.

> [!success]- Answer
> Spouses may jointly apply to court for leave to change their matrimonial property system. The court may grant an order if satisfied that: there are sound reasons for the change; all creditors owed more than R500 were given sufficient notice; and no other person will be prejudiced. Procedure: by way of an ex parte application; served on the Registrar of Deeds at least two weeks before the hearing; a notice published in the Government Gazette at least two weeks before the hearing; the rule nisi sent to all creditors; service and publication confirmed by affidavit on the return date; a draft of the proposed antenuptial contract attached to the application.

## Q2 — Botha divorce: particulars of claim (25)

> [!question] Question
> Barend Botha's marriage broke down due to his wife's alcoholism (repeated failed rehabilitation); she has moved in with a new boyfriend. Two children (14 and 16) live with the client, who wants primary residence as he can provide stability. Both spouses earn the same income; children's expenses are ~R4 000/month. Draft the particulars of claim.

> [!success]- Answer
> Memo checks (impression-marked): did the student draft particulars of claim for a divorce action, in clear language and correct order; correctly cite Plaintiff and Defendant; plead jurisdiction under s2(1)(a) of the Divorce Act 70 of 1979; particulars of the marriage and children; aver that it is in the children's best interests that both parties retain full parental responsibilities and rights of guardianship, that the Plaintiff retains care and primary residence, that the Defendant's contact rights are defined, and that the Defendant contribute R2 000/month towards maintenance; attach the Regulation 2 affidavit under the Mediation in Certain Divorce Matters Act 24 of 1987 as Annexure "A"; state reasons for the breakdown of the marriage; and correct prayers.

> [!question] Question

## Q4 — Moloi: RAF particulars of claim (25)

> [!question] Question
> Mrs Moloi, a homemaker with one 14-year-old daughter (Innocence, who wants to study nursing). Mr Moloi, a civil engineer earning ~R500 000/year, killed when a delivery truck skipped a red light on 24 May 2008. Past medical expenses R40 000. Draft the particulars of claim and prayers (own facts permitted where necessary).

> [!success]- Answer
> Memo checks: correct citation of the plaintiff personally and in her representative capacity for the minor child; correct citation of the RAF; date/time/place and description of the collision and negligence; jurisdiction (place of collision or RAF's Pretoria office); description of the result of the collision; damages set out separately for the plaintiff personally and in her representative capacity, properly calculated per Rule 18 with the relevant factors identified; allegation of compliance with the RAF Act; full and correct prayers; clear, unambiguous drafting.

## Q5 — Thandi: minibus RAF claim, amendment-date distinction (20)

> [!question] Question
> Thandi, injured in a minibus rollover on 9 December 2011 (broken rib, facial laceration, one night in hospital, fully healed with no complications). 5.1 Advise on the legal position under the RAF Act and common law (10). 5.2 Would the outcome differ if the accident happened on 1 July 2008 (ignoring prescription) (10)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 5.1 Under the amended RAF Act the fund may pay past/future medical expenses directly to the provider; a claim for general damages requires lodgment of a serious injury report and is assessed against a tariff; the claimant can only claim past/future medical expenses and loss of earnings, with no right to claim general damages directly from the vehicle owner. 5.2 As a passenger conveyed for reward, Thandi's claim for special and general damages is limited unless she proves she was conveyed for reward — if not proven, she is treated as an ordinary passenger limited to special damages capped at R25 000.

Related: [[Road Accident Fund Claims]]

## Q6 — Mr Broke: sequestration + Debt Review interaction (20)

> [!question] Question
> Mr Broke, sole-proprietor transport operator, in financial difficulty (fuel prices, driver strike, two hijacked trucks). Owes your client, Mr Fix It, R50 000 (unsecured) for mechanical services and parts. Mr Broke asked for a payment postponement while getting "back on his feet" but is seen disposing of property. 6.1 Draft the application and founding affidavit for sequestration (15). 6.2 Would you be able to proceed if Mr Broke had begun Debt Review proceedings under the National Credit Act? Motivate (5).

> [!success]- Answer
> 6.1 Memo checks: Notice of Motion and founding affidavit for sequestration; clear language; correct citation and jurisdiction; notice to the Master; correct and complete cause of the claim; allegations on the act of insolvency; that appointment of a trustee allows recovery/investigation of disposed or concealed assets for creditors' benefit; that a free residue will likely remain for concurrent creditors after preferent claims; submission that sequestration is to the advantage of creditors.
> 6.2 Yes — an application for debt review is itself an act of insolvency; a creditor need not comply with s129 NCA notice requirements before sequestration proceedings begin; debt review does not stop or prohibit sequestration, though insolvency and advantage to creditors must still be proved. See *Naidoo v Absa Bank Ltd* 2010 (4) SA 567 (SCA); *FirstRand Bank Ltd v Evans* 2011 (4) SA 597 (KZD).
