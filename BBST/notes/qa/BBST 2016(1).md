## Statement of Facts (from BBST2018(2) Statement of Facts.pdf)

You are an attorney at Grant & Radebe Incorporated, Pretoria, acting for Mr Tsepo Mavuso, a radio presenter at Station FM (owned by Music Broadcasting (Pty) Ltd). Mavuso, married to Jane with two children studying at UCT, had earlier instructed the firm to defend a simple-summons claim by Peter Smith t/a "The Wedding Planner" for R95 451,99 (goods sold and delivered) — Mavuso maintained he'd paid by EFT but omitted the reference number; his attorneys asked Smith's attorneys to hold the summons for 10 days pending proof of payment, but more than a month elapsed unnoticed and **default judgment was granted against Mavuso on 29 May 2016**, with a warrant of execution sent to the sheriff on 30 May 2016.

Separately, on 14 May 2016 Mavuso posted on Facebook: "I don't think people should vote, maybe it would be better to spend your time doing something more productive." This drew public criticism, including from **Mrs Joan Osborn** of a competing station, 24/7 FM (owned by New Generation Radio (Pty) Ltd, ~300m from Station FM), who called Mavuso a "sell-out" and "incompetent" on-air and said he should be "sent to Robben Island for a few years."

## Legal Issue 1 — Fee agreement (20)

> [!question] Question
> Mavuso feels his employment termination was unlawful and wants advice on the public statements he made, plus wants to sue the New Generation Radio employee for defamation. Draft a fee agreement with Mavuso covering all required clauses.

> [!success]- Answer
> The memo's model fee agreement covers: names/addresses (domicilium); the mandate and its scope; that fees are charged on an attorney-and-own-client basis for necessary/desirable services; that the difference between party-and-party and attorney-and-own-client costs has been explained; disbursements (defined and illustrated); authority to recover, receive and deduct fees/disbursements from capital collected before paying the client the balance; billing frequency at the attorney's discretion; 30-day payment terms with interest on overdue accounts; the hourly rate excluding VAT/disbursements; annual fee escalation from a stated date; a 10-day window for the client to dispute a debit note, after which it is deemed fair and reasonable; a deposit requirement, with non-payment as grounds for withdrawal; dispute resolution by taxation or referral to a professional body at the attorney's election; a third-party costs-drawing/taxation mechanism at the client's cost; a retaining lien on the file for unpaid fees on withdrawal; the domicilium clause; and personal liability of a signing director/member where the client is a company/CC.

## Legal Issue 2 — Urgent interdict for reinstatement and retraction (20)

> [!question] Question
> Mavuso wants urgent relief to restore his contractual relationship with his employer and to compel retraction of harmful statements made about him after termination. Draft the necessary document and annexures (no headings/addresses required).

> [!success]- Answer
> A two-part urgent Notice of Motion: Part A for urgent reinstatement of the contractual relationship and an order that the Respondent retract all harmful statements, operating as an interim order pending finalisation, with costs; standard urgent-application notice paragraphs on dispensing with forms/service and the respondent's obligation to give notice of intention to oppose and file answering/replying affidavits by stated dates. The supporting affidavit must be within the applicant's own knowledge and establish a prima facie or actual right, infringement of that right, and the absence of another remedy — contents of an interdict application: parties, jurisdiction, locus standi, undisputed facts, clear right, harm, no other remedy, balance of convenience favouring the applicant, remedy sought, urgency, and costs. A supplementary confirmatory affidavit from Mr R Solomons is also required.

## Legal Issue 3 — Sequestration risk from public statements (20)

> [!question] Question
> Mavuso is worried his public statements about his financial situation could found a sequestration application. Evaluate all requirements/factors relevant to a sequestration application against him.

> [!success]- Answer
> Section 9/12 of the Insolvency Act: the petitioning creditor must establish (a) a liquidated claim of at least R100; (b) an act of insolvency or actual insolvency; (c) reason to believe sequestration will advantage creditors — failing which the court dismisses the petition, sets aside a provisional order, or requires further proof and postpones (not sine die). "Liquidated claim" means an amount agreed or capable of speedy/prompt ascertainment. Acts of insolvency under s8(g) require a *written* notice of inability to pay debts, tested from a reasonable recipient's perspective, distinguishing genuine inability from mere unwillingness to pay; s8(h) requires Gazette publication under s34 confirming actual inability to pay. Students must weigh whether Mavuso's Facebook statement (which is about voting, not his finances) and the default judgment against him amount to any such notice or publication — on these facts neither element is clearly satisfied by a social-media post about an unrelated topic, though the default judgment itself is a relevant factual backdrop the examiner expects to be discussed.

Related: [[Compulsory Sequestration and Acts of Insolvency]]

## Legal Issue 4 — Defamation claim against Joan Osborn (20)

> [!question] Question
> Mavuso instructs you to sue over Joan Osborn's on-air statements after a letter of demand goes unanswered. Draft particulars of claim to recover R100 000 for defamation.

> [!success]- Answer
> Plead: Plaintiff and both Defendants (Osborn personally and, potentially, her employer/station vicariously); jurisdiction; the specific defamatory statement(s) and their defamatory meaning; publication (broadcast on-air); resulting damages; the letter of demand; and prayers for judgment against both defendants jointly and severally (the one paying, the other absolved), for capital, interest at the legal rate a tempore morae, costs, and further/alternative relief.

## Legal Issue 5 — Rescinding the default judgment (20)

> [!question] Question
> 5.1 Draft a letter to Mavuso informing him of the default judgment and its implications, requesting instructions and documentation (5). 5.2 Draft the application and annexures to remove the default judgment (15).

> [!success]- Answer
> 5.1 An impression-marked general correspondence exercise — advise of the judgment, its consequences (execution risk, credit record impact), and request the payment proof/reference and any correspondence with Smith's attorneys to support an application to rescind.
> 5.2 The rescission application should be brought in the names of both Mr and Mrs Mavuso where the debt affects the joint estate; the deposing attorney must deal squarely with the two Rule 31(2)(b)-style requirements — a reasonable explanation for the default (not wilful or reckless) and a bona fide defence to the claim (proof of payment, absent only the reference number).
