# BBST — 2013(1)

Source question paper: [[BBST2013(1)Q.doc]]
Source combined memo: [[BBST2013(1)Q&A.doc]]

Facts and questions are combined in a single document (Statement of Facts + Questions, 4h30, 100 marks).

## Statement of Facts

John Biko and Sally Field married in community of property in 1998; joint estate valued at R450 000 at marriage, now R315 000 (gambling/travel losses). Mr Biko is a plumber (R15 000/month); Mrs Biko a receptionist (R6 500/month). They bought a house (bond R650 000, R335 000 outstanding, market value now R700 000). One child, Pieter Biko (born 2002).

John Biko is sole member of J&S Motors CC, which buys/resells used vehicles. On 1 February 2012 the CC (via employee Michael Fox) bought four vehicles on a R500 000 loan from Quantum Bank. The CC also owes SARS (income tax R75 000, VAT R45 000), unpaid salaries (R25 000), rental arrears (R40 000) and a Star Newspaper judgment (R55 000). On 5 October 2012 the CC sold the vehicles at a loss to John Biko, Sally Biko and Michael Fox personally. The CC kept no formal accounting records, never appointed an accounting officer, and all money was paid into Mr Biko's personal account. Quantum Bank wants to liquidate the CC and hold the Bikos personally liable. The Bikos want to oppose, deny personal liability, and instruct a divorce to be expedited (uncontested, joint estate awarded to Mrs Biko including the house, R10 000/month maintenance) without alerting the Bank. They hold a joint will leaving the estate to the survivor, or to Pieter if they die simultaneously.

## Q1 — Matrimonial property basics (20)

> [!question] Question
> 1.1 Do spouses married in community of property, after divorce, remain liable for debts incurred by a former spouse during the marriage (5)? 1.2 Name the different matrimonial property systems available in South Africa and explain the practical implications of each (15).

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 Yes — spouses married in community of property remain bound and liable for debts incurred during the marriage after its dissolution, provided the debts were incurred with the requisite lawful consent of both parties (*BP Southern Africa (Pty) Ltd v Viljoen en 'n Ander* 2002 (5) SA 630 (O)). No claim lies against the joint estate where joint consent was lacking.
> 1.2 In community of property (automatic absent an ANC; joint estate; equal powers subject to consent requirements; special death/divorce provisions); accrual system (requires an ANC; each estate's growth over original capital is shared on dissolution; parties act independently); out of community of property (ANC; estates kept fully separate; independent action).

## Q2 — CC personal liability, sequestration, divorce timing (45)

> [!question] Question
> 2.1 Could Mr Biko be held personally liable for the CC's debts (10)? 2.2 Identify and draft the wording of the notice needed to obtain liquidation/sequestration (5). 2.3 Draft the founding affidavit for the liquidation/sequestration orders, motivating why sought (15). 2.4 What are the position/rights of the workers in the liquidation (10)? 2.5 The divorce decree was granted before the provisional sequestration order was adjudicated — what are the implications (5)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 The Close Corporations Act imposes personal liability where core statutory duties are breached: s63 (accounting officer vacancy exceeding 6 months → joint and several liability for debts during the vacancy); s64 (reckless/fraudulent trading); s65 (abuse of juristic personality — court may deem the CC not a juristic person). See *Airport Cold Storage (Pty) Ltd v Ebrahim* — corporate veil pierced where the CC operated with corporate confusion, no proper books, VAT collected but not remitted, and income paid into personal accounts rather than the CC's own account; the court granted a s65 declaratory order and held the individuals personally liable. Also consider s129(7) of the new Companies Act (financial distress triggers business rescue/liquidation/notice obligations).
> 2.2 A Notice of Motion for both the liquidation of the CC and the sequestration of the Bikos' joint estate (since there was no indication the divorce had been finalised at that stage), referring to the supporting affidavit and correct prayers, drafted in clear language.
> 2.3 Founding affidavit must correctly cite the parties, plead jurisdiction, allege notice to the Master, allege compliance with formalities (publication/creditor notice attached), plead the cause of the claim, plead the act of insolvency or factual insolvency, and submit that sequestration is to the advantage of creditors (free residue after preferent claims).
> 2.4 Section 38 of the Insolvency Act: employees' contracts of service are automatically suspended on sequestration (no duty to render services, no remuneration or benefits accrue); employees qualify for UIF benefits from suspension; the trustee/liquidator may terminate contracts after consulting with the relevant collective-bargaining party/union/employees; consultation must aim at consensus on measures to save the business; unless otherwise agreed, suspended contracts terminate 45 days after the trustee's appointment; employees may claim compensation for loss from the suspension/termination and severance benefits under the BCEA.
> 2.5 The timing raises suspicion of a divorce of convenience; the divorce nonetheless divides the joint estate; where the sequestration application was served before the divorce but granted after it, the joint estate as it existed prior to the divorce is what gets sequestrated.

## Q3 — Rescission of the sequestration/liquidation order (10)

> [!question] Question
> 3.1 What must the Bikos aver in an application for rescission to succeed (5)? 3.2 What order(s) might the court make at the rescission hearing, including possible costs orders (5)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 Rescission may be sought under Rule 42 (judgments erroneously sought/granted in a party's absence, containing patent errors, or granted by common mistake), Rule 31(2)(b) (default judgment, 20-day window, good cause, bona fide defence), or the common law. For a *sequestration* order specifically, s149(2) of the Insolvency Act empowers the court to rescind or vary its own order, but only on unusual, special or exceptional circumstances (*Abdurahman v Estate Abdurahman* 1959 (1) SA 872 (C)); there is no fixed time limit for a s149(2) application.
> 3.2 The manner of conducting the divorce (failure to disclose pending sequestration proceedings to the divorce court or the Bank) points to mala fides and an intention to delay creditors' claims — not exceptional circumstances justifying rescission. The court may instead vary the order *mero motu* under Rule 42 to sequestrate the individual estates post-divorce. Costs: party-and-party, attorney-and-client, no order, or reserved — reasoned on the facts.

Related: [[Rescission of Judgment]]

## Q4 — Post-divorce motor collision: SAPS letter of demand + will (25)

> [!question] Question
> Two months after their divorce the Bikos, on holiday together, are hit by a police vehicle (driven by Sergeant Ben Dube, running late for a court appearance) which failed to stop at a robot-controlled intersection. Mr Biko dies instantly; Mrs Biko dies two weeks later in hospital.
> 4.1 Draft the letter to SAPS to recover the damage to the vehicle driven by Mr Biko, including headings and completing the cause of action (15). 4.2 Who inherits under the joint will, given the timing of the deaths and the prior divorce? Discuss the effect where death occurs within three months of the divorce order versus after (10).

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 Letter sent to the National Commissioner of Police under the Institution of Legal Proceedings against Certain Organs of State Act; confirms the mandate (acting for Call-a-Plumber CC, the vehicle owner); gives full accident detail (place/date/vehicles/driver); claims the damage as beyond reasonable repair; pleads vicarious liability (SAPS liable for its employee acting in the course and scope of employment/furthering SAPS's interests); confirms compliance with the Act; states summons will follow if unpaid within one month.
> 4.2 No presumption of simultaneous death exists in our law by age or sex — the timing of each death must be analysed. A change of marital status has no automatic effect on a will's validity — it remains valid until revoked. However, s2B of the Wills Act deems the death of a testator within three months of a divorce to be a temporary revocation of any bequest to the former spouse, unless the will shows an intention to benefit the ex-spouse regardless of the divorce. If death occurs after the three-month window, this deeming provision does not apply and the bequest to the (by then) ex-spouse under the joint will stands as originally drafted (subject to further evaluation of the "simultaneous death" clause given the two-week gap between the deaths).
