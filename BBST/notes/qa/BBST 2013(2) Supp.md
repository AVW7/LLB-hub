# BBST — 2013(2) Supplementary

Source Statement of Facts: [[BBST2013(2)SuppStatement of Facts.doc]]
Source question paper: [[BBST2013(2)Q.doc]]
Source marking memo: [[BBST2013(2)A.doc]]

## Statement of Facts

A variant of the 2013(2) Final scenario. Robin Radebe (42, artist) is engaged (not yet married) to Pieter Radebe (58, business analyst); two children, Jason and Emma. Pieter was seriously injured on 12 January 2012 at the intersection of Long and Short Streets, Cape Town, by Nigel Mansell (SABC television presenter driving an employer-provided BMW, "SABC 123"), who paid an admission-of-guilt fine (R2 500) for speeding through a red light while late for work. Pieter suffered a traumatic brain injury and lost the use of his left arm; his own vehicle (leased from Quantum Bank, R342 000 still owing) was stationary and legally waiting to turn — but his blood-alcohol level, measured three hours after the accident, was over the legal limit. Pieter's vehicle was comprehensively insured with Joint & Centralized Insurance Company ("J&C"), which paid for repairs (R582 000; vehicle valued at R742 000) via MVA Repairers CC without repudiating the claim.

## Q1 — J&C's subrogated claim to recover the repair cost (40)

> [!question] Question
> 1.1 J&C does not want to send a letter of demand before summons, viewing it as a waste of time — advise on the merits (5). 1.2 What are the implications, if any, of Pieter's blood-alcohol level on J&C's ability to recover — focus on whether intoxication could adversely affect recovery in the delictual action (5). 1.3 Who will be the Plaintiff(s) when recovering the vehicle damage by action (5)? 1.4 Who will be the Defendant(s) — evaluate whether vicarious liability applies (10). 1.5 Draft the particulars of claim, including prayers but not the summons headings or citations (15).

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 A letter of demand is not a legal or common-law prerequisite to a cause of action; but sending one may affect costs recovery if the defendant pays promptly on receipt of summons, affects mora interest, and the summons itself can constitute a demand.
> 1.2 Since Pieter's vehicle was stationary and lawfully waiting to turn, his intoxication did not contribute to or cause the accident — apply the reasonable-person test (foreseeable harm and steps to avert it) to establish that intoxication is irrelevant here absent a causal link to the collision.
> 1.3 Pieter (the insured) remains the nominal plaintiff by subrogation — J&C, having indemnified him, steps into his shoes but the action is brought in his name; there is no direct contractual link between Nigel and the insurer, and Pieter cannot claim the same loss twice.
> 1.4 Nigel Mansell is the primary defendant. Vicarious liability of the SABC is probably not in issue on these facts (Nigel's negligent driving on the way to work is not necessarily "in the course and scope" of SABC's business, though the benefit-vehicle relationship should be evaluated against the *Midway Two Engineering* line of authority on the connecting factors: an employment-type relationship plus a link between the delict and the employer's business/instruction/interest).
> 1.5 Particulars of claim: jurisdiction; ownership/risk in the vehicle; date/place/parties to the collision; the defendant's negligence (entering against a red light, excessive speed, failure to keep a proper lookout, failure to avoid, failure to brake); the resulting damage (R582 000 repair cost); demand and non-payment; prayers for payment, interest at the legal rate, and costs.

## Q2 — Matrimonial property systems advice (30)

> [!question] Question
> Pieter and Robin intend to marry. Advise on the different matrimonial property systems available in South Africa, evaluating the practical implications (benefits and disadvantages) of each as applied to their circumstances.

> [!success]- Answer
> In community of property: automatic absent an ANC; all assets fall into a joint estate; spouses have equal powers but need consent for certain transactions; special death/divorce provisions apply. Accrual system: requires an ANC; property may be excluded from the accrual calculation by agreement; parties act independently; on dissolution, the growth in each estate above its starting capital is shared. Out of community of property (no accrual): requires an ANC; estates remain fully separate; each party acts wholly independently.

## Q3 — Rehabilitation options (10 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Tembi Ndlebe, sequestrated one year ago after retrenchment, no prior sequestration or insolvency conviction (claims proved: ABC Bank R300 000 bonded, SARS R20 000, Markhams R7 000; house sold R350 000, other assets R20 000). Advise on rehabilitation options and whether rehabilitation happens automatically.

> [!success]- Answer
> Same guideline as [[BBST 2013(1) Supp]] Q1.1: automatic after 10 years; possibly available after 12 months from confirmation of account; 3 years if previously sequestrated; 5 years following an insolvency-related conviction; 6 months where there are no outstanding claims, no prior sequestration and no fraud.

## Q4 — RAF consultation and mandate steps (20)

> [!question] Question
> Pieter now consults you regarding his personal injuries from the 12 January 2012 accident, wanting to sue the RAF. 4.1 Which 10 questions would you ask him at the first consultation (10)? 4.2 What steps must you take after the consultation to give effect to your mandate (any 5) (10)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 Client particulars; date of accident; particulars of the accident; location; capacity (pedestrian/driver/passenger); income before and after the accident; whether on duty at the time; medical problems arising; hospital/doctor particulars; police reference/any report; particulars of any criminal case; questions on negligence; current status of the claim (any one further relevant question also earns credit).
> 4.2 Obtain the police report; obtain hospital records; obtain an employment certificate; obtain ownership details of the offending vehicle; obtain the insurance file, if any; obtain the statutory medical report.
