# BBST — 2014(2)

Source Statement of Facts: [[BBST2014(2)Statement of Facts.doc]]
Source question paper: [[BBST2014(2)Q.doc]]
Source marking memo: [[BBST2014(2)Q&A.doc]]

Four independent fact clusters ("Facts to Question 1–4"), each with its own scenario.

## Statement of Facts (summary per question)

**Q1 facts:** Egoli Rentals sued June Banks (Menlyn Park shop lease) for eviction and R220 000 arrears; the lease agreement was never attached to the particulars of claim, which alleged it had been lost. Summary judgment was applied for and granted over Banks's opposition (that the lease wasn't cancelled first, and that omitting the agreement rendered the summons "fatally defective").

**Q2 facts:** June Banks, on holiday in Durban, arranged (informally, on the beach) to join a horse ride the next day and signed an indemnity in favour of Egoli Horse Trips CC. A third party's dogs startled the horse ("Albert"); Banks fell and was struck in the face by the horse's hoof, losing her right eye (damages ~R350 000).

**Q3 facts:** June and Kate Banks are the deceased's (Alfred Banks's) children from his first marriage. Alfred married Margaret out of community of property in 2012 with no children; his first will (28 March 2013) left everything to Margaret. Hospitalised in 2014, Alfred dictated a second will which Margaret handwrote and he then signed in the presence of Margaret, her nephew Gert and his girlfriend Natasha (both signing as witnesses) — this second will revoked all prior wills. Alfred also emailed an unsigned "third will" naming ABSA Trust as executor before he died. Margaret was appointed executor and initially found disqualified under s4A of the Wills Act (as the will's writer) but obtained an ex parte order declaring her competent to inherit — June and Kate now seek to rescind that order and have the estate devolve intestate to them instead.

**Q4 facts:** June Banks's neighbouring open property is leased by the Green Development Trust to Egoli Promotions for a public promotional event including a "Longest Drive" golf competition — previously run once already, causing property damage and injuring a visitor to Banks's garden when balls flew off-line toward her house.

## Q1 — Summary judgment in the Magistrates' Court (30)

> [!question] Question
> 1.1 Is this a case where summary judgment may be requested? Consider the requirements for each claim (6). 1.2 Was Egoli required to cancel the lease before issuing summons (4)? 1.3 Does the failure to attach the lease render the summons/application fatally defective (10)? 1.4 Could condonation have been dealt with differently in the High Court (5)? 1.5 Does the supporting affidavit comply with the Rules (5)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 Magistrates' Court Rule 14(1) permits summary judgment only for: a liquid document; a liquidated amount in money; delivery of specified movable property; or ejectment. The claim for damages (unliquidated) cannot support summary judgment; the arrear-rental claim, being a liquidated amount, can.
> 1.2 It is prudent to cancel per the agreement's own terms, but it is trite that service of a summons claiming cancellation itself constitutes proper notice of cancellation, provided the necessary entitlement-to-cancel allegations are pleaded.
> 1.3 Rule 6(6) requires a written contract relied on to be attached; non-compliance is deemed an "irregular step" under Rule 6(13), remediable under Rule 60A — it is an irregularity, not automatically a failure to disclose a cause of action, since the breach of contract cause of action (eviction/arrears/damages) can still be pleaded without the annexure.
> 1.4 The Magistrates' Courts have no general power of condonation — Rule 60(1)–(4) does not confer one, and Rule 60A(3)'s wide "any order it deems fit" power operates only at the hearing of an application to set aside an irregular step, not as a freestanding condonation power (*Hip Hop Clothing Manufacturing CC v Wagener NO*). The High Court, by contrast, has a wide general condonation power under Rule 27 — the same non-compliance could have been condoned there but not in the Magistrates' Court, an anomaly the memo's cited judgment flags for legislative attention.
> 1.5 Rule 14(2) requires the affidavit to allege personal knowledge of the facts, to swear positively to facts *verifying* the cause of action and amount claimed (not merely "confirming" the indebtedness), and to state there is no bona fide defence and the notice to defend was filed solely to delay. A bare "confirmation" of indebtedness, without positively verifying the cause of action, is insufficient — the application was fatally defective in this respect.

## Q2 — Banks's personal injury claim + fee criteria (30)

> [!question] Question
> 2.1 Draft the particulars of claim for Banks's eye-injury claim against Egoli, with headings for each paragraph up to and including prayers (20). 2.2 What criteria determine the fees you would charge for this litigation, per the law societies' rules on reasonable and adequate remuneration (10)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 Citation of Plaintiff/Defendant; jurisdiction; negligence (the classic *Kruger v Coetzee* 1966 (2) SA 428 (A) test: would a reasonable person in the defendant's position have foreseen the reasonable possibility of injury/loss, taken reasonable steps to guard against it, and did the defendant fail to take those steps); consider vicarious liability of Egoli for its representative's conduct; alternatively, plead the *actio de pauperie* — an animal's owner is strictly liable for injury the animal was not reasonably expected to cause, absent owner fault, once the plaintiff proves the animal acted contrary to its nature; damages; demand; prayers for capital, interest and costs.
> 2.2 Reasonable fee criteria: the amount and importance of the work; complexity of the matter; skill, labour and expertise required; volume/importance of documents perused; place of rendering services; time expended; value of the claim; the attorney's experience; the relevant law society tariff (a guide only, not binding); any tariff under s69(d) of the Attorneys Act; whether fees were incurred through the attorney's own over-caution, negligence or mistake (excludable); and the importance of the matter to the client.

## Q3 — Validity of the three wills; rescission and intestate succession (20)

> [!question] Question
> Evaluate the prospects of success of June and Kate's rescission application and their prayer for intestate succession. Discuss the validity of all three wills.

> [!success]- Answer
> **Second will:** s4A(1) of the Wills Act disqualifies a person who writes out a will (or their spouse) from benefiting, subject to s4A(2)(a) — a court may declare the writer competent if satisfied they did not defraud or unduly influence the testator. Historically, at common law, a person acting as the testator's amanuensis (writing at his dictation, gaining nothing extra by doing so) has been permitted to inherit — see *In re Estate Maxwell*. On these facts, Margaret wrote the will at Alfred's dictation with no apparent self-dealing, supporting her competence to inherit under s4A(2)(a).
> **Third will (the email):** s2(3) of the Wills Act allows a court to order the Master to accept a non-compliant document as a will if satisfied the deceased drafted/executed it and intended it as his will — the absence of a signature is not an automatic bar (*Letsekga v The Master*; *Ex parte Maurice*; *Thirion v Die Meester*). The email's title ("WILL"), its survival on the deceased's own computer, and it being sent by him personally all support a finding that Alfred intended it as his will, notwithstanding it was never signed — though the greater the non-compliance with formalities, the more evidence of genuine intent is required.
> The rescission application and intestate-succession prayer accordingly turn on which will (if any) is set aside — if the second (or third) will stands, Margaret inherits per its terms and June/Kate's intestate claim fails; only if all wills are invalidated does the Intestate Succession Act 81 of 1987 apply in their favour.

## Q4 — Interdicting the promotional event (20)

> [!question] Question
> Advise Banks on stopping the promotional event and draft an application (with affidavit) to give effect to her instructions, excluding headings.

> [!success]- Answer
> An urgent application for an interdict, addressing: parties (Banks v Best Connection and Egoli); jurisdiction; locus standi; the undisputed facts; a clear right; harm (property damage and injury to a garden visitor from the previous event); absence of another remedy; balance of convenience favouring the applicant; urgency; costs. Crucially, the relief must be *proportionate*: Banks cannot ask the court to stop the entire promotional event or to guarantee golf balls never travel toward her house (the nature of golf precludes that undertaking) — the correctly targeted interdict is to restrain only the "Longest Drive" competition specifically.
