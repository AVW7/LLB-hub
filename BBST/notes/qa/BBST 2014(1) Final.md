# BBST — 2014(1) Final

Source Statement of Facts: [[BBST2014(1)Statement of Facts.doc]]
Source question paper: [[BBST2014(1)FinalQ.doc]]
Source marking memo: [[BBST2014(1)FinalQ&A.doc]] ("FINAL MEMORANDUM")

## Statement of Facts

Leon Wolf (50, director of W&H Security Services) and Amber Hood (50, unemployed mother of Tanya, 9) cohabited as husband and wife in Durban for ~18 years; Tanya was born of the relationship. Wolf started his security business two years after meeting Hood; she supported him while it grew, then stopped working at his request to raise Tanya and instead ran the household and assisted with his accounting/admin. Wolf proposed ~8 years ago (public engagement) but they never married.

W&H instructed King Attorneys on an oral fee mandate (roughly the High Court tariff, no explicit agreement on billing timing or termination). King worked the matters for two years (copies, tracing agents, correspondence, litigation to trial-readiness on some). W&H disputed King's accounts, terminated the mandate and demanded the files and monies collected on its behalf; King unilaterally deducted its disputed fees from those collected funds.

Wolf later married another woman in Pietermaritzburg while still living with Hood. When Hood discovered this she ended the relationship — leaving her unemployed with no income. Furious, she digitally manipulated a photo (pasting Wolf's face onto a naked man's body) and circulated it via SMS to all of W&H's clients and on Facebook/Twitter with Wolf's name and business details attached.

## Legal Issue 1 — Universal partnership + breach of promise to marry (20)

> [!question] Question
> 1.1 Advise Hood on pursuing a claim to 50% of Wolf's assets based on a tacit universal partnership (10). 1.2 Advise on whether Hood could claim damages for breach of promise to marry (10).

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 *Butters v Mncora* (SCA): a universal partnership of all property (*societas universorum bonorum*) need not be express — it can arise tacitly from the parties' conduct, and the test is whether it is more probable than not that a tacit agreement was reached. The partnership enterprise need not be confined to a commercial undertaking — it can extend to encompass both the family life and the business, so a partner's non-financial contribution (running the home, raising children, foregoing her own career) can found the required contribution even where she earned little or no income herself. On these facts (18 years, Hood's sustained domestic/childcare contribution, sharing in the benefits of Wolf's income), Hood has a reasonable prospect of establishing a tacit universal partnership and a claim to a share of the joint enterprise's value — though students may also argue the contrary view (that the business was substantially Wolf's individual achievement and that cohabitation's normal incidents, without more, are equivocal evidence of a shared-profit intention).
> 1.2 *Van Jaarsveld v Bridges* (SCA): breach of promise can found (a) an *actio iniuriarum* for sentimental damages only if the repudiation was contumelious (wrongful in the delictual sense, animo iniuriandi) — hurt feelings alone are not enough; and (b) a contractual claim, but only for actual losses reasonably within the contemplation of the parties (e.g. wedding costs, a relinquished post) — prospective losses (what the marriage would have brought) are generally too speculative to be recoverable. On these facts Hood would need to show the manner of ending the engagement was objectively insulting/wrongful, and to identify any actual (not speculative) losses she incurred in reliance on the promise.

## Legal Issue 2 — Attorney's fees: set-off, taxation, fee agreement (30)

> [!question] Question
> 2.1 May King effect set-off of its disputed fees against monies collected on W&H's behalf? Deal with whether the fees are a liquidated amount capable of set-off (15). 2.2 If the accounts are taxed and become liquidated, and King sues by simple summons, what is the cause of action, what interest rate, and on what costs scale (5)? 2.3 What issues should a fee agreement have covered to avoid this dispute (10)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 Set-off requires two liquidated, mutually-due debts. An untaxed, disputed bill of an attorney's fees is *not* liquidated — it is "capable of speedy and easy proof" only once taxed (*Blakes Maphanga Inc v Outsurance*; *Tredoux v Kellerman*). Taxation, not agreement or invoicing, is what liquidates an attorney's fee claim where the client disputes it. King could therefore not lawfully set off its disputed, untaxed fees against the funds collected for W&H — the claim had to first be taxed.
> 2.2 Once taxed, the cause of action is the taxed bill of costs (a liquidated amount); interest runs at the legal/prescribed mora rate; costs would ordinarily be sought on the appropriate scale for a simple summons/undefended claim (party-and-party, absent an agreed attorney-and-own-client basis).
> 2.3 A proper fee agreement should cover: the parties and domicilium; the mandate and its scope; the fee basis (attorney-and-own-client) and how it differs from party-and-party costs; disbursements; billing frequency/timing; payment terms and interest on overdue accounts; the hourly/tariff rate and any annual escalation; the client's obligation to query a bill within a set period or it is deemed correct; deposit requirements and consequences of non-payment; dispute resolution (taxation vs referral to a professional body); the attorney's retaining lien on the file pending payment on withdrawal; and the domicilium citandi et executandi.

## Legal Issue 3 — Defamation claim and urgent interdict (30)

> [!question] Question
> Advise Wolf on a defamation claim against Hood over the manipulated photo. 3.1 Assume defamation occurred — draft the Particulars of Claim/Statement of Facts only, with a heading and one averment per paragraph starting with "Citation of Parties" and ending with "Prayers" (20). 3.2 Draft the body and prayers of a Notice of Motion and supporting affidavit for an interdict compelling Hood to stop distributing the photo and to remove it from social media — start "Be pleased to take note that..." (10).

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 A defamation particulars of claim must plead, under separate headings: citation of the parties; jurisdiction; the defamatory statement/image and its meaning; publication (to W&H's clients via SMS, and on Facebook/Twitter); the resulting damages; the letter of demand and lack of response; and prayers for judgment against the Defendant for capital, interest at the legal rate a tempore morae, and costs.
> 3.2 Notice of Motion (urgent, dispensing with the ordinary forms/service): interdicting and restraining further publication of the offending photo; ordering removal from all social media under Hood's control and that she refrain from allowing further publication; that the order operate as an interim order pending finalisation; costs — supported by an affidavit addressing the applicant's personal knowledge, a prima facie or clear right, harm, absence of another remedy, balance of convenience, and urgency; must include a supplementary confirmatory affidavit from a corroborating witness (Mr Solomons, per the memo).

## Legal Issue 4 — Will disqualification and acts of insolvency (20)

> [!question] Question
> 4.1 Is there any impediment to Hood receiving benefits under Wolf's will as it stands? If so, what options exist — without drafting a new will — to ensure she qualifies (10)? 4.2 Regarding communications Wolf sent to his clients about his financial position, has he committed any act of insolvency? Advise fully, as some creditors have complained he is avoiding his debts (10).

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 Section 4A(1) of the Wills Act 7 of 1953 disqualifies anyone who attests/signs a will as a witness (or the spouse of such a person) from receiving a benefit under it. If Hood was one of only two witnesses, she is disqualified as it stands. Section 4A(2)(a) allows a court to declare her competent to receive the benefit if satisfied she did not defraud or unduly influence Wolf when the will was executed — the court will assess Wolf's state of mind and Hood's possible motive at the time of signature; this route does not require drafting a new will.
> 4.2 Applying s8(g)/(h) of the Insolvency Act: a written notice of inability to pay debts (tested from the perspective of a reasonable recipient) is required for an act of insolvency under s8(g); s8(h) requires Government-Gazette publication under s34 that liabilities are due and the trader is then unable to pay. On these facts, no such written notice or Gazette publication of actual inability to pay was given — no act of insolvency has been committed, though Wolf should be cautious about how he communicates his financial position to avoid inadvertently satisfying s8(g).
