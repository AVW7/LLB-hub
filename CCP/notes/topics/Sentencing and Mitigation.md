# Sentencing and Mitigation

Mitigation scenarios are the highest-mark-value CCP questions (often 10–15 marks) and follow a very consistent structure across every paper.

## The State v Zinn triad

Every sentence must weigh:
1. The **nature/seriousness of the crime**
2. The **personal circumstances of the accused**
3. The **interests of society**

## The standard three-bucket mitigation structure

Every marking memo in this module organises mitigating factors the same way — use this structure to answer any mitigation question:

**A. Personal circumstances**
- Age, marital status, number and ages of children
- Employment, income, who else depends on that income (sole breadwinner)
- Spouse's employment status
- First offender status

**B. Remorse**
- Guilty plea
- Cooperation with police during the investigation

**C. Facts of the matter**
- Circumstances reducing moral blameworthiness (e.g. no one injured, only marginally over a legal limit, needed the item to survive)
- Loss already suffered (lost employment, civil claim instituted, restitution already paid)

## Purposes of sentencing

- **Retribution** (punishment)
- **Rehabilitation**
- **Deterrence** (and prevention)

A fine plus a suspended sentence is a recurring "suitable sentence" answer — fine as retribution, suspended sentence as deterrent. Where the accused's livelihood depends on a licence (e.g. a driver), argue the licence should not be cancelled/endorsed.

## Six general sentencing factors (broader list)

Personal circumstances of the accused; interests of the community; seriousness of the offence; possibility of rehabilitating the accused; prevention and deterrence; safeguarding the community.

## Types of punishment (district court)

Imprisonment; periodical imprisonment; a fine; correctional supervision; imprisonment from which the person may be placed under correctional supervision.
