# Legal Representation of Multiple Accused

Recurring 3-mark scenario question, tested with identical facts across several papers.

## Fact pattern

Two accused in the same case, with **conflicting statements**, both ask the same attorney to represent them.

## Answer structure

1. **Can the attorney proceed to represent both?** — **No.** A conflict of interest arises from the conflicting statements; representing both risks compromising the duty owed to each individually.
2. **Can the attorney elect to represent just one of them?** — **No**, not after already being briefed. Once a legal representative has accepted a brief from an accused and then withdraws from defending them, they may not cross-examine that accused on behalf of a co-accused. They must withdraw from the matter entirely and cannot simply choose which of the two to keep as a client.

## Why this rule matters in practice

Cross-examining a former client (even on behalf of a co-accused) risks misusing confidential information obtained during the earlier consultation, and undermines the fairness of the trial for the accused who lost their attorney.
