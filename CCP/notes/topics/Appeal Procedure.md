# Appeal Procedure

Covers both **bail-refusal appeals** and **conviction/sentence appeals**, plus leave to appeal.

## Appealing a bail refusal

- Lies to the **High Court**, heard by any judge of that court.
- Notice of Appeal must address: that justice demands release on bail — fixed address, not a flight risk, will attend all court dates, will not interfere with prospective State witnesses (especially important where the Magistrate gave **no reasons** for refusal).
- Served on the **Director of Public Prosecutions and the Magistrate**.
- If the court finds the Magistrate erred, it gives the decision the Magistrate should have given.
- New facts arising **after** the decision under appeal do not found this appeal — they must first go before the Magistrate for a fresh ruling (or a fresh bail application).
- Before noting an appeal, check whether intervening events (e.g. destroyed exhibits, a deceased witness) change the strategic picture — those facts may support a fresh bail application instead of/alongside an appeal.

## Appealing from the Magistrate's Court

- Notice of appeal: lodged with the **clerk of the court** within **14 days** of sentence.
- If that period has expired: apply for **condonation**.
- Documents required for late noting: application for leave to appeal; notice of appeal; power of attorney.

## Leave to appeal (Regional Court / High Court convictions)

- Application for leave to appeal is made **in the court that convicted** (i.e. before the same court/judge).
- Template structure: court heading, "Application for Leave to Appeal" against conviction and/or sentence, grounds addressed separately **"Ad Conviction"** and **"Ad Sentence"** — see [[CCPExampleAppLeaveToAppeal.doc]] for a full worked example (misdirection on the evidence, failure to weigh contradictions, sentence "shockingly inappropriate", personal circumstances/rehabilitation prospects, first-offender status).
- Steps from grant of leave to appeal through to the hearing: obtain the record; prepare and file heads of argument; set the matter down; serve on the DPP; appear at the hearing.
