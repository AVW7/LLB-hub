# Bail Applications

The single most heavily tested CCP topic — appears in essentially every past paper.

## Core framework

- **s59 CPA** — a police officer may grant "**police bail**" before the accused's first court appearance, for any offence *other than* those in Part II or Part III of Schedule 2, after consulting the investigating officer.
- **s59A CPA** — a prosecutor may authorise "**prosecutor's bail**" for offences listed in **Schedule 7** only.
- Anything more serious than Schedule 7 requires a **Magistrate** to grant bail.
- **s50 CPA** — the 48-hour rule: if the 48-hour period expires over a weekend/holiday when court isn't sitting, the accused must be brought to court before the close of the next court day.

## The test

| Offence category | Test | Standard of proof |
|---|---|---|
| Schedule 5 | Interests of justice | Balance of probabilities |
| Schedule 6 | Exceptional circumstances | Balance of probabilities |

Basic principle underlying all bail decisions: **the interests of justice**.

## s60(4) — grounds for refusing bail

Bail must be refused where there is a likelihood the accused, if released, will:
1. endanger the safety of the public/a particular person, or commit a Schedule 1 offence;
2. attempt to evade trial;
3. attempt to influence or intimidate witnesses, or conceal/destroy evidence;
4. undermine or jeopardise the objectives/proper functioning of the criminal justice system, including the bail system;
5. (in exceptional circumstances) disturb the public order or undermine public peace/security.

## s60(11B) — mandatory disclosures

The accused must disclose to the court:
- whether previously convicted of any offence;
- whether there are pending charges against them, and whether currently on bail for those charges.

**Consequence of wilful non-disclosure:** an offence, punishable by a fine or imprisonment not exceeding **2 years**; the court may also withdraw bail already granted.

## After a bail refusal

Two options, not mutually exclusive:
1. **Fresh bail application** before the same Magistrate, on new facts (e.g. a key State witness has died or emigrated, materially weakening the State's case).
2. **Appeal** the refusal — see [[Appeal Procedure]].

## Practice notes

- Bail refusal appeals lie to the **High Court**, heard by any judge of that court.
- Notice of Appeal is served on the **DPP and the Magistrate**.
- New facts arising *after* the decision under appeal cannot found the appeal itself — they must first be placed before the Magistrate.
