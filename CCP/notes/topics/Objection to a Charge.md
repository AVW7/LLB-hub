# Objection to a Charge (s85 / s106 CPA)

Section 85 gives the accused a right to object to a charge before pleading to it under s106.

## Five grounds for objection

1. The charge does not comply with the CPA's requirements relating to the essentials of a charge.
2. The charge does not set out an essential element of the relevant offence.
3. The charge does not disclose an offence.
4. The charge does not contain sufficient particulars of any matter alleged (proviso: this ground cannot be raised where the accused is required to plead under **s119 or s122A** in the Magistrate's Court).
5. The accused is not correctly named or described in the charge.

## Procedure for raising the objection

- The accused must give **reasonable notice** to the prosecution of the intention to object, stating the ground.
- This notice requirement may be **waived** by the prosecution (the Attorney-General/Prosecutor).
- The court may, on **good cause shown**, dispense with the notice requirement, or **adjourn the trial** to allow notice to be given.
- If the court finds the objection well-founded, it makes such order as it deems fit relating to amendment of the charge or delivery of further particulars.
- If the prosecutor fails to comply with such an order, the court **may quash the charge**.
