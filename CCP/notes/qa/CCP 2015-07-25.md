## Q2 — Bail refusal, appeal to the High Court (11)

> [!question] Question
> Bail refused on assault common without reasons given, no new facts arise after the decision.
> 2.1 Which court? 2.2 Position if that court not in session? 2.3 What to address in the Notice of Appeal? 2.4 On whom served? 2.5 Judgment if the court finds the Magistrate erred? 2.6 Position on new facts arising after the decision?

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 High Court. 2.2 Any judge of that court. 2.3 That justice demands release: fixed address, not a flight risk, will attend all appointed dates, won't interfere with State witnesses. 2.4 The DPP and the Magistrate. 2.5 The court gives the decision the Magistrate should have given. 2.6 New facts don't found this appeal — must go back to the Magistrate first.

Related: [[Bail Applications]], [[Appeal Procedure]]

## Q3 — Duty on cross-examination re a differing version (3)

> [!question] Question
> A State witness's testimony differs materially from the accused's version. 3.1 What is your duty on cross-examination? 3.2 Implications of not cross-examining?

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 Must put the client's version to the witness for comment. 3.2 Failing to do so exposes the accused to a charge of recent fabrication, and implies agreement with the witness's version / no version to put before court.

## Q4 — s115 statement, culpable homicide + discharge (17)

> [!question] Question
> Joe Bloggs kills Van Der Merwe with an iron bar after being stabbed; charged with culpable homicide.
> 4.1 Prepare a complete s115 statement, including headings (12). 4.2 Lesser offences if not convicted of culpable homicide (2)? 4.3 Advice if, at close of the State's case, no prima facie case is proven (1)? 4.4 Criteria for the court's finding on 4.3 (2)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 s115 statement pleading not guilty, admitting the blow and death, pleading private defence following the stabbing.
> 4.2 Assault GBH; common assault.
> 4.3 Advise the client to apply for **discharge under s174**.
> 4.4 s174: if at the close of the prosecution's case the court believes there is no evidence the accused committed the offence, it may return a verdict of not guilty.

Related: [[Plea Procedure]], [[Discharge and Close of State's Case]]

## Q5 — Withdrawal after not-guilty plea (3)

> [!question] Question
> May the prosecutor withdraw a case after a not-guilty plea? Motivate.

> [!success]- Answer
> Amounts to stopping the prosecution — the DPP's prerogative; without authorisation the prosecutor must seek it before withdrawing.
