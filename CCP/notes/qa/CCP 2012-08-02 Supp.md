## Q1 — s115 statement, private defence / warding off a robber (10)

> [!question] Question
> Client indicted for murder. Says he acted in private defence warding off a robber who attacked him with a knife; he fired a shot. Draft a Section 115 statement, including headings.

> [!success]- Answer
> Not-guilty statement in the **High Court**: admits shooting and killing the deceased; denies acting unlawfully; sets out the attack by the deceased leading to the shooting; states he acted throughout in **private defence**.

## Q2 — Police docket access (10)

> [!question] Question
> Discuss the rights of the accused to access the police docket vis-à-vis the rights of the State.

> [!success]- Answer
> Same structure as [[CCP 2010-12-15 Supp]] Q10 and [[CCP 2012-01-28]] Q3: attorney requests copies from the prosecutor; State may resist on fair-trial, informer/state-secret, or witness-intimidation grounds; **Shabalala** application available if refused; defence must not misuse access.

## Q3 — Mitigation and proposed sentence, drunk driving (15)

> [!question] Question
> Client pleads guilty to driving under the influence (blood alcohol 0.06g/100ml, legal limit 0.05g/100ml). Age 50, married, 3 children, employed as a driver earning R7000/month, disabled unemployed wife.
> (a) List mitigating factors in full (10). (b) Suggest a suitable sentence with reasons, referencing the purposes of sentencing (5).

> [!success]- Answer
> (a) Personal circumstances (age, first offender, marriage, 3 minor children, disabled unemployed wife, income, sole breadwinner needing his licence to earn a living); remorse (guilty plea, cooperation); facts (stopped at a roadblock, no one injured, only marginally over the legal limit).
> (b) Purposes of sentencing: retribution, rehabilitation, deterrence. Propose a **fine plus a suspended sentence**, with an application that the driver's licence not be endorsed — fine serves retribution, suspended sentence serves as deterrent.

Related: [[Sentencing and Mitigation]]

## Q4 — Bail refusal appeal to the High Court (12)

> [!question] Question
> Client appears on assault common, bail refused without reasons, instructed to appeal (no new facts arise after the decision).
> 1.1 Which court? 1.2 Position if that court is not in session? 1.3 What to deal with in the Notice of Appeal given no reasons were furnished? 1.4 On whom is the Notice served? 1.5 If the court finds the Magistrate erred, what judgment follows? 1.6 Position regarding new facts arising after the decision appealed?

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 High Court. 1.2 Any judge of that court may hear it. 1.3 That justice demands release on bail — fixed address, not a flight risk, will attend all court dates, will not interfere with prospective State witnesses. 1.4 The Director of Public Prosecutions and the Magistrate. 1.5 The court/judge gives the decision the Magistrate should have given. 1.6 New facts arising after the decision do not found the appeal — they must first go back before the Magistrate for a fresh decision.

Related: [[Bail Applications]], [[Appeal Procedure]]

## Q5 — Options at close of the State's case (3)

> [!question] Question
> Name three options open to the defence once the State has closed its case.

> [!success]- Answer
> Change of plea; application under s174; closing the defence case (open/close case options generally — memo lists change of plea, s174 application, closing the defence case, opening the defence case).
