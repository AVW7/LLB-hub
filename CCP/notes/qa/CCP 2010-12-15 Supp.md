## Q1 — Draft a Section 112 statement, murder (10)

> [!question] Question
> Client is charged with murder — stabbed the deceased twice with a knife, causing death. Draw a Section 112 statement, including headings.

> [!success]- Answer
> Full **s112(2)** guilty statement: admits stabbing the deceased twice, admits acting unlawfully and intentionally, admits guilt freely without duress, notes no defence raised, and notes the accused was advised of and waives the right to an assessor under **s93ter**. Markers use discretion on whether a candidate needs to positively exclude a defence.

Related: [[Plea Procedure]]

## Q2 — Options at close of the State's case (4)

> [!question] Question
> Name four options the accused has at the close of the State's case.

> [!success]- Answer
> Change of plea; application under **s174**; closing the defence case (without evidence); opening the defence case (calling evidence).

Related: [[Discharge and Close of State's Case]]

## Q3 — Changing a plea mid-trial (3)

> [!question] Question
> Client pleaded not guilty to robbery, then during trial decides to change the plea. How would you do this? Motivate.

> [!success]- Answer
> Either inform the court of the client's intention and proceed by way of **s220 formal admissions**, or prepare and deliver a full **s112(2)** statement admitting all elements of the offence.

Related: [[Plea Procedure]]

## Q4 — Representing co-accused with conflicting statements (3)

> [!question] Question
> 4.1 Two accused with conflicting statements ask the same attorney to represent them — can the attorney proceed?
> 4.2 Can the attorney elect to represent just one of them?

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 No. 4.2 Once counsel has accepted a brief and then withdraws, they may not cross-examine that accused on behalf of the other co-accused — they must withdraw from the matter entirely and cannot simply choose which accused to keep.

Related: [[Legal Representation of Multiple Accused]]

## Q5 — Rights during police interrogation (6)

> [!question] Question
> 5.1 Client phones from police custody saying police are forcing a statement — advise on rights and reply to police.
> 5.2 What information is the client obliged to furnish?

> [!success]- Answer
> 5.1 Right to remain silent; not obliged to answer questions on the merits; entitled to have a legal representative present when making any statement.
> 5.2 Obliged only to furnish full names and address without delay.

Related: [[Rights of Arrested and Accused Persons]]

## Q6 — Mitigation, minimum sentence robbery (10)

> [!question] Question
> Client convicted of robbery with aggravating circumstances, faces a 15-year minimum sentence. Accused is 50, married, four children, lost employment due to the case, now earns R3000/month. List mitigating factors in full.

> [!success]- Answer
> Personal circumstances (age, substantial/compelling reasons, marriage, four minor children aged 15/12/8/5, unemployed wife, income); remorse (guilty plea, cooperation with police); facts of the matter (loss of prior employment, civil claim instituted, amount paid back, first offender).

Related: [[Sentencing and Mitigation]]

## Q9 — Forms J88 and SAP13 (6)

> [!question] Question
> Explain in detail the nature and purpose of Forms J88 and SAP13.

> [!success]- Answer
> **J88** — medical report used where an injury was sustained (incl. rape cases); records the nature of injury, medical opinion on causation, and complainant details (age, sex, etc.).
> **SAP13** — exhibit register recording items pertaining to a case, later produced at trial; contains crucial chain-of-custody information.

Related: [[Forms J88 and SAP13]]

## Q10 — Police docket access (10)

> [!question] Question
> Discuss the rights of the accused to access the police docket vis-à-vis the rights of the State.

> [!success]- Answer
> Docket access is invaluable trial preparation. Attorney writes to the prosecutor for copies of witness statements; prosecutor obtains the docket from the IO and quotes copying costs. State may resist access where: (a) not justified for a fair trial; (b) would disclose an informer's identity or state secrets; (c) may intimidate witnesses or otherwise prejudice justice. If refused, attorney may apply to court to enforce access per **Shabalala**; the State must justify the refusal; the defence must not misuse access to manufacture a defence around State evidence.

Related: [[Access to the Police Docket]]

## Q11 — Late noting of appeal; competent verdicts on murder (10)

> [!question] Question
> 11.1 Within what period must a Magistrate's Court appeal be lodged, and with whom? 11.2 Procedure if that period has expired? 11.3 What documents are required for late noting? 11.4 Name five competent verdicts on a murder charge.

> [!success]- Answer
> 11.1 Within **14 days**, lodged with the clerk of the court. 11.2 Apply for **condonation**. 11.3 Application for leave to appeal; notice of appeal; power of attorney. 11.4 Robbery; assault GBH; common assault; culpable homicide; attempted murder.

Related: [[Appeal Procedure]]

## Q12 — Types of punishment in the district court (5)

> [!question] Question
> List 5 types of punishment a district court may impose.

> [!success]- Answer
> Imprisonment; periodical imprisonment; a fine; correctional supervision; imprisonment from which the person may be placed under correctional supervision.
