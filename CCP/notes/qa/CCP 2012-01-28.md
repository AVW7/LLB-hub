## Q1 — s115 statement + lesser offences (14)

> [!question] Question
> Joe Bloggs stabbed by Van Der Merwe with a knife, responds with an iron bar blow causing death; charged with culpable homicide, Regional Court Durban.
> 1.1 Prepare a complete Section 115 statement, including headings (12). 1.2 Of what lesser offences can Bloggs be convicted if not convicted of culpable homicide? (2)

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 Full s115 not-guilty statement pleading private defence, admitting presence, the blow struck, and that it followed being stabbed — acting in private defence.
> 1.2 Assault with intent to do grievous bodily harm; common assault.

## Q2 — s60(11B) disclosures (4)

> [!question] Question
> 2.1 Name the two disclosures required under s60(11B). 2.2 Consequences of wilful failure?

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 Previous convictions; pending charges and current bail status on them. 2.2 Offence — fine or imprisonment up to 2 years; court may also withdraw bail for non/incorrect disclosure of previous convictions.

## Q3 — Police docket access, with case law and remedies (8)

> [!question] Question
> 3.1 Discuss with case law the accused's right to access the police docket vs the State's rights (6). 3.2 What remedies if the State refuses? (2)

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 **Shabalala and 5 Others v Attorney-General of the Transvaal** — the right to a fair trial (s25(3), now s35(3) Constitution) ordinarily includes access to witness statements and relevant docket contents. State may resist on grounds: not justified for a fair trial; reasonable risk of disclosing an informer's identity/state secrets; reasonable risk of witness intimidation or otherwise prejudicing justice — depends on the circumstances of each case.
> 3.2 Application to the trial court, which has a discretion to order access after balancing the risks of disclosure against the risk that a fair trial is otherwise impossible.

Related: [[Access to the Police Docket]]

## Q5 — Rights/compellability of witnesses (5)

> [!question] Question
> 5.1 Client questioned re alleged theft of a motor vehicle, asked if he stole it — obliged to answer? 5.2 Asked for name/address — obliged? 5.3 Client's estranged wife suggested as a defence witness — competent and compellable? 5.4 Co-accused wants to call client's wife but she refuses — compellable? 5.5 Client charged with assaulting the child born of the marriage — is the wife competent/compellable against him?

> [!success]- Answer
> 5.1 No. 5.2 Yes. 5.3 Competent and compellable (s196). 5.4 No. 5.5 Yes.

## Q6 — Mitigation + State v Zinn triad (11)

> [!question] Question
> 6.1 Fast Fingers pleaded guilty to housebreaking and theft but instructs (in consultation) that he entered via an **open** front door — what steps do you take at continuation of trial? (8) 6.2 What is the triad of factors in **State v Zinn**? (3)

> [!success]- Answer
> 6.1 Advise that entry via an open door means he did not commit housebreaking — he wrongly pleaded guilty. Apply under **s113** to correct the plea; accused gives evidence under oath, subject to cross-examination and court questioning; witnesses may be called; addresses on the merits (defence then State); Magistrate rules. If successful, proceeds as if a not-guilty plea was tendered from the start (State/Defence may close on the admitted facts, conviction of theft only, proceed to sentence). If unsuccessful, proceed straight to sentence.
> 6.2 Nature/seriousness of the crime; personal circumstances of the accused; interests of society.

Related: [[Plea Procedure]], [[Sentencing and Mitigation]]

## Q7 — Options at close of State's case (4)

> [!question] Question
> List the options the accused has at the close of the State's case.

> [!success]- Answer
> Close the defence case without calling the accused/witnesses; change the plea; apply for discharge under **s174**; call the accused and/or witnesses.
