## Q1.1 — s60(11B) disclosures (6)

> [!question] Question
> Mr A, charged with culpable homicide (negligent driving, sudden emergency defence), applies for bail.
> 1.1.1 Two facts to disclose under s60(11B) (2). 1.1.2 Consequences of wilful failure (4)?

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1.1 Previous convictions; pending charges/current bail status. 1.1.2 Offence — fine or imprisonment up to 2 years; court makes a credibility finding and denies bail.

Related: [[Bail Applications]]

## Q1.2 — Fresh bail application after witnesses become unavailable (5)

> [!question] Question
> Bail refused on the strength of the State's case (two witnesses). One witness has since died, the other emigrated permanently. What steps do you take? Discuss reasoning and argument.

> [!success]- Answer
> Bring a **fresh bail application** before the same Magistrate who refused it, on the basis that new facts have emerged since the last application — the State's case is now considerably weakened because neither witness will testify, removing any realistic possibility of conviction.

Related: [[Bail Applications]]

## Q1.3 — Draft a not-guilty plea, sudden emergency (10)

> [!question] Question
> Mr A is granted bail and intends to plead not guilty. Draft the necessary plea, including headings.

> [!success]- Answer
> **s115** statement in the Regional Court: pleads not guilty to culpable homicide, denies negligence, admits formal facts under s220 (driving the vehicle, the collision, the post-mortem findings), and raises **sudden emergency** (tyre burst) as the substantive defence — conduct neither intentional nor negligent.

Related: [[Plea Procedure]]

## Q1.4 — Mitigation on conviction (10)

> [!question] Question
> Mr A is convicted at the end of trial. List in full the factors for mitigation of sentence.

> [!success]- Answer
> Personal circumstances (substantial/compelling reasons, age 30, married with children, employed as a teacher — valued member of society, R20,000/month income); remorse (cooperation, conduct not intentional); facts of the matter (civil claim instituted, first offender, cause of the collision).

## Q3 — Objecting to a charge, s85 procedure (6)

> [!question] Question
> Discuss briefly the procedure to object to a charge under s85 CPA.

> [!success]- Answer
> Reasonable notice of intention to object with grounds stated; may be waived by the prosecution; court may dispense with notice on good cause shown or adjourn to permit notice; if the objection is well-founded, the court orders an amendment or delivery of particulars; failure to comply may result in the charge being quashed.

Related: [[Objection to a Charge]]

## Q4 — Conflicted co-accused (3)

> [!question] Question
> 4.1 Attorney approached by two accused with conflicting statements — proceed to represent both? 4.2 Elect to represent one?

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 No. 4.2 No — once a legal representative accepts a brief and withdraws from defending an accused, they cannot cross-examine that accused on behalf of a co-accused.

Related: [[Legal Representation of Multiple Accused]]

## Q5 — Obtaining statements / docket access (6)

> [!question] Question
> 5.1 How does an attorney obtain statements from the State? 5.2 Can the State deny the accused access to the police docket? Discuss the rights on both sides.

> [!success]- Answer
> 5.1 In practice the attorney writes to the prosecutor requesting copies of witness statements/further particulars; the prosecutor obtains the docket from the IO, copies the statements, and advises of costs.
> 5.2 Docket access is invaluable for a fair trial and effective preparation, but the State may resist access where: not justified for a fair trial; would disclose an informer's identity or state secrets; may lead to witness intimidation or otherwise prejudice justice; and an accused in a **bail application** is not entitled to copies of the docket. If refused, apply to court per **Shabalala**; the State must justify its refusal; the defence must not misuse the concession to build a defence around State evidence.

Related: [[Access to the Police Docket]]
