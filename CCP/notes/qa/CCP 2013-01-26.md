## Q1 — Basic bail principle + s60(4) circumstances (6)

> [!question] Question
> 1.1 What is the basic principle the court considers in a bail application? 1.2 List 5 circumstances under s60(4) which jeopardise that principle and lead to refusal.

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 **Interests of justice.** For a Schedule 5 offence: whether it's in the interests of justice to release on bail; for Schedule 6: whether exceptional circumstances permit release. Both decided on a balance of probabilities.
> 1.2 The five s60(4) grounds — see [[Bail Applications]].

Related: [[Bail Applications]]

## Q2 — s60(11B) disclosures (4)

> [!question] Question
> 2.1 Name two facts the accused must disclose under s60(11B). 2.2 Consequences of wilful failure?

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 Previous convictions; pending charges and current bail status. 2.2 Offence — fine or imprisonment up to 2 years.

Related: [[Bail Applications]]

## Q3 — Withdrawal after not-guilty plea (3)

> [!question] Question
> May the prosecutor withdraw a case after a not-guilty plea? Motivate.

> [!success]- Answer
> Amounts to a stopping of the prosecution — the DPP's prerogative; without DPP authority the prosecutor cannot withdraw.

## Q4 — Draft a s112(2) guilty statement, theft (12)

> [!question] Question
> Joe Bloggs stole jewellery worth R999 from Discount Superstore, intends pleading guilty. Draft the necessary statement with headings.

> [!success]- Answer
> Full **s112(2)** Magistrate's Court statement: understands the charge, pleads guilty voluntarily, sets out the factual narrative (removed the jewellery, concealed it, left the store, knew it wasn't his, intended to permanently deprive the store, knew his conduct was unlawful and punishable).

Related: [[Plea Procedure]]

## Q5 — Options at close of State's case (4)

> [!question] Question
> Name four options the accused has at the close of the State's case.

> [!success]- Answer
> Change plea to guilty; apply for discharge under s174; close the defence case without witnesses; open the defence case and call witnesses.

Related: [[Discharge and Close of State's Case]]

## Q6 — Mitigation, housebreaking correction of plea (8)

> [!question] Question
> John Doe pleaded guilty (unrepresented) to housebreaking and theft, but entered via an **open** front door. What steps at continuation of trial? Detail the full procedure.

> [!success]- Answer
> Same **s113** correction-of-plea procedure as [[CCP 2012-01-28]] Q6: advise no housebreaking committed; apply under s113; evidence under oath and cross-examination; addresses on the merits; Magistrate's ruling; if successful proceed as not-guilty from the start (theft conviction only possible); if unsuccessful, proceed straight to sentence.

Related: [[Plea Procedure]]

## Q7 — Mitigation, speeding (10)

> [!question] Question
> Client convicted of speeding (120km/h in an 80km zone), age 35, married, two children, driver earning R5000/month, sole breadwinner. List mitigating factors and substantiate a suitable sentence.

> [!success]- Answer
> Personal circumstances (age, marriage, two children, income, sole breadwinner); remorse; facts of the matter (needs licence to work, first offender-style circumstances). Substantiate a **suspended sentence + fine** balancing deterrence and retribution, motivating that the licence not be cancelled given his livelihood depends on it.

Related: [[Sentencing and Mitigation]]

## Q8 — Three kinds of review (3)
