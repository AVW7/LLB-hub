## Q1 — Full integrated scenario (58 marks)

> [!question] Question
> Mr Joe Bloggs is arrested at 4pm and calls you at 5pm the same day.
> 1.1 Four ways a suspect can be brought before court (4). 1.2 Police forcing a statement — advise on rights and what he's obliged to furnish (4). 1.3 Suspect (not yet charged) in a murder/housebreaking case — can a prosecutor grant after-hours bail? Motivate (3). 1.4 By when must police charge him and bring him to court — state date/time (2). 1.5 Facts: Bloggs and Hogg break into a house via an open door to steal a TV; Hogg (armed, unknown to Bloggs) shoots and kills the occupant; both flee and are arrested the next day, charged with murder and housebreaking with intent to steal.
> — 1.5.1 Test the court uses to decide bail (1). 1.5.2 Information to gather from Bloggs to support the bail application (5). 1.5.3 Two facts an accused must disclose in a bail application (2). 1.5.4 Consequences of wilful non-disclosure (3). 1.6 Sequence of the criminal trial from not-guilty plea to judgment, in point form, incl. options at close of the State's case (10). 1.7 Grounds to object to a charge (5). 1.8 Shabalala — can the State resist docket access? When/on what grounds (7)? 1.9 Draft the combined pleas for both charges, with headings (12).
