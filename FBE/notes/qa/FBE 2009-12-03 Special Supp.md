## Q2 — Pre-incorporation contracts (6)

> [!question] Question
> Explain what a pre-incorporation contract is, and what is required in order for it to be valid.

> [!success]- Answer
> Before a promoter incurs the cost of registering a company, they may want certainty that the company will be able to acquire a particular asset or business. To let the not-yet-existing company become party to a contract, **s35 of the Companies Act** allows a company to adopt and ratify a pre-incorporation agreement concluded on its behalf. The contract binds the company only if:
> 1. It is in writing;
> 2. It was concluded by a person indicating they act as agent or trustee for a company to be formed;
> 3. The memorandum of association must, at the time of registration, incorporate as one of its objects the adoption/ratification of the contract;
> 4. Two copies of the contract, one certified by a notary, must be lodged with the application to register the memorandum and articles of association;
> 5. The company must, after incorporation, adopt and/or ratify the contract;
> 6. The company must be entitled to commence business.

## Q3 — The three types of partnership (6)

> [!question] Question
> What are the distinguishing characteristics of the 3 types of partnership?

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 **Ordinary partnership** — the partners are jointly and severally liable for the debts of the partnership.
> 3.2 **Anonymous (sleeping) partner** — not known to the public, liable only for their pro-rata share of the debts.
> 3.3 **Commanditarian partnership** — such a partner is merely an investor, not known to the public; liability limited to their specific contribution or an agreed amount.

Related: [[Partnerships]]

## Q4 — VAT registration (3)

> [!question] Question
> Who must register for VAT, and when must such registration occur?

> [!success]- Answer
> Any person (legal or juristic) must register for VAT in respect of any business that they operate, once the turnover of that business reaches **R300,000 per annum**.
