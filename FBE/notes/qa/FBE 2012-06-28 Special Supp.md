## Q1 — Disadvantages of a sole proprietorship (3)

> [!question] Question
> Discuss the disadvantages of forming a sole proprietorship.

> [!success]- Answer
> - There is no distinction between the personal estate (assets and liabilities) of the proprietor and those of the business. Therefore, if the business is declared insolvent, the proprietor's personal assets could be attached.
> - The death of the proprietor amounts to a demise of the business — there is no succession.
> - The success or failure of the business is tied to the ability of the owner.
> - The proceeds of the business are taxed in the hands of the owner.
> (Max 3 marks — any three of the above.)

## Q2 — The three types of partnership (6)

> [!question] Question
> What are the distinguishing characteristics of the 3 types of partnership?

> [!success]- Answer
> - **Ordinary partnership** — the partners are jointly and severally liable for the debts of the partnership.
> - **Anonymous (sleeping) partner** — not known to the public, liable only for their pro-rata share of the debts.
> - **Commanditarian partnership** — such a partner is merely an investor, not known to the public; liability is limited to their specific contribution or an agreed amount.

## Q3 — Business Trust parties and inter vivos registration (7)

> [!question] Question
> 3.1 Name the three (3) main parties to a Business Trust. (3)
> 3.2 Briefly describe the procedure for the registration of an Inter Vivos Trust. (4)

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 (a) The founder, creator or donor; (b) the Trustees; (c) the beneficiaries.
> 3.2 A Deed of Trust or Trust Deed needs to be prepared and/or drafted. All Trusts are registered with the Master of the High Court. An amount of R100.00 is payable to the Master of the High Court. The duly signed Deed of Trust or Trust Deed, acceptance by trustees of the appointment, together with letters of consent from an auditor of the Trust, are lodged with the Master of the High Court for registration. Letters of Authority are then issued by the Master of the High Court in terms of section 6 of the Trust Property Control Act.

Related: [[Trusts]]

## Q4 — VAT registration (2)

> [!question] Question
> Who must register for VAT, and when must such registration occur?

> [!success]- Answer
> Any person (legal or juristic) must register for VAT in respect of any business that he/it operates, once the turnover of such a business reaches **R1,000,000 per annum**.

Related: [[Tax Rates and VAT Registration]]

## Q5 — Separate juristic personality (3)

> [!question] Question
> What do you understand by the term "Separate juristic personality" and give an example of an entity that qualifies as such.

> [!success]- Answer
> A juristic person is a non-human entity which, like a human (natural person), is able to incur rights and obligations, and can sue and be sued in its own name. The persons who run a juristic person do not enjoy the rights and obligations of the juristic person themselves — they are merely agents of the juristic person (such as directors of companies, or members of CCs). A close corporation or a company is a juristic person.

Related: [[Private Companies]], [[Close Corporations]]
