## Q1 — Advice letter: auto repair business (10)

> [!question] Question
> X, Y, Z want to commence an Auto Repair business. They come to you for advice as to which form of business enterprise would be suitable for their proposed business. They do not want to be personally liable for the debts of the business should the business run into financial difficulties. Advise them of the form of business enterprise that would be suitable to them and why.

> [!success]- Answer
> **A Close Corporation.** A close corporation has a legal personality separate from its members. The establishment, administration and operation of a close corporation is subject to a minimum number of formalities. It is simple and cheap to register. It provides for perpetual succession and continuity, and exists apart from its members. No shares are issued and the liability of members is restricted.
>
> A single person may establish a close corporation and the object need not be that of making a profit. All the members must be natural persons or trustees of natural persons. Each member is entitled to be involved in the business of the corporation and has equal rights to manage and represent the corporation. Under certain specified circumstances the corporation may buy in members' interests or may render financial assistance to members for the acquisition of members' interests. The income of the corporation is taxed at the current rate of taxation applicable to companies. Transfers and acquisitions of members' interests are not liable to stamp duty. Annual Returns must be submitted to the Registrar of Close Corporations annually.

## Q2 — Judicial management sequence of events (5)

> [!question] Question
> Briefly describe the sequence of events in the judicial management process. (Any five events)

> [!success]- Answer
> An interested party applies to court to have a company placed under judicial management. The court grants a provisional judicial management order. The Master appoints a provisional judicial manager and convenes separate meetings of creditors, members and debenture holders (if any). The provisional judicial manager takes over the management of the company. At the meetings, the provisional judicial manager reports fully on the financial situation and future prospects for the company. If a final judicial management order is granted, the court makes an order vesting the management of the company in the final judicial manager, subject to the supervision of the court. The final judicial manager takes over management and investigates the company's affairs in the same manner as a liquidator would. When surplus funds become available, he distributes these amongst creditors. In due course, the judicial manager may apply either for cancellation of the judicial management order, or for the winding-up of the company.

## Q4 — Ordinary resolution (4)

> [!question] Question
> Describe a resolution taken by simple majority.

> [!success]- Answer
> A company resolution is the formal decision of a company in general meeting to act in a certain way. Normally a company resolution is passed by a simple majority of members present and entitled to vote, and who constitute a quorum. A "resolution" as such is not defined in the Act, and the requirement of a simple majority is the natural result of the principle that the minority must subject themselves to the wishes of the majority acting in accordance with the company's constitution. A resolution taken on a simple majority is generally known as an **ordinary resolution** and is required for all decisions of a general meeting other than those requiring either a specified majority or a special resolution. An ordinary resolution operates from the date of adoption but may be operative from a different date if it so provides.
