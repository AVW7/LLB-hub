## Q1 — Opinion letter: Close Corporation vs Partnership (10)

> [!question] Question
> Draft an opinion in the form of a letter, advising your client why it would be preferable to use a Close Corporation over that of a Partnership.

> [!success]- Answer
> **Close Corporation** (any 4): has legal personality separate from its members; establishment/administration/operation subject to a minimum number of formalities and duties; a single person may establish it and the object need not be to make a profit — may not have more than ten members; no shares are issued, members have members' interests expressed as a percentage of the total; all members must be natural persons or trustees of natural persons (legal persons may not be members, directly or indirectly, save as provided by s29(2) of the Close Corporations Act); each member is entitled to be involved in the business and has equal rights to manage and represent the corporation, with no separate board of directors; under certain circumstances the CC may buy in members' interests or render financial assistance to members for the acquisition of members' interests; the Act has been decriminalised to a large extent, but members risk personal liability for the CC's debts if certain provisions are not complied with or they place the CC and its creditors unduly at risk.
>
> **Partnership** (any 4): the object of a partnership must be to make a profit; a partnership does not have juristic personality separate from the partners; each partner must make a contribution (money, goods, services, or a combination), which determines their interest; the life of the partnership is not separate from the life/lives of the partners — it ends if a partner dies (no continuity); profits and net assets are usually distributed between partners on dissolution in the same proportion as their respective interests; a contractual relationship is established which can come about verbally or in writing, express or implied; not more than 20 members unless allowed in terms of s30(2) of the Companies Act.
>
> 2 marks are allocated for how the student compares the two entities, reaches the conclusion that the CC is better, and drafts the letter incorporating an opinion.

## Q2 — Company registration documents (5)

> [!question] Question
> List the documents which are required for the registration of a company.

> [!success]- Answer
> Any 5 of: Power of Attorney (in favour of the attorney attending to registration); Reservation of Name (Form CM5); Memorandum of Association (Certificate of Incorporation — Form CM1, Memorandum of Association — Form CM2); Articles (Form C44); Registered Office (Form CM22); List of Directors, Auditors and Officers (Form CM29); Appointment of Auditors (Form CM31); Certificate to commence business (Form CM46); Sufficiency of Capital (Form CM47).

## Q3 — Tax rates (4)

> [!question] Question
> What are the Tax Rates for the following Business Enterprises? (a) A Close Corporation? (b) A Private Individual? (c) A Business Trust? (d) A Company?

> [!success]- Answer
> (a) 28%. (b) 18–40%. (c) 40%. (d) 28%.

## Q4 — True/false (6)

> [!question] Question
> (a) The person who is appointed as trustee in terms of a trust instrument is authorized to act in such capacity by any of the Magistrates of the Magistrates Court, granted on application.
> (b) Input Tax is the VAT charged by a vendor on supplies of goods and services made by him.
> (c) Output Tax is the VAT paid by a vendor within the chain, on goods and services acquired by him.
> (d) Tax is levied on capital gain from 1 April 2001.
> (e) When a company is deregistered it loses its juristic personality and its incorporated status.
> (f) In Partnerships — the partner en commandite is purely a financial participant with restricted liability.

> [!success]- Answer
> (a) False. (b) False. (c) False. (d) True. (e) True. (f) True.
