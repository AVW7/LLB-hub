## Q1 — True/false (5)

> [!question] Question
> 1.1 If the sole proprietor dies, the business ceases to exist. (1)
> 1.2 There are no statutory requirements specifically governing the setting up and running of a sole proprietorship. (1)
> 1.3 If a partner in a partnership dies, the partnership ceases to exist. (1)
> 1.4 A partnership is not a taxpayer for income tax purposes. (1)
> 1.5 Any partnership can have up to 30 members. (1)

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 True. 1.2 True. 1.3 True. 1.4 True. 1.5 False.

## Q2 — Pre-incorporation contracts (5)

> [!question] Question
> 2.1 What is the name given to a contract entered into on behalf of a company not yet incorporated/still to be formed? (1)
> 2.2 What are the requirements, as per the Companies Act of 2008, for such a contract to be made binding on and enforceable by the company after it has been incorporated? (2)
> 2.3 If you were acting for the other party in a pre-incorporation contract situation (i.e. not for the company to be formed), what clause would you include in the contract to protect your client? (2)

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 A **pre-incorporation contract**.
> 2.2 It must be **in writing**; and it must be entered into **in the name of or on behalf of a company still to be formed**.
> 2.3 A clause providing: "Should the contract not be ratified, or is partially ratified by the company, or should the company not be formed, then the signator is fully liable in terms of the contract."

Related: [[Pre-Incorporation Contracts]]

## Q3 — Trust beneficiaries; inter vivos vs mortis causa (6)

> [!question] Question
> 3.1 Name the two kinds of beneficiaries that a trust deed can have, and explain each of them. (4)
> 3.2 Explain the difference between an inter vivos trust and a trust mortis causa. (2)

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 **Income Beneficiaries** — receive the net income after the payment of expenses. **Capital Beneficiaries** — receive a portion of, or the whole of, the trust fund.
> 3.2 **Inter vivos trust** — a trust created between living persons. **Mortis causa trust** — created in the will of a deceased person, also known as a testamentary trust.

Related: [[Trusts]]

## Q4 — Choice of entity: supermarket business (5)

> [!question] Question
> Your client wishes to open a supermarket business. Discuss what factors should be considered when advising your client on the best form of business enterprise to be utilized and indicate which form of business enterprise is most suitable in your opinion. Motivate your answer.

> [!success]- Answer
> Factors to consider:
> - Do they require the enterprise to be a separate legal entity?
> - Do they want protection from creditors (limited liability)?
> - Perpetual succession?
> - Cost-effectiveness in establishment and administration?
> - Management style — should the members be entitled to bind the enterprise?
>
> Markers use their own discretion in determining whether the student has motivated their recommendation in light of the above factors.

## Q5 — Converting a public company to a private company (4)

> [!question] Question
> You are consulted by the board of directors of Problemco Ltd who tell you that they wish to change the company to Problemco (Pty) Ltd, since their shares are not held by the public. Advise the client on the steps that have to be taken to effect the change.

> [!success]- Answer
> - The process to convert a public company is not specifically provided for in terms of the Companies Act 2008.
> - The Company must amend its MOI to include the specific criteria which will qualify it as a private company, namely it prohibits it from offering its securities to the public and restricts the transferability of its securities.
> - The company's name must be amended as provided for in terms of the Companies Act.
> - The company has to amend its name by altering the suffix of the name to reflect the category of profit company which it will now fall under, namely from "Ltd" to "(Pty) Ltd".
> - This is effected by completion and lodging of the appropriate form, namely the **CoR 9.1** form.
