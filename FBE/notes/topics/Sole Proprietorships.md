# Sole Proprietorships

## Core characteristics

- **No distinction between the personal estate** (assets and liabilities) of the proprietor and those of the business — if the business is declared insolvent, the proprietor's **personal assets can be attached**.
- **No continuity** — the death of the proprietor amounts to the demise of the business; there is no succession.
- Success or failure of the business is tied to the ability of the owner.
- The proceeds of the business are **taxed in the hands of the owner** — as an individual, on the sliding scale (**18–40%**), **not** a flat rate.
- Can be terminated easily, without formality.
- The owner has the **sole right to all the profits** of the enterprise.
- The **credit worthiness of the sole proprietor is linked to that of the owner**.
- No statutory requirements specifically govern the setting up and running of a sole proprietorship.
- A sole proprietor **can operate more than one business** (a "cannot operate more than one business" statement is false).

## Disadvantages (exam favourite)

1. Business insolvency exposes the proprietor's personal assets.
2. No succession on the owner's death.
3. The business's fate is tied entirely to the owner's ability.
4. Business income is taxed in the owner's hands at individual rates.

## How cited in an agreement

> Mr A Bloggs (Identity No: 6611030077008) trading as ABC Superstores

## True/false traps

- "A sole proprietor is taxed at the flat rate of 40%" → **False** — taxed at individual sliding-scale rates (18–40%), the same as any natural person.
