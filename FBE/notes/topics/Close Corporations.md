# Close Corporations

> [!important] Statutory Update (Companies Act 71 of 2008 & Current Tax Rates)
> - **No New Close Corporations Since 1 May 2011**: Under Schedule 2 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008, **no new CCs may be incorporated** (no new CK1 filings) and **companies may no longer be converted into CCs** after 1 May 2011. Existing CCs continue indefinitely and **can** convert into companies under the 2008 Act.
> - **Corporate Income Tax Rate**: Reduced from **28% to 27%** for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023 (2009–2012 archive memos cite **28%**).

The default "safe" recommendation across the FBE papers — appears in every sitting, usually as the answer to "which entity avoids personal liability?"

## Core characteristics

- Legal personality **separate from its members** (juristic person).
- Establishment, administration and operation subject to a **minimum number of formalities** — cheap and simple to register and to run (no audit required).
- A **single person** may establish a CC; the object need **not** be to make a profit.
- **Maximum 10 members.**
- **No shares** — members have **members' interests**, expressed as a **percentage** of the total members' interests.
- All members must be **natural persons, or trustees of natural persons** — legal persons may not be members, directly or indirectly, save as provided in **s29(2)** of the Close Corporations Act.
- Each member is entitled to be involved in the business and has **equal rights to manage and represent** the corporation — no separate board of directors.
- Perpetual succession / continuity — exists apart from its members.
- Under specific circumstances the CC may **buy back members' interests** or render **financial assistance** to members for the acquisition of members' interests.
- Income taxed at the **company tax rate — currently 28%** — favourable "Small Business Corporation" treatment may apply.
- Transfer/acquisition of members' interests **not liable to stamp duty**.
- Annual Returns must be submitted to the **Registrar of Close Corporations** annually.
- The Act is largely decriminalised, but members risk **personal liability** for CC debts if statutory provisions are not complied with, or if they place the CC and its creditors unduly at risk.
- A **writ can attach a member's interest and loan account** in a CC.
- The **Magistrate's Court does have jurisdiction** to hear an application for liquidation of a CC (a "no jurisdiction" statement is false).

## Formation / registration steps

1. **CK7** — reservation of the entity's name, stamped with a **R50 revenue stamp**.
2. Once the name is approved, a **CK1** — containing the members' names and all relevant CC details — is lodged, stamped with a **R100 revenue stamp**. (CK1 is completed in duplicate and signed by the members or their duly authorised agent.)
3. Together with the CK1, a **letter in which the accounting officer accepts the position** must be lodged.

## Converting a company into a CC

To convert a company into a CC, the company must have **ten or fewer members**.

## Close Corporation vs Private Company (quick comparison)

| | Private Company | Close Corporation |
|---|---|---|
| Legal status | Juristic person | Juristic person |
| Continuity | Yes | Yes |
| Managed by | Directors | Members |
| Membership cap | Not more than 50 | Not more than 10 |
| Founding document | Memorandum of Association | Founding Statement |
