# Trusts

## The three parties

1. **The founder** (creator or donor).
2. **The trustees.**
3. **The beneficiaries.**

## Types of beneficiary

- **Income beneficiaries** — receive the net income after payment of expenses.
- **Capital beneficiaries** — receive a portion, or the whole, of the trust fund.

## Inter vivos vs mortis causa

- **Inter vivos trust** — created between living persons.
- **Mortis causa trust** — created in the will of a deceased person; also known as a **testamentary trust**. The **original Will** must be lodged with the Master of the High Court.

## Registration procedure (inter vivos trust)

1. The **Deed of Trust / Trust Deed** is prepared and drafted.
2. All trusts are registered with the **Master of the High Court**.
3. An amount of **R100** is payable to the Master.
4. The duly signed Deed of Trust, the trustees' acceptance of appointment, together with a **letter of consent from an auditor** of the trust, are lodged with the Master for registration.
5. **Letters of Authority** are then issued by the Master in terms of **s6 of the Trust Property Control Act** — this is what authorises a trustee to act, not appointment by a Magistrate.

## Tax rate

- Business trust: **40%** (per [[FBE 2010-05-22]] Q3c).
- True/false trap: "The rate of tax of a trust is 29% for the year ending 29 February 2008" → **False** per [[FBE 2009-12-03 Special Supp]] Q5.4 — the memo does not state what the correct historical figure was, only that 29% is wrong. Don't memorise 29% as a real rate; the current rate to know is **40%**.

## True/false traps seen in the papers

- "A Trust Deed need not be lodged with the Master" → **False** — it must be lodged.
- "The trustee is authorised to act by any Magistrate of the Magistrate's Court, on application" → **False** — authority comes from the Master's Letters of Authority, not a Magistrate.
- "A trust can be established as an inter vivos trust for the benefit of the beneficiaries" → **True**.
