# Private Companies — Formation, Documents & Resolutions

The broadest topic in the module — covers registration formalities, resolutions, financial assistance, and company conversions.

## Documents required to register a company

Any 5 of:
- Power of Attorney (in favour of the attorney attending to registration)
- Reservation of Name (**Form CM5**)
- Memorandum of Association (Certificate of Incorporation **Form CM1**, Memorandum of Association **Form CM2**)
- Articles (**Form C44**)
- Registered Office (**Form CM22**)
- List of Directors, Auditors and Officers (**Form CM29**)
- Appointment of Auditors (**Form CM31**)
- Certificate to commence business (**Form CM46**)
- Sufficiency of Capital (**Form CM47**)

## Resolutions

A **company resolution** is the formal decision of a company in general meeting to act in a certain way.

- **Ordinary resolution** — passed by a **simple majority** of members present and entitled to vote, constituting a quorum. Required for all general-meeting decisions other than those requiring a specified majority or a special resolution. Operates from the date of adoption unless it provides otherwise.
- **Special resolution** — a Special Resolution is **not** passed on a simple majority vote (a "simple majority" statement about special resolutions is false); a Special Resolution is **not** automatically required to be lodged with the Registrar of Companies within six months (that statement is also false).

## Financial assistance — s38 Companies Act

- **s38** prohibits a company from giving **direct or indirect financial assistance** for the purpose of, or in connection with, the purchase of any shares in that company.
- Example: financing a share purchase via a bank loan secured by a notarial bond over the target company's assets — **not permissible**.
- **Exception**: repaying an existing **Shareholder's Loan Account** does **not** constitute financial assistance relating to the sale of the company's shares — that **is** permissible.

## Widely held company

A company is a widely held company if:
1. Its articles provide for **unrestricted transfer** of its shares; or
2. It is permitted by its articles to **offer shares to the public**; or
3. It decides by **special resolution** to be a widely held company.

## Membership cap and s21 companies

- Membership of a **private company is limited to 50**.
- A **s21 company** (non-profit) may have **more than 50 members** because, for the purposes of the Act, it is regarded as a **Public Company**.

## Converting Ltd → (Pty) Ltd

Conversion of a public company to a private company is not itself provided for as a set procedure in the Companies Act 2008; in practice:
1. Amend the company's **MOI** to include the criteria that qualify it as a private company — prohibiting the offer of securities to the public and restricting transferability of securities.
2. Amend the company's **name** as provided for in the Companies Act — change the suffix from "Ltd" to "(Pty) Ltd".
3. Effected by completing and lodging **Form CoR 9.1**.

## Deregistration and shareholders' authority

- When a company is **deregistered**, it **loses its juristic personality and its incorporated status**.
- **Shareholders** are **not** entitled to conclude contracts on behalf of a company (that is the directors'/agents' function).
- A **Close Corporation cannot** be a member of a company — false as a blanket statement is worth checking against the specific fact pattern, but per the archive this is tested as **false** (i.e. a CC *can* hold shares in a company).

## Private Company vs Close Corporation

| | Private Company | Close Corporation |
|---|---|---|
| Legal status | Juristic person | Juristic person |
| Continuity | Yes | Yes |
| Managed by | Directors | Members |
| Membership cap | Not more than 50 | Not more than 10 |
| Founding document | Memorandum of Association | Founding Statement |
