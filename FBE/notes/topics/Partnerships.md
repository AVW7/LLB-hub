# Partnerships

Tested almost every sitting, usually as either (a) "name the three types" or (b) a true/false trap on profit motive, taxpayer status, or member numbers.

## Core characteristics

- The **object of a partnership must be to make a profit** — a partnership **cannot** be formed as a non-profit / charitable organisation.
- **No juristic personality** separate from the partners.
- Each partner must **make a contribution** — money, goods, services, or a combination — which determines their interest in the partnership.
- **No continuity** — the partnership's life is not separate from the partners' lives; it ends when a partner dies.
- Profits and net assets are usually distributed among partners on dissolution in the same proportion as their respective interests.
- A **contractual relationship**, which can be verbal or written, express or implied.
- **Each partner can bind the partnership**, and carries the full risk of the enterprise if it fails.
- Maximum **not more than 20 members**, unless permitted by **s30(2) of the Companies Act**.
- A partnership is **not itself a taxpayer** for income tax purposes — profits flow through and are taxed in the hands of the individual partners.

## The three types of partnership

| Type | Description | Liability |
|---|---|---|
| **Ordinary partnership** | The standard partnership | Partners are **jointly and severally liable** for the debts of the partnership |
| **Anonymous (sleeping) partner** | Not known to the public | Liable only for their **pro-rata share** of the debts |
| **Commanditarian (en commandite) partner** | Purely a financial participant/investor, not known to the public | Liability **limited to their specific contribution** or an agreed amount |

## How a partnership is cited in an agreement

> A, B and C as partners of the ABC Partnership
