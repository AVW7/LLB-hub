# Choosing a Business Entity — Comparative Advice Letters

The recurring FBE exam pattern: a client fact pattern (wants no personal liability, wants profit, is risk-averse, etc.) and you must **recommend and motivate** a form of business enterprise — often in the form of a drafted opinion letter. This note pulls together the comparison points used across the real memos so you can build the letter fast.

## Master comparison table (from the actual memos)

| | Sole Proprietorship | Partnership | Close Corporation | Private Company | Business Trust |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Legal personality | None — no distinction from owner | None — no juristic personality | Separate juristic person | Separate juristic person | Registered with Master; trustees hold in a fiduciary capacity |
| Liability | Unlimited — owner's personal assets at risk | Joint & several (ordinary); pro-rata (anonymous); limited to contribution (commanditarian) | Limited — restricted to members' interests (subject to personal-liability risk on non-compliance) | Limited — restricted to shares held | Trustees/beneficiaries generally not personally liable for trust debts |
| Continuity | None — ends on owner's death | None — ends on a partner's death | Perpetual succession | Perpetual succession | Continues per the trust deed, independent of founder/trustees |
| Members/formation | 1 (the owner); minimal formality | Up to 20 (unless s30(2) Companies Act allows more); contractual, can be informal | 1–10 natural persons/trustees of natural persons; CK7 + CK1 | Up to 50 members; full set of registration documents (CM1/CM2/C44/CM22 etc.) | Founder, trustees, beneficiaries; Deed of Trust registered with Master, R100 fee |
| Tax | Individual sliding scale, 18–40%, in owner's hands | Not a taxpayer — flows through to partners' individual rates | 28% (company rate; Small Business Corporation relief may apply) | 28% | 40% (business trust rate) |
| Management | Owner alone | Any partner can bind the partnership | All members — equal management/representation rights, no separate board | Directors, separate from members/shareholders | Trustees, per the trust deed and their fiduciary duties |

## Factors to weigh when advising a client (from the real memos)

- Do they require the enterprise to be a **separate legal entity**?
- Do they want **protection from creditors** (limited liability)?
- Is **perpetual succession** important to them?
- **Cost-effectiveness** of establishment and ongoing administration?
- **Management style** — should any/all participants be entitled to bind the enterprise, or should management be centralised?

## Worked patterns seen in the archive

- **"We don't want personal liability for business debts"** → recommend a **Close Corporation** (cheap, simple, limited liability, suits a small number of natural-person owners). Seen in [[FBE 2010-05-22]] Q1 and [[FBE 2012-05-16 Supp]] Q1.
- **"Highly profitable but high financial risk"** → still a **Close Corporation** — limited liability matters most when risk is high. Seen in [[FBE 2010-11-16 Supp]] Q2.
- **"Opening a supermarket"** (general commercial venture) → walk through the five factors above, then recommend — markers award marks for the reasoning, not just the answer. Seen in [[FBE 2012-05-05]] Q4.

## Drafting an opinion letter

The 2010-05-22 memo confirms markers allocate marks specifically for:
1. How the two (or more) entity types are **compared**;
2. How the recommendation is **reached and motivated**; and
3. The **general drafting** quality of the letter itself (proper letter form, not just a bullet list).
