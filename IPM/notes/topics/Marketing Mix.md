# Marketing Mix (the 5 Ps)

Tested both as a "list and describe" knowledge question and as a full "draft the marketing plan" practical/scenario question.

## The 5 Ps

| P | Applied to a law practice |
|---|---|
| **Product** | The quality of the service your firm offers. Ascertain your clients' needs as they express them; every employee must be committed to service quality; credibility and professionalism are key. |
| **Price** | Your firm's fee structure — should be in line with recommended/applicable tariffs (where one exists) and consistent with your firm's objectives. In unregulated fields (e.g. personal injury) you have more discretion, but should benchmark against other practitioners in the field. |
| **Place** | The location of your practice — accessible and convenient. Consider visibility, ease of access (including for clients with disabilities), and proximity to public transport/courts. |
| **Promotion** | Ways and means of promoting your services — communicating with the public and fellow practitioners (e.g. advertising in De Rebus for referrals), sponsorships, entertainment, seminars, lectures. How you handle client complaints is itself a (powerful) promotional tool. |
| **People** | Employing competent staff, and looking after clients well. |

## Practice notes

- A "draft the marketing plan" scenario question wants you to work through **each of the 5 Ps in turn, applied to the given facts** (e.g. a personal injury firm on a busy CBD road) — 1 mark for naming the element, 1 mark for applying it to the facts.
- Don't confuse this with [[SWOT Analysis]] — SWOT assesses your position; the marketing mix is what you actually *do* about it.
