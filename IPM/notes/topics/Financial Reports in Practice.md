# Financial Reports in Practice

The core set of financial statements/reports a practitioner must understand — tested repeatedly as "discuss the significance of" list questions.

## The reports

| Report | What it shows | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Income Statement** | Performance of the practice for a specified accounting period — income and expenses, showing profit or loss. | Measures profitability over a period. |
| **Balance Sheet** | Financial position of the practice at a particular point in time — assets, liabilities and the practitioner's equity. | Determines the financial strength of the business. |
| **Cash Flow Statement** | Inflow and outflow of cash for a particular period, indicating a positive or negative bank balance. | Determines liquidity — ensures there's enough cash to pay day-to-day expenses. |
| **Debtor's Age Analysis Report** | Each debtor's account balance, aged according to the month the debt arose; totals per month. | Management of cash flow / credit control. |
| **Trust Surplus Report** | Indicates the legitimate amount available for transfer from the client's trust account to the firm's business account. | Ensures monies do not accumulate unnecessarily in the trust account — trust surplus should be kept as small as possible. |

## Practice notes

- Don't confuse the **Cash Flow Statement/Report** (a report on actual cash movement) with a **Cash Flow Projection** (a forward-looking forecast) or a **Budget** (profit/income-and-expense planning) — see [[Budgeting and Cash Flow]] for the distinction, which is separately examinable.
- For "discuss the significance" questions, structure each report as: (1) what it shows, (2) why/what it's used for.
