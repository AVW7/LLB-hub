# SWOT Analysis

A strategic-planning tool for assessing your own practice — tested both as a "list the elements" knowledge question and as an "apply it to yourself" practical question (the latter carries much heavier marks).

## The four elements

| Element | What it covers | Locus |
|---|---|---|
| **Strengths** | Personal factors that give your practice an advantage — e.g. qualifications, experience, expertise, no family commitments | Internal |
| **Weaknesses** | Personal factors that work to your disadvantage — e.g. lack of experience, size of firm, a specialisation that's hard to break into | Internal |
| **Opportunities** | Factors in the outside environment with a positive effect — e.g. demand for your services, your target market, being situated next to court, few competing attorneys in the area | External |
| **Threats** | Factors in the outside environment with a negative effect — e.g. competition, the economic climate, a saturated CBD, banks now offering services you specialised in | External |

## Why it matters

A SWOT analysis assists in the preparation of your **strategic plan** — i.e. your long-term and short-term goals. See [[The Business Plan]].

## Practice notes

- When asked to "apply" SWOT to yourself, give **two concrete examples per element** — the memo awards marks per example, not per generic definition.
- Strengths/Weaknesses = look inward at yourself and your practice. Opportunities/Threats = look outward at the market and environment. Don't mix these up.
