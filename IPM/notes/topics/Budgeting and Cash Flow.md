# Budgeting and Cash Flow

Two full assignments in the archive are built entirely around drafting a practical start-up budget — this is the module's signature "practical application" exercise.

## Budget vs Cash Flow Projection

A recurring short-answer distinction:

- **Budget** — relates to **profit**, i.e. income and expense planning.
- **Cash Flow Projection** — relates to the **inward or outward movement of cash** in the practice/business bank account.

## Building a start-up budget — the recurring scenario

The fact pattern repeats across sittings: a newly admitted attorney (often opening with a friend) approaches a bank for a start-up loan, and must submit a budget (often for the first six or twelve months) showing the practice can service loan repayments.

Typical line items expected in the answer:
- **Fee income** (show your basis of calculation — e.g. fees charged per day × number of days)
- Office rent
- Salaries (e.g. receptionist)
- Telephone & fax / cellular
- Electricity and water
- Rental of fax machine / PC / photocopier (where capital expenditure is being limited by renting rather than buying)
- Insurance (on equipment)
- Postage
- Stationery (files, pens, printing paper, envelopes)
- Other office supplies (refreshments, cleaning material)
- Travelling/vehicle expenses
- Advertising
- Accountant's fee (to write up the books)
- Bank charges & interest on overdraft
- Loan repayment (state the rate, e.g. R3 000/month)
- An excess/breakeven figure

## Practice notes

- Marking rewards **format and presentation** as much as content — always present the budget as a structured list/table with a total, not prose.
- Always show your **basis of calculation** for fee income where the question asks for it — this is usually worth separate marks from the line items themselves.
- If a scenario gives you a lump sum split between setup costs and a monthly amount, do the arithmetic explicitly (e.g. balance ÷ 12 months) before listing expenses.
