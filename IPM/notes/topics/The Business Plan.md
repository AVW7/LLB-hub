# The Business Plan

Recurs as a full scenario question — newly admitted attorneys about to open their own practice, needing to draft a business plan. Consistently the highest-mark single question in whichever paper it appears.

## Why draft a business plan

1. It commits and focuses your thinking
2. It's a reminder of your personal objectives
3. It's needed for budget planning
4. It's needed for assessing finance (e.g. loan applications)

## Main elements of a business plan

| Element | What it deals with |
|---|---|
| Heading and introduction | — |
| Brief description of the business | What the practice does, its focus areas |
| Personal objectives | Your own goals for the practice |
| Business structure | Sole proprietorship, partnership, incorporated etc. |
| Resources plan | Staff, equipment, premises |
| Financial plan | See [[Budgeting and Cash Flow]] and [[Financial Reports in Practice]] |
| Strategic plan | Long-term and short-term goals — see below |
| Operational plan | Day-to-day running of the practice |
| Client care plan | See [[Client Liaison and Complaint Handling]] |

## The strategic plan component

The strategic plan sub-section of a business plan typically deals with items such as:
- Long-term goals of the practice
- Short-term goals of the practice
- A [[SWOT Analysis]] to inform those goals

## Practice notes

- "Why is it important to draft a business plan" and "set out the main elements" are consistently asked **together** as one combined question — learn them as a pair.
- When the question gives you named characters ("Mr Peter Pan and Miss Olive Oil," "Ms Baloyi and Ms Mooka"), that's purely a fact-pattern wrapper — the structure of the answer doesn't change.
