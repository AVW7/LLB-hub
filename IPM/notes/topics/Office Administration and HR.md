# Office Administration and HR

Covers the practical, day-to-day running of a practice — instructing staff, and deciding what a practitioner should handle personally versus outsource to a professional.

## Instructions to a secretary — incoming mail

1. Date-stamp all incoming mail
2. Record monies receipted
3. Sort and distribute mail
4. Confidential mail is to remain unopened

## Self vs professional assistance — a start-up checklist

The 2019 paper poses a practical judgment-call question: which of the following should a soon-to-open practitioner do personally, and which should be handed to a professional?

- Working out procedures for opening files and keeping account details for each client
- Job descriptions for staff
- Letters of appointment and conditions of service
- A sick leave policy
- Financial planning, budgeting and control

> [!note] No memo on file for this question
> [[IPM 2019-05 Assignment]] is an orphan (self-assessment, no submission/memo). Reason from first principles: routine administrative drafting (file-opening procedures, basic job descriptions, standard appointment letters, a sick leave policy) is generally something a practitioner can competently do themselves; anything requiring specialist financial or actuarial judgment (detailed financial planning, budgeting and control, or complex HR/labour-law drafting) is safer handed to an accountant, financial planner, or HR/labour law specialist. Confirm this reasoning against your study group or lecturer notes.
