## Q1 — Emotional intelligence at work (10)

> [!question] Question
> You are receiving a lot of complaints from various clients about the bad manners and unprofessional conduct of one of your employees. You need to address the issue of emotional intelligence with the particular employee and have a meeting with her. Mention and discuss the 5 components of emotional intelligence at work.

> [!success]- Answer
> **Self awareness** — the ability to recognize and understand your moods, emotions, and drives, as well as their effect on others.
> **Self regulation** — the ability to control or redirect disruptive impulses and moods; the propensity to suspend judgment — to think before acting.
> **Motivation** — a passion to work for reasons that go beyond money or status; a propensity to pursue goals with energy and persistence.
> **Empathy** — the ability to understand the emotional make-up of other people; skill in treating people according to their emotional reactions.
> **Social skill** — proficiency in managing relationships and building networks; an ability to find common ground and build rapport.

## Q2 — The three basic insurance needs (5)

> [!question] Question
> Insurance can be applied to satisfy the needs which arise when certain events occur. Discuss the three basic forms of these needs and give an example of each.

> [!success]- Answer
> **Permanent need** — to meet certain obligations in the event of death (e.g. paying estate liabilities, making a bequest).
> **Temporary need** — an obligation that should be met at the expiry of a certain period of time, or at death prior to that period (e.g. purchasing a partner's interest when they retire in 10 years' time, but also if the partner should die before that time).
> **Future need** — an obligation to be met at some stage in the future (e.g. a person who wishes to make provision for their child's university studies).

## Q3 — Draft a marketing plan (10)

> [!question] Question
> You are the newly appointed director for a medium-size law firm specializing in personal injury claims. Your offices are situated on a busy road in the business centre of the city, but in the past the directors didn't pay much attention to marketing of the firm. It was decided that, in future, you will be responsible for the marketing. Draft the marketing plan for your firm.

> [!success]- Answer
> Use the marketing mix (5 Ps) to formulate the plan:
> **Product** — you are marketing legal services; field of expertise is personal injury claims; ascertain client needs as expressed by them; every employee must be committed to service quality; credibility and professionalism assist in marketing the product.
> **Price** — only limited control over price; because you specialise in personal injury claims there is no fixed scale/tariff; find out what other practitioners in the same field charge, and ensure fees are in line with the practice's direction.
> **Place** — situated on a busy road in the CBD; the bulk of clients will likely use public transport to reach your offices; ensure the offices are visible and accessible, including catering for people with disabilities (many personal injury victims struggle to navigate staircases).
> **Promotion** — communicate with the public and fellow practitioners; advertise (e.g. in De Rebus) for referrals from non-specialising practitioners; how you handle client complaints is also powerful promotion; sponsorships, entertainment, seminars and lectures can be used to promote the business.
