## Q1 — Striking off for DUI (3)

> [!question] Question
> You are convicted of driving under the influence of alcohol. The Law Society applies to have your name struck from the roll. What is the High Court likely to do, and why?

> [!success]- Answer
> The High Court is not likely to strike the name of an attorney from the roll for an offence such as DUI, as the test is whether the individual remains a fit and proper person to practise. The court will probably still award costs to the Society, as it performs a fiduciary duty. If the offence involved dishonesty, this would clearly affect fitness to practise and the attorney would probably be struck or suspended.

Related: [[Admission as an Attorney and Fitness to Practise]]

## Q2 — Trust theft and prescribed claims (6)

> [!question] Question
> Your former bookkeeper has stolen a large sum of trust monies belonging to various clients. The clients' files are missing and a number of claims have become prescribed. Explain how the clients would go about recovering their losses arising from: 2.1 the theft of the trust funds (4); 2.2 the claims that have become prescribed (2).

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 A claim must be lodged via the statutory Law Society to the Attorneys Fidelity Fund, which is charged with indemnifying clients who have suffered loss arising from theft of money entrusted to the attorney in the course of his practice. The Fund may require the attorney to be excussed before entertaining the claim. Time limits apply.
> 2.2 The client must be advised to consult another attorney to lodge a claim against the negligent attorney, who will refer the claim to the AIIF. The attorney may not admit liability in respect of the prescribed claim, as the AIIF can then repudiate the claim.

Related: [[Trust Account Theft and the Attorneys Fidelity Fund]], [[Prescribed Claims — Ethical Duty and Negligence Liability]]

## Q4 — Cannabis conviction, ongoing intentional illegality (5)

> [!question] Question
> During your articles you were convicted for possession and use of cannabis. You declare your intention to continue breaking the law, using cannabis, on the basis of your religious beliefs. The Law Society refuses to endorse your application for admission and you take the matter to court. What is the High Court likely to do, and why?

> [!success]- Answer
> The Court would likely hold that you are not a fit and proper person to be admitted, because as long as the prohibition on the use or possession of cannabis remains on the statute books you would constantly be breaking the law, which would bring the profession into disrepute.

Related: [[Admission as an Attorney and Fitness to Practise]]

## Q5 — Grounds to refuse a mandate (3)

> [!question] Question
> Name three situations in which an attorney would be obliged to refuse a mandate to act on behalf of a client.

> [!success]- Answer
> Where a conflict of interest exists; animosity between the attorney and client; where the attorney is not competent to handle the type of case.

Related: [[Mandate Acceptance, Refusal and Withdrawal]]

## Q6 — "Without prejudice" (1)

> [!question] Question
> What does the statement "without prejudice" mean?

> [!success]- Answer
> The right of a party to make statements which cannot be proved against him/her.
