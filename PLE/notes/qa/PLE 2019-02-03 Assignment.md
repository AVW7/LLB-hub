## Q1 — Prescribed debt, client instructs collection (9)

> [!question] Question
> The Prescription Act extinguishes most monetary claims three years after the creditor became aware of the claim. Mr Ndlovu instructs you to collect a debt and pays a deposit of R1000; it is clear to you the debt has prescribed. What advice will you give regarding: 1.1 your ethical duty (2); 1.2 prospects of success and legal costs (3); 1.3 civil procedure (3); 1.4 the presiding officer (1)?

> [!note]- Self-check pointers (not an official memo)
> See [[Prescribed Claims — Ethical Duty and Negligence Liability]]. 1.4 — note prescription must be specially pleaded by the defendant; a court will not raise it of its own accord.

## Q2 — Negligently prescribed claim, missing file (10)

> [!question] Question
> Your staff cannot locate Ms Adams's file. You recall she instructed you to claim damages from a motor vehicle accident. When the file is located, you realise you negligently allowed the claim to prescribe. What is your position relating to: 2.1 liability for the client's loss (1); 2.2 advice to the client (2); 2.3 further prosecution of the claim (2); 2.4 any protection for you (2); 2.5 the Law Society and misconduct (1); 2.6 your fees and a possible lien on documents (2)?

> [!note]- Self-check pointers (not an official memo)
> 2.1–2.4 → [[Prescribed Claims — Ethical Duty and Negligence Liability]] (liable to the client; advise the client to consult another attorney; the negligence claim against you proceeds instead of the dead underlying claim; do not admit liability — refer to the AIIF). 2.5 — potentially reportable/disciplinary conduct. 2.6 — weigh any lien over client documents for unpaid fees against your negligence exposure.

## Q11 — Guilty client, reckless/negligent driving with priors (5)

> [!question] Question
> Mr Pillay was involved in a collision with Ms Manzi and is charged with reckless and negligent driving. He admits to you he was distracted by his cellphone and briefly did not keep a proper lookout. Because he has previous convictions, he wishes to plead not guilty. May you accept the mandate and plead not guilty? If yes, are there restrictions on your defence? If no, motivate.

> [!note]- Self-check pointers (not an official memo)
> Apply the same structure as [[Defending a Guilty Client]]: yes, you may accept and plead not guilty; you may not lead evidence or put allegations inconsistent with the client's actual account, but may test the State's evidence and cross-examine on genuinely disputed issues.

## Q12 — Contingency fee calculation and a related mandate (10)

> [!question] Question
> You entered a contingency fee agreement with A for 15% of any court award in a damages claim from a motor vehicle collision. The court finds A and B each 50% negligent; damages suffered were R80 000 (A) and R20 000 (B).
> 12.1 How much is your 15% fee on the court's award? Show your calculation. (5)
> 12.2 B's passenger was injured and wants to claim against the Road Accident Fund, believing you are best placed to assist since you know the facts from acting for A. You have the time and expertise. Should you accept the mandate? Discuss briefly. (5)

> [!note]- Self-check pointers (not an official memo)
> 12.1 → [[Contingency Fee Agreements]] — apply the apportioned award to A, then the 15%, cross-checked against the statutory cap. 12.2 → [[Mandate Acceptance, Refusal and Withdrawal]] and [[Conflict of Interest]] — consider whether your prior involvement for A creates a conflict in now acting for B's passenger.
