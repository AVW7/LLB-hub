## Q1 — Guilty client, rape, full trial conduct (6)

> [!question] Question
> You are consulted by a client charged with rape. Your client admits to you that he committed the offence and is in fact guilty — you agree. However, he does not intend to plead guilty and wants you to represent him at trial.
> 1.1 May you represent the client who wants to plead not guilty? (1)
> 1.2 Explain in detail how you would conduct each step of the trial until judgment. (5)

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 Yes.
> 1.2 s115 — exercise the right to silence; cross-examine State witnesses to check credibility etc.; may not put false statements to them; if no discharge in terms of s174, close your case without leading any evidence and argue on the State's case.

Related: [[Defending a Guilty Client]]

## Q3 — Interviewing the opponent's witness (4)

> [!question] Question
> 3.1 May an attorney interview a witness subpoenaed by the other side in a civil matter, where he believes the witness has evidence relevant to his client's case? (1)
> 3.2 What procedure would you follow? (3)

> [!success]- Answer
> 3.1 Yes.
> 3.2 No one has property in a witness. An attorney is entitled to interview any person believed to have relevant information, and cannot be deprived of this right by the other side having subpoenaed or taken a statement from them. Etiquette requires notifying the other side, and that notification should precede the interview. They cannot refuse or demand to be present — restriction: if the witness has commenced testifying, the other side is entitled to attend.

Related: [[Interviewing an Opponent's Witness]]

## Q4 — Ethics queries, membership fees, Fidelity Fund, fee sharing (9)

> [!question] Question
> 4.1 If unsure whether a proposed step is ethically permissible, how should you go about getting an answer? (2)
> 4.2 What happens if you fail to pay your Law Society membership fees? (2)
> 4.3 What is the purpose for which the Attorneys Fidelity Fund was established? (2)
> 4.4 May a practitioner: 4.4.1 share fees earned with his/her candidate attorney? (1) 4.4.2 pay the candidate attorney commission based on fees generated? (1) 4.4.3 share fees with Counsel? (1)

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 Firstly consult the relevant authorities, then a senior colleague, and finally your Law Society.
> 4.2 You will not be allowed to practise or to charge fees.
> 4.3 To reimburse members of the public who suffer financial loss due to theft by an attorney, employee, or candidate attorney of money or property entrusted to that attorney.
> 4.4.1 May not share fees with a candidate attorney. 4.4.2 May pay a candidate attorney commission on fees generated. 4.4.3 May not share fees with Counsel.

Related: [[Admission as an Attorney and Fitness to Practise]], [[Trust Account Theft and the Attorneys Fidelity Fund]], [[Fee Assessment, Overreaching and Fee Sharing]]

## Q5 — Borrowing money from a client (3)

> [!question] Question
> May you borrow money from a client? Give reasons.

> [!success]- Answer
> Not advisable, unless the client is in the business of money lending. If not, and you decide to continue, the client must obtain legal advice on the money lending transaction — potential conflict of interest.
