## Q1 — Guilty client, armed robbery (5)

> [!question] Question
> You are consulted by X who is charged with armed robbery. He informs you that he did commit the alleged act but insists that you plead not-guilty.
> 1.1 May you represent him and offer the plea of not guilty? (1)
> 1.2 If so, subject to what ethical limits may you accept and execute the mandate? (4)

> [!success]- Answer
> 1.1 Yes.
> 1.2 You may not (a) lead evidence; (b) put allegations to witnesses; in conflict with the version given by the client. You may test the credibility and weight of evidence, e.g. on identity of the accused.

Related: [[Defending a Guilty Client]]

## Q2 — Trust theft and prescribed claims (6)

> [!question] Question
> An audit of your trust account reveals that your bookkeeper has stolen a large sum of trust money belonging to various clients. Upon investigation, many of these clients' files are missing and several claims have prescribed. Discuss what steps you will take to ensure the clients receive compensation for the theft of their trust funds, as well as the procedure to be followed for the prescribed claims.

> [!success]- Answer
> The attorney must repay the stolen trust money. If unable, clients may claim from the Attorneys' Fidelity Fund, which indemnifies the public against loss arising from theft of money entrusted to an attorney by that attorney or employees. Attorneys must refer clients whose claims have prescribed to consult another attorney, who will claim from you — a claim you then refer to the Attorneys' Indemnity Insurance Fund. The attorney must not admit liability, as this will vitiate insurance cover. There are excess payments and maximums on cover.

Related: [[Trust Account Theft and the Attorneys Fidelity Fund]]

## Q3 — Grounds to terminate a mandate (3)

> [!question] Question
> Name three situations in which an attorney is obliged to terminate his/her mandate.

> [!success]- Answer
> Any three: (1) conflict of interest arises or becomes known; (2) animosity/breakdown of trust arises; (3) lack of expertise on new issues; (4) failure to provide instructions; (5) failure to pay agreed deposit.

Related: [[Mandate Acceptance, Refusal and Withdrawal]]

## Q4 — Contingency fee agreement (6)

> [!question] Question
> Client Y instructs you to institute action against the SAPS after being assaulted by members of the Service. He has no funds to pay your fees and asks whether you might agree to be paid only upon successful completion of the litigation. The claim clearly has merit.
> 4.1 Are you allowed to conclude such an arrangement, and under what authority? (2)
> 4.2 What are the prescribed formalities? (2)
> 4.3 What is the maximum fee you may charge? (2)

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 The Contingency Fees Act permits such an agreement.
> 4.2 The agreement must be in writing, in a form and lodged as prescribed by the Regulations.
> 4.3 The practitioner may charge double his/her normal fee, or up to 25% of the amount awarded, whichever is the lesser.

Related: [[Contingency Fee Agreements]]

## Q5 — Hostile client / outside expertise (4)

> [!question] Question
> What would you do as the attorney in the following situations?
> 5.1 A consultation with a client becomes heated and hostile and the client storms out of your office. (2)
> 5.2 You are approached by a client who wishes to have one of his inventions patented. You know nothing about patent law. (2)

> [!success]- Answer
> 5.1 Decline to act further; explain to the client that animosity may lead to conflict of interest; refer the client to another attorney.
> 5.2 Decline to act and refer the person to another (patent) attorney.

Related: [[Mandate Acceptance, Refusal and Withdrawal]]

## Q6 — What is a conflict of interest? (1)

> [!question] Question
> What is a conflict of interest?

> [!success]- Answer
> Anything which may adversely affect the judgment or loyalty to client of an attorney in advising or representing a client.
