## Q1 — Assessing a fair and reasonable fee (5)

> [!question] Question
> State briefly what factors an assessor appointed by a Law Society will take into account in assessing whether a fee charged was fair and reasonable.

> [!success]- Answer
> Any 5: amount and importance of the work done; complexity/difficulty/novelty of the work; skill, labour, specialised knowledge and responsibility involved; number and importance of documents prepared or perused; place and circumstances of the services; time expended; amount or value of money/property involved; importance of the matter to the client; quality of the work done; experience or seniority of the practitioner.

Related: [[Fee Assessment, Overreaching and Fee Sharing]]

## Q2 — Trust theft and prescribed claims (6)

> [!question] Question
> Your former bookkeeper has stolen a large sum of trust monies belonging to various clients. Clients' files are missing and a number of claims have become prescribed. Explain how the clients would recover their losses arising from: 2.1 the theft of the trust funds (4); 2.2 the claims which have become prescribed (2).

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.1 A claim must be lodged via the statutory Law Society to the Attorneys Fidelity Fund, charged with indemnifying clients who suffered loss from theft of money entrusted to the attorney in the course of practice. The Fund may require the attorney to be excussed before entertaining the claim. Time limits apply.
> 2.2 The client must consult another attorney to lodge a claim against the negligent attorney, who refers the claim to the AIIF. The attorney may not admit liability in respect of the prescribed claim, as the AIIF can then repudiate the claim.

Related: [[Trust Account Theft and the Attorneys Fidelity Fund]]

## Q3 — Competing mandates (4)

> [!question] Question
> A client approaches you with instructions. Before you accept, another client approaches with instructions producing a larger profit. Due to time constraints you cannot accept both. Must you accept the first mandate, or are you free to choose either? Motivate.

> [!success]- Answer
> The general rule is that a practitioner is entitled to accept or refuse any work offered unless for special reason(s) there is some obligation to accept or refuse it. The practitioner is free to accept whichever mandate he/she chooses. However, once a mandate is accepted it must be completed — though there are circumstances when an attorney may timeously withdraw.

Related: [[Mandate Acceptance, Refusal and Withdrawal]]

## Q4 — Deposit for costs in advance (5)

> [!question] Question
> You request a client to make a deposit for costs in advance. Once made: 4.1 does the deposit become the practitioner's property? (1) 4.2 which bank account must it be deposited into? (1) 4.3 when can it be used as fees? (1) 4.4 can you transfer an estimated fee amount from trust to business account mid-matter? (1) 4.5 if you may not, what are the consequences of doing so anyway? (1)

> [!success]- Answer
> 4.1 No. 4.2 Trust account. 4.3 When the matter is completed and you have rendered a proper statement of account to the client, unless interim fees were agreed in advance. 4.4 No. 4.5 It constitutes unprofessional conduct and renders you liable to be charged with theft of trust monies.

Related: [[Trust Account Rules — Deposits and Fee Transfers]]

## Q5 — Secretary theft of trust funds (5)

> [!question] Question
> You discover theft of R100 000 by your secretary from your firm's trust account, in a particular file.
> 5.1 Can your client institute a claim against you or your firm for the stolen money, and can you/your firm be held liable? (2)
> 5.2 Would the position differ if a candidate attorney or professional assistant had stolen the funds? Yes/no. (1)
> 5.3 With whom can your client lodge a claim if you/your firm are financially unable to repay? (1)
> 5.4 Within how many months must he lodge that claim? (1)

> [!success]- Answer
> 5.1 Yes — the client can institute a civil claim against the attorney or firm, and they can be held liable.
> 5.2 No.
> 5.3 Attorneys Fidelity Fund.
> 5.4 6 months.
