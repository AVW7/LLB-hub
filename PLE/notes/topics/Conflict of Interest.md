# Conflict of Interest

The underlying concept behind several other PLE rules (mandate refusal/withdrawal, borrowing from clients, acting for co-accused) but also tested as a standalone definition and in two related fact patterns: acting as commissioner of oaths for your own firm's client, and being called as a witness in your own client's matter.

## Definition

> "Anything which may adversely affect the judgment or loyalty to client of an attorney in advising or representing a client." — [[PLE 2015-05-30]] A6

## As a ground to refuse or withdraw from a mandate

Conflict of interest (or the possibility of one arising) is the first-listed ground in every "when must you refuse/withdraw" answer across the papers — see [[Mandate Acceptance, Refusal and Withdrawal]] for the full lists.

## Commissioner of oaths — firm-wide imputed interest

[[PLE 2015-11-14]] Q2: a senior partner at your firm cannot commission an affidavit for your own client, even urgently.
- **No** — clients are deemed to be represented by the whole firm, not just the individual attorney handling the file. Legislation bars anyone with an interest in the matter from acting as commissioner of oaths, and a partner in the instructed firm is deemed to have that interest.
- **Exceptions**: conveyancing transactions, and formal affidavits for government records. See [[Commissioner of Oaths Restrictions]] for the full rule.

## Attorney required as a witness in own client's case

[[PLE 2019-02-03 Assignment]] Q4.3 (no memo — self-check): if, before a civil trial, it transpires that you (the attorney) will be needed as a witness for your own client, this is a conflict-of-interest problem — an attorney should not act as both advocate and witness in the same matter, since your credibility as witness would be compromised by your role as the client's representative and vice versa. Reason from the general conflict-of-interest and withdrawal principles in [[Mandate Acceptance, Refusal and Withdrawal]] if this comes up: the proper course is to withdraw from acting and refer the client to another practitioner, or arrange for the matter to be handled by a colleague while you testify.
