# Interviewing an Opponent's Witness

A civil-practice etiquette rule: may you interview a witness who has already been subpoenaed, or has already testified, for the other side?

## The rule

> "Yes. No one has property in a witness. An attorney is entitled to interview any person who he has reason to believe is in possession of information which may assist his client's case, and cannot be deprived of this right by the fact that the other side has subpoenaed or taken a statement from such person. Etiquette requires that the other side should, however, be notified of the interview, and that notification should precede the interview. They cannot refuse or demand to be present. **Restriction: if the witness has commenced testifying, the other side is entitled to attend.**" — [[PLE 2016-05-28]] A3

Broken down:
- No party "owns" a witness — you may interview anyone with relevant information regardless of who called or subpoenaed them.
- **Courtesy/etiquette**: notify the other side **before** the interview.
- The other side cannot refuse the interview or insist on attending — **unless** the witness has already started testifying, in which case they are entitled to be present at any further interview.

## Two-stage variant: pre-summons vs mid-trial

[[PLE 2019-02-03 Assignment]] Q4.1–4.2 (no memo — self-check) splits this into two moments:
- **4.1** — before action is even instituted (no summons issued yet), you wish to consult with someone who will in due course be your opponent's witness. Your rights/obligations here are at their widest — apply the general rule above.
- **4.2** — the trial has commenced and the witness has already testified, but you now need to interview them. This is the **restricted** scenario flagged above — the other side is entitled to attend that interview.
