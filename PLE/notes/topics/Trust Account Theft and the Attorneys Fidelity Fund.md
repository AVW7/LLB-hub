# Trust Account Theft and the Attorneys Fidelity Fund

The recurring "your bookkeeper/secretary stole trust money" scenario — appears, in near-identical form, in more sittings than almost any other PLE question besides mandate refusal. Always paired with a sub-question on claims that prescribed while the theft was undiscovered.

## The core fact pattern

A bookkeeper, secretary, candidate attorney or professional assistant steals trust money; client files go missing; some claims prescribe in the meantime. What can the client do?

## Recovering the stolen trust money

> "A claim must be lodged via the statutory Law Society to the Attorneys Fidelity Fund, which is charged with the indemnification of clients who have suffered loss arising from the theft of money entrusted to the attorney in the course of his practice as such. The Fund may require the attorney to be excussed before they will entertain the client's claim. Time limits apply." — [[PLE 2016-06-15 Supp]] A2.1, restated in [[PLE 2016-06-28 Special Supp]] A2.1

- The Fidelity Fund indemnifies the public against loss arising from theft of trust money by an attorney, employee, candidate attorney or professional assistant. ([[PLE 2016-05-28]] A4.3)
- The client (or the firm, if it repays first) can institute a **civil claim against the attorney/firm**, who can be held liable — [[PLE 2015-11-26 Supp]] A7.1, [[PLE 2016-06-28 Special Supp]] A5.1. This applies **whether the theft was by a bookkeeper/secretary or a candidate attorney/professional assistant** — the "yes/no" question at [[PLE 2015-11-26 Supp]] A7.2 and [[PLE 2016-06-28 Special Supp]] A5.2 confirms the position does **not** change based on who stole the money.
- If the attorney/firm cannot repay, the client lodges a claim with the **Attorneys Fidelity Fund** — [[PLE 2015-11-26 Supp]] A7.3, [[PLE 2016-06-28 Special Supp]] A5.3.
- **Time limit: 6 months** to lodge that claim — [[PLE 2015-11-26 Supp]] A7.4, [[PLE 2016-06-28 Special Supp]] A5.4.
- Attorneys must not admit liability, as this can vitiate insurance/Fund cover.

## Claims that prescribed while files were missing

> "The client must be advised to consult another attorney to lodge a claim against the negligent attorney, who will then refer the claim to the AIIF [Attorneys' Indemnity Insurance Fund]. The attorney may not admit liability to the client in respect of the prescribed claim, as the AIIF can then repudiate the claim." — [[PLE 2016-06-15 Supp]] A2.2, restated in [[PLE 2016-06-28 Special Supp]] A2.2

This distinguishes two remedies tested together: **Fidelity Fund** for the stolen trust money itself, **AIIF** (professional indemnity insurance, via a *different* attorney's claim against the negligent one) for the separately-prescribed underlying claims.

## How the Fund is funded

[[PLE 2016-05-28]] A4.3 / [[PLE 2015-06-11 Supp]] A4: a practitioner or firm contributes to the Fund's income by **investing trust funds to earn interest for the benefit of the Fund**.

## Is the Fidelity Fund a fund of first instance?

[[PLE 2016-06-15 Supp]] Q9/A9 — **No.** (The attorney/firm must be pursued first; the Fund is the fallback once the attorney cannot pay.)

## Related fact pattern: security held by a disappeared attorney

[[PLE 2019-02-03 Assignment]] Q7 (no memo — self-check): a client deposited 10 Krugerrands with a previous attorney as security; that attorney has since disappeared and closed the practice. Reasoning from the principles above: this is the same underlying mischief as trust theft (property entrusted to an attorney, now unrecoverable from the attorney) — the client's remedy is a claim against the **Attorneys Fidelity Fund**, subject to the Fund's standard requirements (theft/misappropriation of property entrusted in the course of practice, time limits).
