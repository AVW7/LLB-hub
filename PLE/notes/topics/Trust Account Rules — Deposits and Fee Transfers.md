# Trust Account Rules — Deposits and Fee Transfers

Distinct from the trust-theft scenario ([[Trust Account Theft and the Attorneys Fidelity Fund]]): this topic is the mechanics of handling money that is properly in trust — deposits taken in advance, idle trust funds, and when/how fees may be transferred out.

## Deposits taken in advance of fees

[[PLE 2015-12-02 Special Supp]] Q4 (no memo, but the identical question is answered at [[PLE 2016-06-28 Special Supp]] A4):

- **4.1 Does the deposit become the practitioner's property?** No.
- **4.2 Which account must it go into?** The **trust account**.
- **4.3 When can the deposit be used as fees?** Only when the fees are due to you **and your books of account have been written up** — i.e. once the matter is complete (or an interim stage agreed) and a proper statement of account has been rendered, unless interim fees were agreed in advance.
- **4.4 May you transfer an *estimated* amount of fees mid-matter?** No.
- **4.5 Consequence of transferring an estimate anyway?** Unprofessional conduct — renders you liable to be charged with **theft of trust monies**.

## Idle trust funds held for a client

[[PLE 2015-12-02 Special Supp]] Q3 (no memo — self-check): holding a substantial amount in trust for a client that won't be needed for over a year.
- **3.1 The funds sitting idle** — you have a professional duty not to simply let client money sit non-interest-bearing; advise the client and arrange for the funds to be placed in a separate interest-bearing account for the client's benefit (a client-specific interest-bearing trust investment), rather than the general trust account (which only benefits the Fidelity Fund via interest).
- **3.2 Client offers you a short-term loan from those funds at favourable rates** — this raises the same **conflict of interest** problem as [[Borrowing From or Lending to Clients]]: not advisable; if you proceed, the client must get independent legal advice on the transaction.

## Own-name trust ledger accounts

[[PLE 2016-06-15 Supp]] Q8.2/A8.2 — an attorney may **not** hold a trust ledger account in his/her own name or the firm's name.
