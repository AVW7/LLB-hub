# Client Confidentiality and Privilege

Tested directly once as a list question, plus a related one-mark question on "without prejudice" communications (a species of privilege).

## Exceptions to attorney/client confidentiality

[[PLE 2015-11-14]] Q3.2 — instances when attorney/client confidentiality does **not** apply (any 3):
- Where legislation requires disclosure (e.g. FICA)
- Where the client informs you that he/she is going to commit a crime
- The client's name (not privileged)
- Where you have knowledge of an asset in a deceased client's estate of which the executor is unaware
- Information you obtained with your own senses (i.e. not communicated to you in confidence)

## "Without prejudice" communications

[[PLE 2016-06-15 Supp]] Q6: what does "without prejudice" mean?
> "The right of a party to make statements which cannot be proved against him/her." — [[PLE 2016-06-15 Supp]] A6

This is the settlement-negotiation privilege: statements made in a genuine attempt to settle a dispute cannot later be used as an admission against the party who made them.
