# Recusal of a Judicial Officer

The "the magistrate's son is the complainant" scenario — tested twice with an identical fact pattern and a clear escalation sequence to memorise.

## The escalation sequence

> "The magistrate should be requested to recuse himself. Before proceedings start, approach the magistrate in chambers to point out the apparent conflict of interest. Should the magistrate not accede to the request in chambers, proceed to make a formal application in court. Should the formal application still be unsuccessful, proceed with the case and take the matter on review or appeal in the event of the client being convicted." — [[PLE 2015-11-14]] A1.2

Four steps, in order:
1. Approach the magistrate **in chambers**, informally, before proceedings start — point out the apparent conflict.
2. If that fails, make a **formal application** in open court for recusal.
3. If the formal application also fails, **proceed with the case anyway** — you cannot simply refuse to appear.
4. If the client is convicted, take the matter on **review or appeal**, raising the recusal refusal as a ground.
