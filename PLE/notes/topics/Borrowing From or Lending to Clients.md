# Borrowing From or Lending to Clients

A small, consistently-worded rule tested three times: may an attorney borrow money from a client (and the mirror question, lending to one)?

## The rule

> "Not proper/not advisable unless the client is in the business of money lending. If not in the business and you decide to continue, the client [must] obtain independent legal advice on the money lending transaction — potential conflict of interest." — [[PLE 2015-05-30]] A5.2, restated at [[PLE 2016-05-28]] A5

Applied consistently: borrowing from an ordinary client is improper because of the conflict of interest and the imbalance of influence between attorney and client. It becomes acceptable only where:
1. The client is in the business of money lending (so the transaction is an arm's-length commercial one, not exploiting the attorney relationship), or
2. If you proceed despite the client not being a money-lender, the **client must get independent legal advice** on the transaction first.
