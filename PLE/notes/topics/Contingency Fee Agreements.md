# Contingency Fee Agreements

The "no win, no fee" question — client has no money, claim has merit, wants a fee arrangement contingent on success. Tested with identical structure across four sittings, plus a calculation variant.

## Is it permitted, and under what authority?

> "The Contingency Fees Act permits such an agreement." — [[PLE 2015-05-30]] A4.1, [[PLE 2015-11-26 Supp]] A4.1

Yes, an attorney may enter into a contingency fee agreement with a client.

## Formalities

> "The agreement must be in writing, in a form and lodged as prescribed by the Regulations [to the Contingency Fees Act]." — [[PLE 2015-05-30]] A4.2, [[PLE 2015-11-26 Supp]] A4.2

## Maximum fee

> "The practitioner may charge double his/her normal fee, or up to 25% of the amount awarded, whichever is the lesser." — [[PLE 2015-05-30]] A4.3, [[PLE 2015-11-26 Supp]] A4.3

> [!note] Marker's note preserved from the archive
> [[PLE 2015-11-26 Supp]] A4 flags that the position is not uniform in practice: the Law Society of the Northern Provinces accepted that members may choose between a Contingency Fees Act agreement or a common-law contingency arrangement. Markers were told to give full credit for all correct answers on this point.

## Does the cause of action matter?

[[PLE 2019-02-03 Assignment]] Q8.4 (no memo — self-check): if the claim were **defamation** rather than a money claim, would the fee rules differ? Reason from the Contingency Fees Act's scope — it applies to litigious matters generally, but check whether the specific type of claim affects the calculation base (the "amount awarded" cap is harder to apply to non-monetary/reputational relief). Flag for self-study; no source memo addresses this directly.

## Worked calculation

[[PLE 2019-02-03 Assignment]] Q12.1 (no memo — self-check): a 15% contingency fee agreement; court finds contributory negligence (A and B each 50% negligent), damages of R80 000 (A) and R20 000 (B) at issue. Work through: identify what "the amount awarded" to *your* client actually is after any apportionment, then apply the 15% and cross-check against the statutory cap (double normal fee or 25%, whichever is less) as in [[PLE 2015-05-30]] A4.3.
