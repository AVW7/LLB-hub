# Duty of Candour to the Court

The "you find adverse case law right before argument" question — one of the most consistently repeated fact patterns in the archive, always with the same two-part answer structure (duty to the court vs duty to your colleague).

## The rule

Tested in near-identical form across four sittings:

> "An attorney is bound to bring any adverse authority to the notice of the court, but need not inform the colleague, other than in the course of argument." — [[PLE 2015-11-14]] A1.1

Broken into its two limbs, as consistently asked:
- **Duty to the court**: an attorney's duty is first and foremost to the court, and the obligation is to assist the court in dispensing justice. You are obliged to bring to the court's attention cases that are both supportive of and adverse to your own case. ([[PLE 2015-06-11 Supp]] A7)
- **Duty to your colleague/opponent**: no similar obligation exists — you are not required to disclose adverse authority to your opponent directly, though it will come out in the course of argument before the court.
